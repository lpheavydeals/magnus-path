Working out how to sell jewellery online starts with one fact: jewellery is a trust purchase. Buyers are spending significant money on something small, shiny and hard to judge from a photo, and many fear being cheated on purity or stones. The brands that sell well online answer those fears before the buyer has to ask. This guide covers choosing your segment, hallmarking and HUID, pricing and making charges, lab-grown disclosure, photography, video and try-on, payments, insured shipping, returns and the seasons that drive demand in India.

We are an ecommerce growth team, not lawyers or gemmologists. Check regulatory points with your adviser and the relevant authorities.

## Choose your segment first

"Jewellery" covers very different businesses, and each sells online in its own way.

| Segment | Typical buyer behaviour | Online focus |
|---|---|---|
| Fashion and costume jewellery | Quick, often impulse purchases | Reels, catalogue ads, frequent new designs |
| Demi-fine and gold-plated silver | Self-purchase and gifting | Plating and material details, reviews, easy exchanges |
| Sterling silver | Everyday wear, gifting, temple and oxidised designs | Purity marks, care guides, styling content |
| Gold jewellery | Considered purchase, family input | Hallmark, weight, price breakup, consultations |
| Diamond and bridal | Weeks of research, often finished in store | Grading details, video calls, appointments |

Many brands combine segments, but plan each one separately. A fashion earring and a bridal necklace need different pages, ads, payment options and shipping.

## How to sell jewellery online: get hallmarking and HUID right

### BIS hallmarking for gold

In India, hallmarking by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) is mandatory for most gold jewellery sold in the districts covered by the rules. A hallmarked piece carries three marks: the BIS logo, the purity grade (for example 22K916 for 22 carat gold) and a six-character alphanumeric Hallmark Unique Identification number, or HUID. Buyers can check a HUID in the BIS Care app. Jewellers selling hallmarked gold need BIS registration, and some light pieces and categories are exempt.

Rules on districts, grades and exemptions have changed over time, and hallmarking for silver has been introduced on a voluntary basis. Confirm the current position before launch.

### Turn hallmarking into a selling point

- Show purity and hallmark details on every gold product page.
- Explain what the three marks mean in a short guide, with a photo of a real hallmark.
- Tell buyers how to verify a HUID themselves.
- Mention hallmarking in ads and on packaging inserts where it fits.

For silver, state the purity (such as 925 sterling silver) and any marks the piece carries. For plated jewellery, state the base metal, plating type and thickness if you know it, and give honest care advice.

## Pricing and making charges

Indian buyers know how gold jewellery is priced and compare jewellers closely. Show a price breakup on each product page:

| Component | How it is usually shown |
|---|---|
| Metal value | Net weight multiplied by the day's rate for that purity |
| Making charges | A rate per gram or a percentage of metal value |
| Stone value | Diamond or gemstone value, shown separately |
| Taxes | GST shown as a separate line |

If your prices follow the gold rate, use an app or integration that updates prices daily, and show when the rate was last updated. Explain why making charges differ between machine-made chains and handcrafted temple or bridal pieces; buyers accept higher making charges when they understand the work involved.

## Lab-grown and natural: disclose clearly

Lab-grown diamonds have become a large part of the online jewellery market. They are real diamonds chemically, but they are made in a laboratory, and buyers have a right to know which they are getting.

- Use "lab-grown" or "laboratory-grown" plainly in product titles, descriptions, filters, invoices and ads.
- Never use words that could suggest a natural stone for a lab-grown one.
- Share grading report details (cut, colour, clarity, carat and the issuing lab) where you have them.
- Offer separate collections or filters, so buyers can choose deliberately.
- Explain resale and buyback terms for each type honestly.

Regulators in India and abroad expect clear disclosure of lab-grown and treated stones. The same care applies to gemstones that are treated, heated or synthetic.

## Photography, video and try-on

Flat photos rarely sell jewellery. Buyers want to see sparkle, movement, size and how a piece sits on a real body.

### What to shoot for every hero piece

- Close-up photos from several angles on a neutral background
- A short video that shows the piece moving and catching light
- On-body shots on different skin tones
- A scale reference: on a hand, next to a coin, or with dimensions in millimetres
- The hallmark, clasp and back of the piece
- Packaging, so gift buyers know what arrives

### Sizing and try-on

Add ring and bangle size guides with printable sizers or measuring instructions, and chain-length visuals on a model. Virtual try-on tools for earrings and rings can help on platforms that support them. For gold and bridal pieces, a live video-call consultation often works better than any tool, because buyers can ask questions and involve family.

### Product page details

Every page should list metal, purity, weight, dimensions, stone details, hallmark information, care instructions, delivery time and the return policy. Missing details push cautious buyers back to a jeweller they know.

## Payments and order confirmation

Payment choices should follow the segment.

- **Fashion and silver:** offer UPI, cards and COD, and confirm COD orders by OTP or WhatsApp before dispatch.
- **Gold and diamond:** many brands limit COD, use a partial advance, or move high-value buyers to a video consultation before payment.
- **EMI and pay-later:** useful for higher-priced pieces where the payment provider allows them.
- **Custom orders:** take an advance and confirm design, size and timeline in writing.

## Insured shipping and packaging

Shipping valuables needs more care than ordinary parcels.

- Use couriers or valuables logistics providers that accept jewellery and offer insurance or declared-value cover.
- Check cover limits, exclusions and the claim process before shipping.
- Pack in plain outer boxes with no branding or description of contents.
- Use tamper-evident seals and record a packing video for high-value orders.
- Require OTP or signature on delivery, and share tracking on WhatsApp.
- Keep invoices, hallmark details and grading reports with the order record.

Fashion jewellery can go by standard courier, but sturdy packaging still prevents bent pieces and tangled chains.

## Returns, exchange and buyback

A clear policy reassures buyers more than a generous but vague one. Set separate terms for each segment:

- Fashion and silver: returns or exchange within a short window for unworn items with tags.
- Gold: exchange or buyback based on current metal value, with deductions stated clearly.
- Diamonds: exchange or buyback terms that state how natural and lab-grown stones are treated.
- Custom, engraved and resized pieces: usually excluded from returns.

Inspect returns on video, check hallmark and HUID against the order record, and keep evidence for disputes.

## Wedding and festive seasons

Jewellery demand in India follows the calendar. Akshaya Tritiya, Dhanteras and Diwali bring gold buying, and the wedding season drives bridal and gifting demand. Raksha Bandhan, Valentine's Day, Karva Chauth, Onam, Pongal and anniversaries matter for gifting.

- Plan bridal collections, shoots and stock a few months before the wedding season.
- Prepare gold coins and small pieces for Dhanteras and Akshaya Tritiya.
- Show delivery cut-off dates for every occasion, since insured shipping can take longer.
- Retarget enquiries and past buyers before each peak.

Your own store is where trust is built: full details, video, policies and consultations. Brands with showrooms should treat online as the start of the journey and track enquiries and visits alongside orders. ## Getting your first buyers

A new jewellery store has no reviews and no reputation, so the first months are about earning proof.

- **Start with a focused collection.** Twenty to forty pieces with full video and details sell better than hundreds of thin listings.
- **Use reels and creators.** Short styling videos, unboxing and "one piece, three looks" content suit fashion, silver and demi-fine ranges.
- **Run catalogue and search ads.** Meta catalogue ads work for lower-priced ranges; Google search ads on design and occasion terms, such as temple jewellery or lab-grown diamond rings, reach buyers with intent.
- **Ask every early buyer for a photo review.** Reviews with real photos on real people are the strongest trust signal a new brand can collect.
- **Offer consultations for higher-value pieces.** A WhatsApp number and a bookable video call reassure buyers who are not ready to order from a page.
- **Write helpful guides.** Explain hallmarks, ring sizing, lab-grown versus natural and jewellery care. These pages bring search traffic and answer buyers' questions before they ask.

## How to sell jewellery online: a launch checklist

- Segment chosen, with separate plans for fashion, silver, gold and diamond ranges
- BIS registration and hallmarking confirmed for gold, and purity marks stated for silver
- Price breakup with metal value, making charges, stones and taxes on every gold page
- Lab-grown and natural stones labelled clearly in titles, filters and invoices
- Close-up, on-body and scale video for every hero piece
- Size guides for rings, bangles and chains
- Payment rules by order value, with COD confirmation where offered
- Insured courier, tamper-evident packaging and OTP delivery set up
- Return, exchange and buyback terms written separately for each segment
- Collections, stock and creative planned for the next festive or wedding peak
