If you are researching how to start an ecommerce business in Abu Dhabi, the good news is that the emirate has a clear path for online sellers: a licence that permits e-commerce, a business bank account, tax registration when required, then a store, payments and delivery. The harder part is putting those pieces in the right order and making choices that still work once orders grow. This guide walks through each step for founders in the capital, Al Ain and the Al Dhafra region, and for overseas brands planning an Abu Dhabi base.

Everything below is general guidance. We are an ecommerce growth team, not lawyers or accountants, so check each step with a business setup adviser, an accountant and your bank.

## Step 1: Decide what you will sell and to whom

Before any paperwork, write down three things: the products, the buyer and how the order reaches them. "Abayas for working women in Abu Dhabi and Al Ain, delivered next day" is a plan. "Fashion for the UAE" is not.

The answers decide almost everything that follows:

- **Licence activities.** Food, cosmetics, perfume, supplements and electronics can each need specific activities or product approvals.
- **Stock and storage.** Imported goods need a company that can act as importer; perishable or heat-sensitive products need cool storage.
- **Language.** Many Emirati families browse in Arabic, while expat shoppers usually browse in English. Most stores need both.

Spend a week on Amazon.ae, Noon and Instagram looking at what already sells in your category, at what price and with what delivery promise.

## Step 2: Choose a licence route

### Mainland licence from the Abu Dhabi Department of Economic Development

The Abu Dhabi Department of Economic Development (ADDED) issues mainland trade licences. You choose one or more licensed activities, and e-commerce or online trading is available as an activity, alongside general trading or category-specific trading. A mainland licence usually gives the broadest freedom to sell straight to consumers anywhere in the UAE. ADDED has also offered licence types aimed at small, home-based and online traders over the years, so ask whether one fits your size and plans.

### Free zones

Abu Dhabi has several free zones, each with its own focus. In general terms:

| Free zone | Usual focus | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|
| ADGM (Abu Dhabi Global Market) | Financial and professional services | Its own legal framework; less common for a simple product store |
| KEZAD (Khalifa Economic Zones) | Industry, logistics and warehousing | Often considered by importers who need storage near the ports |
| Masdar City Free Zone | Technology, sustainability and start-ups | Popular with founders building tech-led brands |
| twofour54 | Media, content and creative businesses | Relevant if content or media is part of the business |

Free zone companies can be attractive for ownership structure, facilities or a particular industry. The rules on selling directly to UAE consumers from a free zone vary by zone and activity, so get the answer in writing before you commit.

### Questions to ask a business setup adviser

1. Which activities do I need for every product I plan to sell?
2. Can this licence sell to consumers across the UAE, including by cash on delivery?
3. Does it allow me to import stock, and who holds product registrations?
4. What premises or office space does it require?
5. How many visas does it support if I hire later?

## Step 3: Bank account, VAT and corporate tax

With a licence in hand, open a business bank account. Banks review your licence, ownership and business plan, and the process can take longer than the licence itself, so start early.

Tax registrations go through the Federal Tax Authority (FTA):

- **VAT.** Registration is mandatory once taxable sales pass the threshold set in UAE law, and voluntary below it. UAE VAT is 5%, and consumer prices are normally shown including VAT.
- **Corporate tax.** Most UAE businesses generally need to register for corporate tax, whatever their size. Whether tax is payable depends on profits and your structure.

An accountant can set up registrations, invoicing and filing dates. Keep your store's order exports tidy from day one, since they feed directly into tax returns.

## Step 4: Build a bilingual store

Your own store is where your margins, customer data and brand live, even if marketplaces bring most first orders.

- **Platform.** Shopify suits most new stores; WooCommerce suits teams already on WordPress.
- **Arabic and English.** A proper right-to-left Arabic version, not a widget that translates English on the fly. Product names, sizes and delivery messages should be written or checked by a fluent Arabic writer.
- **Prices.** In AED, including VAT, with delivery charges shown before checkout.
- **Policies.** Returns, delivery, privacy and terms written for UAE consumer protection and e-commerce rules, and reviewed by a lawyer. The UAE PDPL covers personal data.
- **Speed.** Most shoppers arrive on mobile, often on Instagram or Snapchat links, so test the store on a mid-range phone.

## Step 5: Payments, COD and BNPL

### Payment gateways

Choose a gateway that supports UAE cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay, settles in AED and works with your platform. Options include bank gateways and providers such as Network International, Telr, Checkout.com and Stripe. Compare settlement times, reserve requirements and how disputes are handled, not only the fees.

### Cash on delivery

Cash on delivery remains common in the UAE, especially for first orders from an unfamiliar brand. It also brings refused parcels, return shipping and cash that arrives weeks later through the courier. To keep it under control:

1. Confirm each COD order by WhatsApp before dispatch.
2. Track refusals by product, area and ad campaign.
3. Offer a small incentive for prepaid orders, such as free delivery.
4. Limit COD on very high-value items if refusals climb.

Run your own numbers through our [COD and returns cost calculator](/tools/rto-cod-calculator/) before deciding how much COD to allow.

### Buy now, pay later

Tabby and Tamara let shoppers split payments, and both are widely recognised. They tend to suit larger baskets such as furniture, electronics, jewellery and gift sets. Show the instalment option on the product page, not only at checkout.

## Step 6: Couriers and fulfilment

Abu Dhabi deliveries cover a wide area, from Al Reem Island and Khalifa City to Al Ain and the Al Dhafra region. Before you choose a courier, ask:

- Which areas get next-day delivery, and which take longer?
- How is COD cash collected and remitted, and how often?
- What happens to refused parcels and how quickly do they return?
- Are vans and warehouses air-conditioned for heat-sensitive goods?
- Can they ship to other GCC countries later?

Aramex, Emirates Post and a range of local same-day couriers serve the emirate. Many stores start by packing orders themselves, then move to a third-party warehouse once volumes grow. Fulfilled by Noon and Fulfilment by Amazon are options for marketplace orders.

## Step 7: Launch and your first ads

Start with a soft launch to friends, family and an early list, then fix whatever slows orders down before spending on ads.

### Your first ad tests

1. **Install tracking properly.** Meta pixel with Conversions API, Google Ads conversion tracking and GA4, checked against real orders.
2. **Test small.** Two or three products, two or three creatives, Arabic and English versions run separately.
3. **Read results by payment type.** A COD order is not revenue until it is accepted, so look at prepaid and COD returns separately.
4. **Scale the winners.** Move budget to products and creatives that make money after refusals and returns.

Instagram, Snapchat and TikTok are strong for discovery in the Gulf; Google Search catches buyers already looking for a product. If you work with creators, check that each holds a UAE Media Council advertiser permit and that paid posts are clearly labelled.

### Plan around the calendar

Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, UAE National Day on 2 December, White Friday and the Dubai Shopping Festival shape demand across the year, and summer is usually quieter as families travel. Launching in early autumn gives you time to learn before the busy cooler months.

## Common first-year mistakes

A few problems come up again and again with new Abu Dhabi stores:

- **Choosing a licence before the product list is final.** Adding activities later is possible but slower; settle the range first.
- **Treating Arabic as a plug-in.** A machine-translated store loses the shoppers who read Arabic first, and they are often the most loyal ones.
- **Judging ads on placed orders.** If a large share of orders are COD, a campaign can look profitable in Ads Manager while losing money once refusals come back.
- **Ignoring the summer.** Many families travel in the hottest months. Use that time to fix pages, photograph new products and test creative, rather than switching everything off.
- **Running out of stock at the peak.** Imported goods can take weeks to arrive, so order for Ramadan, Eid and National Day well ahead.
- **Keeping accounts in someone else's name.** Your domain, store, ad accounts and business portfolio should all be registered to your company from the start.
