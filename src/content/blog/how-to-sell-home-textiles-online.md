If you are working out how to sell home textiles online, the Kongu region gives you a head start. Karur is known for home textile exports such as bed linen, curtains and kitchen linen, Erode for bedsheets, towels and handloom, and Coimbatore for spinning, weaving and finishing. Much of this output goes abroad under other brands' labels. Selling online under your own name means new skills: honest fabric descriptions, photographs that sell, size guides for Indian homes, and handling returns parcel by parcel. This guide walks through each step.

## Why home textile makers are moving online

Export orders can swing with overseas demand, and the retail margin sits with the brand that sells to the household. An own online brand gives a mill:

- **Retail pricing** rather than a buyer's target price
- **Direct feedback** on colours, sizes and fabrics that sell
- **Steadier use of capacity** between large export orders
- **A name buyers remember**, which a supplier relationship never gives you

Many units keep export and contract work as the core business and build the brand beside it.

## Step 1: Choose a focused range

Do not list everything the mill can make. Pick a small range of heroes:

1. **Bedsheet sets** in the sizes Indian buyers use most, in a few colours that photograph well
2. **Towels** in bath, hand and face sizes, sold singly and in sets
3. **One furnishing line**, such as curtains, cushion covers or table linen, if you make it well

Choose products that ship flat, do not break and have clear sizes. Avoid launching with heavy rugs or custom-size items until the basics run smoothly.

## Step 2: Keep export surplus apart from the brand

Surplus and seconds are tempting to sell online, but they can harm a new brand.

- Check every buyer contract; many forbid resale of their designs or labelled goods.
- Remove buyer labels where resale is allowed.
- Sell surplus as a clearly named outlet range or through a separate account.
- Never mix seconds into your main listings; one faulty sheet brings a review that stays for years.

## Step 3: Be honest about thread count and fabric

Home textile buyers often compare on numbers they do not fully understand. Honesty here builds trust and cuts returns.

### Bedsheets

Thread count is the number of threads per square inch. It matters, but fibre, yarn, weave and finishing matter as much. Some sellers quote inflated counts by counting multi-ply yarns. State the count as measured, and add:

- Fibre: cotton, cotton blend, linen or polyester, with percentages
- Weave: percale, sateen, twill or plain
- Finish: mercerised, pre-shrunk, wrinkle-resistant, if true
- Feel in plain words: crisp, soft, cool, heavy

### Towels

GSM, grams per square metre, tells buyers how heavy and absorbent a towel is likely to be. Give the GSM, size, fibre and pile type, and describe how it feels after a few washes.

### Wash care and colour fastness

Give clear wash instructions and say honestly whether dark colours may bleed in the first wash. Buyers forgive a known behaviour; they do not forgive a surprise.

### Labels

Retail packs need the Legal Metrology declarations: manufacturer or marketer details, net quantity or number of pieces, size, month and year of manufacture, MRP and consumer care contact. Check fibre content labelling with your adviser.

## Step 4: Photograph textiles so buyers can feel them

Flat-lay photos made for export catalogues rarely sell to retail buyers. Plan a photo set for every product:

| Shot | What it tells the buyer |
|---|---|
| On a made bed or rail | How it looks at home |
| Close-up of the weave | Texture and quality |
| Fold and drape | Weight and softness |
| Corner and elastic | How fitted sheets hold the mattress |
| Size callout | Dimensions on the image itself |
| Colour in daylight | True shade, not studio-tinted |
| Pack contents | Exactly what arrives |

A short video of a hand running over the fabric, or a towel being folded, often helps more than another still photo. Shoot in natural light and keep colours true; colour mismatch is a common return reason.

## Step 5: Size guides for Indian homes

Indian bed and mattress sizes vary, and window and door heights are not standard everywhere. Publish:

- Bedsheet sizes in centimetres and inches, with the mattress sizes they fit
- Fitted sheet pocket depth, for thicker mattresses
- Curtain widths and drops, with a simple "how to measure your window" guide
- Towel sizes for bath, hand and face

Size guides cut returns and reduce pre-sale WhatsApp questions.

## Step 6: Packaging and pricing for retail

### Packaging that feels like a gift

Export packing is built for cartons and pallets. Retail buyers judge the brand the moment they open the parcel. Plan:

- A simple branded band, box or reusable fabric pouch for bedsheet sets
- A printed card with fibre, size and wash care, so the details stay with the product
- A polybag inside the courier box to keep textiles dry during the monsoon
- Folding that shows the print or border first

Bedsheet sets and towel bundles are popular gifts for housewarmings, weddings and Deepavali, so a gift-ready pack can widen your audience.

### Pricing beyond the mill cost

Mills know their cost per metre and per piece well. Retail adds packaging, shipping, returns, marketplace fees, ads and discounts during sale events. Work out the price per delivered order, not per piece made, and leave room for sale-season offers without selling at a loss. ## Step 7: Pick your channels

### Your own store

Your store holds your brand story, the mill behind it and your full range. Add bundles such as bedsheet plus pillow covers, a WhatsApp button and clear delivery and return terms. ### Amazon and Flipkart

Most home textile searches start here. Fill in every attribute, including size, thread count, GSM and fibre, so your listings appear in filters. Collect genuine reviews and plan stock for the big sale events and Deepavali.

### Home and lifestyle marketplaces

Designed or higher-priced ranges can suit home-focused marketplaces and the home sections of fashion platforms. Check onboarding needs; some review brand, catalogue and photography first.

## Step 8: Handle returns and COD

Expect returns for size, colour and feel. Reduce them by:

- Matching photos and descriptions exactly to the product
- Offering exchanges for size before refunds
- Confirming COD orders on WhatsApp before dispatch
- Tracking returns by product, size and reason, and fixing the listing when a reason repeats
- Inspecting returned items before restocking; send used or damaged pieces to the outlet range

## Step 9: Sell abroad under your own name

Kongu region mills already meet overseas quality expectations, which helps when selling your own brand abroad.

- **Amazon Global Selling:** branded bedsheets and towels with steady demand
- **Etsy:** designed, hand-block printed or handloom pieces you make yourself; see our guide on [how to sell on Etsy from India](/blog/how-to-sell-on-etsy-from-india/)
- **Own store with international shipping:** diaspora buyers who want Indian cotton and designs

Check each country's bed sizes, labelling and fibre declaration rules, and price for shipping weight before you launch.

## Step 10: Marketing that suits home textiles

- Instagram and Pinterest posts of real rooms styled with your range
- Reels showing fabric texture, folding and washing
- Owner and mill videos from Karur, Erode or Coimbatore in Tamil and English
- Seasonal edits for Pongal, Deepavali, weddings and housewarmings
- Customer photos shared with permission

## Common mistakes

- Launching the whole export catalogue instead of a few heroes
- Inflated thread counts that lead to bad reviews
- Export flat-lays used as retail photos
- No size guide for mattress depth or window drop
- Mixing seconds into the main brand

## How to sell home textiles online: a checklist

- Focused hero range chosen
- Export surplus kept apart, buyer contracts checked
- Thread count, GSM, fibre, weave and care stated honestly
- Photo set and fabric video for every product
- Size guides for beds, mattresses, windows and towels
- Own store and one large marketplace live
- Returns tracked by reason, COD confirmed on WhatsApp
- Export tests planned once domestic basics work
