Working out how to sell shoes online? USA shoppers make it both easier and harder than you might expect. Easier because they buy footwear online constantly and know their size in a shared system. Harder because they expect free or cheap returns, many order two sizes to be safe, and width matters more than most product pages admit. This guide covers the steps that decide whether a pair stays sold: range, sizing, photos, labeling, channels, returns and ads.

## Step 1: Define your range and buyer

"Shoes" covers very different businesses. Write down four things before you build pages or ads:

- **Category.** Running, walking, court sneakers, work boots, hiking, dress shoes, sandals and slides, slippers, kids' shoes.
- **Buyer.** Who pays and who wears. Parents buy most kids' shoes; gift buyers pick slippers and boots at the holidays.
- **Price position.** Value, mid-market or premium. It decides your return terms, marketplace mix and how much you can pay per order in ads.
- **Hero styles.** Two to five styles you will push hardest, in the sizes and colors you can keep in stock.

### Know what US shoppers compare

Runners compare cushioning, heel-to-toe drop, weight and width availability. Boot buyers want materials, waterproofing and break-in time. Sneaker fans follow colorways and release dates. Parents care about grip, easy closures and how fast kids outgrow a pair. Your product pages should lead with whatever your buyer compares first.

## Step 2: Base every size chart on foot length

The size chart is the cheapest returns tool you have.

### How to build the chart

1. **Measure each last.** Two styles built on different lasts can fit differently at the same size. Measure a sample of every size you sell.
2. **Use foot length.** List the foot length each size fits, in inches with centimeters alongside.
3. **Separate men's and women's scales.** The same foot length usually lands on a higher number in women's sizing, so never show one shared column.
4. **Add conversions.** EU and UK sizes help shoppers who know another system.
5. **Explain how to measure.** Stand on paper, mark the heel and longest toe, measure, and use the longer foot.

### Example layout

| Foot length (in) | Foot length (cm) | US men's | US women's | EU |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Your measured value | Converted | Your size | Your size | Your size |

Fill this with measurements from your own shoes, not a generic chart from the internet.

### Widths and fit notes

US buyers often know whether they need a wide fit. Show width letters and what they mean, for example men's D as medium and 2E or 4E as wide and extra wide, women's B as medium and D as wide. Then add a fit note to every style: "true to size", "runs about a half size small", "roomy toe box", "snug through the midfoot". Update notes from review comments and exchange reasons.

## Step 3: Photograph and film the shoe as it really is

Shoppers cannot pick the shoe up, so images have to do the job of a store visit:

- Side, front, back, top and sole views on a clean background.
- Close-ups of materials, stitching, lining, insole and tread.
- On-foot photos on real people with different foot shapes.
- A short walking clip and a flex test showing how the sole bends.
- Accurate color under daylight, so the box that arrives matches the screen.

Follow each marketplace's image rules, such as the white-background main image on Amazon US.

## Step 4: Get labeling and claims right

We are not lawyers, so confirm specifics with counsel, but these areas come up for most US shoe brands:

- **Country of origin.** Imported shoes are generally marked with their country of origin under customs rules, and many brands repeat it on product pages. Keep it consistent across listings.
- **Made in USA.** The FTC expects an unqualified "Made in USA" claim to mean all or virtually all of the product is made here. Shoes assembled in the US from imported parts usually need a qualified claim.
- **Materials.** Describe uppers, linings and soles accurately. Words such as "genuine leather", "vegan leather" or "recycled" need to match what the shoe is made of, and environmental claims need support.
- **Comfort, not medicine.** "Arch support" and "cushioned midsole" describe the product. Claims that a shoe treats a foot condition need strong evidence and can raise regulatory questions.
- **Kids' shoes.** Styles for children 12 and under are generally children's products, with testing and certificate requirements.
- **Prop 65.** Some materials may need California warnings; check with your suppliers and counsel.

## Step 5: Choose where to sell

### Your own store

A Shopify store holds the full range, every width, exclusive colorways, your fit finder and the customer list you use for launches. It is also where exchanges are easiest to manage. Our [footwear marketing agency page for US brands](/us/footwear-marketing-agency/) shows how we plan store, ads and marketplaces together.

### Amazon US

Most US shoe buyers check Amazon at some point. Build variation families by size, width and color, upload your own chart rather than relying on a generic one, and answer fit questions in bullets and A+ content.

### Walmart Marketplace, TikTok Shop and eBay

Walmart Marketplace reaches value-minded families and works for everyday and kids' styles. TikTok Shop suits trend-led sneakers, slides and boots that look good on foot in short video. eBay is strong for sneakers, limited releases and collectors, and buyers there expect authenticity details.

### Retail context

Zappos built American expectations around generous returns and helpful service. You do not need to match every policy, but buyers will compare your terms with that standard.

### Keep everything consistent

Use the same charts, fit notes, photos and prices across channels. Mismatched size information is one of the fastest ways to collect bad reviews.

## Step 6: Set returns and exchanges that protect margin

Most shoppers who order the wrong size want the right size, not their money back. Design the policy around that:

| Policy choice | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| Free size exchanges | Turns potential refunds into kept sales |
| Clear return window | Fewer disputes and support emails |
| "Try indoors on clean floors" note | Returned pairs can be resold at full price |
| Small fee on refunds, if you choose | Discourages ordering several sizes to send back |
| Stock held back for swaps in big sales | Exchanges ship before the size sells out |
| Return reasons recorded by style and size | Shows which charts or photos to fix |

## Step 7: Run ads on a clean catalog

Footwear catalogs are large: one style can have many colors, sizes and widths. Catalog ads on Meta and Shopping ads on Google can match the right variant to each shopper, but only with a clean feed.

### Feed checklist

- Size, size system (US), color, gender and age group filled in.
- Width in titles or attributes where you sell more than medium.
- Variants grouped under the right parent.
- Sold-out sizes removed quickly, so you do not pay for clicks you cannot fill.
- GTINs for every variant, especially for Amazon and Google.

### Where to advertise

Google Shopping and search catch shoppers typing specific needs, such as wide toe box running shoes or waterproof work boots. Meta and TikTok work for discovery with on-foot video and creator try-ons. Amazon Sponsored Products cover shoppers already on the marketplace. Judge every campaign on net sales after returns, not gross orders.

## How to sell shoes online (USA): listings that answer fit questions

### Titles that match searches

Include brand, style, gender, category and a key feature, such as "Women's Wide Width Walking Shoe, Slip-Resistant". Avoid stuffing every keyword into one line.

### Descriptions that cover fit, materials and care

Cover upper and lining materials, closure type, sole and tread, weight, heel height, fit notes, width options and care instructions. Short and scannable beats long and vague.

### Reviews and questions

Ask for reviews after the shoes have been worn for a week or two, and prompt buyers to mention fit. Answer marketplace questions quickly; those answers become part of the listing.

### Price and margin

Shoes carry hidden costs: return shipping, inspection, repackaging and markdowns on pairs that come back worn. Before you set prices, work out margin per pair after marketplace fees, ad cost and your expected return rate by style. Our [profit margin calculator](/tools/profit-margin-calculator/) makes the sums quick. A style that looks profitable on gross sales can lose money once returns are counted.

## The US footwear calendar

Plan stock by size around spring running season, Prime Day in July, back to school in late summer, fall boots, Black Friday and Cyber Monday, and holiday slippers and gifts. January brings New Year fitness resolutions, which lift running and training shoes.

## Common mistakes when selling shoes online

- Copying a generic size chart instead of measuring your own lasts.
- Hiding width or offering only medium without saying so.
- Promoting styles with broken size runs.
- Making refunds easier than exchanges.
- Making origin or material claims you cannot support.

## A simple launch checklist

- A measured size chart for every last, in inches and centimeters.
- Width options and fit notes on every style.
- Five or more images plus an on-foot clip per hero style.
- Origin, materials and care details checked against your supplier documents.
- Exchange and return terms visible on product pages.
- A clean feed with GTINs, sizes and widths, ready for Google and Meta.
