Knowing how to find buyers for export comes down to three things: choosing the right markets, being visible where importers search, and following up faster and more professionally than your competitors. For Indian manufacturers, especially in Tamil Nadu clusters like Chennai, Coimbatore, Tiruppur and Hosur, the opportunity is real. Tamil Nadu's electronics exports rose about 78% to roughly US$9.56 billion in FY24, around a third of India's total (KNN India). This guide covers every major channel, how to check buyers before you ship, and a 90-day plan you can start this week.

## Step 1: Research your product and target markets

Do not try to sell to the whole world. Pick two or three markets where demand is proven and where you can compete.

- **Find your HS code.** Your product's Harmonised System code is the key to every trade database and customs record.
- **Check import data.** Use free government and international trade databases to see which countries import your product, in what volumes and from which suppliers.
- **Study competitors.** Look at which Indian and overseas suppliers already sell there, their pricing and how they present themselves online.
- **Check regulations.** Certifications, labelling rules, testing requirements and import duties vary by country. These can rule a market in or out.
- **Match your strengths.** If you are strong on small batches and customisation, target buyers who value flexibility over the lowest price.

Write a one-page profile for each target market: buyer types, typical order sizes, certifications needed and main competitors. Every channel below works better when you know exactly who you are looking for.

## Step 2: Use export promotion councils and trade fairs

Export promotion councils (EPCs) exist to help Indian exporters find markets. Examples include AEPC for apparel and EEPC India for engineering goods, with councils for most major sectors. Membership typically gives you:

- Access to buyer enquiries and trade leads.
- Subsidised participation in international trade fairs and buyer-seller meets.
- Market reports, policy updates and export scheme guidance.

**Trade fairs** remain one of the best ways to meet serious buyers face to face. Before you go, research the exhibitor and visitor lists, book meetings in advance through email and LinkedIn, and prepare samples, catalogues and a price sheet. After the fair, follow up within a few days, while the buyer still remembers you.

Reverse buyer-seller meets held in India, where overseas buyers visit, are often cheaper than travelling abroad and worth tracking through your council.

## Step 3: List on B2B marketplaces

B2B marketplaces put your products in front of buyers who are actively searching.

- **IndiaMART and TradeIndia** are strong for domestic B2B buyers and also attract some overseas enquiries.
- **Alibaba and other global B2B platforms** are built for international sourcing and connect you with importers worldwide.

To get results from any marketplace:

- Complete your profile fully: factory details, certifications, capacity and photos.
- Write product titles and descriptions with the exact terms buyers search, including specifications and materials.
- Use real, high-quality photos of products, packaging and your facility.
- Reply to enquiries quickly. Speed of response is often what wins the first conversation.

Expect a mix of genuine buyers, small traders and spam. Qualify every lead before investing time in samples.

## Step 4: Build an export website with an RFQ form

Serious importers check your website before they reply to your email. A weak or outdated site loses deals quietly.

An export website should include:

- **Product pages with full specifications:** dimensions, materials, grades, tolerances, packaging and MOQ.
- **A "Request a Quote" (RFQ) form** on every product page, asking for product, quantity, destination country and timeline.
- **Trust signals:** certifications, test reports, factory photos and videos, quality process and export markets served.
- **Clear contact options:** business email, phone with country code and WhatsApp.
- **Fast loading** and a clean mobile layout, since many buyers first view your site on their phone.

## Step 5: Use export SEO to be found by importers

Export SEO means ranking your website for the searches overseas buyers make, such as "[product] manufacturer India", "[product] supplier" or "[material] [product] wholesale".

- **Target buyer-intent keywords.** Focus on words like manufacturer, supplier, exporter, wholesale and bulk, combined with your product names and specifications.
- **One page per product or product family.** Each with unique content, specifications and an RFQ form.
- **Write for target markets.** Use the terms and units buyers in that country use. Consider language versions for non-English markets.
- **Add helpful content.** Buying guides, material comparisons and specification sheets attract engineers and procurement teams.
- **Prepare for AI answers.** Clear, factual pages with specifications and FAQs are more likely to be quoted by Google AI Overviews and AI assistants.

## Step 6: LinkedIn outreach and LinkedIn ads

LinkedIn is where procurement managers, sourcing heads and importers spend their working time.

**Outreach:**
- Optimise your personal and company profiles with clear product and export details.
- Search by job title (purchase manager, sourcing manager, category buyer), industry and country.
- Send short, personal connection requests. Do not pitch in the first message.
- Share useful posts: factory updates, new products, quality processes and shipments.

**LinkedIn ads:**
- Target by job title, industry, company size and country.
- Promote a specific offer, such as a product catalogue download or sample request.
- Start with a small budget in one market, measure enquiries and expand only what works.

## Step 7: Google Ads for international buyer searches

Google Ads lets you appear the moment an importer searches for your product. It is faster than SEO and highly targeted.

- **Target specific countries,** not "all locations".
- **Use buyer-intent keywords** like "supplier", "manufacturer" and "wholesale". Add negative keywords like "jobs", "retail", "DIY" and "free".
- **Send traffic to product pages with an RFQ form,** not your homepage.
- **Track every form submission and WhatsApp click** so you know which keywords bring real buyers.
- **Schedule ads for the buyer's working hours** in their time zone.

## Step 8: Appoint distributors and agents

In many markets, buyers prefer to work through a local distributor or agent who handles language, logistics and after-sales service.

- **Distributors** buy your product and resell it. They carry stock and credit risk.
- **Agents** find buyers for you and earn a commission. You still sell and ship directly.

Find them through trade fairs, chambers of commerce, LinkedIn and your EPC. Start with a trial period and clear targets before granting exclusivity. Put territory, pricing, commission, payment terms and termination clearly in writing.

## Step 9: Handle enquiries like a professional exporter

Many exporters lose buyers after the first enquiry, not before it. Importers compare several suppliers at once, and the one who replies clearly and quickly usually gets the next conversation.

A strong first reply includes:

- **A thank-you and a short company introduction:** what you make, where your factory is and which markets you already serve.
- **Answers to their exact questions:** specifications, MOQ, lead time and packaging.
- **A clear quotation:** price per unit, currency, Incoterm (such as FOB Chennai or CIF destination port), validity date and payment terms.
- **Supporting documents:** catalogue, certifications and test reports as attachments or links.
- **A next step:** an offer of samples, a video call or a factory visit.

Keep a simple CRM or shared spreadsheet of every enquiry, its source, status and next follow-up date. This also tells you which channels bring real buyers, so you can spend more where it works.

## Step 10: Verify buyers and protect your payments

Finding buyers is only half the job. Getting paid safely matters just as much.

### Check the buyer
- Confirm company registration through official business registries in the buyer's country.
- Check their website, address, phone numbers and online footprint.
- Ask for trade references and speak to them.
- Hold a video call and, for large orders, consider a visit.
- Watch for warning signs: urgent shipment pressure, free email addresses, changing bank details or unusual payment requests.

### Choose safe payment terms

| Payment method | Risk to exporter | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Full advance payment | Lowest | New buyers, samples, small orders |
| Partial advance + balance before shipment | Low | Early repeat orders |
| Confirmed letter of credit (LC) | Low, if documents are correct | Larger orders with new buyers |
| Documents against payment (D/P) | Medium | Buyers with some track record |
| Open account / credit terms | Highest | Long-standing, trusted buyers, ideally with export credit insurance |

With letters of credit, every document must match the LC terms exactly, so work closely with your bank. For credit terms, export credit insurance from ECGC can protect you against buyer default.

## A 90-day plan to find your first export buyers

### Days 1–30: Foundations
- Finalise your HS code, two or three target markets and a buyer profile for each.
- Confirm your IEC, GST and EPC membership.
- Prepare a product catalogue, price sheet, specification sheets and certifications.
- Fix or build your export website with product pages and an RFQ form.
- Set up complete profiles on one global and one Indian B2B marketplace.
- Optimise your LinkedIn company page and personal profile.

### Days 31–60: Outreach and visibility
- Build a list of 100–200 target importers per market from directories, LinkedIn and fair exhibitor lists.
- Start LinkedIn outreach daily and send personalised emails to shortlisted buyers.
- Publish SEO-focused product pages and one or two buying guides.
- Launch a small Google Ads campaign in your strongest market.
- Shortlist upcoming trade fairs and buyer-seller meets through your EPC.

### Days 61–90: Convert and refine
- Follow up on every enquiry within one working day.
- Send samples on clear terms to qualified buyers only.
- Review which channels brought real enquiries and shift budget towards them.
- Approach potential distributors or agents in your best-responding market.
- Set monthly targets for enquiries, samples and orders.

Export sales cycles are often long, and first orders can take time. Consistency across these channels is what builds a reliable pipeline.
