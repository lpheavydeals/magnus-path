The ecommerce website cost in India ranges from little more than your own time for a do-it-yourself template store to a significant project budget for a custom or headless build. Most growing brands land in the middle: a styled template store set up by an agency, or a custom Shopify build once they outgrow it. The setup fee is only part of the bill, though. This guide explains what moves the cost, separates one-time from recurring costs, and shows how to set a budget and compare quotes without overpaying.

## Ecommerce website cost in India by store type

There are four common routes. Each one costs more than the one before it, because each adds design, development and testing work. The table shows what you get and what usually pushes each route's cost up or keeps it down.

| Store type | Best for | What raises the cost | What keeps it lower |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIY template store | Testing an idea, very small catalogue | Paid theme, many paid apps, fixing your own setup mistakes | Free theme, few products, simple shipping |
| Template + agency setup | New D2C brands, small to mid catalogues | Large product upload, many variants, extra integrations | Clean product spreadsheet, ready photos and copy |
| Custom Shopify store | Brands with a clear identity and growing sales | Many custom sections, complex filters, ERP or CRM links | Reusing a good theme as the base, a clear brief |
| Custom or headless build | Complex catalogues, B2B price lists, unusual workflows | Bespoke features, multiple warehouses, heavy integration | Phased launch, starting with the must-have features |

A few notes on reading this table:

- **DIY is not free.** You still pay the platform plan, a domain, possibly a premium theme, and your own hours. Mistakes in payments, tax or shipping settings can cost more than an agency would have.
- **Template does not mean generic.** A good template store is styled to your brand, structured for search and fast on mobile. Most new brands should start here.
- **Custom is worth it when the template blocks you.** If your product needs a configurator, bundles, B2B price lists or deep ERP integration, a custom build pays for itself over time.

## One-time and recurring costs

It helps to sort every cost into three groups before you speak to anyone.

**One-time costs** are paid once, at the start: the agency or developer fee, a premium theme licence, product photography, product descriptions and category copy, data migration from an old site, and any setup work for tax, shipping and payments.

**Recurring costs** continue every month or year: the platform plan or hosting, domain renewal, business email, paid apps and plugin licences, and maintenance or a support retainer.

**Per-sale costs** scale with orders: payment gateway fees, shipping charges, and returns or cash-on-delivery losses. These are easy to forget because they are small per order, but they apply for the life of the store.

| Recurring cost | Shopify | WooCommerce | Custom / headless |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform or software | Monthly Shopify plan (check current India pricing) | Free core software | Depends on stack; may include licences |
| Hosting | Included in plan | Paid separately, monthly or yearly | Paid separately, often cloud hosting |
| Domain | Yearly renewal | Yearly renewal | Yearly renewal |
| Payment gateway | Per-transaction fee; check your gateway | Per-transaction fee; check your gateway | Per-transaction fee; check your gateway |
| Apps and plugins | Monthly app fees | Yearly plugin licences | Custom code or third-party services |
| Maintenance | Light: theme and app updates | Regular: core, plugin and security updates | Developer retainer usually needed |

Payment gateway fees deserve a closer look. Most Indian gateways charge a percentage per transaction, with different rates for some cards, international payments or EMI. Check the provider's current pricing before you launch, because the fee applies to every order.

## Shopify and WooCommerce: where the money goes

**Shopify** is hosted, so you do not pay for hosting separately. Your costs are the build, the monthly plan (which rises with the plan tier), an optional premium theme, and paid apps for reviews, subscriptions, WhatsApp, upsells and loyalty. Shopify suits most D2C brands because it is stable, secure and quick to manage without a developer. See our [Shopify store development service](/shopify-development/) if you want a team to build it.

**WooCommerce** is a free plugin that runs on WordPress. The software costs nothing, but the running costs move elsewhere: good managed hosting, yearly plugin licences, and regular updates, backups and security checks. Cheap shared hosting is the most common reason WooCommerce stores feel slow. It makes sense if you already run WordPress, want full control of your data and code, or need content-heavy pages alongside the shop. Our [WooCommerce development team](/woocommerce-development/) can help you decide if it fits.

## Factors that change ecommerce website development cost

Two stores on the same platform can differ in cost by several times. These factors drive ecommerce website development cost the most:

- **Design approach.** A styled template is the most economical route. Custom page designs, custom product page layouts and brand-specific sections add design and development time.
- **Catalogue size and product data.** Fifty products with clean data upload quickly. Thousands of products with variants, size charts and missing images take real effort. A clean spreadsheet saves money.
- **Integrations.** Each one adds scope: shipping aggregators, ERP or inventory software, accounting tools, WhatsApp, CRM, marketplaces like Amazon and Flipkart, or B2B quote systems.
- **Features beyond the basics.** Subscriptions, bundles, product customisers, multi-currency selling, wholesale price lists, loyalty programmes and multi-language stores all increase cost.
- **Content and photography.** Product photos, lifestyle shoots, descriptions and category copy are often left out of quotes, yet they matter for both sales and SEO.
- **Speed, SEO and conversion work.** A store that loads fast on mobile, has clean URLs and structured data, and keeps checkout simple takes more care to build. It also sells more, so this is not the place to cut.

## Hidden costs most brands miss

These are the line items that surprise founders after launch:

- **Paid apps that add up.** Five apps with a modest monthly fee each quickly become a meaningful recurring cost.
- **Premium theme licence.** Often not included in the build fee.
- **Product photography and copy.** Needed before launch, not after.
- **Tax and invoicing setup.** GST-compliant invoices and correct tax settings may need an app or accountant time.
- **Post-launch fixes.** Small changes in the first few months are normal. Check whether your quote includes a support period.
- **Migration.** Moving from an old site means redirects, data transfer and SEO protection. Skipping redirects can lose rankings you already have.
- **Marketing.** A store with no traffic makes no sales. Budget for ads, SEO or marketplace work from the start.

## How to set a first-year budget

Instead of asking "what does a website cost?", work out what the store costs to own for the first twelve months. List these lines in a simple spreadsheet:

1. **One-time setup:** the agency or developer fee for your chosen route.
2. **Pre-launch content:** product photography, descriptions and any brand assets you still need.
3. **Platform and hosting:** twelve months of your Shopify plan, or hosting plus plugin licences for WooCommerce.
4. **Apps:** each paid app's monthly fee multiplied by twelve.
5. **Domain and email:** yearly renewals.
6. **Payment fees:** your gateway's rate applied to your expected sales.
7. **Maintenance:** a support retainer or a budget for changes.
8. **Marketing:** ads, SEO or marketplace management to bring buyers to the store.

This view often changes how you judge the ecommerce website cost in India. A cheaper setup that needs many paid plugins and frequent developer help can cost more over a year than a slightly larger, cleaner build. A good rule: spend enough to get a fast, trustworthy, mobile-friendly store, then put the rest into getting customers.

## How to compare quotes and spot red flags

Send every agency the same brief and ask the same questions:

- What exactly is included: pages, sections, number of products uploaded, integrations?
- Is the theme free or premium, and who pays for the licence?
- Which paid apps or plugins will the store need?
- How many design revisions are included?
- Who owns the store, domain, theme and code after launch?
- What does post-launch support cover, and for how long?
- How will you handle speed, mobile experience and basic SEO setup?
- If we are migrating, how will you protect existing rankings with redirects?

Then put the answers side by side. Watch for these red flags:

- **A single figure with no scope.** You cannot compare a number without knowing what it covers.
- **The store or domain registered in the agency's name.** You should own both.
- **No mention of redirects** when you are moving from an old site.
- **Promises of fixed sales numbers or rankings.** Nobody controls buyers or Google.
- **A very low quote that leaves out product upload, content or support**, which then return as extras.

## Timelines

A template-based Shopify or WooCommerce store usually takes a few weeks once products, photos and content are ready. A custom Shopify build takes longer because of design rounds, development and testing. Custom or headless stores take longest, and are often launched in phases. The most common delay is not the build itself but waiting for product data, images, logins and approvals, so prepare those first.
