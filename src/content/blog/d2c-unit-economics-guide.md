D2C unit economics decide whether growth builds a business or burns through cash. Revenue can double while each new customer still loses money, and that only shows up months later in the bank balance. This guide explains the numbers in the order you should work them out: margin on one order, the cost of winning a customer, payback, lifetime value, RTO and COD losses, and how to read ROAS against MER. Every worked example uses hypothetical round numbers in rupees for a brand's own costs, so replace them with yours.

## Step 1: Contribution margin on one order

Start with a single order, not a monthly profit and loss statement. Monthly totals mix sale weeks with full-price weeks and paid with free traffic.

### A worked example (hypothetical numbers)

Imagine a product priced at ₹999 including GST, using an 18% example rate.

| Line | Amount | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| Selling price (incl. GST) | ₹999 | |
| Revenue excluding GST | | ₹847 |
| Product cost | −₹200 | CM1: ₹647 |
| Packaging | −₹30 | |
| Shipping | −₹70 | |
| Payment gateway fee | −₹20 | |
| Returns and RTO allowance | −₹60 | CM2: ₹467 |
| Marketing cost (new customer) | −₹600 | CM3: −₹133 |

Three things stand out. GST is not your revenue, so margin is worked out on ₹847, not ₹999. CM2 of ₹467 is the most you could spend to win this order without losing money on it. And at a ₹600 CAC, the first order loses ₹133, so the business only works if customers come back.

Our [profit margin calculator](/tools/profit-margin-calculator/) runs this sum for your own products, including break-even CAC and break-even ROAS.

### Define the layers once

Teams use CM1, CM2 and CM3 slightly differently. Write down which costs sit in each layer and keep the definitions fixed, or month-on-month comparisons mean nothing.

## Step 2: Customer acquisition cost, blended and paid

CAC is marketing spend divided by **new customers**, not by orders. Counting repeat orders makes CAC look lower than it is.

- **Paid CAC**: ad spend on Meta, Google, marketplaces and quick commerce divided by new customers from those channels.
- **Blended CAC**: all marketing cost, including creators, content, tools, sampling, first-order discounts and agency or freelancer fees, divided by all new customers.

Paid CAC helps you manage campaigns. Blended CAC tells you what growth really costs. Investors usually ask for both and will notice if only the lower one is shown.

## Step 3: CAC payback

Payback answers a cash question: how long until a customer repays what you spent to win them?

Using the example above, each order earns ₹467 at CM2 and CAC is ₹600. ₹600 ÷ ₹467 is about 1.3 orders, so the CAC is recovered on the second order. If customers buy about four times a year, the second order arrives around three months after the first, which gives a payback of roughly three months. This matches how our [LTV CAC calculator](/tools/ltv-cac-calculator/) works it out: payback order = CAC ÷ margin per order, rounded up; months = (payback order − 1) × 12 ÷ purchases per year.

Short payback lets you reinvest sooner. Long payback needs funding to cover the gap, which is why funded brands can sometimes outspend bootstrapped ones for a while.

## Step 4: LTV and cohort retention

### Lifetime value on margin

Lifetime value should be counted on margin, not revenue. With a hypothetical repeat rate of 40% after each order, a typical customer places 1 ÷ (1 − 0.4), or about 1.67 orders.

- LTV on revenue: ₹847 × 1.67 ≈ ₹1,412
- LTV on margin: ₹467 × 1.67 ≈ ₹778
- LTV:CAC: ₹778 ÷ ₹600 ≈ 1.3:1

A ratio of 1.3:1 means customers are profitable over their lifetime, but with little left for salaries, rent and mistakes. Many founders use 3:1 as a rule of thumb for a healthy target. In this example, cutting CAC to ₹500 and lifting the repeat rate to 50% would give LTV on margin of about ₹934 and a ratio near 1.9:1, closer but still short. That is the value of running the numbers: it shows how much each lever needs to move.

### Read cohorts, not averages

A cohort is every customer acquired in one month, tracked over time. Averages mix loyal customers from two years ago with this month's discount hunters. A simple cohort table looks like this (hypothetical shares):

| Cohort | Ordered again by month 1 | By month 3 | By month 6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | 8% | 18% | 26% |
| February | 9% | 20% | 29% |
| March (sale month) | 5% | 12% | — |

Here the sale-month cohort repeats less, a common warning that discounts brought in buyers who may not return. Split cohorts by channel and first product as well, so you know which ads and products bring repeat customers.

## Step 5: RTO and COD drag

Cash on delivery widens your audience, but refused parcels quietly damage unit economics. A hypothetical example: of 100 shipped orders, 40 are COD and one in five of those is refused, so 8 parcels return.

- Each RTO costs forward and return shipping plus packaging, say ₹170, so 8 returns cost ₹1,360.
- The ads that won those 8 orders produced no revenue. If ad spend was ₹600 per shipped order, ₹60,000 bought only 92 delivered orders, so the real CAC is about ₹652, not ₹600.

This is why the returns and RTO allowance belongs in CM2 and why CAC should be measured on delivered orders. Confirm COD orders by OTP or WhatsApp, offer a prepaid benefit and block high-risk pin codes. ## Step 6: ROAS, blended ROAS and MER

| Metric | Formula | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Platform ROAS | Revenue the platform claims ÷ its ad spend | Comparing campaigns and creatives inside one platform |
| Blended ROAS | Total revenue ÷ total ad spend | A quick check across all ad platforms |
| MER | Total revenue ÷ total marketing spend | Judging whether all marketing is working for the business |
| Break-even ROAS | Revenue per order excl. GST ÷ CM2 per order | Setting the floor below which ads lose money on the first order |

In our example, break-even ROAS is ₹847 ÷ ₹467 ≈ 1.8. If your ad platform reports revenue including GST, multiply by 1.18 and compare against about 2.1 instead. Our [ROAS calculator](/tools/roas-calculator/) does the same sum for any product.

Platforms each claim credit for orders that several ads touched, so the sum of platform ROAS often looks better than reality. MER uses your store's actual revenue, so if platform ROAS climbs while MER falls, fix attribution before raising budgets.

### Which number to watch each week

You do not need every metric every day. A simple rhythm works for most teams: check paid CAC, platform ROAS and RTO weekly; check MER, blended CAC and CM3 monthly; review cohorts, payback and LTV:CAC quarterly or before any fundraise. Keep one sheet with fixed definitions, so a new hire or an investor reads the same numbers the same way.

## Step 7: D2C unit economics investors ask for

Founders raising money, particularly in Bengaluru's funded D2C scene, are usually asked for a standard pack. Prepare it monthly, not the night before a call:

1. CM1, CM2 and CM3 per order, with definitions.
2. Blended and paid CAC by channel.
3. CAC payback in months by cohort.
4. Repeat rates by monthly cohort at month 1, 3, 6 and 12.
5. LTV on margin and LTV:CAC.
6. RTO rate and cancellation rate by payment method.
7. Sales split across your own store, marketplaces and quick commerce, each with its own margin.
8. Average order value and discount depth over time.

Consistency matters more than one standout month. A deck showing steady payback across six cohorts is more convincing than a single sale month with high ROAS.

## Unit economics differ by channel

The same product can earn very different margins depending on where it sells, so work out CM2 for each channel separately.

| Channel | Extra costs to include | What often improves |
|---|---|---|
| Your own store | Gateway fees, COD and RTO, shipping | Customer data, repeat orders, order value |
| Marketplaces | Referral and closing fees, fulfilment fees, sponsored ads | Reach and trust from reviews |
| Quick commerce | Platform margin, listing and visibility ads, wastage | Speed and impulse buying in dense cities |

A marketplace sale may have a lower CAC because shoppers already search there, but higher fees and no customer email or phone for reorders. A quick-commerce sale may arrive in minutes but carry the thinnest margin. Report each channel on its own line rather than averaging them.

## Common mistakes

- **Using revenue LTV against CAC.** Revenue LTV includes money that pays for products and shipping. Always compare margin LTV.
- **Leaving out discounts.** A first-order coupon is an acquisition cost. Count it in CAC or in revenue, not nowhere.
- **Measuring CAC on all orders.** Repeat orders lower the number without making acquisition cheaper.
- **Ignoring GST.** Margins worked out on tax-inclusive prices look several points better than they are.
- **Trusting one strong month.** Sale months pull in buyers who often repeat less. Wait for cohorts before scaling.

## How to improve the numbers

- **Raise order value** with bundles and free-shipping thresholds, so fixed costs per order are shared.
- **Win the second order** with a timely email or WhatsApp reminder; it usually decides LTV.
- **Cut wasted spend** on audiences, placements and search terms with no new customers.
- **Lower RTO** with COD confirmation and address checks.
- **Move budget to products with the strongest CM2**, not the highest revenue.
- **Review costs yearly**: courier rates, gateway fees and packaging change as volumes grow.
