Working out how to start a cosmetics brand, UK style, means dealing with two things at once: a creative project and a regulated business. The creative side, shades, packaging and the brand story, is usually what gets people excited. The regulated side, safety reports, a Responsible Person and product notification, is what decides whether you can legally sell. This guide walks through both in the order most founders need them, from choosing a niche to running the first months after launch.

Treat the regulatory sections as general guidance, not legal advice, and confirm the details with your Responsible Person or adviser.

## Step 1: Choose a niche and a first range

### Pick a clear buyer

The UK cosmetics market is crowded with big brands, supermarket ranges and viral dupes. A new brand needs a specific person in mind:

- People with deep skin tones underserved by mainstream shade ranges.
- Mature skin that wants lightweight, non-settling textures.
- Sensitive eyes or skin that reacts to fragrance.
- Minimalists who want a short, multi-use kit.
- Buyers who care about refillable or low-waste packaging.

### Keep the first range small

Three to six products, or a single hero product in a well-chosen shade range, is plenty. Each shade adds stock, testing, photography and documentation. A tight launch lets you put money behind what sells.

### Check the market honestly

Look at what is already on Amazon UK, TikTok Shop, Boots, Superdrug, LOOKFANTASTIC and Cult Beauty. Read the negative reviews of similar products: they tell you what buyers wish existed. Talk to twenty people in your target group before committing to a formula.

## Step 2: Find a manufacturer

You have three main options:

| Option | What it means | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| White label | An existing formula, sold under your brand | Fast, low-minimum launches |
| Private label | An existing base, adjusted for shade, scent or finish | Some distinctiveness without full development |
| Custom formula | A formula developed for you | A truly different product, with more time and cost |

When comparing manufacturers, ask:

- Do you work to good manufacturing practice for cosmetics, and can I see your quality procedures?
- What minimum order quantities apply, per shade and per component?
- Who arranges stability and compatibility testing, and how long does it take?
- What documentation will you provide for the safety report and product information file?
- Can you produce samples from a pilot batch before the full run?

Get every answer in writing, and agree who owns the formula if you ever move manufacturer.

## Step 3: Sort out UK compliance

### Safety report

Every product needs a cosmetic product safety report from a qualified safety assessor. It covers the ingredients, their concentrations, impurities, stability, microbiological quality and how the product will be used. Shades of the same product may need consideration individually.

### Responsible Person

A UK-based Responsible Person must be named for each product placed on the GB market. Your own UK company can take this role, or you can appoint a specialist service. They hold the product information file and respond to Trading Standards and other authorities.

### SCPN notification

Each product is notified through the government's Submit Cosmetic Product Notification service before it goes on sale.

### Labels

UK labels typically include the Responsible Person's name and address, nominal contents, a durability date or period-after-opening symbol, precautions, batch number, function where not obvious and the full ingredient list using standard ingredient names. Small items such as lipsticks may use outer packaging or an attached leaflet for some information; check with your adviser.

### Claims

Cosmetics can cleanse, perfume, protect, change appearance or keep the skin in good condition. Wording that suggests treating a condition can push a product towards medicines rules and the MHRA. The ASA enforces the CAP Code on ads and creator posts, including rules on filtered images. Animal testing for cosmetics is banned in the UK, so take care not to present that legal requirement as a unique feature.

### Northern Ireland and exports

Northern Ireland broadly follows EU cosmetics rules. If you plan to sell into the EU, you will need an EU Responsible Person and notification there as well.

## Step 4: Set up the business properly

- **Structure.** Register as a sole trader or form a limited company with Companies House, and tell HMRC.
- **Trademark.** Search and register your brand name with the UK Intellectual Property Office before printing packaging.
- **Insurance.** Arrange product liability and public liability cover.
- **VAT.** Register once your taxable turnover passes the registration threshold, or earlier if it suits you. Consumer prices must include VAT once you are registered.
- **Packaging duties.** Larger businesses have packaging reporting duties under extended producer responsibility rules; check whether they apply as you grow.
- **Data protection.** Your store and mailing list must follow UK GDPR and cookie consent rules.

An accountant and a regulatory adviser early on usually cost less than fixing mistakes later.

### A realistic timeline

Starting a cosmetics brand takes longer than most founders expect. A white-label launch can sometimes come together in a few months; a custom formula with stability testing often takes much longer. Build in time for samples, changes, safety assessment, label artwork, printing and delivery, and add a margin for delays. Announcing a launch date before your safety report is signed off puts pressure on every later step.

## Step 5: Design packaging that sells and ships

Packaging has to look good on a phone screen, survive a courier and carry all the legal information.

- Choose stock components for your first run to keep minimums low, and customise through colour, label and outer carton.
- Test closures, mirrors and pans in transit; broken pressed powders become refunds.
- Make shade names and numbers easy to read, since buyers will reorder by them.
- Leave room for the required label information without crowding the design.

## Step 6: Work out unit economics

For every product and shade, list:

- Formula and filling cost per unit.
- Components, labels and outer packaging.
- Safety assessment, Responsible Person and notification costs, spread across units.
- Photography and swatch content.
- Postage, packing materials and breakages.
- Payment and marketplace fees.
- Returns and refunds.
- Advertising cost per order.

## Step 7: Build your store and listings

### Your own Shopify store

Your store is where buyers learn your shade range and where you keep their data. Include:

- Swatches on a wide range of skin tones, in natural light, without filters.
- Undertone guidance and a simple shade finder.
- Texture, finish and wear time, shown on video.
- Full ingredients and how to use each product.
- VAT-inclusive prices, delivery options and a clear returns policy.

### Marketplaces

Amazon UK suits search-led products and fast reorders. TikTok Shop suits products that look striking on video, especially through live selling and affiliate creators. Etsy only fits genuinely handmade products. Keep shade names, images and prices consistent everywhere.

Do not try to open every channel at once. Start with your own store and one marketplace, learn how listings, fees, stock and customer messages work there, then add the next. Each marketplace has its own rules for cosmetics listings and may ask for safety documents or Responsible Person details, so keep a folder ready with your paperwork for each product.

## Step 8: Plan the launch

1. **Build a waitlist** six to eight weeks ahead with close-up texture and swatch teasers.
2. **Seed creators** whose skin tones and ages match your buyers, a few weeks before launch, with clear rules on labelling ads and avoiding filters.
3. **Open to the waitlist first**, then to everyone, with Meta, TikTok and Google ads switched on together.
4. **Follow up** with tutorials and real reviews in the first weeks.
5. **Watch stock by shade** and reorder before popular shades run out.

Pick a launch date with the UK calendar in mind. Launching just before Black Friday means competing with every major brand's discounts; a quieter month gives you more attention for the same spend. Plan gift sets for Christmas, Valentine's and Mother's Day in March once the core range is selling.

## Step 9: Run operations and handle returns

UK online buyers generally have a cancellation period. Hygiene-sensitive products that are unsealed after delivery can be excluded if you explain this clearly before purchase, but damaged or faulty products always need putting right. Track returns by shade: a shade that is returned often may be photographed badly or named confusingly. Keep batch records so you can respond quickly to any complaint or recall. If a customer reports a reaction, reply promptly and kindly, record the batch number and details, and pass serious cases to your Responsible Person, who may need to report them. Read every review and support message in the first months; they will tell you more about your products and packaging than any survey.

## Mistakes to avoid in your first year

- Launching too many shades and running out of money for marketing.
- Ordering custom packaging before demand is proven.
- Skipping the safety report or notification to save time.
- Using filtered or retouched images in ads.
- Pricing too low to afford advertising.
- Ignoring returns data that points to shade or photography problems.
