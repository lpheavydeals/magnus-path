Many Indian makers, exporters and UK importers want to know how to sell Indian products in the UK, and the demand is real. Britain has large South Asian communities who miss the brands and textures of home, and a wider public that cooks Indian food, buys Indian textiles and looks for handmade homeware. But every product must meet UK rules for its category, and getting stock across the border takes planning. This guide covers importing, product rules, labelling, buyers, channels and seasons in order.

We are an ecommerce growth agency, not a customs broker or regulatory lawyer. Use this as a general overview and confirm duties, codes and product rules with a customs broker and the relevant regulator before you ship.

## Who buys Indian products in Britain

The core audience is South Asian households across the UK. London has large communities in areas such as Southall, Wembley, Harrow, Ilford, East Ham and Tooting. Leicester's Belgrave Road and the Golden Mile, Birmingham's Soho Road and Ladypool Road, and Manchester's Wilmslow Road are well known shopping streets. These buyers know what they want: familiar names, authentic sourcing, sensible pack sizes and reliable reordering.

A second, wider audience buys Indian products for what they are: spices for home cooking, block-printed cotton, brass and copper, handloom textiles, incense and tea. They need more explanation and more reassurance about quality, but they widen your market well beyond the diaspora.

## Step 1: check UK product rules by category

Before anything ships, place each product under the UK rules that apply. An Indian licence or certificate does not make a product legal in Britain on its own.

| Category | Main UK rules in general terms | Points to check |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmetics and hair oils | UK Cosmetics Regulation | UK Responsible Person, safety report, notification, ingredient list |
| Food, spices and snacks | Food import controls and UK food labelling | Border checks on some foods, allergens, local authority registration |
| Toys and children's items | UK toy safety rules and UKCA or accepted marking | Testing, warnings, UK importer details on product or pack |
| Electricals | UK product safety rules and UKCA marking | Plug type, testing, instructions in English |
| Clothing and textiles | Fibre content labelling and general product safety | Fibre names, care labels, drawstrings on children's wear |
| Gold and silver jewellery | Hallmarking law | Items above set weights generally need a UK-recognised hallmark |
| Herbal and Ayurvedic products | Cosmetic, food or herbal medicine routes | No medicinal claims without UK authorisation |

Herbal and Ayurvedic ranges have their own complications, which we cover in our guide to [selling Ayurvedic products in the UK](/blog/how-to-sell-ayurvedic-products-in-the-uk/).

## Step 2: relabel for British shoppers

Most Indian packs need changes before they go on a UK shelf or website:

- **English throughout**, with metric units and UK date formats
- **UK importer or Responsible Person details** where the category requires them
- **Ingredient and allergen information** in the format UK rules require, with allergens emphasised on food
- **Claims rewritten** to remove medicinal or unproven wording that UK advertising rules do not allow
- **No Indian MRP** printed on packs, which confuses shoppers and retailers
- **Batch numbers** that match your test reports and shipping documents

A separate UK pack run usually looks better than stickers over the Indian label, and it stops labels peeling in transit.

## Step 3: import from India

### Customs basics

In general terms, the business bringing goods into Britain needs an EORI number, the correct commodity code for each product, a commercial invoice, a packing list and any certificates the category requires. A customs broker or freight forwarder can lodge declarations for you, advise on codes and flag products that face extra checks. Agree early who is the importer of record.

### Duties and VAT

Import duty depends on the commodity code and where the goods were made, and import VAT is usually charged on the value plus duty. VAT-registered importers can often use postponed VAT accounting to avoid paying import VAT up front at the border. The UK and India signed a trade agreement in 2025, and tariff changes from it may apply to some goods once in force, so ask your broker to check the current position for each code.

### Bulk stock beats single parcels

Posting single parcels from India to UK customers looks simple but usually disappoints. Delivery is slow, parcels can be held at customs and buyers may face charges they did not expect. Most sellers ship in bulk to a UK fulfilment partner or into Amazon UK, then deliver within a few days through Royal Mail, Evri or DPD.

## Step 4: choose your channels

### Amazon UK

Amazon UK suits staples with clear search demand: spice blends, teas, hair oils, pooja items and cookware. Shoppers trust its delivery and returns, and many diaspora buyers already search there for familiar brand names. Expect category approvals for some products, document checks, referral fees and strong competition on price. ### Your own store

Your own store is where you tell the story: where the product comes from, how it is made and how to use it. It is the right home for gift boxes, festive bundles, subscriptions and anything handmade. It also gives you customer emails, which matter most at festival time. Build it in pounds with VAT-inclusive prices and UK delivery options.

### Other routes

Etsy suits handmade textiles and crafts. TikTok Shop UK can work for visual products such as jewellery and homeware. Wholesale to South Asian grocers, gift shops and boutiques gives volume but slimmer margins. ## Step 5: market to two audiences

Diaspora buyers and wider audiences need different messages, so run them as separate campaigns.

### Diaspora buyers

Use the product names people already search for, in English and, where natural, in Hindi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Tamil or other languages your buyers use. Show where and how the product is made, offer bundles of everyday staples and make reordering easy. Search ads on product names, Amazon UK listings and community groups on WhatsApp and Facebook often do most of the work. Local creators in London, Leicester and Birmingham can show products in real British homes and kitchens.

### Wider audiences

People new to a product need context. Short videos showing how to cook with a spice blend, style a block-printed dupatta or care for brassware answer their questions before they ask. Recipe pages, styling guides and clear sizing build trust with search engines and shoppers alike. Keep every claim honest and within UK advertising rules, and show a UK contact address and returns policy prominently, because an unfamiliar overseas brand has to earn trust.

## Step 6: plan around festive seasons

Indian products sell in seasons. Plan stock and campaigns around:

- **Diwali**, the biggest gifting and sweets season for many Hindu, Sikh and Jain households
- **Navratri and Durga Puja**, for clothing, jewellery and pooja items
- **Eid**, for clothing, gifts, dates and sweets in Muslim households of South Asian heritage
- **Vaisakhi and Raksha Bandhan**, smaller but focused gifting moments
- **Wedding season**, which runs through much of the year and drives clothing and jewellery
- **Christmas and Black Friday**, when wider audiences buy gifts and homeware

Stock often needs to leave India six to ten weeks before a festival to clear customs and reach UK warehouses in time. Late stock is the commonest reason festive campaigns fall flat.

## How to sell Indian products in the UK: a first-year plan

1. **Months one and two:** shortlist products with simpler UK rules and known demand, appoint a customs broker and any required UK representative, and arrange testing.
2. **Months three and four:** finish UK labels, book freight, set up a 3PL or Amazon UK fulfilment and build your store.
3. **Months five and six:** launch to diaspora buyers through search ads, Amazon UK and community social channels.
4. **Months seven to twelve:** add explanatory content for wider audiences, review which products reorder, and plan the next festive season early.

## Working between India and the UK

Selling across two countries means two time zones, two sets of paperwork and suppliers who may not know UK rules. Keep one shared folder with product specifications, test reports, label artwork, commodity codes and shipping documents for every product, so anyone on either side can answer a customs, marketplace or customer question quickly. Agree a reorder routine with your Indian suppliers well before stock runs low, because production and shipping together take longer than most UK sellers expect. Review sales by product each month and cut slow sellers early, so cash is not tied up in stock sitting in a UK warehouse.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming an Indian certificate means a product can be sold in the UK
- Shipping single parcels from India and leaving buyers to pay surprise charges
- Guessing commodity codes instead of asking a broker
- Launching the whole catalogue at once
- Missing Diwali because stock arrived too late
- Copying Indian product descriptions and claims onto a UK store
