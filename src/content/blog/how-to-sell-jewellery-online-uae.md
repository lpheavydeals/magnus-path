Anyone working out how to sell jewellery online UAE buyers will trust has to compete with the gold souk. Generations of residents and visitors have bought gold in Deira and in mall showrooms by weight, comparing the day's rate, the making charge and the design across several counters before deciding. Many treat gold as savings as much as adornment. Online, those same buyers expect the same facts, the same price logic and the same confidence, without being able to hold the piece. This guide is for UAE jewellers going online, Indian jewellery brands entering the Emirates and newer fine, lab-grown and demi-fine labels.

The rules below are described in general terms. We are an ecommerce growth team, not lawyers, so confirm hallmarking, VAT and consumer protection details with your supplier, accountant and adviser.

## How UAE buyers shop for jewellery

A few habits shape the market:

- **Purity first.** Buyers ask the karat before the design. Emirati and Arab buyers often favour 21K, Indian families look for 22K, and 18K is common for diamond and daily-wear pieces.
- **Weight and rate.** Gold is priced by weight at the day's rate, with a separate making charge, and buyers compare those numbers between shops.
- **Exchange and resale.** Many buyers trade old gold for new, so exchange and buy-back terms influence where they buy.
- **Showroom habit.** Large chains such as Damas, Joyalukkas and Malabar have shaped expectations of choice and service, and many buyers still like to see a piece before paying.

An online store does not need to replace the counter. It needs to answer the counter's questions so well that buyers either order with confidence or arrive at your showroom ready to buy.

## Choose your segment

| Segment | Typical buyer | What the online store must show |
|---|---|---|
| 21K and 22K gold | Families buying for weddings, Eid, Diwali and savings | Karat, weight, rate-based price, making charge, exchange terms |
| 18K diamond and fine jewellery | Gifts, engagements and everyday luxury | Stone details, grading reports, sizing, video |
| Lab-grown diamond jewellery | Younger buyers and design-led shoppers | Clear lab-grown disclosure, specifications, comparison guidance |
| Silver and demi-fine | Gifts and self-purchase | Materials, plating thickness, care, scale shots |
| Fashion jewellery | Impulse and trend buyers | Price, styling, fast delivery, easy returns |

Each segment needs a different payment, delivery and returns setup, so decide which you lead with.

## Purity and hallmarking

In Dubai, gold jewellery is generally tested and hallmarked through the Dubai Central Laboratory, which sits within Dubai Municipality, and the purity is stamped on the piece. The UAE has also been working towards national hallmarking standards. Requirements and marks change over time, so confirm what applies to your products with your supplier or adviser.

On your site, turn hallmarking into reassurance:

- State the karat and metal colour in the product title
- Show a close-up photo of the hallmark stamp
- Explain in one short paragraph what the mark means
- Repeat the karat on the invoice and certificate card in the box

## How to sell jewellery online UAE buyers trust: prices tied to the daily gold rate

Nothing loses a gold buyer faster than a price that ignores the day's rate. Gold prices in the UAE move through the day, and buyers check them.

### Show the price breakdown

For every gold piece, display:

1. Net gold weight in grams, with stones weighed separately
2. The current rate for that karat
3. The making charge, per gram or per piece
4. Stones, enamel or other extras
5. VAT, applied as your accountant advises

### Keep every channel in step

If your platform supports it, pull the gold rate from a reliable feed and update prices automatically. Your Google Merchant Center feed, Meta catalogue and any marketplace listings should update at the same time; an ad showing yesterday's price creates complaints and disapprovals.

### Decide how you handle rate changes

Say whether the price is locked at the moment of payment, and what happens to reservations or part-payments if the rate moves before collection. Writing this down early prevents arguments later.

## Lab-grown and natural: disclose clearly

Lab-grown diamonds are popular with design-led buyers, and they deserve clear, unashamed disclosure. Use "laboratory-grown" or "lab-grown" in titles, filters, product pages, invoices and ads. Never use wording that lets a buyer assume a stone is mined. List carat, cut, colour and clarity, say whether a grading report is included and from which laboratory, and explain how the stones differ. Keep natural and lab-grown pieces in separate collections so filters and catalogue feeds stay accurate.

## Photos, video and sizing

Jewellery sells on detail that flat photos lose.

### What to film for every hero piece

- A slow macro video catching light on stones and metal
- An on-body clip showing how a chain falls or a bangle sits
- A scale shot beside a hand or coin
- The hallmark and the packaging

### Sizing

Publish ring, bangle and chain length guides, with a printable ring sizer. Offer a video call or a showroom appointment for bridal and high-value pieces. ### Arabic and English

A bilingual store with a right-to-left Arabic layout serves more of the market. Arabic copy should be written or checked by a fluent Arabic writer, since jewellery terms and design names do not always translate directly.

## Payment and order confirmation

- **Cards and bank transfer:** the usual route for gold and diamond pieces.
- **Tabby and Tamara:** helpful for silver, demi-fine and fashion ranges, where the provider allows the category.
- **Cash on delivery:** common in the UAE, but most jewellers limit it to lower-value pieces because of refusal and fraud risk.
- **Confirmation call or WhatsApp:** for high-value orders, confirm the buyer, address and sizing before dispatch.

Watch how many visitors reach checkout and how many complete it. The [conversion rate calculator](/tools/conversion-rate-calculator/) helps you see whether changes to payment options are helping.

## Insured delivery and packaging

Use a courier or specialist valuables service that insures each parcel for its full value, with signature or ID checks on delivery. Pack discreetly: no brand name or contents visible outside, tamper-evident seals, and a separate inner gift box. Film the packing of high-value orders as a record. Offer click-and-collect from your showroom where you have one, since many buyers prefer to collect gold in person.

Delivery speed still matters. Buyers in Dubai and Abu Dhabi are used to next-day parcels for other categories, so state realistic times for each emirate, and give a tracking link and the courier's contact as soon as an order ships. For gifts, let the buyer choose a delivery date and add a message card, and make sure the invoice does not travel inside the gift box unless the buyer asks for it.

## Returns, exchange and buy-back

Publish clear terms for each segment:

- Returns on fashion and demi-fine pieces within a set period, unworn and with tags
- Exchange on gold pieces, with how the value is calculated
- Buy-back terms, if you offer them, with the rate and deductions explained
- Custom and engraved pieces usually excluded, stated before checkout

UAE consumer protection rules expect returns policies to be clear before purchase, so check yours with an adviser.

## Channels

- **Your own Shopify or WooCommerce store:** the full range, live gold pricing, showroom bookings and customer data.
- **Amazon.ae and Noon:** suit silver, demi-fine and fashion pieces; high-value gold is harder to sell on marketplaces.
- **Namshi:** fashion jewellery alongside clothing and accessories.
- **Instagram, Snapchat and TikTok:** discovery, with WhatsApp for questions and reservations.

## Getting your first online buyers

A new jewellery store rarely sells high-value gold to strangers in its first weeks. Trust builds in steps:

1. **Start with your existing customers.** Showroom buyers, past wedding clients and WhatsApp contacts who opted in are the first people to tell about the site.
2. **Lead online with lighter pieces.** Daily-wear chains, earrings and lab-grown or demi-fine designs are easier first online purchases than bridal sets.
3. **Use reservations.** Let buyers reserve a gold piece online and pay or collect at the showroom, which suits shoppers who want to see weight and finish in person.
4. **Collect video reviews.** A short clip of a happy buyer wearing the piece does more than a written review, especially in Arabic.
5. **Answer quickly.** Buyers often message several jewellers at once; the one who replies first with a clear video and price usually wins.

Track enquiries and showroom visits alongside online orders, because much of the return from early campaigns appears offline.

## Seasons that drive the jewellery year

- **Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha:** family gifts and gold purchases.
- **Ramadan:** quieter, respectful creative and gifting ahead of Eid.
- **Diwali, Dhanteras and Akshaya Tritiya:** major gold-buying moments for the Indian community.
- **Onam:** traditional gold for Malayali families.
- **Wedding season:** bridal sets, often planned months ahead.
- **Valentine's and Mother's Day:** diamond, lab-grown and demi-fine gifts.
- **Dubai Shopping Festival, White Friday and UAE National Day:** promotions and gifting.

### Growing into the GCC

Saudi Arabia and the rest of the GCC share much of the UAE's gold culture, but each has its own hallmarking, import and VAT rules. Expand with a tested range and a local delivery and insurance partner.
