This guide explains how to sell shoes online: Australia-specific advice on sizes and widths, product information, marketplaces, returns, delivery and ads. Footwear is one of the harder categories to sell from a screen. A loose T-shirt still gets worn, but a boot half a size too short goes straight back. Australian buyers also face a sizing puzzle, because local labels do not follow one system. Brands that solve fit before they spend on ads keep more of their sales, earn better reviews and win repeat buyers who trust the chart next time.

## Step 1: Be clear about your range and buyer

Start by naming who you sell to and what they wear the shoes for. The work changes a lot between categories:

- **Sneakers and casual shoes.** Driven by colourways, drops and how the shoe looks on foot.
- **Sandals, slides and thongs.** Simple sizing, strongly seasonal, often bought as an add-on.
- **Work and safety boots.** Bought on durability, grip, width and comfort over a long shift.
- **School shoes.** Bought by parents in a short window, with width and growing room front of mind.
- **Running and sports shoes.** Compared on cushioning, weight and fit for a specific activity.

Write down your main buyer's top three questions before building any page. A tradie choosing boots asks about different things from a parent buying school shoes in January.

## Step 2: Build size and width charts on foot length

### Why AU sizing is confusing

Men's shoes sold in Australia often use sizes that match UK sizing. Women's shoes tend to sit closer to US sizes. Kids' sizes follow their own scale, and many imported ranges only carry EU sizes. A buyer who wears a "9" might mean three different lengths. The fix is to make foot length the anchor and treat every size label as a conversion.

### How to build the chart

1. Measure the internal length of each size for each last, or get the figures from your manufacturer.
2. Map each size to a foot length range in centimetres.
3. Show AU, UK, US and EU sizes in one table, with men's, women's and kids' separated.
4. Add width information. Many school shoes and work boots come in more than one width, and letter codes vary by brand, so explain what each width means in plain words.
5. Add a short "how to measure your foot at home" guide with a photo of a heel against the wall and a ruler.

### Example layout

| Foot length (cm) | AU women's | AU men's | UK | US women's | EU |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 23.5 | 6 | 4 | 4 | 6 | 37 |
| 24.5 | 7 | 5 | 5 | 7 | 38 |
| 25.5 | 8 | 6 | 6 | 8 | 39 |

These rows show the layout only. Use the measurements from your own lasts, because brands differ.

### Fit notes on every style

Add a line to each product page: true to size, runs small, roomy in the toe, snug across the instep or suits wider feet. Update the notes from return reasons and reviews every month, so they reflect what customers actually experience.

### Kids' and school shoes

Parents buy school shoes in a rush in January, often for more than one child. Give them a printable foot-measuring guide, explain how much growing room to allow, and show which widths each school style comes in. A short note on how school shoes should fit at the heel and toe saves a lot of swaps. Make it easy to order two sizes for different children in one checkout, and send a reminder before the next school year.

## Step 3: Describe materials and origin honestly

Footwear buyers care about what a shoe is made of and where it comes from, and both are areas where the Australian Consumer Law expects accuracy. Country-of-origin labelling is mandatory for most food but generally voluntary for footwear. If you do make a claim, such as "made in Australia" or "designed in Australia", it must be true and must not mislead. Describe uppers, linings and soles accurately; words such as "genuine leather", "waterproof" or "non-slip" need to hold up. If you sell work or safety footwear, only refer to safety standards that your manufacturer's test reports actually support. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

## Step 4: Photograph and film every style properly

Shoppers need to see the shoe as if it were in their hand. For each style, shoot the side profile, front, back, top, sole and a close-up of the material, then add on-foot photos with the model's usual size stated. A short walking clip and a flex test of the sole answer questions that still images cannot. For sandals and thongs, show the strap fit on a real foot.

Keep lighting and backgrounds consistent so colours look the same across the range, and photograph each colourway rather than recolouring one image. Buyers who receive a shade that differs from the screen often return the pair. Add a size-in-hand or side-by-side shot for chunky soles and platform styles, which are hard to judge from a flat image.

## Step 5: Choose where to sell

### Your own Shopify store

Your store holds the full range, the fit tools and the customer list. Add the size chart and fit notes to every product page, a size-swap explanation near the add-to-cart button, and Afterpay or another buy now, pay later option, which many buyers look for on higher-priced pairs. ### Amazon AU

Amazon AU suits everyday, sports and kids' shoes. Fill in every size, colour and material attribute, use the size chart fields, and keep variations grouped under one parent listing so reviews build in one place.

### eBay AU and Kogan

Both can move core styles and end-of-season stock. Keep the same size chart and photos you use elsewhere so buyers get the same experience.

### TikTok Shop

TikTok Shop may suit creator-led sneaker and sandal launches. Check that it is available for your category, and plan the size range carefully, because a viral clip can sell out popular sizes within hours.

### Retail as context

Specialist chains such as The Athlete's Foot shape what shoppers expect from fit advice. You cannot measure feet in person, so your chart, fit notes and help messages have to do that job instead.

## Step 6: Set returns terms that respect consumer rights

Change-of-mind returns are your decision, and the policy should say plainly what you offer. Easy size exchanges are worth considering, because a swap keeps the sale. Separately, customers have consumer rights under the Australian Consumer Law when goods are faulty or not as described, and these apply whatever your policy says. Avoid wording such as "no refunds" or "sale items cannot be returned" that suggests otherwise; the ACCC has acted against misleading returns policies. Make returns simple to start online, ask for a reason, and log it by style and size.

## Step 7: Get delivery and packaging right

Shoes travel well in their box inside a satchel or carton. Use a box that survives the trip, because buyers who plan to return a pair may need it. Compare Australia Post, Sendle and couriers on speed to regional areas, and tell buyers realistic delivery times. Include a prepaid or easy returns label process for exchanges. Set your free-delivery threshold so it covers the cost of the second leg on exchanges; the [free shipping threshold calculator](/tools/free-shipping-threshold-calculator/) helps with that.

## Step 8: Run ads on a clean catalogue

A style in six colours and ten sizes is sixty products, so feeds get messy quickly.

### Feed checklist

- Size, colour, gender, age group and material filled in for every variant
- GST-inclusive AUD prices that match the store
- Sold-out sizes removed or marked quickly
- On-foot images as the main image where the platform allows
- Product titles that include the style type, such as "wide fit leather work boot"

### Where to advertise

Meta catalogue ads work well when paired with walking video and creator try-ons. Google Shopping and search reach high-intent searches such as "wide fit school shoes". Judge both on sales after exchanges and refunds, not on the purchase value the platform reports.

## How to sell shoes online: Australia listings that convert

**Titles.** Lead with the brand, style type, key feature and gender: "Women's leather ankle boot, side zip, wide fit".

**Descriptions.** Cover fit, feel, materials, care and occasion in short sections, with the fit note at the top.

**Reviews and questions.** Ask reviewers for their usual size and whether the pair fitted. Answer public questions quickly; other buyers read them.

## Australian footwear calendar

- **Summer:** sandals, slides and thongs
- **Late January:** back to school
- **EOFY in June and winter:** boots and clearance
- **September:** Father's Day gifts
- **Click Frenzy, Black Friday, Christmas and Boxing Day:** the big sale windows

## Common mistakes

- Copying a supplier's size chart without checking it against your own lasts
- Listing EU sizes only and leaving buyers to guess
- Studio photos with no on-foot shots or walking video
- A returns policy that hides or limits consumer rights
- Ads that keep spending on sizes you no longer have
- Measuring success on orders placed rather than pairs kept

Most of these come from moving fast at launch. An hour a month reviewing return reasons by style and size will show which one is costing you most.

## A simple launch checklist

- Foot-length size charts with AU, UK, US and EU sizes
- Width and fit notes on every style
- Accurate material and origin wording
- Six or more photos plus an on-foot clip per style
- Returns policy reviewed against consumer law
- Clean feed for Meta and Google
