If you want to know how to sell hair oil online, start with one fact: hair oil is one of the most familiar products in Indian homes. Almost every buyer already has a brand, a habit and a family opinion. Your job is to give them a clear reason to switch, deliver a bottle that arrives without leaking, and stay in their routine long enough to see results. This guide covers licences, packaging, product pages, channels, ads, delivery and reorders in a practical order.

## Step 1: Decide if your oil is a cosmetic or Ayurvedic

This decision shapes your licence, label and claims, so make it first. What follows is general guidance, not legal advice.

### Cosmetic hair oil

Many hair oils are sold as cosmetics: coconut, almond, argan or blended oils for nourishment, shine and manageability. These fall under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Cosmetics Rules. The manufacturer needs a cosmetic manufacturing licence from the state licensing authority. Claims stay within cosmetic territory: conditioning, shine, reduced breakage, scalp comfort.

### Ayurvedic hair oil

Oils based on classical Ayurvedic formulas or herbs such as bhringraj, amla and brahmi are often sold as Ayurvedic products. These usually come under the AYUSH framework, with a licence from the state AYUSH authority and specific rules on formulas, labels and claims. Calling a product "Ayurvedic" on the label or in ads without the right licence can cause problems with marketplaces and regulators.

### Claims to avoid

Promises such as "regrows hair", "stops hair fall in 7 days" or "reverses baldness" can attract action under drug and advertising rules, including the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act. ASCI guidelines also apply to ads and influencer posts. Keep claims modest and backed by testing.

## Step 2: Choose a manufacturer and formula

Most new brands use a contract manufacturer rather than building a factory.

- Check the manufacturer's licence covers your category (cosmetic or Ayurvedic).
- Ask for minimum order quantities; small first batches reduce risk.
- Agree on stability testing, so the oil does not separate, smell off or change colour on the shelf.
- Clarify who owns the formula and whether they can sell it to other brands.
- Ask for batch records and a test report for each batch.

Choose a clear point of difference. Buyers already have a family favourite, so "another coconut oil" will struggle. It might be a specific herb blend, a cold-pressed base oil, a non-sticky texture, a fragrance-free option, a regional recipe or an oil for a particular hair type.

## Step 3: Get packaging right for couriers

Leaking bottles are one of the commonest reasons for poor reviews and returns in this category.

- **Bottle:** sturdy PET or HDPE, or glass only if you can pack it well.
- **Closure:** an induction seal or inner plug, plus a tight cap and a shrink band.
- **Dispensing:** a flip-top, nozzle or dropper suited to how the oil is used.
- **Secondary pack:** a sealed pouch around each bottle and a strong carton with cushioning.
- **Testing:** shake, invert and drop-test sample parcels before launch.

Your label also needs the mandatory fields: product name, ingredients, net quantity, MRP, manufacturer details, licence number, batch, manufacturing and expiry dates, and directions for use. Marketplaces check these during listing.

## Step 4: Build product pages by hair type

Hair oil buyers want to know whether an oil suits their hair, how to use it and how long it takes to notice a difference.

### What to include

- Hair type and concern it suits: dry, frizzy, oily scalp, fine hair, curly hair.
- Key ingredients and why they are there, in plain language.
- Texture and smell: light or heavy, fragrance level, how easily it washes out.
- How to use: amount, warm or cold, massage time, how long to leave on, how often.
- A realistic timeline: feel and shine on first use, other benefits over weeks of regular use.
- A patch-test note for sensitive scalps.
- Reviews that mention hair type.

### Video helps

Short videos showing pour, texture, a scalp massage and the hair after washing build more trust than polished photos. Regional language versions help in markets where buyers are used to oiling rituals described in their own language.

## Step 6: Price for profit, not just for clicks

Hair oil prices are often compared per millilitre. Before setting MRP:

- Calculate full cost per bottle: oil, bottle, cap, label, carton, inner pack.
- Add shipping, payment fees, marketplace commission and expected COD returns.
- Add a realistic ad cost per order.
- Compare against similar oils on Amazon and Nykaa by price per 100 ml.

Larger bottles usually carry better margins and reduce shipping cost per ml. Use small packs as low-risk trials, not as your main business.

## Step 7: Advertise within the rules

### Meta ads

Show the ritual: pouring, massaging, the oil on hair, the ingredients. Avoid side-by-side before-and-after images and growth promises; these are commonly rejected and can restrict your ad account. Test angles such as ingredient story, family ritual, a specific hair type and a creator demo.

### Google ads

Search for terms like "hair oil for dry frizzy hair" or "bhringraj oil" catches buyers with clear intent. Google Shopping with a clean feed helps comparison shoppers see your price and reviews.

### Marketplace ads

Sponsored Products on Amazon and sponsored listings on Flipkart and Nykaa help new products gather reviews. Read our [Amazon PPC guide](/blog/amazon-ppc-guide-india/) for a simple campaign structure.

## Step 8: Control COD and returns

Many hair oil buyers, especially in smaller towns, still prefer cash on delivery. Protect your margin:

- Confirm COD orders by OTP or WhatsApp before dispatch.
- Offer a small prepaid incentive.
- Verify addresses at checkout.
- Track refused parcels by pin code, product and campaign.
- Watch for damaged deliveries, which turn into refunds.

## Step 9: Keep buyers through the result window

Hair oil is judged over weeks. Buyers who stop early often leave poor reviews and never reorder.

1. Send usage tips by WhatsApp or email in the first week.
2. Remind buyers how long to use the oil before judging results.
3. Ask for a review after several weeks of use, not on delivery day.
4. Send a refill reminder before the bottle usually runs out.
5. Offer subscribe-and-save or a larger bottle on reorder.

Pair the oil with a shampoo or hair mask later to build a routine and lift order value.

Watch your reviews closely in the first months. Complaints about smell, stickiness or leaking caps are easy to fix early and expensive to ignore, because they stay on your marketplace listings for a long time. Reply to every critical review politely, explain what you changed, and use the feedback to improve the next batch.

## Common mistakes new hair oil brands make

- **Copying a big brand's claims.** A large company's evidence does not cover your formula, and a rejected ad account or delisted product costs weeks.
- **Too many variants at launch.** Five oils for five concerns split your budget and reviews. Start with one or two hero oils.
- **Ignoring smell and texture.** Buyers forgive a slow result more readily than a sticky oil or a smell their family dislikes. Mention both honestly.
- **Discounting from day one.** Constant offers train buyers to wait, and they make the price per ml look unreliable next to familiar brands.
- **Selling only on your own website.** Many buyers find you on Instagram and then check Amazon or Flipkart for reviews before ordering.
- **No stock plan for quick commerce.** Going out of stock on Blinkit or Zepto for a week can hand your regular buyers to a rival.

## Selling hair oil abroad

Indian hair oils have a loyal audience among the diaspora in the UK, UAE, US and Australia. Before shipping, check each market's cosmetic rules on ingredients, labels and claims; some Ayurvedic herbs and claims may not be allowed as-is. Shipping liquids by courier can face restrictions and leakage risk over long distances. Many brands start with Amazon in the target market, or send a batch to a local fulfilment partner, rather than shipping each order from India.
