If you are working out how to sell supplements online, USA rules shape almost every decision before the first ad runs. Vitamins, protein, gummies and botanicals are regulated as a type of food, yet the claims you can make, the factory you choose and the documents Amazon wants all follow their own logic. This guide sets out the steps in the order US founders usually need them, from formula to subscription.

We are an e-commerce marketing team, not lawyers or regulatory consultants. Read the rules sections as a general map, then confirm the position of your own products with qualified counsel.

## Step 1: Know how dietary supplements are regulated in the US

The Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994 (DSHEA) created the category most brands sell in. Under it, a dietary supplement is regulated by the FDA as a kind of food, not a drug. The FDA does not review or approve products before they go on sale. Instead, the brand carries the responsibility for safety, labeling and truthful marketing, and the agency acts when something goes wrong.

In practice that means a few things for an online seller:

- **Ingredients.** An ingredient that was not marketed in the US before October 1994 may count as a new dietary ingredient, which can require a notification to the FDA before launch.
- **Facility registration.** Facilities that make, pack or hold supplements generally need to be registered with the FDA.
- **Adverse events.** Serious adverse events reported to you must be passed to the FDA, so your label needs a US address or phone number for consumers to contact.
- **Advertising.** The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) oversees the ads, emails, influencer posts and product pages you publish.

Two agencies, two sets of expectations. The FDA mostly looks at the bottle; the FTC looks at everything you say to sell it.

## Step 2: Choose a manufacturer that follows supplement cGMP

The FDA has a current good manufacturing practice (cGMP) rule written specifically for dietary supplements. It covers identity testing of ingredients, batch records, quality control, cleaning, complaints and more. If a contract manufacturer makes your product, the rule still matters to you, because the brand that puts its name on the label shares responsibility for the product meeting its specifications.

Questions worth asking a potential manufacturer:

- How do you confirm the identity of each incoming ingredient?
- Can I see a sample batch record and a certificate of analysis for a finished lot?
- What happens when a batch fails a specification?
- Do you have recent FDA inspection history or third-party audit reports you can share?
- Who handles stability testing for my expiry dates?

Keep one shared folder with specifications, certificates of analysis, batch records you receive and any audit reports. Amazon, retailers and your own customer service team will all ask for pieces of it.

## Step 3: Write a label and claims you can defend

The label is the anchor for every word you publish online. It needs a statement of identity, net quantity, a Supplement Facts panel with serving size and amounts per serving, the other ingredients, allergen information where it applies, and the name and place of business of the manufacturer, packer or distributor.

### Structure/function claims and the FDA disclaimer

Supplements may carry structure/function claims, such as "supports a healthy immune system" or "helps maintain energy levels". They may not claim to diagnose, treat, cure, mitigate or prevent a disease. When a structure/function claim appears on a label, it generally needs the standard disclaimer that the FDA has not evaluated the statement and that the product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease. Brands are also expected to notify the FDA within 30 days of first marketing a product with such a claim.

### Evidence comes first

The FTC's guidance on health products expects competent and reliable scientific evidence behind every health-related claim, matched to your ingredient, your dose and the people you sell to. A study using five times your dose, or a different form of the ingredient, is weak support. Collect the evidence before the claim appears anywhere.

Wording that tends to cause trouble includes condition names used as hooks, words such as "detox", "fat burner" or "immune shield", and any timeline for results. Wording that describes the formula ("15 g of collagen peptides per scoop") and the routine ("one capsule with breakfast") is safer and often more persuasive.

## Step 4: Build product pages that answer skeptical buyers

US supplement shoppers are cautious. They have seen underdosed products, fake reviews and big promises. A strong product page answers those doubts quickly:

- Front, back and side label images that are readable on a phone.
- The Supplement Facts panel repeated as text, so search engines and screen readers can read it.
- Servings per container and a cost-per-serving line, so buyers can compare fairly.
- Testing information you actually hold, described accurately, with batch references where useful.
- Directions for use, timing, mixing and who should talk to a doctor first.
- Taste, texture and capsule size, which drive more returns than most founders expect.
- The FDA disclaimer placed near any structure/function claim on the page.
- Reviews about routine, taste and experience, never edited to add health outcomes.

Page speed matters as much as copy, because most supplement traffic arrives on a phone from social media. ## Step 5: Pick your US sales channels

### Your own Shopify store

Your store is where subscriptions, bundles, customer data and education live. It is also the only channel where you set the rules for presentation and follow-up. Expect to pay for most of your early traffic; the store becomes profitable as reorders build.

### Amazon US

Many shoppers start a supplement search on Amazon, compare ratings and buy in one session. Amazon has its own dietary supplement policy on top of federal law. Many listings must pass testing and document review through third-party organizations that Amazon accepts, with checks against the label and the listing. Requirements change, so read the current policy in Seller Central before you commit inventory. Once live, enroll in Brand Registry, watch for unauthorized resellers and expired stock, and build A+ content that explains ingredients without stretching claims.

### Walmart Marketplace

Walmart Marketplace reaches a different shopper and is often less crowded for newer supplement brands. It has its own approval process and content rules for the category. Many sellers add it once Amazon is stable, using the same documents and images.

### TikTok Shop

TikTok Shop can move volume quickly for gummies, electrolytes and other visual products through creator videos and live sessions. Health products are a restricted category there, so plan for an approval step and some ingredients that cannot be sold at all.

### Retail context

Chewy matters if you also sell pet supplements, which follow a different set of rules. Target, drugstores and grocery chains may come later, but for most direct-to-consumer brands the first year runs on the store, Amazon and one social channel.

## Step 6: Advertise within platform and FTC limits

Meta, Google and TikTok all allow supplement ads with restrictions. Policies change, so read them before each campaign. In general:

- **Meta** rejects ads that imply knowledge of personal health, use before-and-after images or create negative self-perception. Weight management products often face age limits.
- **Google** blocks some ingredients outright and limits ads that promise health outcomes. Certain products need extra review.
- **TikTok** applies strict rules to health and weight content, both in ads and in Shop listings.
- **Creators** must disclose payment or free product clearly in the post itself, and they cannot say anything your brand could not say.

Build ads around taste, ingredients, testing, routine and convenience. Keep the landing page consistent with the ad, because platform reviewers check both.

## Step 7: Run subscriptions that customers trust

Supplements have a predictable reorder date. A 30-serving tub taken daily runs out in about a month, so you know when to send a reminder and when a subscription will renew.

Subscriptions bring their own rules. The federal Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act and a growing number of state automatic renewal laws, California's among them, generally expect clear terms before checkout, express consent, a confirmation message and an easy online cancellation path. Some states also require reminders before a renewal or a free-trial conversion. Ask counsel to review your flow, then:

- Show the renewal price, frequency and how to cancel next to the subscribe button.
- Offer skip, pause and change-frequency options in the customer account.
- Send a reminder a few days before each shipment.
- Sell 60- or 90-day sizes for customers who prefer fewer deliveries.

## Step 8: Plan for the US supplement calendar

- **January.** New Year resolutions bring the biggest wave of first-time buyers for protein, creatine, greens and sleep products. Have stock, pages and creative ready by mid-December.
- **Spring and summer.** Hydration, travel packs and fitness products pick up.
- **July.** Prime Day brings deal-led shoppers on Amazon, and other retailers run competing events.
- **August and September.** Back to school brings routine resets for students and parents.
- **November and December.** Black Friday, Cyber Monday and the holidays suit bundles, gift sets and subscriber offers more than deep single-bottle discounts.

## How to sell supplements online: USA launch checklist

- [ ] Formula reviewed for ingredient status and any new dietary ingredient question.
- [ ] Manufacturer chosen with supplement cGMP records you have seen.
- [ ] Label reviewed by counsel, including Supplement Facts and the disclaimer.
- [ ] Evidence file for every structure/function claim.
- [ ] Product pages with label images, facts as text, directions and FAQs.
- [ ] Amazon testing and documents prepared before inventory ships.
- [ ] Walmart and TikTok Shop approvals requested where relevant.
- [ ] Ad and creator scripts checked against platform policies and the FTC's endorsement guidance.
- [ ] Subscription terms, reminders and online cancellation tested.
- [ ] Reporting for acquisition cost, reorder rate and margin per product.

## Mistakes US supplement brands make

- **Copying a competitor's claims.** Their risk does not become your permission.
- **Letting creators improvise.** A single unscripted line about a medical condition can sink an ad account or a listing.
- **Ordering inventory before Amazon testing is clear.** Stock that cannot be listed ties up cash.
- **Hiding the cancel button.** It invites chargebacks, complaints and regulator attention.
- **Judging on first-order return alone.** In supplements, the second and third orders pay for the first.
