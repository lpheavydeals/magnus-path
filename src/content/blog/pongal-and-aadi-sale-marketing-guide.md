Aadi sale marketing is one of the most Tamil Nadu-specific skills an online brand can learn. Every July, textile and jewellery showrooms across the state fill with "Aadi offer" banners, and buyers have come to expect discounts for the whole month. Online brands that understand why, and plan for it alongside Pongal, Tamil New Year, Deepavali and Karthigai Deepam, can sell more without giving away their margin. This guide covers the Tamil festive calendar, the culture behind Aadi discounts, how to phase ad budgets, what creative works, and how to plan stock and couriers.

Festival dates follow the Tamil and lunar calendars and can shift by a day or more between sources. Our [ecommerce sale calendar](/resources/sale-calendar/) lists confirmed dates; check a Tamil calendar before you fix delivery cut-offs.

## The Tamil festive calendar for online brands

National sale calendars focus on Diwali, the big marketplace sales and Republic Day. Tamil Nadu has its own rhythm on top of those.

| Festival | Usual timing | What sells online |
|---|---|---|
| Pongal (Bhogi, Thai Pongal, Mattu Pongal, Kaanum Pongal) | Mid-January, usually 14 or 15 Jan | Sarees, veshtis, ethnic wear, brass and clay pots, kitchenware, sweets, gifts |
| Thai month weddings | After Pongal, January to February | Silk sarees, jewellery, return gifts |
| Tamil New Year (Puthandu) | Around 14 April | New clothes, gold, home goods, festive food |
| Aadi | Mid-July to mid-August | Textiles, sarees, jewellery, appliances, home goods on offer |
| Avani weddings | After Aadi, August to September | Silk sarees, jewellery, gifting |
| Deepavali | October or November | Sarees, ethnic wear, sweets, gifts, electronics, home decor |
| Karthigai Deepam | November or December | Lamps, decor, puja items |

Two details often missed by brands planning from outside the state:

- **Deepavali in Tamil Nadu** is celebrated in the early morning, often on Naraka Chaturdashi, which can fall a day before the Lakshmi Puja date shown in national calendars. Set delivery cut-offs from the Tamil date.
- **The northeast monsoon** brings heavy rain to Chennai and coastal Tamil Nadu from about October to December, which can slow deliveries around Deepavali and Karthigai Deepam. Build in extra delivery days.

## Why Aadi is a discount month

Aadi is traditionally seen as a month to avoid weddings, housewarmings and some major new beginnings. With fewer weddings and functions, demand for silks, jewellery and household purchases would normally dip. Retailers in Tamil Nadu responded long ago with Aadi discounts, and the "Aadi sale" became a fixture, especially for textile and saree showrooms, jewellers and appliance stores.

This creates three useful patterns for online brands:

1. **Buyers wait for Aadi.** Some shoppers delay purchases until the Aadi offers appear, so a quiet June is normal in some categories.
2. **Aadi buying feeds the wedding season.** Many families buy silks and jewellery during Aadi for weddings in Avani and the months after, so Aadi is not only about clearance.
3. **Aadi Perukku is a peak.** The 18th day of Aadi, usually in early August, is considered auspicious, and many people make purchases on or around it.

Aadi also overlaps with Amazon Prime Day and the Independence Day marketplace sales, so marketplace ad costs can rise during the same weeks.

## Aadi sale marketing: set the offer and margin first

The most common Aadi mistake is copying a showroom's "flat 50% off" banner without the showroom's margins. Before you design a single post:

1. **Calculate profit per order** after marketplace or payment fees, shipping, packaging, expected returns and ad cost.
2. **Decide what you can give away** and still make money. Our [discount calculator](/tools/discount-calculator/) shows how a discount changes your margin and how many extra orders you need to break even.
3. **Choose the offer type.** Bundles (two sarees, a saree with a matching blouse piece), gifts with purchase, free shipping above a threshold and limited-time collections often protect margin better than deep percentage cuts.
4. **Separate clearance from the brand.** Put older stock in a clearly named Aadi clearance collection, and keep new arrivals at full price or a lighter offer.
5. **Set a clear end date.** Aadi has a natural end, which makes urgency honest.

## Phasing ad budgets for Pongal and Aadi

Pongal and Aadi have different shapes, so they need different budget plans.

### Pongal: a short, sharp peak

- **Four to three weeks before:** test creatives and offers on small budgets, and build remarketing audiences from video viewers and store visitors.
- **Final two weeks:** scale the winning ads, push the delivery cut-off before Bhogi, and add retargeting with catalogue ads.
- **During the festival:** reduce prospecting, keep retargeting for late buyers who can still get delivery, and promote gift cards or post-Pongal offers.

### Aadi: a month-long season

- **Two to three weeks before Aadi:** test offers and creatives; announce "Aadi collection coming soon" to warm up followers and your WhatsApp list.
- **Opening days:** a strong push, because many buyers have been waiting.
- **Middle weeks:** steady spend, heavier on retargeting and on top sellers.
- **Around Aadi Perukku and the final week:** raise spend again with "last days" messaging.
- **Throughout:** cut ad sets that stop earning a profit.

## Creative that works for Tamil festive sales

- **Tamil and English versions.** Test Tamil voice-overs and captions against English for the same offer. Have Tamil copy written or checked by a fluent Tamil writer.
- **Festival visuals done with care.** Kolams, pongal pots, sugarcane, oil lamps and jasmine are familiar and warm; use them naturally rather than as clip art.
- **Show the product in context.** A saree draped for a family function, a brass pot on a Pongal kolam, lamps lit for Karthigai.
- **Put the offer and the date in the first frame.** "Aadi offer till [date]" or "Order by [date] for Pongal delivery".
- **Make the delivery promise visible.** In ads, on product pages and at checkout.
- **Use WhatsApp and email.** Your own lists are the lowest-cost way to reach past buyers before the ads even start.

## Festive playbooks by category

The same calendar plays out differently by category. A few patterns to plan around:

### Sarees and ethnic wear

Pongal, Aadi and Deepavali are all strong. During Aadi, lead with silks and wedding collections for the Avani season as well as clearance, and offer blouse stitching early enough to finish before the next function. Pongal is a good moment for veshtis and matching family sets.

### Jewellery

Aadi offers are a long-standing habit at jewellers, often on making charges rather than the metal price. Online, show the price breakup clearly and keep any offer easy to understand. Tamil New Year and Deepavali bring gold and gifting demand, and Aadi buying often feeds weddings in the months that follow.

### Food, sweets and gifting

Pongal and Deepavali drive orders for sweets, savouries, jaggery, ghee and gift boxes, including corporate gifting and parcels to family abroad. Fix shelf life, packaging and delivery cut-offs first; late food deliveries do the most damage to reviews.

### Home, kitchen and decor

Bhogi clean-ups and new pots for Pongal lift kitchenware and home goods, and Karthigai Deepam brings demand for lamps and decor. Aadi offers on appliances and furnishings are common. Heavy or fragile items need sturdy packing and realistic delivery dates.

### Marketplace or your own store

Amazon and Flipkart sale events often overlap Aadi and the Deepavali run-up. Use marketplaces for reach during their own events, and use your own store for bundles, Tamil-language landing pages and offers to past buyers on WhatsApp and email. Keep prices consistent so one channel does not undercut the other.

## Stock, packing and courier planning

A festive sale that runs out of stock or ships late wastes the ad budget that created the demand.

- **Forecast by product.** Use last year's festive orders, or recent sales trends if you are new, and add stock depth to the top sellers and core sizes.
- **Order packaging early.** Boxes, tape, tissue and festive inserts run short in peak weeks.
- **Book courier capacity.** Tell your courier or aggregator about expected volumes. Pickups and hubs slow down around Pongal's four days and during monsoon rain.
- **Verify COD orders.** Festive impulse orders raise refusal risk. Confirm COD orders on WhatsApp before dispatch; our guide to [reducing RTO and COD returns](/blog/how-to-reduce-rto-cod-returns/) explains how.
- **Staff customer service.** More orders bring more "where is my order" questions; reply in Tamil and English.
- **Plan returns.** Expect more exchanges after the sale and keep stock available for size swaps.

## After the sale: measure what you kept

Revenue is the wrong final number. After each festival, review:

- Orders, revenue and profit after returns, refunds and ad cost.
- RTO and return rates by product and pin code.
- New customers acquired and how many bought again within 60 days.
- Which creative, language version and offer type earned the most profit.

Write these down. They become next year's plan, and they turn Aadi sale marketing from guesswork into a repeatable season.
