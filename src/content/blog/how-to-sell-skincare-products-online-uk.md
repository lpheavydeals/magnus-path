If you are working out how to sell skincare products online, UK shoppers are a demanding but rewarding audience. They know their actives, they compare strengths across supermarket own labels and high-street chemists, and they read reviews before trying anything new on their face. They also come back when a product suits them. This guide takes a new or growing UK brand through the work in order: compliance, range, store, marketplaces, advertising, delivery and returns, and the second order that makes the numbers work.

Nothing here is legal advice. Treat the rules below as a map of what to ask your Responsible Person, safety assessor or adviser, not as the final word.

## Step 1: Meet the UK cosmetics rules before you sell

Skincare sold in Great Britain sits under the UK Cosmetics Regulation. It does not need a licence in the way a medicine does, but a few duties must be in place before your first sale.

### A Responsible Person

Every cosmetic placed on the GB market needs a Responsible Person based in the UK. This can be your own company if it is UK-registered, or a specialist firm if you are based abroad. The Responsible Person holds the product information file, makes sure the safety assessment is in place and answers to regulators if something goes wrong.

### A safety assessment

Each formula needs a cosmetic product safety report, prepared by a suitably qualified safety assessor. It looks at the ingredients, their concentrations, stability, preservative performance and how the product will be used. If you change the formula, the report usually needs updating too.

### SCPN notification

Before a product goes on sale, it is generally notified through the government's Submit Cosmetic Product Notification (SCPN) service. Do this for every product and variant you list. Northern Ireland broadly follows EU cosmetics rules, so check what applies if you plan to ship there from day one.

### Labels and claims

UK labels typically need the Responsible Person's name and address, the nominal contents, a durability date or period-after-opening symbol, precautions, a batch number, the product's function where it is not obvious and the full ingredient list. Claims must be truthful and supported by evidence. Trading Standards enforces product safety and labelling, and the ASA polices advertising under the CAP Code. If your copy drifts towards treating a skin condition, the product may be seen as a medicine, which brings in the MHRA.

## Step 2: Choose a short range you can explain

A UK brand does not need twenty products at launch. It needs a reason to exist next to the cheap own-label serums in every supermarket.

- **Sell a routine.** Three to five products that work together, such as a cleanser, a treatment serum, a moisturiser and a daily SPF, make it natural to buy more than one item.
- **Own one angle.** A specific active at a stated strength, sensitive skin, mature skin, a fragrance-free line or skin that suffers in central heating all give buyers something to remember.
- **Include a replenishment product.** Cleanser and sunscreen run out regularly. Masks and treatments do not, so a range built only on occasional products struggles to grow.
- **Plan sizes for trial and value.** Minis lower the risk of a first purchase. Larger sizes and duos raise order value on your own store.

Before you order packaging, work out what each pack actually earns after manufacturing, packaging, postage, payment fees, marketplace fees, refunds and the advertising cost of winning each order. ## Step 3: Build a Shopify store that answers careful buyers

Your own store is where you control prices, bundles and the customer relationship. Shopify suits most independent UK skincare brands because payments, Klarna or Clearpay, reviews, subscriptions and email tools are quick to add.

### What a skincare product page needs

UK buyers often arrive having already read about the active on Reddit or watched a creator test it. Your page has to go further than they have:

- The key active, its percentage and what it does, in plain English.
- Which skin types and concerns it suits, and who should patch test or avoid it.
- Texture, finish and scent, shown in a short video rather than described.
- When to use it, how much, and which actives not to pair it with.
- A realistic idea of what someone might notice after a few days and after several weeks.
- The full INCI list and the pack size.
- Reviews that show skin type and age range, so shoppers find people like themselves.

### Prices, delivery and trust

Show VAT-inclusive prices if you are VAT registered, clear delivery options (Royal Mail, Evri or DPD, with cut-off times) and a plain returns policy near the add-to-basket button. Make checkout short on mobile, and offer Apple Pay and Google Pay alongside cards.

### Content that earns search traffic

Write guides on how to introduce retinol, why SPF matters with exfoliating acids, and simple routines for different skin types. These pages bring in shoppers from Google and AI assistants, and they give your ads somewhere better to land than a bare product page.

## Step 4: Add Amazon UK, TikTok Shop and other marketplaces

Marketplaces put you in front of people who are already buying. Each one plays a different part.

| Channel | Who you reach | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon UK | Shoppers searching by ingredient, need and delivery speed | Search-led titles, A+ content, Sponsored Products on hero items |
| TikTok Shop UK | Younger buyers who discover through video and live selling | Shoppable videos, affiliate creators, short-run offers |
| eBay | Value-focused buyers, including for gift sets | Sets, bundles and end-of-line stock |
| Etsy | Shoppers looking for handmade or small-batch products | Small-batch balms and gift sets, if genuinely handmade |
| Own Shopify store | People who already know your brand | Full range, bundles, subscriptions, loyalty |

Large beauty retailers such as Boots, Superdrug, LOOKFANTASTIC and Cult Beauty are where many UK buyers browse, but they choose what they stock. Strong direct sales and reviews help any future conversation with a buyer there; nobody can promise you a listing.

Keep prices aligned across channels. If your store always costs more than Amazon, loyal customers learn to buy elsewhere and you lose their data. Give the store its own reasons to visit: routine sets, refill sizes or a loyalty scheme.

## Step 5: Advertise inside the rules

Paid social and search bring most young skincare brands their first steady orders. They are also where rejected ads and complaints start.

### Meta and TikTok ads

Show texture, application and a real morning or evening routine. Avoid language that points at personal characteristics ("Got acne?"), dramatic before-and-after shots and filtered faces. The ASA has ruled against beauty ads that used filters to exaggerate results, so keep footage honest. Paid and gifted creator posts must be clearly labelled as ads.

### Google Search and Shopping

Search ads catch people who know what they want, such as "fragrance-free moisturiser for sensitive skin" or "SPF 50 for oily skin". A clean Merchant Center feed with clear titles, accurate GTINs and good images is as important as your bids.

### Amazon Sponsored Products

Sponsored ads help new listings collect their first sales and reviews. Watch cost per order against margin, and move spend towards products that bring customers back.

Judge each product, not only each campaign. A serum that is cheap to sell once but rarely reordered can be worth less than an SPF that costs more to win and returns every few months.

## Step 6: Get delivery, returns and VAT right

UK shoppers expect quick, tracked delivery and clear terms. A few points to settle before launch:

- **Packaging.** Pumps, droppers and tubes need seals and padding that survive a sorting centre. Leaks become refunds and poor reviews.
- **Postage size.** Pack dimensions decide whether an order goes as a large letter or a parcel, which changes your delivery cost and speed.
- **Returns.** Online buyers generally have a cancellation period. Sealed products that are opened after delivery can be an exception on hygiene grounds, if you explain this clearly before purchase. Faulty or misdescribed goods are always your responsibility.
- **VAT.** Register once your taxable turnover passes the registration threshold, or earlier by choice. Check HMRC guidance or an accountant, especially if you sell from abroad.
- **Data.** UK GDPR and cookie consent apply to your store and email list. Collect only what you need and keep consent records.

## Step 7: Plan for the UK beauty calendar

Skincare sells all year, but some weeks matter more than others:

1. **Black Friday and Cyber Monday:** gift sets and routine bundles, with stock and ads ready by early November.
2. **Christmas:** gifting pages, last-order dates by courier and gift wrap if you can offer it.
3. **Boxing Day and the January sales:** routine resets, new-year habits and value sizes.
4. **Valentine's Day:** sets for couples and self-care gifts.
5. **Mother's Day in March:** gift sets and minis; remember it falls earlier than in many other countries.
6. **Spring and summer:** daily SPF and lighter textures as the days get longer.

Planning stock, creative and offers a month ahead of each peak avoids running out just as demand arrives.

## Step 8: Build repeat orders and reviews

Skincare is a replenishment category. The second and third orders are where profit lives.

### A simple post-purchase journey

1. Days 1–3: how to use the product and where it sits in a routine.
2. Week 2: a friendly check-in and links to useful guides.
3. Shortly before it runs out: a refill reminder with a small loyalty reward.
4. After the reorder: a review request with photo upload.

### Minis that lead to full size

Paid minis and discovery kits work when you follow up with usage tips and a credit towards the full size. Free samples handed to deal hunters rarely convert.

### Honest reviews

Ask for a review once the buyer has used the product long enough to judge it. UK consumer law now treats fake or incentivised reviews that mislead buyers seriously, so never buy reviews or hide negative ones.

### Customer service

Buyers will ask whether a product suits their skin, whether they can use it with prescribed creams, or why they reacted. Reply quickly, stay within cosmetic wording and suggest a pharmacist or GP for anything medical. Add the common questions to your product pages so the next buyer finds the answer without asking.
