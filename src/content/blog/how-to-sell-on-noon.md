If you are working out how to sell on Noon, the short version is this: register a seller account with a UAE or Saudi business, prepare clean product data in English and Arabic, price after Noon's fees, choose how you will fulfil orders, and use Noon Ads to get early visibility. Noon is one of the two main online marketplaces in the Gulf, alongside Amazon.ae and Amazon.sa, and many shoppers check both before they buy. This guide covers each step, from registration to peak sales, with honest notes on where the rules change.

Noon updates its requirements, fees and programmes regularly. Treat this guide as a map, and confirm the details in Noon's seller centre before you commit stock or money.

## Who can sell on Noon

Noon is built for registered businesses rather than casual individual sellers. Noon runs separate marketplaces for the UAE and Saudi Arabia, and each one expects a business set up in that country.

In general you will need:

- **A registered business.** In the UAE, a valid trade licence (mainland or free zone) whose activity covers trading or e-commerce. In Saudi Arabia, a commercial registration.
- **VAT registration where required.** The UAE charges VAT at 5%, and businesses above the registration threshold must register with the Federal Tax Authority. Saudi Arabia has its own VAT rules through ZATCA. If you are registered, Noon will ask for your tax number.
- **A local business bank account** in the company's name for payouts.
- **The right to sell your products.** Brand owners, authorised distributors and resellers are all common, but some brands and categories need approval or proof of authorisation.

Some categories, such as beauty, health, food and electronics, can carry extra requirements, for example product registrations or certificates from local authorities. Check these before you source stock, not after.

## Documents usually needed

Keep these ready before you open the registration form. The exact list depends on your country, business type and categories:

- Trade licence or commercial registration (valid, not close to expiry)
- Emirates ID or passport and visa of the owner or authorised signatory
- VAT registration certificate, if registered
- Bank account details, often with an IBAN letter or bank statement in the company's name
- Brand registration or authorisation letters for the brands you plan to sell
- Product barcodes (GTIN/EAN) where Noon requires them
- Category-specific approvals or certificates where relevant

Most onboarding delays come from documents that do not match: a different company name on the bank letter and the licence, an expired visa, or a licence activity that does not cover online trading.

## Registration steps

The registration flow changes over time, but the broad path looks like this:

1. **Start registration** on Noon's seller site and choose the country you want to sell in.
2. **Create your account** with a business email and mobile number.
3. **Enter business details** and upload your licence, identity, VAT and bank documents.
4. **Wait for verification.** Noon reviews your documents and may ask for corrections or extra information.
5. **Set up your store profile**, warehouse or pickup address, and return address.
6. **Add your first products**, either by matching existing listings or creating new ones.
7. **Choose your fulfilment model** and send stock or prepare to ship orders.

Use the waiting time well. Build your product spreadsheet, shoot photos and get Arabic content reviewed while your account is being approved.

## Listing products in English and Arabic

Noon's catalogue runs in both English and Arabic, and shoppers can switch between them. Strong listings in both languages are one of the clearest advantages a seller can build.

### Match or create

If a product already exists on Noon, you can usually offer against the existing listing. If it is your own brand, you create the listing and own the content. Either way, check that the existing content is accurate before you attach your offer to it.

### Titles and attributes

Write titles with the brand, product type, key feature, size or variant, in the order shoppers search. Fill every attribute: colour, size, material, compatibility, pack size. Filters on Noon rely on these fields, and incomplete attributes hide products from filtered searches.

### Arabic content

Do not rely on raw machine translation. Arabic product names, sizes and units need to read naturally, and some English brand terms are better kept in Latin script. Have a fluent speaker review titles, key bullet points and descriptions. Good Arabic content helps you appear in Arabic searches and builds trust with shoppers who browse in Arabic.

### Images

Use a clean main image on a white background, then close-ups, scale shots and lifestyle images. Follow Noon's current image guidelines for size and background. If your packaging is in English only, show any Arabic labelling that is on the product, as buyers in some categories look for it.

## Pricing and fees

Noon charges fees that vary by category, plus fulfilment, storage or shipping costs depending on how you deliver, and any advertising you choose to run. The rates change, so always work from Noon's current fee schedule in the seller centre. Do not copy fee figures from old blog posts or forums.

Build each product's price from the bottom up:

1. **Landed cost:** product cost, freight, customs and duties.
2. **Noon fees:** the referral or commission fee for your category, plus any fixed fees.
3. **Fulfilment:** Fulfilled by Noon charges, or your own courier and packing costs.
4. **Returns:** an allowance for returned, damaged or unsellable units.
5. **VAT:** shopper-facing prices in the UAE and Saudi Arabia are normally shown VAT-inclusive, so make sure your margin is calculated on the net amount.
6. **Advertising:** a realistic budget per unit, especially at launch.

Shoppers compare prices across Noon and Amazon.ae in seconds. If a product cannot make money at a competitive price after fees, it is better to know before you list it. Our [marketplace profit calculator](/tools/marketplace-profit-calculator/) can help with the first pass.

## Fulfilment options

Noon broadly offers two ways to get orders to customers.

### Fulfilled by Noon

With Fulfilled by Noon (often shortened to FBN), you send stock to Noon's warehouses. Noon stores it, picks and packs orders, delivers them and handles many returns. Products are usually eligible for faster delivery promises, which matters on a marketplace where shoppers expect quick delivery. You pay fulfilment and storage fees, and you must follow Noon's inbound rules on labelling, packaging and appointments.

### Seller-fulfilled

You keep stock in your own warehouse and ship orders yourself or through a courier arrangement Noon supports. This gives you more control and suits bulky, slow-moving or made-to-order items. The trade-off is that you carry the delivery promise, and late dispatch or cancellations hurt your account health.

Many sellers use both: fast-moving products in FBN, and the long tail seller-fulfilled. Cash on delivery is still common in the region, so plan for some orders being refused at the door, whichever model you use.

## Noon Ads basics

Noon Ads lets you promote products in search results and on product and category pages. The basics are similar to other marketplace ads:

- **Start with sponsored product campaigns** on your best listings, using both automatic and keyword-targeted campaigns.
- **Include Arabic keywords.** Many shoppers search in Arabic, and competition there can differ from English.
- **Fix the listing first.** Ads send traffic; poor images, thin content or uncompetitive prices waste it.
- **Review search terms weekly.** Add terms that convert and exclude ones that spend without selling.
- **Track profit, not only sales.** Measure ad cost against margin after fees, not against revenue.

Ad tools evolve, so check the current options in your seller account.

## Ramadan, Yellow Friday and other peaks

Sales on Noon follow the regional calendar. The main moments most sellers plan for are:

- **Ramadan and Eid.** Shopping patterns shift during Ramadan, with more late-evening browsing and strong demand for food, home, fashion, gifting and Eid outfits. Eid al-Adha brings another gifting and travel peak.
- **Yellow Friday.** Noon's big annual sale around late November, its answer to Black Friday. Amazon.ae runs its own White Friday event in the same period, so shoppers compare deals across both.

For each peak, confirm deal deadlines in the seller centre early, as submissions often close weeks before the event. Send FBN stock in good time, because inbound capacity tightens before big sales, and raise ad budgets gradually rather than on the day.

## Noon vs Amazon.ae

Both marketplaces matter in the UAE. Here is how they compare in broad terms:

| Area | Noon | Amazon.ae |
|---|---|---|
| Markets | UAE, Saudi Arabia and Egypt marketplaces | UAE, with separate Amazon stores for Saudi Arabia and Egypt |
| Seller set-up | Registered local business usually required | Local business; some international sellers may qualify through Amazon's global selling options |
| Languages | English and Arabic | English and Arabic |
| Fulfilment | Fulfilled by Noon or seller-fulfilled | Fulfilment by Amazon or seller-fulfilled |
| Advertising | Noon Ads | Amazon Ads (Sponsored Products, Brands, Display) |
| Big sale | Yellow Friday | White Friday, Prime Day |
| Fees | Vary by category; see Noon's fee schedule | Vary by category; see Amazon's fee schedule |

Neither is simply better. Look at where your category has stronger demand, how crowded each search results page is, and which fulfilment programme fits your stock. Many UAE brands start on one and add the other within months.

## Common mistakes

Most early problems on Noon come from a short list of mistakes:

- **Mismatched documents.** Company names, licence activities and bank details that do not line up slow down approval.
- **Weak Arabic content.** Machine-translated titles look careless and miss Arabic searches.
- **Pricing before fees.** Sellers set prices from cost plus margin, then find fees, fulfilment and returns have eaten the profit.
- **Running out of stock before peaks.** Late FBN shipments mean missing Yellow Friday or Ramadan entirely.
- **Ignoring account health.** Late dispatch, cancellations and poor reviews reduce visibility over time.
- **Copying Amazon listings as they are.** The basics transfer, but each marketplace has its own attributes, search behaviour and content rules.

Avoid these and learning how to sell on Noon becomes a matter of steady, repeatable work.
