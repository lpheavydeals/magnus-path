These footwear marketing ideas Australia's shoe brands can use are built around a simple truth: an order only counts if the pair stays on someone's feet. Sizes vary between brands, local labels mix UK and US systems, and many shoppers order two sizes to be safe. Good marketing reduces that guesswork while still making people want the shoe. The fourteen ideas below are grouped into fit, desire, timing and reach, followed by marketplace and store ideas, a measurement plan and a 30-day start.

## Why footwear marketing works differently

- **Fit decides profit.** Every return costs postage both ways, handling and often a discount on resale.
- **Sizing is inconsistent.** Men's AU sizes often follow UK sizing, women's sit nearer US sizes, and imports may be EU only.
- **Catalogues are big.** One style in several colours and many sizes creates dozens of variants for ads and listings to track.
- **Demand is seasonal.** Thong and sandal season, the January school rush and winter boots each need their own stock and creative.

## Fit-focused ideas

### 1. Make your size guide a piece of content

Turn the chart into a short video, a printable measuring sheet and a page that ranks on Google.com.au for searches such as "how to measure foot size at home". Link to it from every product page and from your ads. Include a photo of a foot against a wall with a ruler, a note on measuring late in the day when feet are slightly larger, and a reminder to measure both feet and use the longer one. Content this practical gets saved, shared in parent groups and quoted by AI assistants when someone asks how shoe sizes work in Australia.

### 2. Let customer feedback write your fit notes

Ask reviewers for their usual size and how the pair fitted. Summarise the pattern on each style: "Most buyers take their usual size; if you are between sizes, go up." Refresh the notes monthly from return reasons.

### 3. Explain width plainly

Many school shoes and work boots come in more than one width, and letter codes confuse people. Show a simple diagram of what each width means and which styles suit broader feet. Buyers with wide feet often struggle to find options, so clear width information can win loyal customers.

### 4. Offer live size help

A chat or message option staffed by someone who knows the range answers "I'm a 9 in another brand, what do I take?" before the order, not after. Save the common answers as templates and turn them into FAQs.

## Look and desire ideas

### 5. Film every style being worn

A short walking clip, a sole flex and a close-up of the stitching sell more than a white-background render. Shoot outdoors in natural light to show true colours, and state the model's usual size in the caption.

### 6. Style shoes by occasion

Build collections and ads around moments: a summer weekend, the first day of school, a long shift on site, a wedding, a winter commute. People shop for the occasion before the product.

### 7. Work with creators who wear your kind of shoe

Runners, tradies, parents, nurses and sneaker collectors each speak to a different buyer. Ask creators to say their usual size and how the pair fitted, and make sure every paid post is clearly disclosed in line with the AANA codes.

### 8. Show how the shoe is made

If you design locally, use a particular leather or rebuild soles, film it. Only say "made in Australia" or "Australian designed" when the claim is accurate under the Australian Consumer Law; origin labelling for footwear is generally voluntary, but misleading claims are not allowed.

## Launch and timing ideas

### 9. Plan limited releases

New colourways and small runs give you a reason to email, post and advertise. Build a waitlist, announce a date and share the size range in advance so buyers can measure before release day. Show the release on your store, email list and social channels at the same time, and keep a small reserve of popular sizes for exchanges, so early buyers who need a swap are not left without one.

### 10. Follow the Australian footwear calendar

Sandals and thongs in summer, school shoes from late January, boots and clearance around EOFY in June, gifts for Father's Day in September, then Click Frenzy, Black Friday, Christmas and Boxing Day. Prepare stock by size and creative several weeks ahead of each.

### 11. Prepare for marketplace events

Amazon AU and eBay AU run their own promotions around the big sale windows. Decide in advance which styles join, which sizes you can support and what discount still leaves margin after fees and returns.

## Reach and ads ideas

### 12. Keep catalogue ads in step with stock

Feed size, colour, gender, age group and material for every variant, use GST-inclusive AUD prices and remove sold-out sizes fast. Ads that promote unavailable sizes waste money and annoy buyers.

### 13. Match retargeting to the shopper's doubt

Someone who viewed the size chart needs fit reassurance. Someone who abandoned the cart may want to know about Afterpay or size swaps. Someone who browsed several colours may respond to a styling video. Split audiences by behaviour and change the message.

### 14. Follow up once the shoes arrive

Send care tips and a fit check a few days after delivery, with a quick swap link if needed. Ask for a review with size feedback a couple of weeks later, then suggest socks, care products or a second colour.

## Marketplace ideas

**Amazon AU.** Complete every size and material attribute, use the size chart fields and keep colours under one parent listing so reviews build together.

**eBay AU and Kogan.** Useful for core styles and older stock. Use the same charts and photos as your store.

**TikTok Shop.** Check it is open to your category, then test creator-led drops for sneakers and sandals. Hold back stock in the popular sizes.

## Email and SMS ideas

Your list is the cheapest channel you own, so use it for more than discounts:

- **Welcome series.** Introduce the size guide in the first message, then the story behind the brand, then customer favourites with fit notes.
- **Back-in-stock alerts by size.** Let buyers register for their size, not just the style. These messages often convert strongly because the intent is already there.
- **Replacement reminders.** Running shoes, work boots and kids' shoes wear out or are outgrown. A friendly reminder months after purchase, with the buyer's last size, makes reordering easy.
- **Seasonal edits.** A short "school shoes are here" or "boot season" email with sizes in stock beats a generic sale blast.

Keep SMS for time-sensitive messages such as release days and size restocks, and follow the Spam Act rules on consent and unsubscribing.

## Ideas for your own store

- **A fit finder** that asks for usual size in two or three well-known brands and suggests yours
- **Collection pages people search for**, such as "wide fit work boots" or "black leather school shoes"
- **Sensible bundles**, such as school shoes with socks, or boots with care products
- **Exchanges as a selling point**, explained near the add-to-cart button, with Afterpay shown for pricier pairs

## Offline and community ideas

Even an online-only brand can build presence in the real world. Pop-up fitting days at markets or events let people try your sizing and give you useful fit feedback. Local running clubs, school uniform shops, workwear stores and trade suppliers can all introduce your shoes to the right buyers, either as stockists or as referral partners with their own discount code. Sponsor a local junior sports team and photograph the kids in your school or sports range, with parents' consent. Every offline contact should point back to your store's size guide.

## How to measure footwear marketing

Report net revenue after exchanges and refunds by style, size and campaign. Track the exchange rate, the main return reasons, cost per new customer and how many buyers come back for a second pair. Use the [ROAS calculator](/tools/roas-calculator/) with net figures rather than platform-reported revenue, so you can see which campaigns truly pay.

Look at the numbers by size as well as by style. If one style's smallest and largest sizes come back far more often than the middle sizes, the chart probably needs adjusting at the ends of the range. If one campaign brings buyers who exchange more than others, check whether its creative shows the shoe accurately. Review these figures monthly with whoever writes your product pages, so fixes reach the store quickly rather than sitting in a report.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Scaling ads before the size chart is reliable
- Advertising styles whose popular sizes are sold out
- Comfort or performance claims you cannot support
- Returns wording that suggests customers have no consumer rights
- Judging creators on views rather than pairs kept

## Footwear marketing ideas: Australia 30-day starting plan

**Week 1.** Rebuild the size chart on foot length, add width and fit notes, and publish a measuring guide.

**Week 2.** Shoot on-foot photos and walking clips for your top ten styles, and clean the product feed.

**Week 3.** Launch catalogue ads with video, set up retargeting by behaviour and brief two or three creators.

**Week 4.** Add post-delivery fit checks and review requests, and start planning stock and creative for the next sale window.
