If you are wondering how to sell on Blinkit, Zepto or Swiggy Instamart, you are not alone. Quick commerce has changed how many Indian city shoppers buy groceries, snacks, personal care and everyday items, and brands of every size want a place on those apps. Getting listed is only the first step. Staying in stock across cities, pricing for a margin and staying visible on a crowded screen decide whether quick commerce becomes a real channel or an expensive experiment.

This guide explains how quick commerce works, which products fit, what you need to get started, the routes to getting listed, and how to plan pricing, ads, stock and metrics.

## How quick commerce works

Quick commerce for brands is different from a regular marketplace. Platforms like Blinkit, Zepto and Swiggy Instamart run networks of **dark stores**: small warehouses inside city neighbourhoods that are closed to walk-in customers. Each dark store holds a limited range of products and serves a small delivery radius, which is how orders reach customers within minutes.

This model has three big consequences for a brand:

- **Assortment is city-level and often store-level.** Your product may be live in one city and missing in another, or stocked in some dark stores and not others.
- **Shelf space is limited.** A dark store cannot hold every brand. Platforms favour products that sell steadily.
- **Availability is everything.** If the nearest dark store is out of stock, the shopper usually cannot buy your product at all, and will pick a competitor in seconds.

## Which products fit quick commerce

Quick commerce works best for products that people want soon and buy repeatedly. Good fits include:

- Snacks, beverages, breakfast items and staples
- Personal care, beauty and grooming
- Baby care and household essentials
- Health and wellness products with clear labelling
- Small gifting items, especially around festivals

Products that are bulky, fragile, slow-moving or need a long buying decision are harder to sell, though the range of categories on these apps keeps growing. Before you apply, ask a simple question: would a shopper want this delivered in the next few minutes, and would they buy it again?

## Typical requirements

Exact requirements vary by platform and category, and they change. In general, expect to need:

- **GST registration** for your business.
- **FSSAI licence** for any food or beverage product, with correct details on the label.
- **Other category licences** where relevant, such as for cosmetics or certain health products.
- **Barcodes** on every product unit, so dark stores can receive, store and pick items accurately.
- **Compliant packaging and labels** with MRP, net quantity, manufacturer details, dates and other legally required information.
- **Sturdy, retail-ready packaging** that survives fast handling and rider delivery.
- **Clean product data:** titles, images, pack sizes, ingredients or specifications, shelf life and case sizes.
- **Supply capacity** to deliver to the platform's warehouses or dark stores on schedule.

Treat this list as a starting point, not a complete checklist. Confirm the current documents and standards with each platform before you print packaging or plan production.

## Getting listed: direct vs distributor

There are two broad routes onto quick commerce apps.

### Direct brand or seller programmes

Each platform has some way for brands to apply directly, often through a brand partnership or seller onboarding process. You share company details, documents, product information and pricing, and the platform's category team reviews it. If approved, you agree commercial terms and supply arrangements.

**Strengths:** direct relationship with the platform, more control over pricing and promotions, better data access in many cases.
**Watch-outs:** you handle supply, replenishment and invoicing yourself, which needs operations capacity.

### Through a distributor

Many brands, especially newer ones, enter through distributors who already supply quick commerce dark stores. The distributor handles purchase orders, stock movement and often replenishment.

**Strengths:** faster entry, less operational load, access to the distributor's existing relationships.
**Watch-outs:** the distributor takes a margin, and you may have less control and less data.

Onboarding processes, approval criteria and commercial terms differ between Blinkit, Zepto and Swiggy Instamart and change over time. We do not quote specific fees, margins or onboarding charges here. Check each platform's current process directly, and get all commercial terms in writing before you commit.

## Pricing and margins

Quick commerce can look attractive on revenue and still lose money. Work out your unit economics before you launch:

- Selling price on the app (and how it compares with other channels)
- Product, packaging and freight cost to the platform's warehouse
- Platform margin or commission, and any listing or other charges in your agreement
- Distributor margin, if you use one
- Promotional discounts and visibility spend
- Damages, expiry and returns

Keep pricing consistent across channels where you can. Shoppers compare, and big gaps between quick commerce, marketplaces and your own store create confusion. Our [marketplace profit calculator](/tools/marketplace-profit-calculator/) helps you check margin per unit before and after ad costs.

## Visibility and ads

Even when you are stocked, your product competes on a small mobile screen. Shoppers decide fast, usually from search results, category pages or banners. Visibility comes from three things:

1. **Strong product content.** Clear main image, readable pack size, accurate title and the words shoppers search for.
2. **Steady availability.** Platforms tend to show products that are in stock and selling well.
3. **Ads.** Each platform offers ad formats such as sponsored search placements, category placements and banners. Formats, minimum spends and targeting options vary by platform, so check the current options in each ads dashboard.

Ad competition is rising. Quick-commerce advertising in India is projected to grow from about ₹4,000 crore to ₹6,000 crore in 2026 (Pitch Madison). Practical tips:

- Advertise only where you are in stock. Ads on out-of-stock cities waste money.
- Start with your hero products and your strongest cities.
- Bid on category terms (such as "peanut butter" or "face wash") as well as your brand name.
- Track results by city and by product, not just in total.
- Plan bigger pushes around festivals, weekends and seasonal demand.

## Stock planning per city

Stock is where most quick commerce launches fail. Because assortment is city-level and storage is tight, you need to plan inventory city by city:

- **Start focused.** Launch in a few cities where you already have brand awareness or strong demand, rather than everywhere at once.
- **Forecast from sell-through.** Use the platform's sales data to estimate weekly demand per city and adjust purchase orders.
- **Watch shelf life.** Food and beauty products with short shelf life need careful batch planning to avoid expiry losses.
- **Keep buffer stock.** Festivals, weather changes and promotions can spike demand quickly.
- **Coordinate with ads.** Before raising ad spend in a city, confirm stock can keep up.

If you use a distributor, agree how often replenishment happens and how stock data will be shared with you.

## Metrics to watch

Track these regularly, ideally weekly, and broken down by city and product:

- **Availability (in-stock rate):** the share of relevant dark stores where your product is in stock.
- **Sell-through:** how fast units move, which drives future orders from the platform.
- **Search and category rank:** where your products appear for key terms.
- **Ad metrics:** spend, impressions, clicks, orders and return on ad spend, by city.
- **Net margin per unit:** after platform terms, distributor margin, discounts and ad costs.
- **Repeat purchase:** quick commerce rewards products people buy again.
- **Ratings and reviews:** where the platform shows them.

## A simple first 90 days

If you are launching for the first time, a staged plan keeps risk low:

- **Weeks 1 to 4:** finalise documents, barcodes, packaging and product content. Agree commercial terms in writing and confirm which cities and dark stores you will supply first.
- **Weeks 5 to 8:** go live in a small number of cities with your hero products. Watch availability daily and fix stock gaps quickly. Run modest ads on category and brand terms.
- **Weeks 9 to 12:** review sell-through, margin and ad results by city. Increase stock and ads where products move well, and fix or pause what does not. Only then plan the next cities.

This approach gives you real data before you commit large stock and ad budgets.

## Common mistakes

- **Launching in too many cities at once.** Stock spreads thin and stock-outs follow.
- **Ignoring unit economics.** Revenue grows while every order loses money after platform terms and discounts.
- **Weak packaging.** Damaged or leaking products lead to complaints and delisting risk.
- **Advertising without stock.** Spend goes to cities where shoppers cannot buy.
- **Poor product images.** A small, unclear pack shot loses to a competitor on a crowded screen.
- **No one owning the channel.** Quick commerce needs weekly attention to stock, content and ads.
