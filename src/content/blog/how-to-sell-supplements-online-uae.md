Anyone researching how to sell supplements online UAE customers will come back for soon learns that a good formula is just the entry ticket. Vitamins, protein, gummies and herbal blends usually need to be registered before they reach a shelf or a doorstep, labels are expected to carry Arabic, marketplaces ask for paperwork, and ad platforms review health products closely. This guide takes the work in the order most brands meet it, from working out your category to the refill that makes the business pay.

One caveat before the steps. Our work is ecommerce growth; regulatory and legal advice sits with specialists. The rules below are described in general terms, and they are revised from time to time. Confirm your own products' position with a qualified adviser, a registration agent or your distributor before you print packs or book a launch date.

## Step 1: Work out which category your product falls into

In the UAE, the route a supplement takes depends on what it contains, how much of it and what you say it does. In broad terms there are three groups:

- **Health and nutritional supplements.** Vitamins, minerals, many botanicals and other products sold in dose form are generally registered at federal level. This has been the job of the Ministry of Health and Prevention (MOHAP), and registration of health products is moving to the Emirates Drug Establishment. Check which body handles your product type at the time you apply.
- **Products treated as food.** Protein powders, bars, sports drinks and some functional foods are often handled as food. In Dubai, food products are generally registered through Dubai Municipality's Montaji system. In the capital, food safety is the remit of ADAFSA, the Abu Dhabi Agriculture and Food Safety Authority, which may have a role for products handled there.
- **Borderline products.** High doses, unusual botanicals or anything that sounds medicinal may be treated more like a medicine, with a slower and stricter path.

The same powder can fall into a different group depending on the dose and the wording on the tub. Settle the category before you design packs, because changing it later means new artwork, new documents and sometimes a new application.

## Step 2: Decide who holds the registration

Most overseas brands cannot register and import on their own. A UAE-licensed company usually holds the product registration and brings the stock in. Common setups:

- **A distributor holds it.** This is often the quickest route. The risk is control: if you part ways, moving the registration can take time. Agree in writing who owns it and what happens if the contract ends.
- **Your own UAE company.** A mainland or free zone entity with the right licensed activity can hold registrations and import. Selling from a free zone to consumers across the country has its own conditions, so check them with your setup adviser.
- **Marketplace cross-border programmes.** These suit some categories, but supplements are frequently restricted. Read the current category rules before you build a plan around them.

Keep one shared folder with registration certificates, certificates of analysis for each batch, free sale certificates from the country of origin, ingredient specifications, label artwork in both languages and a halal certificate where it applies. Gelatin capsules and gummies prompt halal questions from many UAE shoppers, so have the answer ready.

## Step 3: Get the Arabic and English label right

Treat the approved label as the master copy for all marketing. Any line the approved label and registration cannot back up should stay out of ads, marketplace copy and influencer scripts.

### What a UAE supplement label usually covers

- Product name and type in Arabic and English.
- Ingredients, with the amount of each active per serving.
- Recommended daily dose, directions and warnings.
- Net quantity, production date and expiry date.
- Batch number, storage conditions and country of origin.
- Name and address of the manufacturer and of the UAE importer or registration holder.
- Allergens, clearly shown.

Imported packs often carry an Arabic sticker. That can be acceptable, but the sticker must be accurate, must not cover required information and must match what was registered. Ask your registration agent before you rely on it.

### Claims that cause trouble

Claims are generally expected to match what was accepted at registration. Anything that says a product treats, cures or prevents a disease is out. Weight-loss wording, "immunity" promises and "detox" language draw extra scrutiny from regulators and ad platforms. Check the Arabic copy as carefully as the English: a translator who adds a friendly flourish can create a claim you never meant to make.

## Step 4: Build product pages for a mixed, careful audience

UAE supplement buyers range from gym regulars comparing protein per scoop to Emirati families stocking vitamins for the household and expatriate professionals looking for a brand they used at home. Each group reads your page differently, but they all want to see what is in the pack.

| Page element | Why it matters in the UAE |
|---|---|
| Arabic and English label photos | Shows the product is the registered version, not a grey import |
| Ingredient amounts as text | Readable on mobile, by search engines and by AI assistants |
| Halal status and capsule source | Answers a frequent question before it reaches customer service |
| Servings per pack and AED price including VAT | Lets buyers work out cost per day themselves |
| Storage and heat advice | Gummies and some capsules suffer in a hot car or a summer delivery van |
| Who should check with a doctor first | Pregnancy, prescribed medicines, children |
| Taste, format and capsule size | Drives reviews and repeat orders |

Build an Arabic version of the store with a proper right-to-left layout rather than a translated widget. Product names, filters, checkout and order messages all need to read naturally. A fluent Arabic writer should write or review the copy, not just a translation tool. ## Step 5: Choose your UAE sales channels

Most brands that grow use several channels, each with a clear job.

### Your own Shopify store

Your store is where you own the customer list, run subscriptions, sell bundles and keep the margin on repeat orders. Prices should be in AED with the 5% VAT already included. At checkout, give people Apple Pay or card, cash on delivery and Tabby or Tamara, and keep a WhatsApp link close by for quick questions. Put test reports and ingredient explainers here.

### Amazon.ae and Noon

A large share of supplement research in the UAE happens on marketplace search results, with tubs lined up side by side. Expect category approval steps and document requests. Once you are live, use brand registration tools, watch for unauthorised sellers and parallel imports, and keep AED prices aligned with your own store. Noon runs its own sale events and ad products, which reward brands that plan stock early.

### Talabat and Careem quick commerce

Bars, electrolytes, sachets and gummies sell well through quick commerce, often as a top-up after a workout. Access usually comes through a distributor or a retail partner rather than a direct seller account, and cold or shaded storage matters in summer.

### Other routes

Pharmacy chains and gym retail are separate routes with their own buying teams. Fashion-led platforms such as Namshi are built around clothing and beauty, so most supplement brands leave them aside.

## Step 6: Advertise in Arabic and English within the rules

Supplement ads can run on Meta, Google, Snapchat, TikTok and the marketplaces, with limits that change often. In general:

- Do not suggest a product treats, cures or prevents an illness, in either language.
- Avoid before-and-after images and copy that points at someone's body or weight.
- Some health-related advertising in the UAE may need approval from a health authority before it runs. Ask your adviser whether yours does.
- Paid creators are expected to hold the advertising permit that UAE media rules require. Check it before they post.
- Keep the landing page in line with the ad, because reviewers look at both.

Snapchat and TikTok reach many younger shoppers, Instagram is where people research wellness brands, and Google Search catches buyers who already know what they want. Write the Arabic and English creative side by side so neither is an afterthought, and keep the tone calm during Ramadan.

## Step 7: Handle cash on delivery, instalments and heat

Plenty of UAE shoppers still prefer to pay the driver, and each parcel turned away at the door eats into whatever a small first order was going to earn. A few habits help:

- Confirm COD orders by WhatsApp before dispatch.
- Offer a modest reason to prepay, such as free delivery or a sample.
- Show Tabby and Tamara on larger bundles so a bigger basket feels manageable.
- Use couriers such as Aramex or marketplace fulfilment that can meet same-day or next-day expectations in Dubai and Abu Dhabi.
- In summer, pick packaging and routes that keep heat-sensitive products safe.

## Step 8: Build refills from the first order

Supplements come with a natural reorder date. A 60-capsule pot taken twice a day lasts about a month, so the date a customer needs more is easy to estimate from the order itself.

- Send a WhatsApp, SMS or email reminder a few days before that date, with consent captured at checkout in line with UAE data protection law.
- Run a subscription option that customers can pause, move or stop themselves without emailing you.
- Suggest family packs or 60- and 90-day sizes after the second order.
- Request reviews once the product has become part of someone's routine, roughly three to four weeks in.

## Step 9: Plan around the UAE calendar

| Period | What happens | What to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| Ramadan | Meals move to suhoor and iftar, training moves late into the evening | Hydration, protein and simple daily vitamins, with calm messaging |
| Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha | Gifting, travel and a return to the gym afterwards | Bundles, gift sets and post-Eid training content |
| June to August | Heat, school holidays and many residents travelling | Lighter spend, refill reminders, heat-safe delivery |
| Late August | Back to school | Family vitamins and lunchbox-friendly snacks |
| October to March | Cooler weather, outdoor events, new gym members | New-customer campaigns and starter bundles |
| November to January | White Friday, UAE National Day, Dubai Shopping Festival | Stock, approved creative and marketplace deals booked early |

Because the Islamic calendar is lunar, Ramadan and both Eids arrive roughly eleven days sooner each Gregorian year. Rebuild the plan annually.

## Thinking beyond the UAE

Once the UAE is working, many brands look at Saudi Arabia and the rest of the GCC. Each country has its own registration and labelling process, so treat every market as a fresh application rather than a copy of your UAE file. Building your Arabic content properly now saves a lot of rework later.

Knowing how to sell supplements online in the UAE comes down to doing the groundwork in the right order: category, registration, label, claims, then channels and ads. Brands that put that base in place spend far less time on rejected ads and paused listings.
