The website design cost in Bangalore varies widely, from a modest budget for a simple WordPress site to a much larger project for a custom corporate website or online store. Where you land depends on what the site must do, how much is designed from scratch, and how much content you already have. This guide explains what moves the number, separates one-time from recurring costs, and shows how to set a budget and compare quotes from Bengaluru freelancers and agencies without overpaying.

## Website design cost in Bangalore by website type

Each type of site below usually costs more than the one before it, because it adds pages, design work, features or integrations. Use the table to see what pushes each type's cost up and what keeps it down.

| Website type | Best for | What raises the cost | What keeps it lower |
|---|---|---|---|
| WordPress website | Startups, consultants, clinics, service firms | Custom layouts, many plugins, copywriting | A styled theme, content ready |
| Business / corporate site (5–10 pages) | Small and mid-size companies | More page types, careers or investor sections | A clear sitemap and approved copy |
| Custom corporate website | Companies with a strong brand and many pages | Bespoke design, multilingual pages, integrations | Reusable page templates, one language at launch |
| WooCommerce or Shopify store | Brands selling online | Large catalogues, variants, shipping and ERP links | Clean product data, standard checkout |
| Custom e-commerce | Complex catalogues, B2B price lists | Bespoke features and heavy integration | A phased launch with must-haves first |

A few points on reading this table:

- **Template does not mean generic.** A good template site is styled to your brand, fast on mobile and structured for search. Most small businesses should start here.
- **Custom pays off when the template gets in the way.** If you need unusual layouts, many page types, a product configurator or deep integrations, custom design is the better long-term choice.
- **E-commerce is a different budget.** If you plan to sell online, read our full guide to [ecommerce website cost in India](/blog/ecommerce-website-cost-in-india/) before you decide.

## What drives the price of a website

Two websites with the same page count can differ in cost by several times. These are the factors that matter most.

### Number and type of pages
A five-page site with home, about, services, blog and contact pages is quick to build. Fifteen pages with separate service, industry and location pages, each with its own layout, take far more design and content work.

### Custom design or a template
A styled template is the most economical route. Custom design means wireframes, mock-ups and revision rounds for each key page, which adds time but gives you a site that looks like nobody else's. Our [corporate website development](/corporate-website-development/) work covers both options.

### Content and photos
Content is the most underestimated cost. Who writes the page copy? Do you have good photos of your team, office and work, or will you need a shoot or stock images? Quotes that leave content out look smaller than they are.

### Integrations
Each connection adds scope: booking tools, payment links, a CRM or lead system, email marketing, live chat, WhatsApp, job applications or an ERP. Ask for each integration to be listed separately in the quote.

### Multilingual pages
Bengaluru businesses often serve customers who prefer Kannada, Hindi or Tamil alongside English. A multilingual site needs translation, a language switcher and correct technical setup so search engines show the right version. Content work grows with every language you add.

### SEO setup, speed and mobile
Basic SEO setup (page titles, meta descriptions, clean URLs, headings, an XML sitemap, Google Search Console and local business details) should be part of any professional build. A site that loads fast on mobile needs optimised images, lean code and good hosting. Both take care, but a slow or invisible site loses visitors, so this is not the place to cut corners.

## One-time and recurring costs

Sort every cost into one of two groups before you compare quotes.

**One-time costs:** the design and build fee, copywriting, photography or stock images, premium theme licences bought outright, data migration and redirects from an old site.

**Recurring costs:** these continue every month or year and belong in your budget from day one.

- **Domain name.** Renewed every year. Prices vary by extension (.com, .in, .co.in), so check current prices with your registrar.
- **Hosting.** WordPress and WooCommerce sites need hosting, paid monthly or yearly. Cheap shared hosting is the most common reason sites feel slow. Shopify includes hosting in its plan; check Shopify's current pricing.
- **Licences.** Premium themes, page builders, form plugins, security tools and backup tools often carry yearly licences.
- **Business email.** Usually a per-user fee from your email provider.
- **Maintenance.** WordPress core, themes and plugins need regular updates, backups and security checks. Our [website maintenance services](/website-maintenance-services/) cover this, scoped to the size of your site.

Third-party prices change often and depend on the plan you pick, so ask your developer for a list of every paid service the site will use and check the provider's current pricing yourself.

## How to set a first-year budget

Work out what the website costs to own for twelve months, not just what it costs to build:

1. List the pages and features you need now, and mark the ones that can wait for a second phase.
2. Note what content you already have and what must be written or shot.
3. Add each recurring cost: domain, hosting, email, licences and maintenance.
4. Keep a separate line for growth: SEO, content and ads to bring visitors.

This often shows that a slightly larger, cleaner build costs less over a year than a cheap one that needs constant fixes.

## Freelancer or agency?

Bengaluru has a large pool of skilled freelancers and a wide range of agencies, so you will get quotes across a broad range. Neither option is right for everyone.

**A freelancer can suit you if** the site is small and well defined, you can write your own content, and you are comfortable managing the project yourself. The headline number is often lower.

**An agency can suit you if** you need design, development, content, SEO and support from one team, or if the site is important enough that you cannot afford a single point of failure. You get more people, a defined process and continuity if someone leaves.

Whichever you choose, ask who will maintain the site after launch, who owns the domain, hosting and code, and what happens if your main contact becomes unavailable.

## Hidden costs to watch for

These are the items that surprise business owners after they sign:

- **Content writing and photography** not included in the design fee.
- **Premium theme or plugin licences** billed to you separately, sometimes every year.
- **Extra revision rounds** beyond the number included in the quote.
- **Stock images** that need a paid licence.
- **Migration from an old site**, including redirects to protect existing search rankings.
- **Post-launch changes** after a short free-support period ends.
- **Hosting upgrades** when the cheap plan in the original quote turns out to be too slow.

## Red flags in website quotes

Be careful when a quote shows any of these signs:

- A single figure with no list of pages, features or integrations.
- No mention of who owns the domain, hosting account, theme licence and code.
- No mention of mobile layout, speed or basic SEO setup.
- A very low figure paired with a long-term lock-in or a mandatory annual fee.
- Promises of first-page rankings or a fixed number of leads. Nobody controls Google's results.
- The agency registers the domain in its own name instead of yours.

## How to compare website design quotes in Bengaluru

Comparing quotes on the headline number alone rarely works, because each vendor scopes different work. Send every vendor the same brief and ask the same questions:

1. How many pages and page layouts are included?
2. Is the design custom or a styled template? How many revision rounds?
3. Who writes the content and sources the photos?
4. Which integrations are included, and which are extra?
5. Which paid themes, plugins or apps will the site need, and who pays for them?
6. What SEO setup is done before launch?
7. What support is included after launch, and for how long?
8. Who owns the domain, hosting, design files and code?

Put the answers side by side in a spreadsheet. This often shows that the lowest quote leaves out content, licences or support, and that a mid-range quote is better value for the same website design cost in Bangalore.

## How long a website takes

Timelines depend more on preparation than on the build itself. Content readiness matters most: a site with approved copy and photos moves quickly. Each design round adds days, so agree who signs off and keep the group small. Custom layouts, multilingual pages and third-party connections need extra development and testing. Domain logins, hosting access and legal review of policies can hold up launch if they are not sorted early. As a rough guide, a template-based WordPress or business site takes a few weeks once content is ready; custom corporate sites and online stores take longer.
