A consumer electronics marketing strategy Australia's gadget brands can rely on starts from how local buyers actually shop. They research before they buy, compare models on Amazon AU and Google, watch reviews on YouTube, check plug types and compliance marks, and measure everything against familiar names from JB Hi-Fi's shelves. A smaller brand cannot outspend those names, but it can be clearer, more specific and more helpful. This plan covers positioning, product explanation, trust, channels, sale seasons and measurement, with a 90-day roadmap at the end.

## Why electronics marketing is different

- **Buyers compare specs line by line.** Vague claims lose to a clear table.
- **Trust carries a lot of weight.** Shoppers worry about safety, compatibility and what happens if something fails.
- **Sale events dominate.** Demand bunches into a few busy weeks around Click Frenzy, Black Friday and Boxing Day.
- **Rules shape the message.** The RCM mark, ACMA requirements, button battery standards and consumer rights under the Australian Consumer Law all affect what you show and say.
- **Models age quickly.** A product launched this year may be replaced next year, so launch plans need to recover costs fast.

## Part 1: Positioning

### Name the one thing you do better

Write a single sentence for each product: who it is for and the one thing it does better than the alternatives. "Earbuds for tradies that stay put and block site noise" gives ads, listings and creators a clear brief. "Premium wireless audio" does not.

### Know who you are not for

Saying who a product does not suit is as useful as saying who it does. A compact speaker that is not built for parties, or earbuds tuned for calls rather than bass, sets expectations that reduce returns and disappointed reviews. Buyers trust brands that are honest about limits, and the people who do buy are more likely to be happy.

### Choose hero models

Most brands have two or three models that do most of the work. Put content, reviews and budget behind them, and let the rest of the range benefit from the brand searches they create.

### Fit the price band

Decide whether you compete as the value choice, the mid-range choice with better support, or the premium choice with standout features. Each needs different proof. Value buyers want to know it will not fail; premium buyers want evidence the extra spend is worth it.

## Part 2: Explain the product

### Turn numbers into everyday outcomes

Battery hours become "a week of commutes between charges". Charging wattage becomes "from flat to half full during breakfast". Waterproof ratings become "fine in the rain on a bike ride". Keep the numbers, but lead with what they mean.

### Build comparison content

Compare your own models side by side so buyers choose the right one first time. Where you compare against competitors, keep the comparison fair, current and supported by evidence.

### Show the product working

Short videos of setup, pairing, sound tests, real battery drains and durability checks answer the questions that cause hesitation. Film in ordinary Australian settings, such as a train carriage, a backyard or a home office.

Plan a small library for each hero model: a 15-second hook for ads, a one-minute feature walkthrough, a full setup video and a few answers to common questions. The same footage serves Amazon AU listings, product pages, YouTube, Meta ads and support emails, so one well-planned shoot goes a long way.

## Part 3: Build trust

### Genuine reviews

Ask every buyer for feedback through the methods each platform allows. Never pay for positive reviews, post fake ones or hide negative ones; the ACCC has acted against misleading review practices. Reply to critical reviews helpfully, because shoppers read the replies.

### Warranty and consumer rights as marketing

A clear warranty is a selling point. State the period, what it covers and how to claim, and explain that it sits alongside consumer rights under the Australian Consumer Law, which give buyers remedies for major failures regardless of any warranty. Honest wording reassures buyers more than fine print.

### Compliance as reassurance

Show the RCM details you hold, battery safety warnings and the plug type on listings and product pages. Buyers notice when this information is missing. Only show what you can support with test reports and registrations, and confirm requirements with a testing lab or adviser.

### Creators and tech reviewers

Independent reviewers and creators with a relevant audience can build credibility quickly. Give them the product early, let them test it honestly and make sure paid content is clearly disclosed under the AANA codes. Avoid scripting claims you cannot prove.

## Part 4: Channels

### Amazon AU

For many categories, Amazon AU is where comparison happens. Strong listings and A+ content turn clicks into orders, ratings decide whether buyers choose you over a known name, and Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands put you in front of category, competitor and brand searches. Structure campaigns by product line and price band.

### Google search and Shopping

Google Shopping and search capture people looking for model names, comparisons and categories. Keep your feed accurate with GST-inclusive AUD prices, current stock and full product attributes, and build search campaigns around use-case searches, such as "earbuds for running" or "fast charger for laptop".

### Your own website

Your Shopify store carries the full range, bundles, warranty registration and support content. Add Afterpay or another buy now, pay later option for higher-priced items. ### Social video and TikTok Shop

Meta, YouTube and TikTok suit demos, unboxings and retargeting. TikTok Shop may work for accessories and small gadgets that look good in a short clip; check it is available for your category before planning a launch around it.

### eBay AU and Kogan

Both carry steady electronics demand in many categories and can suit older models and bundles. Keep specs, prices and warranty wording consistent with your other channels.

### Search, SEO and AI answers

Setup guides, comparison pages and buying guides on your own site rank on Google.com.au and are often quoted by AI assistants. Answer the real questions people ask, such as compatibility with particular phones or whether a device works on Australian networks.

### Email and owned audiences

Use email for setup tips, firmware news, accessory suggestions and early access to sale deals. Owned audiences cost far less per sale than ads during busy periods, when click costs rise.

### Promotions that protect the brand

Constant discounting teaches buyers to wait for the next sale. Use bundles, free accessories and extended return windows during sale events instead of cutting the headline price of new models. Keep discount depth consistent across channels so marketplace buyers and store buyers see the same deal, and make sure any "was" price is genuine; the ACCC watches for misleading price comparisons.

## Part 5: Sale seasons

The Australian electronics calendar runs from back to school in late January, through EOFY in June, to Father's Day in September, then Click Frenzy, Black Friday, Christmas and Boxing Day. Each needs its own plan:

1. Pick two or three hero models per event.
2. Agree stock, deals and bundles with margin in mind.
3. Build reviews and video views in the weeks before.
4. Check bids and budgets daily during the event.
5. Follow up with setup help and accessory offers.

### Launch plan for a new model

Confirm compliance first, then build the listing and support content, seed early reviews through genuine buyers and creator demos, start Amazon AU and Google campaigns, and add Meta once you have proven creative. Avoid launching a new model straight into a discount event.

### Accessories and repeat buyers

Cases, cables, chargers and replacement parts turn one sale into several. Recommend them in post-purchase emails and on product pages, and bundle them in sale periods instead of cutting the price of the main product.

## Part 6: Measurement

Report contribution profit by model each month: revenue minus product cost, fees, shipping, returns and ad spend. Watch conversion rate, average rating and the main return reasons beside it. The [ROAS calculator](/tools/roas-calculator/) helps set targets that still leave profit once marketplace fees and returns are counted.

Split reporting into always-on and event periods. Sale-event results look strong on revenue but often weaker on margin, while always-on campaigns build the reviews and brand searches that make events work. Track branded search volume on Google and Amazon AU over time as a sign that video, creators and reviews are building recognition. When a model's rating drops or its return reasons shift, pause scaling and fix the cause before adding budget.

## A 90-day roadmap for a consumer electronics marketing strategy: Australia

**Days 1 to 30.** Write positioning sentences, choose hero models, fix listings and spec tables, and check compliance and warranty wording.

**Days 31 to 60.** Launch Amazon AU and Google campaigns, film demo and setup videos, and start review requests.

**Days 61 to 90.** Add Meta and creator content, prepare for the next sale event and review profit by model.

### What good looks like at 90 days

By the end of the first quarter you should know which hero model earns the most profit after every cost, which channel brings buyers at an acceptable cost, what the main return reasons are and which videos hold attention. That evidence decides where the next quarter's budget goes.

## Common mistakes

- Spreading budget evenly across every model
- Listing specs without explaining what they mean
- Missing compliance, plug or battery details
- Warranty wording that implies buyers have no other rights
- Measuring only platform-reported ad returns
