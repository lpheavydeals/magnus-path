If you are working out how to start a pet food brand, Australia offers an engaged audience and a handful of rules worth understanding before the first batch is made. Owners here call their dogs and cats family, follow breed accounts on Instagram and read the side of the bag before they switch. They can also grab a familiar brand at the supermarket or a large chain such as Petbarn, so a newcomer needs a sharp reason to exist. This guide covers the order of work: niche, recipe, standards and labels, packaging, channels, the local sales calendar and the reorder habits that keep a brand alive.

## Step 1: Choose a niche you can explain in one line

Complete dry food for every breed and age is crowded and needs a lot of capital. A small brand does better when it is specific.

- **Species.** Dogs take up most shelf space. Cats, especially indoor cats in apartments, often have fewer independent brands to pick from.
- **Format.** Air-dried or dehydrated treats, single-protein chews, baked biscuits, freeze-dried toppers, wet food or gently cooked meals.
- **Owner need.** Large working breeds, small apartment dogs, older cats, limited-ingredient recipes or local proteins such as kangaroo.
- **Use moment.** Training rewards, long-lasting chews for days at home alone, puppy starter boxes or a topper for fussy eaters.

Try finishing this sentence: "We make ___ for ___ who ___." For example: "We make air-dried kangaroo treats for small dogs whose owners want a single protein." If the sentence sprawls, your packaging and ads will sprawl too.

### Check demand before spending on stock

Read the three-star and one-star reviews on leading products in your format on Amazon AU and eBay AU. Complaints about crumbling, smell, hardness or awkward pack sizes are a product brief you did not have to pay for. Join breed and suburb Facebook groups and note what owners keep asking. Then talk to groomers, trainers, dog walkers and independent pet stores near you. They hear the same objections every week, and a few dozen honest conversations can stop you printing packaging for the wrong product.

## Step 2: Build the recipe with a manufacturer

Most new brands work with a contract manufacturer, often called a co-packer, that already makes pet food or treats in their chosen format. Building your own kitchen or plant rarely makes sense for a first range.

### What to ask before you sign

- Which formats can you produce, and what is the minimum batch size?
- Do you manufacture to AS 5812, and are you a member of the PFIAA?
- Where do meat and other raw materials come from, and how are they stored and checked?
- What microbiological and nutritional testing happens on each batch?
- How was the shelf life worked out, and can we see the data?
- Can you fill our printed pouches or bags, or only supply bulk?
- Who owns the recipe and specifications if we move production?

Visit the facility before committing. Look at how raw and finished goods are kept apart, how batches are coded and how a recall would actually work. Put the specification, tolerances, lead times and recipe ownership into a written agreement.

### Nutrition and formulation

If you plan to sell a complete and balanced food, meaning one designed to be fed on its own, work with a qualified animal nutritionist and formulate against recognised nutrient profiles. Treats, toppers and chews are fed alongside a main diet, so they are simpler to launch, but they still need clear daily amounts so owners do not overdo them. Keep a product file holding every recipe version, supplier specification, test result and label proof. Marketplaces, stockists and curious owners will ask questions, and a tidy file lets you answer in minutes.

## Step 3: Understand AS 5812, labelling and import rules

Pet food in Australia is not regulated like food for people. The framework is a mix of a national standard, industry self-regulation and general consumer law. Treat what follows as an overview, not legal advice, and confirm your own obligations with a regulatory adviser.

### The Australian Standard AS 5812

AS 5812 covers the manufacturing and marketing of pet food, including hygiene, ingredients and labelling. Members of the Pet Food Industry Association of Australia (PFIAA) are expected to follow it, and many stockists and buyers treat it as the benchmark even for brands outside the association. Ask your manufacturer how they apply it, and keep it open when you write your labels.

### What the label usually needs

- Product name and the species it is intended for
- Whether it is complete and balanced or meant to be fed with other food, plus the life stage it suits
- An ingredient list
- A nutrient analysis panel covering items such as protein, fat, fibre and moisture
- Feeding directions by body weight
- Net weight, batch code and a date mark showing how long the food keeps
- Storage instructions and the name and Australian contact details of the responsible business

### Imports, biosecurity and the APVMA

If any ingredient or finished product comes from overseas, check the biosecurity import conditions with the federal agriculture department before you commit to a supplier. Animal-derived ingredients face strict conditions, and some products cannot be imported at all. Separately, products that control pests or treat animals, such as flea and tick treatments or medicated washes, fall under the Australian Pesticides and Veterinary Medicines Authority (APVMA) and generally need registration. A food should never drift into that territory through its wording.

### Keep claims away from veterinary territory

Describe ingredients, texture, sourcing and feeding guidance. Do not say a food treats, cures or prevents any condition. The Australian Consumer Law prohibits misleading claims and the ACCC enforces it, while ads are also judged against the AANA codes through Ad Standards. Words such as "natural", "human grade" or "Australian made" need to be accurate and supportable. Ask a specialist to review pack and listing copy before anything is printed.

### Business setup

Register an ABN and a company, register for GST when you reach the threshold, apply for a trade mark through IP Australia and arrange product liability insurance before the first sale.

## Step 4: Design packaging for heat, distance and quick decisions

Australian parcels travel long distances and sit in hot vans and letterboxes. Choose barrier materials that hold up in heat and humidity, and use resealable pouches for treats eaten over several weeks. Send test parcels through Australia Post and at least one courier to a regional address before launch, then open them and check for punctures, crumbs and swelling.

On the front, make species, life stage, protein and weight readable on a phone screen. On the back, give the ingredient list, nutrient analysis panel, a feeding table by weight, storage advice and your contact details.

| Packaging check | What it does for you |
|---|---|
| Species and life stage on the front | Answers the first question owners ask |
| Complete meal or treat stated clearly | Sets correct feeding expectations |
| Feeding table by body weight | Cuts overfeeding and complaints |
| Heat-tolerant, resealable closure | Keeps product fresh after a hot delivery |
| Pack size tied to weeks of feeding | Makes reorder timing predictable |
| QR code to a switching guide | Helps owners change food gradually |

## Step 5: Pick your first sales channels

### Your own Shopify store

Your store holds the customer relationship, the subscription and the email list. Each product page should show pack sizes by pet weight, the ingredient list and nutrient analysis panel, a feeding table, real customer photos and reviews. Offer Afterpay or another buy now, pay later option on larger orders, and set free-delivery thresholds with freight in mind, because heavy bags cost a lot to send to regional postcodes. ### Amazon AU

Amazon AU is a common starting point for owners comparing treats, chews and premium food. Build complete listings with clear images of the label, variations by size and flavour, and A+ content. ### eBay AU, Kogan and TikTok Shop

eBay AU and Kogan add reach among value buyers and people stocking up on bulk packs. TikTok Shop may suit treats and toppers that film well with pet creators, but check that it is open to your category and that your products meet its rules before you plan around it. Keep prices, stock and label images consistent on every channel.

### Retail as context

Petbarn, supermarkets and large online pet retailers set what owners expect on price and range. Independent pet stores, groomers, doggy daycares and weekend markets are more realistic early stockists and a reliable source of blunt feedback.

## Step 6: Plan around the Australian calendar

- **Summer, December to February.** Heat changes how pets eat and how packs travel. Push frozen-treat recipes, practical heat-safety content and delivery advice.
- **Back to school in late January.** Routines shift and pets spend more time alone, so long-lasting chews and enrichment treats get attention.
- **EOFY in June and Click Frenzy.** Owners stock up. Offer multi-bag bundles rather than deep cuts on new lines.
- **Father's Day in September.** Gift boxes for the dog-loving dad sell well with the right photography.
- **Black Friday, Christmas and Boxing Day.** Advent calendars, stocking fillers and bulk deals. Order packaging early, because print runs and freight both get tight.

## Step 7: Earn the second, third and tenth order

The first order costs the most to win. The margin sits in the reorders.

1. **Trial packs** lower the risk of a fussy eater rejecting a full bag.
2. **A switching guide** shows how to mix old and new food over about a week.
3. **A check-in email** a week after delivery asks how the change is going and spots problems early.
4. **Reminders** arrive a few days before the bag is likely to run out, based on pack size and pet weight.
5. **Flexible subscriptions** let owners pause, skip or change size without emailing you, and cancelling is as easy as signing up.

Track repeat rate by product, pack size and acquisition channel. The number that matters is how many first-time buyers come back for a second bag, and how quickly.

## How to start a pet food brand: Australia launch checklist

**Months 1 and 2.** Lock the niche, shortlist manufacturers, run sample batches, test with owners, groomers and trainers, and check import conditions for anything sourced overseas.

**Months 3 and 4.** Finalise the recipe and label against AS 5812, get a specialist review of claims, order packaging, build the Shopify store and Amazon AU listings, and photograph real pets eating the product.

**Months 5 and 6.** Launch with modest Meta and Amazon AU budgets, gather reviews, switch on reminders and subscriptions, then add eBay AU, Kogan or TikTok Shop once stock and reorders are steady.

### Mistakes to avoid

- Launching a full range before one product has proven people come back for it
- Printing large runs before the label has been reviewed
- Borrowing health wording from overseas brands
- Ignoring freight for heavy bags to regional and remote postcodes
- Pack sizes that run out at awkward intervals
