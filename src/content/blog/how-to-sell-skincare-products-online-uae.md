Working out how to sell skincare products online in the UAE means selling to an unusually varied group of buyers, in a climate that is hard on skin for much of the year. Shoppers move between strong sun, humid coastal evenings and dry, air-conditioned offices, and they compare every serum against mall counters and marketplace deals before they commit. This guide takes a new or growing brand through the work in a sensible order: registration and labels, the range, your store, marketplaces, ads and creators, payment and delivery, the calendar and the reorder.

Treat it as general guidance rather than legal advice. Use it to prepare good questions for your importer, distributor or regulatory adviser.

## Step 1: Sort registration and labels before you list anything

Selling skincare in the UAE is not just a matter of opening a store. The rules sit at federal and emirate level, and they apply to online sales as much as to shop shelves.

### Montaji registration in Dubai

As a general rule, Dubai asks for each cosmetic and personal care product to be registered with Dubai Municipality on the Montaji platform before anyone sells it. The application is normally made by a UAE-licensed company, which might be your own entity, an importer or a distributor. Requests can include ingredient lists, label artwork, manufacturer details and supporting documents from the country of origin. Amazon.ae and Noon may ask to see proof before they approve a listing, so build the time into your launch plan.

### Arabic label details

Packs normally need their key details in Arabic alongside English, whether printed directly or added with a sticker. That usually means the product name and function, ingredients, how to use it, warnings, country of origin, batch and dates. Have your adviser check artwork before you order a large packaging run; a reprint costs far more than a review.

### Claims that stay cosmetic

A cosmetic changes how skin looks and feels. Promising to treat acne, eczema, melasma or any other condition can move a product towards a medical category, with stricter rules and rejected ads. Write about hydration, texture, the look of dark spots or a more even appearance, and hold evidence for any number you quote, including SPF values.

### Halal-friendly and alcohol-free wording

Halal-friendly and alcohol-free are terms many local buyers search for. Put them on a page only when a recognised halal certificate or a transparent ingredient list stands behind them. Toners, facial mists and fragranced products deserve a second look, because some contain alcohol that buyers would not expect.

## Step 2: Build a range for heat, humidity and dry indoor air

A new brand does not need a long range. It needs a reason to be chosen over familiar mall brands and cheaper marketplace imports, and the climate gives you one.

- **Daily sun protection people will reapply.** Light, non-greasy SPF that sits well under makeup or a headscarf answers a real need for most of the year.
- **Textures that suit the weather.** Gels and fluid lotions for summer; richer creams sold as a night step or for skin dried out by air conditioning.
- **A thorough but gentle cleanser.** Sweat, sunscreen and dust build up over a long day, and a cleanser is a product people use up and buy again.
- **Notes on water.** Many residents feel that shower water leaves their skin tight. You can speak to that feeling with hydrating steps, without making claims about the water itself.
- **Sizes for trial, travel and families.** Minis suit cautious first buyers and frequent flyers; larger bottles suit households that share a routine.

Before you order packaging, work out what each product actually earns after manufacturing, freight, registration, packaging, delivery, payment and marketplace fees, refused COD parcels and the ad cost of winning each order. ## Step 3: Set up an Arabic and English store

Your own store is where you control the story, the bundles and the customer relationship. Shopify suits most young UAE skincare brands because Arabic storefronts, cash on delivery, Tabby, Tamara, Apple Pay and review apps are straightforward to add.

### What each product page needs

UAE buyers often arrive after watching a creator on Instagram, Snapchat or TikTok, and they want detail the video skipped:

- The lead active, its percentage and its job, explained simply.
- Which skin types it suits, how it feels in heat, and who should patch test first.
- A short video of the texture and finish, filmed in natural light.
- When to use it, how it layers with SPF, and what not to combine it with.
- A fair picture of early changes versus those that need weeks of use.
- The full ingredient list, pack size and storage advice for the summer months.
- Reviews in Arabic and English that show skin type, so people find buyers like themselves.

### Arabic pages that read naturally

Have a fluent Arabic writer produce or review the Arabic pages, set them right to left, and never rely on a translation tool alone. Ads in Arabic should land on Arabic pages wherever you can manage it, or the click is wasted.

### Prices, payment and trust

Show prices in AED, including VAT if you are registered (VAT is currently 5%). Take cards, Apple Pay, cash on delivery and Tabby or Tamara instalments, and keep delivery times and the returns policy within sight of the buy button. A visible WhatsApp button answers the many shoppers who prefer to ask a quick question first.

## Step 4: Add Amazon.ae, Noon and Namshi

Each marketplace reaches a different shopper, so give each a clear job.

| Channel | Who you reach | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon.ae | Buyers who search for an active or a concern and want fast delivery | Keyword-rich titles, A+ content and sponsored ads on two or three heroes |
| Noon | Price-aware buyers, heavy around White Friday and Ramadan | Hero products, bundles, Noon Ads and fulfilment by Noon where it fits |
| Namshi | Fashion and beauty browsers on mobile | Giftable sets and products with a strong visual angle |
| Own Shopify store | Returning customers and creator traffic | Whole range, Arabic pages, routine sets, subscriptions and loyalty points |

Sephora, Faces and Boots UAE shape what many shoppers expect from a skincare brand, from packaging to sampling, but they choose what they stock. A record of direct sales and good reviews strengthens any later pitch, though no one can promise shelf space.

Keep prices consistent across channels. If marketplaces always undercut your store, loyal customers learn to buy there and you lose their data. Give your site its own reasons to visit: routine sets, refill sizes and loyalty points.

## Step 5: Advertise and work with creators inside UAE rules

Paid social and search bring most of the early orders for a young skincare brand. They are also where rejected ads and complaints begin.

### Instagram, Snapchat and TikTok

Across the Gulf, Snapchat and TikTok reach very large audiences, while Instagram is still where many beauty buyers check a brand before ordering. Show texture, application and a real morning routine, with Arabic and English versions of each hero ad. Avoid wording that singles out personal problems and dramatic before-and-after shots, and never use filters that change how skin looks.

### Google Search and Shopping

Search ads catch shoppers who already know what they want, such as "lightweight SPF for oily skin" or "fragrance-free moisturiser". Run campaigns in both languages and keep your product feed clean, with clear titles and accurate barcodes.

### Creators and the advertiser permit

Anyone paid to promote products on social media in the UAE needs an advertiser permit from the UAE Media Council, and the scheme's details have shifted over time. Ask every creator for their permit details before paid or gifted work, check them against current guidance, and make sure posts are clearly labelled as advertising.

### Ramadan and good taste

Advertising in the UAE is expected to reflect Islamic values and public decency. During Ramadan, creative usually becomes calmer and more family-centred, with ads timed for the evening and gifting building towards Eid. ## Step 6: Get COD, delivery and summer storage right

- **Cash on delivery.** Many shoppers still pay on the doorstep. Confirm COD orders by WhatsApp before dispatch, and judge campaigns on delivered, paid orders. Put your refusal rate into our [RTO and COD calculator](/tools/rto-cod-calculator/) to see what it costs each month.
- **Heat in transit.** Serums and creams left in hot vans or warehouses can separate. Find out where your courier or fulfilment partner keeps stock in July and August, and add home storage tips to each page.
- **Packaging.** Pumps and droppers need seals and padding. A leak means a refund and a poor review, often in two languages.
- **Returns.** Set clear rules for opened products on hygiene grounds, and always take back faulty or wrongly described items. Check your policy against UAE consumer protection rules.
- **Data.** The UAE's personal data protection law applies to customer details and marketing consent. Collect only what you need and keep records of opt-ins; we are marketers, not lawyers.

## Step 7: Plan around the UAE calendar

Skincare sells all year, but some weeks carry more weight:

1. **Ramadan:** browsing moves late into the evening; self-care and gift sets build towards Eid al-Fitr.
2. **Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha:** gift boxes, family bundles and quick delivery promises.
3. **Mother's Day in March:** gift sets and minis, often close to Ramadan in recent years.
4. **Summer:** SPF, light textures and travel minis for residents leaving the heat.
5. **White Friday in November:** routine bundles with stock and ads ready by late October.
6. **UAE National Day and the Dubai Shopping Festival:** offers and gift sets from early December into January.
7. **Diwali:** gifting for the large Indian community, with English creative that respects the festival.

Because the Islamic calendar shifts roughly eleven days earlier every year, rebuild this plan each year instead of reusing old dates.

## Step 8: Turn first orders into reorders

Skincare is a replenishment category, and the second and third orders are where profit sits.

### A four-message journey after purchase

1. On delivery: a WhatsApp or email message with how to use the product in this climate.
2. Week two: a check-in, with answers to common questions.
3. Shortly before it runs out: a reorder reminder, with loyalty points for coming back.
4. After the reorder: a review request, with photo upload in either language.

### Reviews you can trust

Ask for reviews a few weeks after delivery, never buy them and never hide negative ones. Reply in the language the customer used, and stay within cosmetic wording when people ask about medical conditions; point them to a pharmacist or doctor.

## Expanding to Saudi Arabia and the GCC

Once the UAE is working, Saudi Arabia is the usual next step, followed by Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman. Registration and labels differ in each country, and in Saudi Arabia the SFDA is the authority, so budget compliance work for every market even if the ads carry over with little change.
