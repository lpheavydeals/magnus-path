Working out how to sell electronics online in India means getting three things right before you think about ads: compliance, clear product information and after-sales trust. Buyers compare dozens of similar products on one screen, worry about fakes and faulty units, and judge brands by how warranty claims are handled. Marketplaces check registrations before listing many products. This guide walks through each step for gadget, accessory and appliance brands, from BIS registration to sale events and return fraud.

## Step 1: Understand the compliance basics

Regulation for electronics in India is detailed and changes regularly. The points below are general orientation, not legal advice. Confirm requirements for your exact products with the relevant authority or a compliance consultant.

### BIS Compulsory Registration Scheme

Many electronics and IT products fall under the Bureau of Indian Standards Compulsory Registration Scheme (CRS). Examples on the list have included power adapters, power banks, LED products, mobile phones, laptops and many other items. For covered products, the process generally involves:

- Testing samples at a BIS-recognised laboratory against the relevant Indian Standard.
- Applying for registration, which links to the specific manufacturing site.
- Marking the product with the Standard Mark and registration number.
- Renewing registration and re-testing when models or factories change.

The list of covered products grows over time, and marketplaces often ask for registration details at listing. Importers usually need an authorised Indian representative for the foreign manufacturer.

### Legal Metrology labelling

Packaged products generally need declarations on the pack: manufacturer, packer or importer name and address, country of origin, net quantity, maximum retail price, month and year of manufacture or import, and consumer care contact. E-commerce rules also expect key declarations to be shown on the listing itself. Check amendments before printing boxes.

### Other rules that may apply

- **Wireless products.** Devices using Bluetooth, Wi-Fi or other radio features may need approval from the Department of Telecommunications' Wireless Planning and Coordination wing.
- **E-waste.** Producers and importers of covered electrical and electronic equipment may have extended producer responsibility obligations under e-waste rules.
- **Batteries.** Battery management rules and safe shipping requirements can affect products with lithium cells.
- **Imports.** Customs, import registration and duty rules apply to imported products and components.

Keep every registration, test report and approval in one folder, with expiry dates tracked.

## Step 2: Choose products and positioning

Electronics categories are crowded. Before launching, check:

- **How many similar products exist** on Amazon and Flipkart, and what reviews complain about.
- **Whether you can show a real difference**: battery life, build, sound, warranty, local service or a specific feature.
- **Your landed cost** including duties, registration, packaging, marketplace fees, shipping, returns and warranty provision.

Use a [marketplace profit calculator](/tools/marketplace-profit-calculator/) to check margin per unit for each channel before you set prices. Electronics margins can disappear quickly once fees, returns and sale-event discounts are added.

## Step 3: Write spec-led listings

Electronics buyers read specifications closely, but they also want to know what those specs mean in daily use.

### Titles

Include brand, product type, model, key specs and colour: "Brand X Wireless Earbuds with 30-hour playback, ENC mic, Bluetooth 5.3, Black". Keep titles readable and within each marketplace's limits.

### Bullet points

Explain each important spec in plain words. Instead of only "40mm drivers", add what the buyer gets from it. Cover:

- Core performance specs
- Battery and charging details
- Compatibility (phones, operating systems, connectors)
- What is in the box
- Warranty period and how to claim
- Registration details where relevant

### Images and A+ content

Use a clean main image, then lifestyle shots, size comparisons, port and button close-ups, and an infographic for key specs. A+ content can add comparison charts across your own models, which helps buyers pick the right one and reduces wrong-model returns.

### Spec table

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Model number | Buyers compare exact models and search by them |
| Compatibility | Wrong compatibility is a common return reason |
| Battery and charging | One of the most read specs for portable devices |
| Dimensions and weight | Sets expectations for size |
| Warranty and service | Reduces doubt and support calls |

## Step 4: Make warranty and service clear

Trust in electronics depends on what happens when something goes wrong.

- **State the warranty precisely**: period, start date, what is covered and what is not.
- **Explain the claim process**: pickup, service centre or drop-off, repair or replacement, and expected timelines.
- **Offer warranty registration** on your own website, which also gives you customer data.
- **Publish setup guides and FAQs** for common problems.
- **Keep terms identical** on the listing, box, invoice and website.

Never describe warranty terms more generously in ads than they are in reality. Buyers notice, and reviews will say so.

## Step 5: Choose your channels

### Amazon and Flipkart

Most online electronics buying in India happens on the two big marketplaces, especially during sale events. Focus on complete listings, competitive delivery speed, reviews and in-platform ads. Fulfilment programmes can help with delivery speed and badges.

### Your own store

A Shopify or WooCommerce store holds the full range, comparison pages, warranty registration, manuals and support. It also ranks in Google for model and comparison searches. ### Quick commerce and offline

Fast-moving accessories such as chargers, cables, earphones and power banks can suit quick commerce in cities. Offline retail and distributors still matter for some categories, especially appliances.

## Step 6: Plan for sale events

A large share of electronics demand in India arrives during marketplace sale events such as Amazon Great Indian Festival, Prime Day and Flipkart Big Billion Days, as well as year-end and festive sales.

Plan several weeks ahead:

1. **Pick hero models.** Two or three products to push hardest.
2. **Secure stock.** Running out mid-sale wastes ad spend and ranking momentum.
3. **Agree deals.** Coupons, bank offers and lightning deals, within healthy margins.
4. **Warm up early.** Run ads and collect reviews in the weeks before.
5. **Watch closely.** Check stock, price and ad budgets several times a day during the event.
6. **Follow up.** Send setup help, ask for reviews and suggest accessories after delivery.

## Step 7: Collect genuine reviews

Reviews heavily influence electronics buying. Use each platform's own review request tools, include a card in the box pointing to setup help, and respond to negative reviews with practical solutions. Never offer incentives for positive reviews or post fake reviews; marketplaces penalise this, and consumer protection rules in India address fake reviews.

## Step 8: Manage returns and fraud

Electronics returns are expensive and some are fraudulent. Common problems include buyers returning a different or damaged unit, empty boxes, missing accessories and claims of non-delivery.

### Protection checklist

- Record serial or IMEI numbers against each order.
- Film packing of higher-value orders.
- Use tamper-evident seals and weigh parcels before dispatch.
- Inspect returned units against the recorded serial number before restocking.
- File marketplace protection claims quickly, with evidence.
- Track return reasons by model, pin code and buyer account.
- Confirm cash on delivery orders before dispatch to reduce RTO.

Separate genuine faults from expectation-based returns. Genuine faults point to product or quality control issues; "did not match expectations" returns usually point to listing problems.

## Step 9: Support customers after the sale

After-sales support is part of selling electronics, not an extra. A buyer who cannot pair earbuds or set up a smart device will often return it or leave a poor review, even when nothing is wrong with the product.

- **Setup content.** Short videos and step-by-step guides for pairing, charging, app setup and resets, linked from a QR code in the box.
- **Reachable support.** A WhatsApp number, email or phone line with stated hours, so buyers contact you before they contact the marketplace.
- **Clear escalation.** Explain what happens if the first fix does not work: replacement, repair or pickup.
- **Accessory and spare parts.** Cables, ear tips, straps and chargers keep customers in your brand and reduce returns for minor issues.

Track the questions support receives. If many buyers ask the same thing, add the answer to the listing, the box insert and the website. Over time this lowers return rates and lifts ratings, which in turn improves marketplace ranking and ad performance.

Keep your response proportionate. Most buyers who return electronics are genuine, and a suspicious tone in support messages damages reviews. Use evidence quietly, and save disputes for clear cases.

## Common mistakes

- Listing products before registrations are complete, then having listings removed.
- Copying specs from a supplier sheet without checking them.
- Vague warranty terms.
- Launching into a sale event without enough reviews or stock.
- Ignoring return reasons that point to unclear compatibility information.
