Searching for makeup brand marketing ideas? Australia gives you a buyer who is used to swatching at a Mecca or Sephora counter, scrolling TikTok for new launches and comparing prices at Priceline before deciding. An online-first label has to give the same confidence through a screen. The 15 ideas below are grouped by the job they do: helping buyers choose a shade, building creator content, running launches and ads, and making the most of Australian seasons and repeat orders.

## Ideas that help buyers pick the right shade

### 1. Swatch every shade on many skin tones

Australia's population covers a very wide range of skin tones and undertones. Show each shade on several of them, in daylight and indoor light, on the face and the inner arm. A single model leaves most buyers guessing, which leads to abandoned carts and exchanges. Film short clips as well as stills, because movement shows finish better than a flat photo.

### 2. Explain undertones in plain words

Many buyers are unsure whether they are warm, cool or neutral. A simple self-check, such as how gold and silver jewellery look on their skin, plus shade names that include an undertone letter, makes choosing easier. Put the explanation on every base product page, not only in a blog post.

### 3. Add a shade quiz beside the swatches

A short quiz asking about current shades from well-known brands, undertone and coverage can narrow choices fast. Virtual try-on can help for lip and eye colour. Always keep real swatches next to any tool, because buyers trust skin more than software.

### 4. Sell sample cards and minis for base products

Paid shade samples or minis lower the risk of a first foundation purchase. Follow each with a credit towards the full size and a reminder before the sample runs out. Track how many sample buyers come back, by shade and channel.

## Ideas for creators and content

### 5. Build a steady creator roster

A few gifted posts produce a spike and then nothing. A roster of creators across skin tones, ages and audience sizes, posting regularly, gives you a constant flow of content. Get written agreements that cover disclosure, reuse in ads and how long you can use each piece. Smaller creators with engaged followers often bring buyers who reorder.

### 6. Keep content unfiltered and clearly disclosed

Ask creators to film on bare skin without beauty filters on the product, and to label paid, gifted and affiliate posts clearly at the start. The AANA code expects advertising to be distinguishable as advertising, and Ad Standards hears complaints. Under the Australian Consumer Law, a filtered swatch can mislead.

### 7. Run wear tests in Australian conditions

Get-ready-with-me videos are common. Wear tests are more persuasive: a full day at the beach, a humid commute, a long shift or a wedding reception. Ask creators to check in at midday and in the evening. If you make long-wear or waterproof claims, make sure your own testing backs the wording.

### 8. Reflect Australia's communities

Work with creators from the many cultural communities that make up Australia, including South Asian, East Asian, Middle Eastern, African, Pasifika and First Nations creators where they choose to work with you. Their looks, events and skin tones show buyers that your range is for them. Pay them fairly and let them speak in their own voice.

## Ideas for launches and paid ads

### 9. Treat launches as drops

Share close-up swatches before shade names, open a waitlist, give past buyers early access and have creators film a few weeks ahead. Ads, email, SMS and listings should all go live on the same day. Back-in-stock alerts turn a sell-out into a waiting list rather than a lost sale.

Watch stock closely during a drop. Selling out a limited shade can build interest, but running out of a core shade halfway through a paid campaign wastes budget and annoys buyers who clicked an ad for it. Agree a stock level at which ads pause automatically, and plan restock lead times before launch day, not after.

### 10. Put spend behind creator posts that already work

Spark Ads on TikTok and partnership ads on Meta let you boost a creator's post from their own handle, which often feels more natural than a brand ad. Pick the posts with strong saves and comments, and send the click to a landing page that shows the exact shade from the video.

Refresh creative often. Makeup ads tire quickly because the same audience sees them many times, so plan a steady supply of new clips from your roster. Test hooks that answer a question in the first seconds, such as how a shade looks on deeper skin or whether a lipstick survives a coffee, rather than opening with a logo.

### 11. Reach shoppers already searching

Google Shopping and search reach buyers looking for "long wear concealer for oily skin" or "nude lipstick for olive skin". Use clear titles with shade family and finish, GST-inclusive AUD prices and good images. A tidy feed often matters more than clever ad copy.

### 12. Give each marketplace a job

Amazon AU suits repeat staples such as mascara, brow gel and setting spray. TikTok Shop, where available to your business, suits lip, blush and nail products that look good on camera. eBay AU and Kogan can clear older shades and sets. Keep pricing aligned with your own store so loyal buyers have no reason to go elsewhere.

Marketplace listings need the same shade detail as your own pages. Use images that show the shade on skin, name the undertone in the title or bullets, and answer common questions in the description so buyers do not have to guess. Reviews on Amazon AU often mention shade accuracy, so read them weekly and fix the listing when the same complaint comes up twice.

## Ideas for seasons, gifting and repeat orders

### 13. Build kits for the Australian calendar

Plan gift sets for Valentine's Day, Mother's Day in May, Click Frenzy, Black Friday, Christmas and Boxing Day. Kits built on lip, cheek, nail and palettes are easier to give than foundation. For communities that celebrate them, Lunar New Year, Eid and Diwali can matter too. Show Afterpay on larger sets and publish Australia Post and courier cut-off dates clearly. Use our [discount calculator](/tools/discount-calculator/) to check that a gift-set offer still leaves a margin.

### 14. Plan for formal, wedding and racing season

Spring brings Year 12 formals, wedding season and the spring racing carnival. Buyers want long-wear base, setting products and bold lips that survive a long day and night. Tutorials for formal looks, creator wear tests and ready-made kits suit this window well.

Start content for this season in late winter, when students and wedding guests begin planning. Parents often pay for formal makeup, so a gift card or a kit that a parent can buy without knowing the shade is useful. For brides and bridal parties, a page that groups long-wear base, setting spray and a lip kit makes it easy to buy for several people at once. Keep delivery cut-off dates visible, since nobody wants a parcel arriving the day after the event.

### 15. Remember each buyer's shade

Save the shade a customer bought and use it. Pre-fill it on reorder, mention it in restock emails and recommend new launches that suit it. Ask for reviews that name the shade and the reviewer's skin tone, and show them on the product page. This turns one good match into a long relationship.

## Small store changes with large effects

- Swatches shown before the price and add-to-cart button on mobile.
- A shade filter on collection pages.
- Reviews filterable by skin tone and undertone.
- Clear exchange terms for unopened shade mistakes.
- Afterpay and delivery times by state shown near the button.

| Measure | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Sell-through by shade | Which shades to restock or drop |
| Return and exchange reasons | Where shade guidance fails |
| Quiz use and conversion | Whether the tool is helping |
| Orders by creator | Which creators to rebook |
| Repeat rate by product | Which products build loyalty |

Look past return on ad spend. A creator who brings fewer first orders but more repeat buyers is often worth more.

## Claims and compliance

This is general guidance, not legal advice. Long-wear, waterproof, transfer-proof and non-comedogenic claims need testing behind them. Vegan, cruelty-free and clean claims need supplier records and a clear explanation, because the ACCC treats ethical and environmental claims seriously. Makeup with SPF can bring TGA sunscreen rules into play, so check how each product is classed before you advertise sun protection. Most cosmetics must list their ingredients, and importers of cosmetic ingredients may need AICIS registration. Never write or buy reviews, and do not hide negative ones.

## Mistakes that hold makeup brands back

- Launching twenty shades with one model photographed for all of them.
- Paying creators for a single post with no rights to reuse the content.
- Letting filters or heavy editing creep into swatches.
- Running ads to a shade that is about to sell out.
- Treating gift buyers and self-buyers with the same message.
- Discounting new launches so quickly that buyers learn to wait.
