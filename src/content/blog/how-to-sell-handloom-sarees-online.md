Anyone learning how to sell handloom sarees online soon finds that the product is half the sale and the story is the other half. A cotton handloom saree from Coimbatore, Chettinad or Madurai is light, breathable and made by hand, but in a phone-sized photo it can look just like a cheaper powerloom copy. Buyers who choose handloom want to know who made it, how, and whether the claim is true. This guide covers Kovai Kora and other Tamil Nadu weaves, GI and Handloom Mark context, honest weaver stories, photography, care instructions, returns, Instagram and WhatsApp selling, and diaspora buyers.

We are an ecommerce growth team, not lawyers or textile testers. Check GI, labelling and consumer law questions with a qualified adviser.

If you sell heavy silk sarees for weddings, our guide to [selling Kanchipuram silk sarees online](/blog/how-to-sell-kanchipuram-silk-sarees-online/) covers that higher-value segment. This guide focuses on cotton and everyday handlooms.

## Tamil Nadu handlooms buyers already search for

Tamil Nadu has a long list of weaves with their own names, and several are registered Geographical Indications. Useful context for your listings:

- **Kovai Kora cotton sarees** from the Coimbatore region, woven from cotton and silk yarn, crisp and light, often with zari or contrast borders. Registered as a GI.
- **Chettinad cotton (Kandangi) sarees** from the Karaikudi area, known for bold checks, stripes and earthy colours.
- **Madurai Sungudi**, a tie-and-dye cotton with dotted patterns.
- **Other clusters**, such as Arani and Salem silks, Paramakudi cottons and many smaller weaving villages across the state.

Use the name buyers search for, but only where it is true. A powerloom saree sold as a GI handloom is a legal risk and a fast way to lose the trust you are trying to build.

## Handloom or powerloom: be clear on every page

The honest answer to "is this handloom?" is the foundation of your brand.

1. **State the method on every product page.** Handloom, powerloom, or a mix such as handloom body with machine-made border. Buyers who want handloom will thank you, and buyers who only want a price will still find what they need.
2. **Explain the Handloom Mark and India Handloom Brand as context.** These are government-backed labels that help buyers identify handwoven products. Show them only on products your registration covers, and say in plain words what each label means.
3. **Point to the signs of handwoven cloth.** Small slubs, slight variations in tension and a soft, uneven selvedge are part of the craft. Mention them so buyers do not treat them as defects.
4. **Keep your sourcing records.** Know which weaver, society or cluster each saree comes from. Buyers sometimes ask, and you should be able to answer.

## Tell weaver stories honestly

Weaver stories sell handloom, but they are easy to get wrong.

- **Ask permission** before using a weaver's name, face or home in photos and videos.
- **Use real details**: the village, how many days a saree took, the yarn, the motif and its local name.
- **Avoid pity framing.** Show weavers as skilled makers, not as charity cases.
- **Do not exaggerate.** If a saree was made by a cooperative society or a master weaver's unit, say so. If you cannot verify a detail, leave it out.
- **Share the credit.** A short "meet the weaver" page or reel, updated with new pieces, gives buyers a reason to come back.

Short, unedited loom videos often outperform polished brand films, because they show the work as it is.

## Photography that shows cotton truthfully

Cotton handlooms are less shiny than silk, so the challenge is texture and colour rather than zari glare. A practical shot list for each saree:

| Shot | What it shows | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Full saree spread flat | Overall colour and design | Daylight, no filters |
| Border and pallu close-ups | Motifs and contrast colours | Shoot both sides of the fabric |
| Macro of the weave | Count, slubs, hand-woven texture | Use a plain background |
| Draped on a model or mannequin | How it falls and fits | Front, back and pleats |
| Folded with a hand for scale | Weight and thickness | Natural light |
| A 20 to 30 second video | Movement and true colour | Unedited, near a window |

Add a line under the photos: "Colours may vary slightly by screen; the video shows the truest colour." It sets expectations and reduces disputes.

## Care instructions and returns

Cotton handloom buyers, especially first-time buyers, worry about shrinking, colour bleeding and stiffness. Answer before they ask.

### Care card

- First wash: gentle hand wash or dry clean, cold water, mild detergent
- Some natural or deep dyes may bleed at first; wash separately
- Dry in shade, not direct sun
- Starch lightly if you want the crisp Kora finish back
- Iron warm, on the reverse side
- Store folded in cotton cloth, refolding now and then

Put this on every product page and as a printed card in the parcel, in Tamil and English.

### Returns and COD

Write simple rules and show them on every page. Many sellers accept unused sarees with tags within a short window, exclude stitched or altered sarees, and offer exchange rather than refund on sale pieces. For cash on delivery, an order confirmation on WhatsApp before dispatch cuts refused parcels. ## How to sell handloom sarees online on Instagram and WhatsApp

For most handloom sellers, Instagram and WhatsApp do more selling than any website. They suit a product that needs a story and a conversation.

### Instagram

- **Drops**: post a small batch of new sarees at a set time each week, so followers learn when to check.
- **Reels**: loom clips, drape tutorials, "one saree three ways", and customer photos with permission.
- **Lives**: show sarees one by one and take orders in the comments or DMs. Keep a clear numbering system so nobody is confused about which saree they asked for.
- **Tamil and English captions**: our guide to [Instagram marketing in Tamil](/blog/instagram-marketing-in-tamil/) covers voice and scripts. Every Tamil line should be written or checked by a fluent Tamil writer.

### WhatsApp

- A WhatsApp Business catalogue with photos, prices and saree codes.
- Broadcast lists for new arrivals, sent selectively, not daily.
- Quick replies for care, delivery times and return rules.
- Payment links, so buyers can pay without leaving the chat.

## Collections, seasons and fair pricing

Handloom stock is limited by how fast a loom can weave, so plan collections early. Tamil Nadu buyers shop for cotton sarees ahead of summer, for Pongal and Tamil New Year, and for Deepavali, while office-wear cottons sell steadily through the year. Agree designs and quantities with weavers two to three months ahead of each peak, and photograph pieces as they arrive rather than waiting for a full collection.

Price with the weaver's work in mind. Buyers who choose handloom often want to know that the maker was paid fairly, so explain your sourcing simply, without claims you cannot back up. Avoid deep discounts on handwoven pieces; they teach buyers to wait for a sale and squeeze the weavers who supply you. Offer bundles, gift packing or free blouse-piece finishing instead.

## Diaspora buyers abroad

Tamil and other Indian families in the UK, US, Singapore, Malaysia, the Gulf and Australia buy cotton handlooms for festivals, office wear and gifts, often because good handwoven cotton is hard to find where they live.

- Enable international shipping and show prices in the buyer's currency where your platform allows it.
- State who pays import duty and taxes, and realistic delivery times to each main country.
- Plan around Pongal, Tamil New Year, Deepavali and community events abroad, with order cut-offs well ahead.
- Offer video calls in their time zone for larger orders or family bundles.
- Ship tracked, and send tracking updates on WhatsApp or email.
