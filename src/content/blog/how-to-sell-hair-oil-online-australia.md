Before you work out how to sell hair oil online, Australia asks you to settle one question: is your oil a cosmetic, or does the way you talk about it make it a therapeutic good? The answer shapes your label, your ads and what creators can say. Once that is clear, hair oil is a good online product. It is light to ship, easy to show on video and used up at a steady rate. This guide covers the steps in order, from classification to the refill.

Nothing here is legal advice. Treat it as a checklist of questions for your regulatory adviser.

## Step 1: Decide what kind of product your hair oil is

Most hair oils are cosmetics. They are sold to condition, add shine, tame frizz, soften lengths or make the scalp feel comfortable, and they do not need approval before sale.

### When a hair oil stops being a cosmetic

The trouble starts with claims. Wording about regrowing hair, stopping hair loss, thickening thinning hair or treating dandruff or another scalp condition suggests a therapeutic purpose. In Australia, that can bring the product under the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA), with ARTG inclusion and the TGA advertising code. Some herbal and Ayurvedic oils are sold with traditional claims, which also fall into the therapeutic system if they mention health benefits.

### The rosemary oil problem

Rosemary and other botanical oils have been heavily discussed on social media as growth remedies. Riding that trend with growth claims is exactly what can turn a cosmetic into an unapproved therapeutic good. Talk about the scent, the massage ritual and how hair feels after washing, and leave growth out of your copy, ads and creator briefs.

## Step 2: Check ingredients, AICIS and labels

- **AICIS registration.** If your business imports or manufactures cosmetic ingredients or finished cosmetics, you may need to register with the Australian Industrial Chemicals Introduction Scheme and categorise what you bring in. This matters if you import oils or blends from overseas suppliers. Confirm who holds the duty if you use a contract manufacturer.
- **Ingredient list.** Australia's mandatory information standard for cosmetic ingredient labelling covers most products, so print the list on the pack and repeat it on your product page.
- **Essential oil warnings.** Some essential oils and fragrance components carry extra labelling or packaging requirements at certain strengths, such as warnings or child-resistant closures. Ask your adviser to review the formula against current scheduling rules.
- **Origin and quantity.** Show net contents correctly, and make sure any "Australian made" claim is accurate under consumer law.

## Step 3: Package for heat, distance and rough handling

Hair oil leaks are the fastest route to refunds and poor reviews. Australian distances and summer temperatures make packaging harder than in many markets.

- Add an induction seal or shrink band beneath every cap.
- Ship with a screw cap, and include the dropper or pump separately if needed.
- Put each bottle in a sealed bag inside the carton, so one leak does not ruin a whole order.
- Pad glass well, or use sturdy PET if your formula suits it.
- Test packs in warm conditions, because parcels can sit in hot vans for days on longer routes to regional areas, WA or the NT.

Compare Australia Post, Sendle and couriers on delivery times by state, and publish those times on your site.

## Step 4: Write product pages by hair type and climate

A buyer cannot smell or feel your oil through a screen, so the page has to do that work.

### What each page should cover

- What the oil is for: pre-wash treatment, scalp massage, finishing or all three.
- Hair types it suits, from fine and straight to thick, curly and coily.
- How much to use, how long to leave it in and how to wash it out.
- Scent, texture and how greasy it feels, shown in a short video.
- Key ingredients and what they do for feel and appearance, in plain words.
- An honest timeline: what someone notices after one use, and after regular use.
- The full ingredient list, size and any warnings.

### Write for Australian conditions

Humid summers in Queensland and the north bring frizz. Dry inland heat and heated winter rooms leave ends brittle. Beach and pool regulars deal with salt water and chlorine. Pages and guides that name these situations feel more useful than generic copy. For buyers from South Asian and other communities who grew up with hair oiling, a page that respects the ritual and explains your blend clearly will win more trust than a trend-led pitch.

### Give men's and beard oils their own page

Many hair oil brands add a beard or men's scalp oil once the first product sells. It reaches a different buyer, who usually wants a short explanation, a clear scent description and a quick demo rather than a long ritual. Give it its own page, its own photos and its own ads instead of a line on the main product. The same claim limits apply: talk about softness, itch-free comfort and a tidy finish, not about growing a fuller beard.

Gift buyers matter here too. Partners and family often buy grooming oils for Father's Day in September and for Christmas, so a small set with a comb or balm can be worth planning ahead.

## Step 6: Price each size for profit

Hair oil margins depend heavily on size and postage. A small bottle that ships in a padded bag can carry a very different cost from a large glass bottle in a box. Work through these numbers before you print labels:

1. Cost of oil, bottle, cap, seal, label and carton.
2. Postage by zone and size band.
3. Payment fees, including Afterpay if you offer it.
4. Marketplace fees on Amazon AU or TikTok Shop.
5. A realistic allowance for leaks, refunds and ad cost per order.

## Step 7: Advertise within Australian rules

### What the rules expect

Under the Australian Consumer Law, claims and images must not mislead. Side-by-side hairline shots in mismatched lighting, or touched up to suggest density, risk misleading buyers and run against Meta and TikTok rules on personal attributes. The AANA codes apply to advertising generally, and Ad Standards hears complaints. Paid, gifted and affiliate creator posts must be clearly disclosed as advertising.

### Creative that works and stays safe

- Warming the oil, sectioning hair and a slow scalp massage.
- The finish on lengths, in natural light.
- A wash-day routine from start to finish.
- Honest customer clips about scent and feel.

On Google, target searches by oil type and use, such as "argan oil for frizzy hair" or "pre-wash hair oil for dry ends". On Amazon AU, Sponsored Products help a new listing collect its first sales and reviews.

## Step 8: Delivery, returns and consumer rights

Set a clear change-of-mind policy for opened oils on hygiene grounds, and explain it before purchase. Whatever that policy says, buyers still hold consumer rights under the Australian Consumer Law if an oil turns out faulty, unsafe or unlike its description. A leaking bottle is your problem to fix. Handle customer data in line with the Privacy Act where it applies, and keep consent records for email and SMS.

## Step 9: Keep buyers using the oil, then win the refill

A hair oil needs regular use before many buyers form a view. Without follow-up, many stop after a few tries.

- Send usage tips on delivery: how much, how often and how to wash it out.
- Check in after the first week about scent and feel.
- Around week four, share routine tips and a reminder to keep going.
- Ask for a review once regular use has had a fair go.
- Time a refill reminder to how long a bottle actually lasts.

Offer refill pouches or larger bottles on your own store, and a subscription with an easy pause. Plan offers for Click Frenzy, Black Friday, Christmas gift sets, Mother's Day in May and EOFY in June. ## Common mistakes new hair oil brands make

- Copying growth claims from social media trends.
- Shipping glass bottles with loose droppers and no inner seal.
- Using the same page for a pre-wash oil and a finishing oil.
- Pricing a small bottle without counting postage and leaks.
- Asking for reviews within days, before regular use has started.
