A supplement marketing strategy UK brands can rely on has to work within some of the tightest advertising rules in ecommerce. Health claims come from an authorised register, the Advertising Standards Authority can rule on a single complaint, and Meta, Google, TikTok and Amazon all review health products closely. The brands that grow steadily treat these limits as part of the brief, then win on clarity, trust and repeat orders. This guide sets out how to build that plan.

We are marketers, not regulatory advisers. Treat the rules described here as a general orientation and confirm your own claims with a qualified specialist.

## Start with one buyer, one product and one claim

Trying to speak to everyone usually means saying nothing memorable. Pick the buyer you can serve most clearly in the next six months, such as a woman in her forties looking at magnesium for sleep routines, a club runner comparing electrolytes, or a vegan wanting a simple daily multi.

Then choose one hero product and anchor its promise in a claim you are allowed to make. Ask three questions:

1. Which authorised claim from the GB register does this product qualify for, given its dose?
2. What do buyers in this group worry about: taste, capsule size, sourcing, value per day?
3. Why would they pick this pot over the near-identical ones beside it on Amazon UK?

The answer to the last question is often not a health benefit at all. It might be a better nutrient form, a cleaner ingredient list, a flavour people enjoy, or a refill pouch that cuts plastic. Those are claims you can usually make freely, as long as they are true and evidenced.

## Prove it before you pay for reach

Paid traffic sent to a weak product page is wasted. Before raising budgets, make sure the page can convince a sceptical UK buyer on a phone.

- Show the front and back of the pack in sharp images.
- Put ingredients, amounts per daily dose and nutrient reference values in text.
- Explain each nutrient form in plain English.
- Link batch test reports where you have them.
- Say who should talk to a GP or pharmacist first.
- Feature reviews that describe taste, routine and service rather than health outcomes.

Your product page is also what the ASA and ad platforms will read alongside the ad, so consistency matters. Our [supplement marketing agency for UK brands](/uk/supplement-marketing-agency/) begins most projects with this page-level work for that reason.

## Write ads that stay within the CAP code

### What tends to cause trouble

- Disease language, direct or implied: treats, cures, prevents, heals, fights.
- General wellbeing claims with no specific authorised claim beside them.
- Suggesting that a normal diet cannot provide enough nutrients.
- Before-and-after images and copy that plays on weight or appearance worries.
- Testimonials describing medical results, which become your claim once you use them.
- Weight-control ads shown to under-18s.

### Angles that usually work

- **Ingredients and dose.** "1,000 IU vitamin D3 in one small softgel."
- **Format and taste.** Chewable, dissolvable, flavour-free, vegan capsule.
- **Routine.** One with breakfast, a scoop after training, a sachet in the gym bag.
- **Transparency.** Batch testing, UK manufacturing, full label shown.
- **Value per day.** Cost per serving versus a larger pack, shown clearly.
- **Authorised claims, quoted faithfully.** Short, accurate and placed beside the product.

## Plan your channel mix

### Meta (Facebook and Instagram)

Meta remains a strong discovery channel for supplements. Use broad audiences with sharp creative rather than narrow health interests, which are limited. Test several hooks around format and routine, and expect ads to be reviewed more slowly than in other categories.

### TikTok and TikTok Shop UK

Short creator demos suit gummies, drinks and anything with a visible texture or ritual. TikTok Shop lets viewers buy without leaving the app, which helps trial packs. Check eligibility for your products first, as some supplement types are restricted.

### Google Search and Shopping

Search catches people who already know what they want: "magnesium glycinate 400mg", "vegan omega-3". A clean Shopping feed with accurate titles, sizes and VAT-inclusive prices does much of the work. Some ingredients are blocked in Google Ads entirely, so check before building campaigns.

### Amazon UK

Amazon UK is where many buyers compare and reorder. Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands perform more strongly on listings that already have label images, A+ content and a steady flow of reviews. Protect your listings from unauthorised sellers.

### Email and SMS

These carry the retention side of the plan: welcome series, how-to-take content, refill reminders and subscription offers. Under UK GDPR and the electronic marketing rules, collect clear consent and keep an easy unsubscribe in every message.

## Use creators carefully

UK creators can build trust quickly, but supplements are a category where one careless video can trigger a complaint. A good creator programme includes:

- A written brief listing the exact claims they may use and words they must avoid.
- A requirement to label paid or gifted content clearly as advertising.
- Approval of scripts or drafts before posting where your agreement allows it.
- No filming of "results" after a few days, and no body-transformation content.
- Rights to reuse strong content in ads, with the same checks applied again.

Smaller creators in specific niches, such as runners, new parents or plant-based cooks, often bring more relevant buyers than large general accounts.

## Publish content that answers real questions

People search before they buy: "what is the difference between vitamin D2 and D3", "how long does a 90-capsule pot last", "can I take magnesium and zinc together". Short, factual answers to questions like these can earn steady Google traffic and give AI assistants something accurate to cite. Point to NHS pages for health questions, keep claims inside your authorised list, and link each guide to the relevant product.

AI assistants increasingly answer these questions directly, and they draw on pages that are specific, well structured and consistent. Give each product a short, factual summary near the top of the page, use the same ingredient names and amounts across your store, Amazon UK and your guides, and add plain FAQs that a shopper could quote. Comparison pieces work well if they are fair: explaining when citrate suits someone better than glycinate builds more trust than claiming your version wins every time. Update guides when your formulation or the rules change, and date them so readers know they are current.

## Make retention the centre of the plan

The economics of supplements depend on the second, third and fourth order. A UK brand that wins a customer in January and loses them by March has usually lost money.

### Retention checklist

- [ ] Welcome emails that explain when and how to take the product.
- [ ] A refill reminder timed to the number of days in each pot.
- [ ] Subscribe-and-save with skip, pause and cancel available online.
- [ ] Subscription terms written plainly, in line with UK consumer rules.
- [ ] A larger-pack offer after the second order.
- [ ] Review requests after a few weeks of use.
- [ ] A win-back message for lapsed customers that does not lead with a discount.

## Use offers that protect the brand

Constant discounting teaches customers to wait. Prefer offers that add value: a free shaker with a protein tub, a travel pouch with a subscription, a bundle of two products that make sense together. Keep deep discounts for Black Friday and the January launch window, and make sure any "was" price is genuine. UK pricing rules are strict about reference prices, so keep records.

## Plan around the UK supplement calendar

Demand for supplements in the UK follows a familiar pattern, and a strategy that ignores it ends up chasing the market rather than meeting it.

| Period | What happens | What to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| October to December | Planning window for January; Black Friday and Christmas gifting | Stock, approved creative, gift sets, subscription offers |
| January | Resolutions drive the busiest weeks for protein, multis and fitness ranges | New-customer campaigns plus a strong welcome series |
| February to April | Many January buyers drift; a second wave arrives with spring training | Refill reminders, how-to-take content, outdoor sport angles |
| Summer | Holidays, travel and outdoor events | Travel packs, electrolytes, sachets for bags |
| Autumn | Darker evenings and back-to-routine habits | Vitamin D and daily-routine messaging within authorised claims |

Brief creators and book stock at least six weeks before each peak. Ad review for health products can take longer than usual in busy periods, so submit January creative before Christmas rather than on New Year's Eve.

### Two quieter levers worth using

- **Branded search.** As awareness grows, people search for your brand name on Google and Amazon UK. Bid on it so competitors cannot take those clicks cheaply.
- **Post-purchase surveys.** A one-question survey asking where buyers first heard of you fills the gaps that attribution tools miss, especially for creator and TikTok activity.

## Measure what actually matters

Track these by product and by channel:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Cost to win a new customer | Shows whether acquisition is sustainable |
| Second-order rate within 60 days | The earliest sign of a healthy product |
| Value per customer at 6 and 12 months | Sets how much you can spend on the first order |
| Subscription pause and cancel rate | Flags taste, value or delivery problems |
| Ad rejection and complaint count | Shows where copy needs work |

## Supplement marketing strategy UK: mistakes to avoid

- **Copying claims from bigger brands.** Their approach may not survive a complaint.
- **Pausing everything after January.** Spring and autumn bring their own demand.
- **Judging creators on immediate sales.** Look at reach, saves and branded search too.
- **Running subscriptions without clear terms.** Confused customers cancel and complain.

## Putting it together: a 90-day plan

1. **Weeks 1 to 3.** Choose the buyer and hero product, map claims to the register, and fix the product page.
2. **Weeks 4 to 6.** Launch Meta and Google tests with three or four angles, set up Amazon UK listings and brief a small group of creators.
3. **Weeks 7 to 9.** Switch on refill reminders and subscriptions, and cut angles that are rejected or underperform.
4. **Weeks 10 to 12.** Shift budget to products with healthy second-order rates and plan the next seasonal peak.
