If you are working out how to sell perfume online (USA customers, US carriers, US marketplaces), the core problem is the same one every fragrance brand faces: the shopper cannot smell the product. In a department store or at a Sephora counter they spray a strip, walk around and decide. Online they see a bottle, a price and a list of notes. This guide covers the formats to launch with, US cosmetic rules, scent descriptions, sampling, hazmat shipping, marketplaces, "inspired by" ranges, the American gifting calendar and the costs to plan for.

## Pick formats US shoppers already buy

| Format | What it is | Selling notes |
|---|---|---|
| Eau de parfum | Higher fragrance concentration in alcohol | The usual hero product and the margin carrier |
| Eau de toilette or cologne | Lighter concentration | Everyday and warm-weather scents |
| Body mist | Light, often sweet | Impulse buys, younger shoppers, gift add-ons |
| Perfume oil or roll-on | Fragrance in a carrier oil | Close-wearing, travel-friendly, often easier to ship |
| Solid perfume | Fragrance in a wax or balm | Pocket size, low leak risk |
| Travel spray | Small refillable or disposable atomizer | Gifting, repeat buyers, trying a second scent |
| Discovery set | Several small vials | The entry product for new customers |

A short range is easier to explain and stock. Many new brands launch with four to eight scents spread across clear families (fresh, floral, woody, amber, gourmand) and one discovery set that holds them all.

## US cosmetic rules for fragrance

Perfume is generally regulated as a cosmetic in the US. Cosmetics do not need FDA approval before sale, but they must be safe for their intended use and correctly labeled. The Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act (MoCRA) added duties for many businesses: facility registration, product listing, keeping evidence that products are safe, and reporting serious adverse events. Fragrance allergen labeling rules are also expected, so watch for FDA updates.

Two practical points:

- **Labels.** English labels with the product identity, net quantity, ingredients (fragrance can usually be listed as "fragrance"), warnings such as flammability, and the name and place of business of the responsible company.
- **Claims.** A scent that "calms anxiety" or "helps you sleep" moves into drug territory. Describe mood and feeling, not health effects.

Many fragrance houses and oil suppliers also follow the IFRA Standards, the industry's safety limits for fragrance ingredients. They are not US law, but asking your supplier for IFRA conformity documents is a sensible check, and some retailers expect them.

## Describe scents for buyers who cannot smell them

### Translate the notes

List the top, heart and base notes, then say what they mean in ordinary words: a sharp grapefruit opening that softens into jasmine, then settles into warm cedar by the afternoon. Keep the list to the notes a buyer will actually notice.

### Be honest about performance

State the concentration and give a typical wear time and how far the scent carries, from close to the skin to filling a room. Say that wear changes with skin, climate and clothing. Shoppers in Arizona and shoppers in Maine will notice the difference, and overpromising longevity is a fast route to poor reviews.

### Compare without borrowing names

Point buyers by preference rather than by someone else's brand: "if you reach for fresh, clean scents in summer, start here." A short scent quiz that asks about moods, seasons and occasions can recommend two or three of your scents and lead to the discovery set.

### Use video and reviews

Short videos of real people wearing a scent across a day, from first spray to evening, help shoppers imagine it. Ask reviewers specific questions: when did you wear it, how long did it last, did anyone comment on it? Those answers sell better than star ratings alone.

## Sampling: discovery sets and credits

Sampling is the most dependable way to lower the risk of a first purchase.

- **Paid discovery set.** Priced as a fair trial, so it attracts serious buyers.
- **Credit toward a full bottle.** The set's cost comes off a later full-size order, so the sample feels like a first step.
- **A vial in every order.** Add a different scent to each parcel, chosen to match what the customer bought.
- **A timed follow-up.** An email or text near the end of the set asks which scent they liked and links straight to that bottle with the credit applied.

Track how many discovery-set buyers buy a full bottle within two months, by channel. That rate tells you how much you can afford to pay to acquire a sample buyer.

## Shipping alcohol-based perfume in the US

### Why perfume is hazmat

Most perfumes contain enough alcohol to be classed as flammable liquids under US transport rules, which makes them hazardous materials for shipping. Retail-size bottles can often travel under limited-quantity provisions, which ease some requirements but still call for proper packaging and box markings.

### Carriers and services

In practice, many US brands ship perfume by ground. USPS generally limits flammable liquids to ground transport, and UPS and FedEx have their own hazmat conditions and agreements. Each carrier changes its rules from time to time, so get current conditions in writing and keep the safety data sheet for every product on file.

### Where ground cannot reach

Ground-only rules make Alaska, Hawaii, US territories and military addresses harder or impossible to serve. Set checkout rules that flag these addresses, explain the options plainly and avoid taking orders you cannot deliver.

### Packaging and fulfillment

- Secure caps, shrink bands or seals on every bottle
- Fitted inserts that keep bottles upright and still
- A sealed bag around each bottle in case of leaks
- A third-party logistics provider that is set up to handle hazmat parcels

Glass is heavy, and ground shipping is slower in peak season. Our [free shipping threshold calculator](/tools/free-shipping-threshold-calculator/) helps you set a threshold that covers those costs.

### Returns

Opened perfume usually cannot be resold. Many brands accept returns of unopened bottles, replace damaged or leaking items, and lean on sampling to prevent disappointment returns. Returned perfume is still hazmat, so give customers clear instructions.

## "Inspired by" ranges and trademark risk

Dupe culture is big in the US, and many shoppers search for affordable alternatives to designer scents. The scent profile is rarely the main problem. The name is.

- A designer's trademarked name in your product title, ad copy, keywords, hashtags or URL can bring trademark complaints and legal claims.
- Amazon, TikTok Shop and Walmart act quickly on rights-owner complaints and can remove listings or restrict accounts.
- Google and Meta may limit ads that use trademarked terms.
- Copying a bottle shape, cap or box design adds a separate layer of risk.

Give every scent its own name, describe it by notes and mood, and let shoppers make their own comparisons in reviews and community posts. Register your own brand name early, and ask a trademark lawyer to review any comparative wording before you use it.

## The US fragrance calendar

Fragrance is one of America's most popular gifts, so the calendar drives stock and budget:

- **Black Friday through Christmas.** The largest peak. Gift sets, ground-shipping cut-offs and stock must be ready by early November.
- **Valentine's Day.** Couples' gifts and date-night scents.
- **Mother's Day in May and Father's Day in June.** Gift sets and travel sizes.
- **Graduation season.** First "grown-up" fragrance gifts.
- **Prime Day in July.** Important if Amazon is a major channel.
- **Diwali and Eid.** Gifting moments for South Asian and Muslim American buyers, especially for attar and oud ranges.

Because ground shipping is slower, publish order-by dates on product pages and in emails, and offer a digital gift card or discovery set for late shoppers.

## Know your costs per bottle

Before you set prices, add up the bottle, atomizer, cap and box (which often cost more than the juice), fragrance oil and alcohol, samples given away or sold below cost, hazmat-ready fulfillment, breakage and leaks, marketplace fees, returns and the advertising needed to reach someone who has never smelled your work. Then compare one-off gift buyers with self-buyers who move from a discovery set to a full bottle and reorder; the second group can justify a higher acquisition cost.

## How to sell perfume online (USA): a launch checklist

- Four to eight scents in clear families, plus a discovery set and travel sizes
- Labels, safety documents and MoCRA duties reviewed with an adviser
- Every scent named by you, with no designer names in titles, ads, keywords or tags
- Product pages with plain-word notes, concentration, honest wear time and occasions
- Discovery-set credit and follow-up messages live before ads start
- Carrier hazmat conditions confirmed in writing and packaging tested
- Checkout rules for addresses ground shipping cannot reach
- Amazon dangerous goods documents uploaded before stock is sent
- Gift sets and order-by dates planned for the holidays, Valentine's Day and Mother's Day
