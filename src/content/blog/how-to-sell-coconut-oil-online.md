If you are working out how to sell coconut oil online, you are likely close to the source. The Pollachi area grows much of Tamil Nadu's coconut, and Kangeyam, near Tiruppur, has long been known for its coconut oil mills. Many families in the belt have pressed oil for generations and sold it in bulk to traders and brands. Selling your own oil online under your own name is a different job: licences, honest labels, packing that does not leak, and a reason for buyers to come back each month. This guide covers each step.

We are an ecommerce growth team, not food safety or regulatory consultants. Treat the rules below as general guidance and check them with a qualified adviser.

## Step 1: Decide what you are selling

Coconut oil sits in two worlds, and the rules differ.

### Edible coconut oil

Oil for cooking is a food. It needs FSSAI registration or a licence, food labelling, and the right category on marketplaces. Your claims are judged as food claims.

### Coconut hair oil

Oil sold for hair or skin is a cosmetic, even when it is pure coconut oil. It falls under the cosmetics rules, which need their own manufacturing licence or a licensed contract manufacturer, and cosmetic labels. Our guide on [how to sell hair oil online](/blog/how-to-sell-hair-oil-online/) covers that side in more detail.

### Doing both

Many Kongu region mills sell both. The simplest route is two clearly separate products with their own labels and listings, rather than one bottle that tries to be both. Ask your adviser before you combine them.

## Step 2: Source and press with a story you can prove

Buyers increasingly ask where their oil comes from. You have a real answer.

- **Name your source honestly:** "pressed from copra sourced around Pollachi" is useful if it is true; do not claim a single farm if you buy from many.
- **Describe your process:** sun-dried or machine-dried copra, the type of press, filtering and settling.
- **Show it:** short videos of the copra drying, the press running and the oil settling build more trust than any slogan.

### Cold-pressed, wood-pressed and refined

| Type | What it usually means | How to describe it honestly |
|---|---|---|
| Cold-pressed | Mechanically pressed without added heat | Say how you control heat, and only use the term if it is true |
| Wood-pressed (chekku or ghani) | Pressed in a traditional wooden press | Show the press; do not use the word for a steel expeller |
| Refined | Processed to remove smell, colour and impurities | Explain it plainly; refined oil has its own buyers |
| Virgin coconut oil | Usually made from fresh coconut milk, not copra | Check the FSSAI standard before using the term |

Every term must match what is in the bottle. A buyer who smells a refined oil labelled wood-pressed will leave a review that says so.

## Step 3: Licence, label and claims

### Licence

Check which FSSAI category applies to you: basic registration, a state licence or a central licence, depending on your scale and activities. Display your FSSAI number on labels and listings. If a mill presses for you, check both their licence and yours.

### Label checklist for edible oil

1. Product name and type of oil
2. List of ingredients, and if blended, the oils and proportions as required
3. Net quantity by volume or weight
4. Name and address of the manufacturer, packer or marketer
5. FSSAI logo and licence number
6. Batch or lot number
7. Date of manufacture and the date mark or use-by date the rules require
8. MRP and consumer care details
9. Veg mark and storage instructions

Legal Metrology rules apply on top, so the net quantity, MRP and address must be right on the pack and match the listing.

### No health claims

Do not say the oil treats or prevents any condition, and avoid medical words in ads, listings and labels. Platforms reject ads that promise health results, and food claims are regulated. Talk instead about taste, aroma, how it is pressed, the source and how families cook with it.

### Test every batch and keep records

Online buyers cannot smell or taste the oil before ordering, so consistency is what earns the second order. Build a simple routine:

- Keep a batch record for each pressing: copra source, drying method, press date, filtering and bottling date.
- Have batches tested at an accredited food testing laboratory at the frequency your licence and adviser suggest.
- Keep a retained sample of each batch for the shelf life of the product, so you can check any complaint.
- Print the batch number on every pack, and ask buyers to quote it when they write in.

If a buyer says one bottle smells different, you can trace it in minutes rather than guessing. You can also share, in plain words, what you test for and how often, which reassures careful buyers without making claims you cannot support.

## Step 4: Pack so it arrives clean

Leakage is the most common complaint for oil sold online, and it can damage other parcels too.

- **Bottles:** use sturdy PET, HDPE or glass with caps that seal well. Glass looks premium but adds weight and breakage risk.
- **Seals:** an induction seal or inner seal under the cap stops most leaks.
- **Pouches:** place each bottle in a sealed plastic pouch before boxing.
- **Box:** use dividers for multi-bottle orders and fill empty space.
- **Heat:** test packs in the hottest months; caps loosen and oil expands.
- **Solidifying:** coconut oil turns solid in cooler weather and air-conditioned rooms. Tell buyers this is normal and how to melt it gently, so they do not think the oil has gone bad.

Track leakage by pack size, cap supplier and courier, and fix the weakest link first.

## Step 5: Choose your channels

### Your own store

Your store tells the full story: groves, mill, process and testing. It is also where subscriptions and refill packs work well. Add UPI, cards and COD, a WhatsApp contact and clear delivery times.

### Amazon and Flipkart

Buyers searching "cold pressed coconut oil" or "wood pressed coconut oil" land here first. Fill in every attribute, use clear images of the label and pack size, and collect genuine reviews. Price after fees and shipping, not before.

### Quick commerce

Coconut oil is a grocery staple, which suits Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart once supply is reliable and margins work after platform charges. Start in a few cities where you already see demand. Our guide on [how to sell on Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart](/blog/how-to-sell-on-blinkit-zepto-instamart/) explains onboarding and ads.

### Local and B2B

Bakeries, restaurants and sweet shops buy in larger packs. Keep this side alongside online retail; it smooths out monthly volumes.

## Step 6: Build repeat orders

A family uses coconut oil every week, so the second order matters more than the first.

- **Pack sizes:** a small trial bottle, a regular size and a larger family or refill pack.
- **Subscriptions:** a monthly or two-monthly delivery with a small benefit, easy to pause or cancel.
- **Refills:** pouches or larger cans that refill a glass bottle cut packing waste and cost.
- **Reminders:** WhatsApp messages timed to the usual reorder point, in Tamil or English, with permission.
- **Recipes:** Kongu and Kerala dishes, festival sweets and everyday cooking content.

## Step 7: Market honestly

Content that works for coconut oil:

- Process videos from the grove to the bottle
- Side-by-side clips of your oil's colour and aroma, without knocking other brands
- Home cooks using the oil in Tamil and Malayalam dishes
- Owner videos in Tamil, Kongu Tamil and English
- Customer photos and reviews shared with permission

## Selling to buyers abroad

The Tamil and Malayali diaspora and many other buyers abroad look for traditionally pressed coconut oil. Exporting food needs its own registrations and checks for the destination country, including labelling and import rules. Start with a small test through a cross-border marketplace or an importer, and only after domestic packing and supply are steady.

## Common mistakes

- Selling one bottle as both edible oil and hair oil without checking the rules
- Calling expeller-pressed oil wood-pressed
- Health or medical claims in ads and labels
- Leaky caps that turn five-star products into one-star reviews
- No refill or subscription option for a product people buy every month

- Product type decided: edible, cosmetic or both, kept separate
- FSSAI or cosmetics licence in place
- Label checked for every required detail
- Process described honestly, with video proof
- Packs tested for leaks in hot weather and on real courier routes
- Own store, a marketplace and a subscription or refill option live
