Most advice on herbal ecommerce is written for Ayurveda, so founders asking how to sell Siddha products online often have to translate it for themselves. Siddha is a traditional system of medicine from Tamil Nadu, with its own texts, preparation methods and vocabulary, and a buyer base that is strongest among Tamil speakers in India and abroad. It shares a legal framework with Ayurveda but needs its own product pages, keywords and content. This guide covers what is different about Siddha, and how a brand that sells both can keep them clear. For the general Ayurveda playbook, read [how to sell Ayurvedic products online](/blog/how-to-sell-ayurvedic-products-online/).

## What makes Siddha different to sell

Siddha traces its knowledge to the Siddhars, and its classical texts are written in Tamil. Chennai has a strong Siddha community of practitioners, colleges and manufacturers, including the National Institute of Siddha at Tambaram. For buyers, that heritage is a reason to trust a product, but only if your brand speaks about it accurately.

### Product forms and their names

Siddha products often have Tamil names that buyers search for directly. Some have rough Ayurvedic counterparts, which helps when you sell both:

| Siddha form | What it usually is | Closest Ayurvedic term |
|---|---|---|
| Chooranam | Fine herbal powder | Churna |
| Kudineer | Decoction made from a coarse powder | Kashayam or kwath |
| Thailam | Medicated oil | Taila |
| Legiyam | Herbal jam or paste | Lehyam |
| Manapagu | Herbal syrup | Similar syrups |
| Parpam and chendooram | Herbo-mineral preparations | Bhasma and sindura |

The herbo-mineral group is a distinctive part of Siddha. It needs the most care with testing, marketplaces and exports, which we cover below.

### Who buys Siddha online

- Tamil families in Tamil Nadu and Puducherry who grew up with Siddha remedies at home
- Tamil speakers in Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi and other cities who cannot find products locally
- The Tamil diaspora in Singapore, Malaysia, Sri Lanka, the Gulf, the UK and North America
- Buyers already using Ayurveda who are curious about a related tradition

These buyers search in Tamil, Tanglish and English, often by product name rather than brand.

## Step 1: Classify every product

The first question is what each product legally is. A Siddha range might contain:

- **Classical Siddha medicines** made to formulas from the recognised texts, listed in the Siddha formulary
- **Proprietary Siddha medicines** with the brand's own formula
- **Herbal cosmetics** such as hair oils, bath powders or face packs sold for appearance, not treatment
- **Herbal foods** such as health mixes or teas, which fall under food safety rules

The classification decides the licence, the label, which marketplace category you can use and what you can say in ads. Do not move a product between categories to make marketing easier. If a hair oil is licensed as a Siddha medicine, it is sold and advertised as one.

## Step 2: Licensing and labels in Tamil Nadu

### AYUSH licensing in general terms

Siddha, Ayurveda and Unani medicines are covered together under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and its rules, with national oversight from the Ministry of AYUSH. Manufacturing licences come from the state licensing authority for Indian systems of medicine. In Tamil Nadu this sits with the state's Indian medicine and homoeopathy administration, separate from the office that licenses cosmetics and modern medicines. Units are expected to follow good manufacturing practice for these medicines.

If you work with a contract manufacturer, products are made under that maker's licence or under a loan licence in your name. Ask for a copy of the licence and check that each of your products is approved on it. Offices, forms and procedures change, so check with your adviser.

### Labels

A Siddha medicine label usually needs the product name, whether it is classical or proprietary, ingredients with quantities, the licence number, batch number, manufacturing and expiry dates, directions and the manufacturer's details. Classical products often reference the source text. Use the label as the single source of truth for your website and listings, so every channel says the same thing.

### Herbo-mineral products

For parpam, chendooram and similar preparations, keep heavy-metal and quality test reports for each batch, follow label directions exactly and advise use under the guidance of a qualified practitioner. Marketplaces may ask for these reports or restrict the listings altogether.

## Step 3: Say nothing that sounds like a cure

The Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act restricts advertisements that claim to treat a list of diseases and conditions. On top of that, Meta, Google, Amazon and Flipkart limit health claims, and many Siddha products are traditionally linked with conditions such as fever, joint pain or blood sugar. That makes careless copy a real risk.

| Avoid | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "Cures fever in three days" | "A traditional kudineer from the Siddha formulary, prepared as the label directs" |
| "Controls sugar naturally" | Ingredients, source text and how the product is taken |
| "No side effects" | "Use as directed on the label, and consult a qualified Siddha practitioner" |
| Before-and-after health stories | Reviews about taste, preparation, packaging and delivery |

Remove disease-first claims from product names, images, creator scripts and WhatsApp replies too, not just from ads. Have your adviser review wording for every medicine product.

## Step 4: Build product pages in Tamil and English

Siddha buyers trust brands that use the right names. On each product page:

- Show the Tamil name in Tamil script, a common English spelling and a plain English description.
- List ingredients with their Tamil and botanical names where you can.
- Explain the preparation method and, for classical medicines, the source text.
- Repeat the directions exactly as they appear on the label.
- Add a line advising buyers to consult a qualified practitioner, especially for herbo-mineral products.
- Show licence and batch details, shelf life and storage advice.

Write short guides on how to prepare a kudineer, how a thailam is traditionally used, or the difference between Siddha and Ayurveda. These pages bring search visitors and answer the questions buyers would otherwise ask on WhatsApp. A fluent Tamil writer should write or check every Tamil page, because wrong names or loose directions undermine trust fast.

## Step 5: Marketplaces and channels

Your own store is where Siddha brands can explain their heritage fully and keep customer data. Marketplaces bring buyers who already search for product names.

- **Category choice:** marketplaces often group Siddha with Ayurveda or herbal health. Pick the category that matches the licence, and call the product Siddha in the title and attributes.
- **Approvals:** expect to upload licence copies, label images and sometimes test reports.
- **Practitioner partnerships:** registered Siddha practitioners can offer consultations or content. Keep them independent, and never let support staff prescribe.
- **YouTube and Instagram:** longer Tamil videos on preparation and tradition build trust, and short clips work for discovery.

## Step 6: Sell Siddha and Ayurveda side by side

Many Tamil Nadu brands offer both. That works well if each product stays clearly labelled.

- Keep separate collections for Siddha and Ayurveda, with a short explanation of each.
- Never mix system names on one product, such as calling a Siddha legiyam an Ayurvedic lehyam.
- Use Tamil-first content for Siddha and wider Indian content for Ayurveda, while keeping both bilingual.
- Bundle products that are used together, not products from two systems that happen to sell well.

## Step 7: Repeat orders and diaspora buyers

Chooranams, kudineers and thailams run out on predictable dates. Send WhatsApp or email reminders a few days before, offer refill packs and keep reorder links simple. Track whether reorders cover the cost of finding each buyer with our [LTV to CAC calculator](/tools/ltv-cac-calculator/).

For buyers abroad, start with herbal products permitted in each destination, avoid herbo-mineral exports unless your adviser confirms they are allowed, and show shipping times and import duties clearly. ## Mistakes Siddha sellers often make

- **Copying claims from old pamphlets.** Traditional leaflets often list diseases a remedy is used for. Reusing that text online breaks advertising rules and gets listings removed.
- **Calling every product Ayurvedic.** It may seem easier for marketplaces, but it misleads buyers and loses the Tamil searches that Siddha names attract.
- **Loose transliteration.** The same product can be spelt several ways in English letters. Pick one main spelling, mention common variants in the description, and keep it consistent.
- **Skipping test reports.** Without batch reports, herbo-mineral listings are easy for marketplaces and buyers to doubt.
- **Letting support staff give medical advice.** Train them to share label directions and refer health questions to a qualified practitioner.

## How to sell Siddha products online: a checklist

- Every product classified as classical, proprietary, cosmetic or food
- Licence copies on file and each product confirmed on the licence
- Labels complete and matching every listing
- Test reports kept for herbo-mineral batches
- No disease or cure claims in names, images, ads, scripts or chat replies
- Tamil and English pages checked by a fluent Tamil writer
- Marketplace categories chosen to match the licence
- Siddha and Ayurveda collections kept separate
- Refill reminders and a plan for diaspora orders
