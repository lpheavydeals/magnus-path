Working out how to sell baby products online UAE parents will trust takes more care than almost any other category. Parents here bring shopping habits from many home countries, check ingredients closely and are used to very fast delivery. Many products need to meet conformity rules or be registered before they are sold, labels are expected to carry Arabic, and infant formula faces strict limits on promotion. This guide works through the steps in order, from checking the rules to the refill and the birth gift that keep a baby brand growing.

Our job is growing online sales; we do not give legal or regulatory advice. What follows is a broad outline, and the rules are updated now and then. Confirm your own products' position with a qualified adviser, a conformity body or your distributor before you import or advertise.

## Step 1: Check product conformity and registration first

### Toys and children's products

For toys and a wide set of childcare goods, the usual reference point is the Emirates Conformity Assessment Scheme (ECAS), run under the Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology (MOIAT). In practice that can mean testing against recognised standards, a conformity certificate for the product and the right marking on the pack. Which items are covered, and how, depends on the product, so confirm it for each line before you place a stock order.

### Baby food, formula and snacks

Baby food and formula are treated as food products with extra rules. Dubai Municipality handles food product registration through its Montaji platform, while in the capital ADAFSA, the emirate's agriculture and food safety authority, normally has a say. Specialist infant nutrition may also involve federal health authorities. Allow time for this before you plan a launch.

### Toiletries and skincare

Baby wash, lotion and nappy cream are usually treated as cosmetic or personal care products and may need registration before sale. Words like "hypoallergenic" need test evidence you can produce on request.

### Arabic labelling

Packaged products sold to consumers are generally expected to carry Arabic, often alongside English. Age guidance, warnings, ingredients and instructions matter most. If you use Arabic stickers on imported packs, make sure they are accurate and acceptable for the product type.

## Step 2: Understand the limits on infant feeding and claims

UAE rules restrict the marketing of breast-milk substitutes, which broadly covers infant formula and can extend to related products such as bottles and teats. Restrictions can apply to advertising, discounts, free samples, gifts and contact with parents. If your range includes any of these, take advice before you list them, and keep them out of promotions, bundles, emails and creator briefs unless you have confirmed what is allowed.

For other products, keep claims modest and specific. Do not suggest that a product prevents illness, speeds development or replaces medical advice. Describe what it is made of, how to use it and who it is for.

## Step 3: Build product pages that answer parents' questions

UAE parents compare carefully, often in more than one language, and many ask friends in WhatsApp groups before they buy. A page that answers their questions first wins the order.

| Section | What to include |
|---|---|
| Age range | Months or years it suits, plus any weight or height limits |
| Safety | Standards met, conformity details, warnings and supervision advice |
| Materials or ingredients | Full list in Arabic and English, matching the physical label |
| Size and fit | Measurements, size charts, fit for cars, prams or cots |
| Heat and storage | How to store products and use gear in summer heat |
| Use and care | Cleaning, sterilising and washing instructions |
| Delivery and returns | Dispatch times, emirates covered, which items cannot be returned once opened |

Build an Arabic version of the store with a proper right-to-left layout, and have copy written or reviewed by a fluent Arabic writer. Parent reviews that mention fit, ease of use and delivery are more useful than star ratings alone. ### Photos and video parents trust

Show products in use in homes and cars that look like the UAE, with safe sleep and car seat positions in every image. A short video of a pram folding, a car seat fitting or a bottle being assembled answers questions faster than text. Add close-ups of fabric, seams and labels, and show the Arabic side of the pack as well as the English.

## Step 4: Choose your UAE sales channels

### Your own Shopify store

Your store is where subscriptions, gift options, bundles and customer data live. List prices in AED with VAT included, accept payment on delivery, wallets and Tabby or Tamara, and put a WhatsApp contact where a worried parent can find it.

### Amazon.ae and Noon

Many parents buy essentials on Amazon.ae and Noon, and both can need category approval and product documents for baby items. Keep prices aligned with your own store and watch for unauthorised sellers.

### Quick commerce

Nappies, wipes, toiletries and feeding basics such as bibs and bowls often run out at the worst moment. Talabat and Careem quick commerce reach parents who need them within the hour. Access usually comes through a distributor or retail partner.

### Other routes

Baby and children's clothing and shoes may fit fashion platforms such as Namshi. Specialist retailers such as Mumzworld have their own buying process and can be a useful route once your direct sales are established.

## Step 5: Market responsibly to parents

Platform policies and UAE media rules do not allow ads aimed at children. In general:

- Aim ads at adults: parents, grandparents, relatives and gift buyers.
- Avoid copy that guesses a personal situation, such as "Just found out you are pregnant?". Speak to the moment instead: "Everything for the first weeks home".
- Use broad audiences with strong creative, and your own consented customer lists.
- Confirm every paid creator has a current UAE advertiser permit and tags sponsored posts plainly.
- Keep breast-milk substitutes out of all promotional content.
- Avoid images that show unsafe sleep positions, loose bedding in cots or unsupervised bath time.

Write Arabic and English creative together rather than translating at the end, and use settings that look like UAE homes.

## Step 6: Handle parents' data with care

Parents share sensitive details: due dates, a baby's age, sometimes health notes. The UAE has a federal personal data protection law, and some free zones have their own rules. Collect only what you need, ask for clear consent for WhatsApp, SMS and email marketing, and make it easy to opt out. If a family tells you about a loss, stop pregnancy and baby emails immediately.

## Step 7: Plan repeat orders, life stages and gifting

### Repeat essentials

Nappies, wipes and toiletries are bought every few weeks. Offer subscriptions with easy skip and pause, and send a reminder before a pack is likely to run out.

### Life stages

Babies change size fast. With consent, send next-size and next-stage messages: the move to the next nappy size, the first spoon, the first shoes.

### Gifting for births

In the UAE, a birth sets off a wave of presents: from colleagues, neighbours, aunts and grandparents. Large expatriate families often have relatives in other countries who order online for delivery here. Offer ready-made gift sets at a few sizes, wrapping, a card message and delivery to homes and hospitals, with cut-off times shown on the product page.

## Step 8: Plan operations around the UAE calendar

| Period | What happens | What to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| Ramadan | Shopping moves later in the day; families prepare for gatherings | Evening ad schedules, essentials bundles |
| Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha | New clothes and gifts for children, family visits | Gift sets, outfits, delivery cut-offs |
| Summer | Heat and travel; many families away | Travel packs, heat-safe storage, lighter spend |
| Late August | Back to school for older siblings | Lunch boxes, bottles, bundles for whole families |
| November to January | White Friday, UAE National Day, Dubai Shopping Festival | Big-ticket gear, gift sets, stock booked early |

## Common mistakes when selling baby products online

- **Ordering stock before conformity is confirmed.** A container held at the port, or goods that cannot be listed, ties up cash for months.
- **Translating labels and pages at the last minute.** Parents notice awkward Arabic, and errors in warnings or age guidance are a safety issue, not a style issue.
- **Promoting formula alongside everything else.** A bundle or discount that includes a breast-milk substitute can breach the rules even if it was meant kindly.
- **Treating gift buyers like parents.** Gift buyers need sets, wrapping and delivery dates, not size charts and refill offers.
- **Ignoring summer heat.** Toiletries, snacks and some materials suffer in a hot van, and parents remember a melted or spoiled delivery.
- **Chasing cheap first orders.** In this category, trust and repeat purchases decide whether the business works.
