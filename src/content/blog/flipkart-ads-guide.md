This Flipkart ads guide explains how sellers can use Flipkart's advertising tools to get more visibility and orders without burning money. Flipkart is one of India's largest marketplaces, and on busy categories your product can sit pages deep in search results even when it is a good product at a fair price. Ads are the fastest way to move it up. Used well, they bring in orders and build the sales history that helps your organic ranking. Used badly, they quietly eat your margin.

Retail and marketplace advertising is growing fast in India. E-retail ad spends in India rose about 56% in 2025 (dentsu-e4m report). That means more sellers are bidding for the same shoppers, so a clear method matters more every year. This guide covers formats, setup, budgets, targeting, sale events and how to measure real profit.

## How Flipkart ads work

Flipkart ads are run from Seller Hub. You choose which products to promote, set a budget and bids, and Flipkart shows your products in sponsored positions. In most formats you pay when a shopper clicks. The auction considers your bid along with relevance and listing quality, so the highest bid does not always win.

Flipkart updates its ad products, placements, minimum budgets and bidding rules from time to time. Treat this guide as a working method, and confirm the current options and terms inside Seller Hub before you commit budget.

## Flipkart ad formats in general terms

### Product listing ads (PLA)

Flipkart PLA ads are the core format for most sellers. They promote a specific listing in search results, on category pages and on other product pages, with a small "Ad" marker. Because shoppers see them while actively searching, they are usually the best place to start. If you sell a few hundred SKUs, most of your ad spend will likely sit here.

### Brand and display ads

Brand-level and display formats show your brand, banner or a group of products in more visible spots, such as the top of search results, category pages or the home page. They are useful for brand recall, launches and sale events, and less about direct, trackable orders. They tend to suit established brands with a recognisable name and enough budget to run both PLA and brand campaigns.

### Which to start with

Start with product listing ads on your best sellers. Add brand or display formats once your PLA campaigns are profitable and you have a clear reason, such as a launch, a new range or a major sale.

## Listing quality comes first

Ads send shoppers to your listing. If the listing does not convert, every click is wasted. Before you spend on Flipkart ads for sellers, check each promoted product against this list:

- **Title:** clear product name, key attribute (size, material, quantity) and the words shoppers actually search.
- **Images:** a clean main image, plus lifestyle, size, detail and packaging shots. Most shoppers browse on mobile, so test how images look on a phone.
- **Price and offers:** competitive with similar products, with any bank or coupon offers set up.
- **Ratings and reviews:** products with very few or poor ratings struggle to convert even with top placement.
- **Stock and delivery:** in stock, with a reasonable delivery promise. Ads on low-stock items cause stock-outs and hurt ranking.
- **Description and specifications:** complete, accurate attributes so the product appears in filtered searches.

A strong listing also lowers your effective cost, because a product that converts well usually needs fewer clicks per order.

## Campaign setup, step by step

Here is how to run ads on Flipkart in a structured way:

1. **Pick the products.** Choose a handful of in-stock, well-rated products with healthy margins. Do not promote your entire catalogue on day one.
2. **Group similar products.** Keep products of the same type and price range in the same campaign so results are easy to read.
3. **Set the goal.** Decide whether the campaign is for profit, for a launch (where you accept lower returns for a period) or for clearing stock.
4. **Choose targeting.** Start with automatic targeting to discover search terms, and add manual campaigns as data builds up.
5. **Set budget and bids.** Use a daily budget you can sustain for a few weeks. Start bids moderately and adjust based on data.
6. **Name campaigns clearly.** For example, "Kurtas – Auto – Bestsellers" or "Kurtas – Manual – Exact". Clear names save time later.
7. **Review on a schedule.** Check every two or three days at first, then weekly once campaigns settle.

## Budgets and bidding

Budget and bids decide how often you win placements and what each click costs.

- **Budget:** give each new campaign enough budget to collect meaningful data, usually two to four weeks of steady spend. Very small budgets run out early in the day and tell you little.
- **Bids:** bid higher on proven, high-converting terms and lower on broad or experimental ones. If a product sells well organically, you may not need high bids on its main keyword.
- **Placements:** some placements, such as top of search, often convert better but cost more. Compare placement performance where Seller Hub reports it.
- **Scaling:** raise budgets gradually on profitable campaigns. Large overnight jumps often push spend into weaker placements and search terms.

Check Flipkart's current minimum daily budgets, bid limits and billing method in Seller Hub. These details change, and we do not quote them here.

## Keyword vs automatic targeting

**Automatic targeting** lets Flipkart decide where to show your product based on the listing content. It is good for discovery and needs little setup. The downside is less control: some spend will go to irrelevant searches.

**Keyword (manual) targeting** lets you choose the search terms and set bids for each. It gives more control and usually better efficiency once you know which terms convert.

A practical workflow for Flipkart PLA ads:

1. Run an automatic campaign for two to three weeks.
2. Download the search term or keyword report.
3. Move terms that bring orders at a good cost into a manual campaign with their own bids.
4. Add negative keywords (where available) for terms that get clicks but no orders, or are clearly irrelevant.
5. Keep the automatic campaign running at a lower budget to keep finding new terms.

## Planning ads for Big Billion Days and other sales

Flipkart's sale calendar shapes the year for many sellers. The Big Billion Days sale around the festive season is the biggest, and there are other sale events across the year, such as end-of-season and category sales. Dates and seller participation rules vary each year, so watch Seller Hub announcements.

**Three to six weeks before:**
- Confirm stock levels and inbound shipments for your top products.
- Finalise prices, offers and any deal participation.
- Fix listing gaps: images, titles, attributes, size charts.
- Increase ads gradually to build sales history and reviews.

**During the sale:**
- Raise budgets, because traffic and competition both rise and click costs usually go up.
- Check campaigns several times a day. A budget that runs out by noon wastes the evening peak.
- Pause ads on products that are about to go out of stock.

**After the sale:**
- Bring budgets back down in steps rather than all at once.
- Review which products and search terms drove profitable orders, and use that to plan the next event.

## Measuring ROI the right way

Flipkart reports metrics such as impressions, clicks, spend, orders, revenue and ROI or ROAS (revenue divided by ad spend). These are useful, but they do not tell you whether you made money.

To judge real profit, look at each product's unit economics:

- Selling price
- Product cost and packaging
- Marketplace fees and commissions (check your current rate card in Seller Hub)
- Shipping and fulfilment costs
- Returns and RTO losses
- Ad cost per order

Only after all these costs can you set a break-even ROAS for each product. A product with thin margins needs a much higher ROAS to stay profitable than a high-margin one. Our free [ROAS calculator](/tools/roas-calculator/) helps you work out break-even and target ROAS before you set bids.

Also track the wider effect. Ads can lift organic sales by improving sales velocity and reviews. Compare total sales and total ad spend (often called TACoS on Amazon) over time, not just ad-attributed revenue.

## Common Flipkart ads mistakes

- **Advertising weak listings.** Poor images, few reviews or an uncompetitive price waste clicks.
- **Promoting everything at once.** Budget spreads thin and you cannot tell what works.
- **Ignoring search term reports.** Irrelevant terms keep draining spend.
- **Judging on ROAS without margins.** A "good" ROAS can still lose money after fees and returns.
- **Changing too much too often.** Daily bid swings stop campaigns from settling.
- **Running out of stock mid-campaign.** It wastes spend and can hurt your ranking.
- **Last-minute sale prep.** Starting ads on the day of a big sale means competing without history.
