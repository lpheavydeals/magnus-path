Working out how to sell supplements online UK shoppers will trust takes more than a good formula and a Shopify theme. Vitamins, protein, gummies and botanical blends are treated as foods in Great Britain, yet the claims you can make about them are tightly limited, marketplaces check paperwork before you list, and ad platforms treat the category with caution. This guide sets out the steps in the order most founders meet them, from registration to the second order.

A quick note before we start: we run ecommerce growth, we do not give legal or regulatory advice. Use the rules sections below to get your bearings, then confirm your own position with a qualified adviser, a trading standards officer or your local authority.

## Step 1: Know which rules you are working under

Food supplements in the UK are regulated as food. In broad terms, that means several sets of rules overlap:

- **Food supplement regulations** in each nation of the UK, which cover which vitamin and mineral forms may be used and what the label must say.
- **Food information rules**, which govern ingredient lists, allergen emphasis, net quantity and date marking.
- **The GB nutrition and health claims register**, which lists the health claims a food can carry and the conditions attached to each.
- **Novel food rules**, which apply to ingredients without a history of significant consumption before 1997. CBD is the example most people know.
- **Advertising rules** in the CAP code, enforced by the Advertising Standards Authority, which apply to ads, social posts, influencer content and your own website.

If a product makes medicinal claims, or contains an ingredient at a level that acts on the body like a drug, it may be treated as a medicine by the MHRA instead. That boundary is worth checking before you design packs. Northern Ireland follows some EU food rules, so if you ship there, check what differs.

## Step 2: Register your business and organise your documents

Anyone who makes, packs, imports, stores or sells food as a business is generally expected to register with their local authority, and the usual guidance is to do this before trading starts. It is normally free and applies to online-only and home-based sellers too.

Common setups for a new UK brand:

- **Contract manufacturing.** A UK manufacturer makes and packs your product under its own registration. You register your business as the brand owner and seller.
- **Importing.** If you bring finished supplements in from abroad, you take on responsibility for the label meeting UK rules, and you may need to register as an importer.
- **Own production.** Your premises will be registered and inspected like any other food business.

Create one shared folder that holds your registration confirmation, your manufacturer's details, specifications, certificates of analysis for each batch and any third-party test reports. Marketplaces, retailers and the occasional curious customer will ask for them.

## Step 3: Get the formulation and label right

Think of the pack as the master copy for all of your marketing. If a claim is not on the pack and not supported by the register, it does not belong in an ad, a listing or a creator brief.

### What a UK supplement label usually needs

- The name "food supplement" and the category of nutrients or substances that characterise the product.
- A full ingredient list with allergens emphasised.
- The amount of each vitamin, mineral or other active per daily dose, with the percentage of the nutrient reference value where one exists.
- The recommended daily dose and a warning not to exceed it.
- A statement that supplements should not replace a varied diet.
- A warning to keep the product out of reach of young children.
- Net quantity, a date mark, batch code, storage advice and the name and UK address of the responsible business.

### Claims to treat with care

Health claims must match authorised wording closely, and the product must meet the claim's conditions. Disease language is out entirely: nothing should treat, cure, prevent or heal. Be wary of vague words such as "detox", "immunity booster" or "fat burner". General phrases about wellbeing usually need a specific authorised claim beside them. Plain descriptions of what is in the pot and how to take it are safer and often more persuasive.

## Step 4: Build product pages that answer sceptical buyers

UK supplement buyers have read about under-dosed tubs and inflated promises. A strong page settles their doubts in the first scroll.

| Page element | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Front and back label images | Shows you have nothing to hide and helps marketplace reviewers |
| Ingredients and amounts as text | Readable on mobile and by search engines and AI assistants |
| Form of each nutrient | Buyers compare, for example, magnesium citrate against glycinate |
| Servings per pack | Lets buyers work out cost per day themselves |
| Batch test reports | Answers quality questions before they are asked |
| Who should check first | Pregnancy, prescribed medicines, under-18s: advise speaking to a GP or pharmacist |
| Taste, size and format | Capsule size and flavour drive reviews and repeat orders |

Reviews that talk about taste, routine and delivery are useful. Reviews that describe health outcomes become your claims if you republish them in ads, so moderate what you reuse. ## Step 5: Choose your UK sales channels

Most growing brands use several channels, each with a clear job.

### Your own store

A Shopify or WooCommerce site is the one channel where you own the customer list, set subscription terms and keep the full margin when someone reorders. Put your test reports, ingredient explainers and founder story here. Prices should be shown including VAT, and checkout should support the payment methods UK shoppers expect, including card wallets and, if you choose, a buy-now-pay-later option.

### Amazon UK

Many buyers line up supplements side by side on Amazon UK before they buy. Expect category checks that can include label images and documents, so leave a few weeks in the launch plan for it. After approval, use Brand Registry, check who else sells your products and act quickly on unauthorised or out-of-date stock.

### TikTok Shop UK

Creator videos can move gummies, electrolytes and trial packs quickly. TikTok restricts some health products and claims, and creators are bound by the same rules as you. Give them a written brief listing the claims they may use and the ones they may not.

### eBay and OnBuy

Both can add reach for established ranges and clearance lines. Keep pricing aligned with your other channels so you are not undercutting your own store.

High-street retailers such as Holland & Barrett are a different route with their own buying process. Many brands build an online sales record first and approach retail later.

## Step 6: Advertise within the CAP code and platform rules

Supplement ads are allowed on Meta, Google, TikTok and Amazon, with limits. Policies change, so check them before each campaign. In general:

- Ads must not suggest a supplement prevents, treats or cures a disease, or imply that a normal diet cannot provide what people need.
- Weight-control products face extra restrictions and age targeting rules on most platforms.
- Before-and-after images and body-focused copy attract rejections and complaints.
- Paid creator content must be labelled clearly as advertising, for example with "#ad".
- The landing page must match the ad, because platforms and the ASA look at both.

Build creative around ingredients, format, taste, routine and convenience: the morning glass, the gym bag, the travel pouch.

## Step 7: Plan subscriptions and refills from day one

Supplements come with a built-in reorder date. Divide the servings in a pot by the daily dose and you can predict, within a few days, when a customer needs the next one.

- Send a reminder by email or SMS a few days before the pot is likely to be empty, with consent captured at checkout.
- Offer subscribe-and-save with a simple way to skip, pause or cancel online.
- Write subscription terms plainly. UK consumer law already expects clear renewal and cancellation terms, and new subscription rules are being phased in, so check current requirements.
- Suggest larger packs once a customer has reordered twice.
- Ask for a review after a few weeks of use rather than the day after delivery.

## Step 8: Sort out operations, returns and the UK calendar

- **Returns.** Distance selling rules generally give buyers a cancellation window, but sealed health products can lose that right once opened. State this before purchase.
- **Shelf life.** Record date marks against each batch, and keep stock with little life left out of Amazon fulfilment centres, which set their own minimums.
- **Delivery.** Royal Mail, Evri and DPD all suit small parcels; letterbox-friendly packaging can lower costs and missed deliveries.
- **Seasons.** January resolutions bring the biggest spike for many ranges. Spring brings a second wave, autumn turns attention to vitamin D, and the weeks from Black Friday to Christmas reward gift boxes and multi-packs. Have stock and approved creative ready weeks ahead.

## How to sell supplements online UK: launch checklist

- [ ] Food business registered with your local authority.
- [ ] Formulation checked for permitted forms and novel food status.
- [ ] Label reviewed for mandatory wording, allergens and date marks.
- [ ] Every claim matched to the GB register and its conditions.
- [ ] Batch certificates and test reports filed in one place.
- [ ] Product pages with label images, ingredient text and clear advice on who should check first.
- [ ] Amazon UK category approval requested with documents ready.
- [ ] Ad copy and creator briefs checked against the CAP code and platform policies.
- [ ] Returns wording for sealed goods shown before checkout.
- [ ] Refill reminders and subscriptions live, with consent recorded.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Copying a competitor's claims.** Their risk does not become your safe harbour.
- **Launching on every channel at once.** Prove the product on your site and one marketplace before you spread budget thinner.
- **Ignoring margin per order.** Count marketplace fees, fulfilment, VAT, returns and ad costs.
- **Judging success on January alone.** The second and third orders decide whether the business works.

Knowing how to sell supplements online in the UK is mostly about doing the careful groundwork first: registration, formulation, label, claims, then channels and ads. Brands that put that base in place scale with fewer rejected ads, listing removals and complaints.
