Learning how to sell agarbatti online starts with a fact every incense maker knows: buyers cannot smell your sticks through a screen. In a shop they lift a pack, sniff it and decide. Online they rely on your words, your reviews and your brand. This guide is written for agarbatti, dhoop and sandalwood product makers, many of them in the Bengaluru and Mysuru clusters, who want to sell online across India and abroad. It covers honest fragrance and ingredient copy, the Mysore sandalwood heritage, labelling basics, packaging and shipping, festival seasons, buyers, marketplaces and exports.

We are an ecommerce growth team, not lawyers or standards consultants. Check regulatory points with a qualified adviser.

## Why Karnataka's incense makers have an edge online

Bengaluru and Mysuru have long been centres of agarbatti making, with large manufacturers alongside thousands of small family units that roll, dip and pack sticks. Mysore's sandalwood heritage is known across India, and some Mysore sandalwood and agarbatti products are registered as geographical indications.

That heritage helps online, but only when it is told truthfully. Buyers can now compare dozens of brands in seconds, so a clear story about who makes your sticks, where and how is a real reason to choose you.

## Step 1: Decide your range

| Product | What buyers expect | Online notes |
|---|---|---|
| Agarbatti (incense sticks) | Daily pooja, home fragrance | Core range; sell by fragrance and pack size |
| Dhoop sticks and cones | Stronger, smokier scent | Fragile; needs firm packing |
| Sambrani and cups | Traditional fumigation and pooja | Explain how to use; check any flammable parts |
| Sandalwood powder and paste | Pooja and personal use | Honest sourcing and purity details |
| Gift boxes and pooja kits | Festivals, housewarmings, corporate gifts | Strong in festival seasons |
| Fragrance oils and attars | Diffusers, personal fragrance | Often dangerous goods for shipping |

Start with a focused range: a handful of fragrances that sell steadily, two or three pack sizes and one gift box. Add festival specials once the core range sells online.

## Step 2: Write honest fragrance and ingredient descriptions

Incense buyers increasingly ask what they are breathing in. Honest descriptions build trust and reduce complaints.

### Describe the fragrance in plain words

Name the main scent (sandalwood, rose, jasmine, champa, lavender, guggul, loban) and describe it simply: soft and woody, sweet and floral, smoky and resinous. Say whether it is light enough for a small flat or strong enough for a large pooja room.

### Be clear about ingredients

Say whether the scent comes from natural materials, a fragrance blend or both. If a "sandalwood" stick uses a sandalwood-type fragrance rather than real sandalwood, say so. State whether the stick uses charcoal, and whether it has a bamboo core. Avoid health, spiritual or healing claims in product copy and ads; describe the fragrance, the craft and the occasion.

### Give pack facts that help comparison

List the stick count, net weight, approximate burn time per stick and the pack format. Buyers comparing marketplace listings look for these first.

## Step 3: Sandalwood products and sourcing

Sandalwood is precious and has long been regulated in India. If you sell sandalwood powder, oil, paste or sticks with real sandalwood, keep records of where and how it was sourced, and buy only from suppliers who can show the wood or oil was obtained legally. Exports of sandalwood products can need specific permissions, so check before you list abroad.

On product pages, say what share of a product is sandalwood if you claim it, and do not call a blend "pure". If you want to use a name protected as a geographical indication, such as a Mysore sandalwood name, take legal advice first. Your Karnataka location is still a fair and useful part of your story.

## Step 4: Labelling basics

Agarbatti and dhoop are sold as packaged commodities, so legal metrology rules apply. Packs usually need:

- Product name and the manufacturer, packer or importer's name and address
- Net quantity by weight or number of sticks
- MRP inclusive of taxes
- Month and year of manufacture or packing
- A customer care phone number or email address

BIS has published an Indian Standard for agarbatti that covers safety aspects, including chemicals that should not be used. Ask an adviser whether it applies to your products. Add simple safety guidance on packs and listings: burn on a heat-proof holder, keep away from children and curtains, and never leave burning sticks unattended.

## Step 5: Packaging and shipping

### Protect fragile products

Loose paper sleeves are fine on a shop shelf but often break in courier sorting. Use rigid tubes or boxes, fill any empty space, and pack dhoop cones and cups in trays. Send test parcels to friends in several cities before launch and fix whatever arrives broken.

### Fragrance and freshness

Seal packs or wrap them in film so the fragrance does not fade and nearby items do not pick up the scent. Tell buyers how to store sticks once opened.

### Flammability and dangerous goods

Plain sticks and dhoop are usually accepted by couriers, but kits with matches or lighters, liquid fragrance oils and attars can fall under dangerous-goods rules, particularly by air and for exports. Get each courier's and marketplace's rules in writing before you list these items.

Agarbatti packs are light but bulky, so shipping cost can matter more than you expect. Our [free shipping threshold calculator](/tools/free-shipping-threshold-calculator/) helps you set a minimum order that protects margin.

### Price per stick, not only per pack

Buyers compare agarbatti by price per stick and burn time, so check your pricing against those numbers as well as the pack price. Include rolling, perfuming, drying, packaging, breakage, shipping and marketplace fees in your costing, and keep larger value packs for daily users who reorder.

## Step 6: Know your buyers

- **Daily pooja households.** They buy the same fragrance again and again. Subscriptions, combo packs and refill reminders keep them with you.
- **Temples, mutts and pooja stores.** Bulk and wholesale buyers who care about consistent supply, pack sizes and invoicing. A simple wholesale enquiry page on your store helps.
- **Gifting buyers.** Housewarmings, weddings, festivals and corporate gifts. Gift boxes with a few fragrances and a holder do well.
- **Home fragrance and wellness buyers.** Younger urban buyers who use incense for mood and meditation, often found on Instagram and through creators.

## How to sell agarbatti online through the right channels

| Channel | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Your own store | Full range, gift boxes, subscriptions, wholesale enquiries | You drive all the traffic |
| Amazon and Flipkart | Search-led demand for agarbatti and pooja items | Price competition, breakage returns |
| Quick commerce | Everyday packs in larger cities | Margins and dark-store stock planning |
| Instagram and WhatsApp | Discovery, festival offers, repeat orders | Needs a clear path to checkout |
| Etsy and marketplaces abroad | Diaspora and natural-incense buyers | Import rules and shipping costs |

On marketplaces, put fragrance, stick count and weight in titles, and use images that show the sticks, the pack and the burning stick in a real home. Collect reviews that mention fragrance strength and burn time.

### Selling abroad

Indians in the UK, USA, UAE, Singapore and Australia buy agarbatti for festivals and daily pooja, and natural incense has buyers well beyond the diaspora. Start with one channel, such as Etsy or your own store shipping abroad; our guide on [how to sell on Etsy from India](/blog/how-to-sell-on-etsy-from-india/) explains the set-up. Check each country's import, labelling and safety rules before sending stock.

## Step 7: Plan around festival seasons

Agarbatti demand rises before Ugadi, Varamahalakshmi, Ganesha Chaturthi, Navratri and Mysuru Dasara, Deepavali and the wedding season, with regional festivals adding peaks elsewhere in India. Check our [sale calendar](/resources/sale-calendar/) for upcoming dates. Production takes time because sticks need rolling, drying, perfuming and packing, so plan stock and gift boxes well before each peak and start ads a few weeks ahead.

## Common mistakes

- Calling a fragrance blend "pure sandalwood" or using a protected name without advice
- Shipping sticks in soft sleeves that break in transit
- Listing without stick count, weight or burn time, so buyers cannot compare
- Leaving festival stock until the last few weeks, when production cannot keep up
- Adding matches or oils to kits without checking courier rules

## Launch checklist

- Focused range with clear fragrances, pack sizes and one gift box
- Honest descriptions of fragrance, ingredients and sandalwood content
- Legal metrology details on every pack, BIS standard checked with an adviser
- Sandalwood sourcing records kept and protected names checked
- Breakage-tested packaging and courier rules for oils and matches confirmed
- Own store plus Amazon or Flipkart, with subscriptions for daily users
- Festival stock and ads planned weeks ahead
