If you are working out how to sell tea online, the Nilgiris give you a story many brands would envy. Tea has been grown on these hills for well over a century, the region's orthodox tea carries a GI tag, and Coimbatore, at the foot of the hills, gives easy access to packaging suppliers, couriers and the airport. The challenge is that buyers see many teas claiming the same hills, most of them hard to tell apart in a thumbnail. This guide covers origin claims, orthodox and CTC, blends, FSSAI labelling, freshness, subscriptions, gifting, marketplaces, Tea Board registration and exports.

We are an ecommerce growth team, not food-safety consultants or lawyers. Confirm licence, labelling and export points with a qualified adviser.

## Why selling Nilgiris tea online is different

Tea looks like an easy online product: light, long-lasting and bought every day. In practice:

- **Origin claims are everywhere.** Many packs say "Nilgiris" or "estate tea" without detail. Buyers have learned to be sceptical.
- **Most Indians drink milk chai.** A delicate orthodox leaf tea is lost under milk and sugar, so the product has to match how the buyer drinks.
- **Order values are low.** One pack rarely covers ads and delivery. Subscriptions, bundles and gifting make the numbers work.
- **Freshness matters more than people think.** Tea that sits for months loses aroma, and buyers notice.

## Step 1: Understand your tea, orthodox or CTC

Much of the Nilgiris crop is made as CTC (crush, tear, curl), which brews fast and strong for milk chai. Orthodox tea is rolled leaf, brewing a brighter, more aromatic cup that many drink without milk. The Nilgiris are also known for teas made in the cool winter months, which some buyers seek out.

| Tea | Typical buyer | Main channels |
|---|---|---|
| CTC blends | Families making daily milk chai | Quick commerce, Amazon, Flipkart, own store |
| Orthodox leaf | Buyers who drink black tea or care about origin | Own store, subscriptions, gifting |
| Green and white teas | Curious buyers trying something lighter | Own store, sampler packs |
| Spiced and herbal blends | Masala chai lovers, gift buyers | Own store, gift boxes, marketplaces |
| Seasonal and single-estate lots | Enthusiasts and collectors | Own store, small batches, export |

Pick one or two lines to lead with. A brand that does strong daily chai well can add a leaf range later; doing everything at launch blurs the message.

## Step 2: Make honest origin claims

Nilgiris orthodox tea is a registered Geographical Indication, with its own logo for authorised use. Treat it as context and use it only where it applies.

1. **Name the source where you can.** The estate, the bought-leaf factory or the auction lot, and the area of the hills.
2. **State blend shares honestly.** If a blend mixes Nilgiris tea with tea from Assam or elsewhere, say so. "Made with Nilgiris tea" and "100% Nilgiris tea" mean different things.
3. **Keep invoices and lot details.** Buyers, marketplaces and export partners sometimes ask.
4. **Avoid health claims entirely.** Describe taste, aroma, strength and how to brew. Medical claims create legal risk and get listings rejected.
5. **Show the hills honestly.** Photos and short videos of the estate or factory, used with permission, beat stock images of somewhere else.

## Step 3: Blends, packaging and freshness

### Building blends

- Keep a fixed recipe for each blend, so a repeat buyer gets the same cup every time.
- Test blends with real buyers: the way your family makes chai is not the way everyone does.
- For spiced blends, list every ingredient and its form, such as dried ginger or whole cardamom.
- Give each blend a short brewing guide: water, time, milk or not.

### Packaging for freshness

- Use airtight, light-proof pouches with a zip lock, or tins for premium and gift lines.
- Print the packing date next to the date mark, so buyers can see how fresh the stock is.
- Buy recent lots and keep stock lean, so tea does not sit for months.
- Store and pack away from strong-smelling goods; tea absorbs odours.
- Offer smaller packs of leaf teas that are drunk slowly.

## Step 4: FSSAI, labels and Tea Board registration

Before you list:

- **FSSAI registration or licence** matching your size and activity, with the number on every pack and listing.
- **A complete label**: product name, ingredients for blends, net quantity, packing date, a use-by or date mark, batch number, packer address, FSSAI logo and number, MRP and customer-care details.
- **Legal metrology declarations** on packs and marketplace listings.
- **GST registration** for marketplaces and quick commerce.
- **Tea Board of India**: depending on your activity, registration may apply, especially if you export. Check the current rules with the Tea Board.

Have an adviser check your label artwork before printing.

## Step 5: How to sell tea online across your channels

### Your own store

Your Shopify or WooCommerce store is where the origin story, brewing guides, subscriptions and gift boxes live. It is also where you can capture buyers' WhatsApp numbers with consent, and remind them before they run out.

### Subscriptions

Tea is a daily habit, which makes it a natural fit for subscriptions. Offer a simple plan with an easy skip or pause, a small benefit such as free delivery, and a reminder before each dispatch. Seasonal leaf teas can work as a limited subscription box for enthusiasts.

### Gifting

Tea is a popular gift for Deepavali, Pongal, weddings and corporate events. Build gift boxes at a few clear price points, with a sampler of blends, a brewing card and sturdy outer packaging. For corporate orders, add a simple bulk enquiry form with branding options and lead times.

### Marketplaces and quick commerce

Amazon and Flipkart bring buyers searching for "Nilgiri tea" or "CTC tea 1 kg". Quick commerce suits everyday chai blends that households reorder. ## Step 6: Marketing that tastes of the hills

- **Short videos** from the estate or factory, of leaf being plucked, withered or sorted, used with permission.
- **Brewing reels**: the right way to make a strong Nilgiris chai, or a clear cup of orthodox leaf.
- **Tamil and English content**, with every Tamil line written or checked by a fluent Tamil writer, and English for city and diaspora buyers.

## Reviews, samplers and repeat buyers

Tea buyers rarely commit to a large pack of a tea they have not tasted. A sampler of three or four small packs lets them find a favourite, and a note inside points them to the full-size version. Ask for a review a couple of weeks after delivery, once they have brewed a few cups, and ask what milk, sugar and brewing time they used, because that tells you how to describe each blend. Reply to critical reviews about strength or aroma with a brewing tip rather than a defence. Then time WhatsApp or email reminders to when a pack usually runs out, and offer the subscription at that moment, when the habit is already formed.

## Step 7: Exports and diaspora buyers

Indian families in the UK, US, UAE, Singapore and Australia often look for the tea they grew up with, and specialty buyers abroad know the Nilgiris name.

1. Get an Importer-Exporter Code and check Tea Board registration for exporters.
2. Check the importing country's food rules, including residue limits and labelling language.
3. Start small: your own store with international shipping, or Amazon in one country. Our guide on [selling on Amazon USA from India](/blog/how-to-sell-on-amazon-usa-from-india/) covers one route.
4. State delivery times and who pays duty clearly.
