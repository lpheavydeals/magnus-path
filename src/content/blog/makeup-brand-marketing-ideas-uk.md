The makeup brand marketing ideas UK brands get the most from all tackle the same problem: shoppers cannot test a foundation through a screen. They guess a shade, get it wrong and either send it back or stick with the brand they already know. Add a sceptical audience that spots filters instantly, a regulator that has acted on misleading beauty ads and a calendar packed with gifting moments, and you have a market that rewards honesty and planning. Here are fifteen ideas, grouped by what they solve, plus how to choose and measure them.

## Makeup brand marketing ideas to help buyers choose the right shade

### 1. Show every shade on many skin tones

Photograph and film each shade on several skin tones, from very fair to very deep, in natural light and without filters or retouching. Show the same shade on different undertones so buyers can see how it shifts.

Use the same lighting, camera and framing for every swatch so shades can be compared fairly. Add an arm swatch, a face swatch and a short clip of the product being blended. Grey British daylight is fine; it is closer to how most buyers will see their makeup than a ring light.

### 2. Explain undertones in plain English

Many buyers do not know if they are warm, cool or neutral. A short guide with simple tests, such as how silver and gold jewellery look against the skin, helps them narrow the range before choosing. Name shades in a way that hints at depth and undertone, so a buyer can tell at a glance whether a shade is light, medium or deep and warm, cool or neutral.

### 3. Add a shade finder

A simple quiz or a "matches your current shade from brand X" tool reduces guesswork. Keep it honest: if you are unsure of a match, say so and suggest a sample.

### 4. Offer sample cards and minis

Paid sample cards or deluxe minis let buyers try two or three shades before committing to a full size. Credit the sample cost against a full-size order to encourage the next step.

## Ideas for creators and content

### 5. Build a steady creator roster

Work with a small group of creators over months rather than one-off posts. Repeated, genuine use builds more trust than a single glowing review.

Agree a simple monthly rhythm: one tutorial, one wear test and a story set around launches. Give creators early access to new shades and ask for honest feedback before launch, not just content after it. Track each creator by sales through their code or link, return rate on their orders and the comments their posts generate.

### 6. Insist on unfiltered content

Brief every creator in writing: no filters or retouching on footage showing your product, and clear ad labels on paid or gifted posts. The ASA has acted against filtered beauty ads, and viewers notice too.

### 7. Run wear tests and get-ready-with-me videos

Show the product at application, after a commute in the rain and at the end of a long day. Wear tests answer the question every buyer has: does it last?

### 8. Reflect the UK's range of looks and ages

Include creators across skin tones, ages and styles, from everyday office makeup to festival looks, prom and wedding guests. Buyers want to see someone like themselves using the product.

## Ideas for launches and paid ads

### 9. Treat launches as drops

Build anticipation with texture teasers and a waitlist, give past buyers early access and launch across your store, TikTok Shop and ads on the same day. A drop with a clear date gets more attention than a quiet listing. Check stock by shade before launch day, and be honest if something sells out: a clear restock date keeps buyers waiting for you rather than buying elsewhere.

### 10. Put creator content into Meta and TikTok ads

With the creator's permission, run their tutorials as ads. Real faces and real application often feel more convincing than glossy studio shots. Keep ad labels and claims intact.

### 11. Use TikTok Shop live selling

Live sessions let you show shades on several people, answer questions in real time and offer launch bundles. Pair them with affiliate creators who earn commission on sales.

Keep a host who knows the range well, have two or three models with different skin tones on camera, and prepare answers on ingredients, wear time and returns. Run sessions at times your buyers are free, often weekday evenings, and repeat the formats that hold viewers.

### 12. Capture search on Google and Amazon UK

Bid on specific searches such as "foundation for olive skin" or "long-wear concealer for mature skin". Use accurate shade names and swatch images in Google Shopping and on Amazon UK listings.

## Ideas for seasons, gifting and repeat orders

### 13. Plan gifting around the UK calendar

Map your year: January sales, Valentine's Day, Mother's Day in March, prom and festival season in early summer, Halloween, then Black Friday, Christmas and Boxing Day. Sets, advent calendars and minis need planning many months ahead, because components, filling and gift packaging all have long lead times. Publish last order dates for Christmas delivery by courier, and keep a gift page live through December for late shoppers. ### 14. Remember each buyer's shade

Store the shades each customer buys and use them in emails: "Your shade is back in stock" or "A concealer that matches your foundation". It makes reordering easy and cross-selling relevant. Show the saved shade in the customer's account too, so a reorder takes seconds rather than another round of guessing.

### 15. Reward reorders and reviews

Loyalty points for reviews with photos, refills and second purchases do more for growth than discounts on every order. Ask for a review once someone has worn the product for a week or two.

## Ideas for building a community

A makeup brand with a community spends less to win each buyer, because customers recommend it for you. A few ways to start:

- **A private group or close-friends list** for your most engaged buyers, with previews of new shades and a say in what comes next.
- **Customer looks on product pages**, shared with permission and unfiltered, so new buyers see the product on real people.
- **Pop-ups and masterclasses** in UK cities, even small ones, where people can try shades in person and meet the founders.
- **Shade requests**, where buyers vote on the next shade or finish. It turns launches into something customers feel part of.

## Your own website: small changes with big effects

Before scaling ads, check that your Shopify store:

- Shows swatches on several skin tones on every shade page.
- Lets buyers filter by undertone, finish and coverage.
- Has a short video of each product being applied.
- Shows VAT-inclusive prices, delivery options and returns terms clearly.
- Explains whether opened products can be returned, in line with UK consumer law.
- Loads quickly on mobile, where most social traffic lands.

## Common mistakes UK makeup brands make

- Launching a wide shade range but photographing only a few skin tones.
- Letting creators use filters, then facing complaints or ad rejections.
- Planning Christmas sets in October, when components are already sold out.
- Discounting new launches so heavily that full price never recovers.
- Using different shade names on Amazon UK, TikTok Shop and your store.
- Judging creators on views alone instead of sales and returns.

## How to measure what is working

Track these by product and by shade:

- Cost to win a new customer, by channel.
- Return rate and the reasons given.
- Sample-to-full-size conversion.
- Second-order rate and time between orders.
- Sales from creator codes and links, not only views.

A shade that sells well but is often returned may need new photos, not more ad spend.

## Claims and compliance

Makeup sold in Great Britain falls under the UK Cosmetics Regulation, with a Responsible Person, safety report and SCPN notification needed before sale. Ads and creator posts fall under the CAP Code, enforced by the ASA, and consumer protection law covers misleading pricing and fake reviews. Keep long-wear, waterproof and coverage claims tied to tests you hold, avoid medical wording for products marketed as suitable for skin conditions, and label every paid or gifted post. This is general guidance; check final wording with your adviser.
