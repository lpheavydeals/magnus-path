If you want to sell South Asian fashion online, UK buyers are some of the most informed you will meet. Many have shopped on Southall Broadway, Green Street in Upton Park, Ealing Road in Wembley, Ilford Lane, Leicester's Belgrave Road or Birmingham's Soho Road all their lives. They know how a good georgette falls, what heavy handwork should cost and how long a stitched lehenga really takes. Online, you have to answer the questions a shopkeeper would answer in person. This guide covers how to sell South Asian fashion online in the UK: range, sizing, made-to-order terms, imports, seasons and social selling.

The legal and tax points are general guidance only. Confirm them with an adviser or customs broker.

## Know who you are selling to

"South Asian fashion" covers very different buyers and garments. A Gujarati family planning a garba night, a Punjabi bride, a Bengali family preparing for Durga Puja, a Tamil family buying for a temple wedding and a Pakistani family shopping for Eid all have their own styles, colours and expectations. Mixed-heritage couples and friends attending their first Asian wedding are a growing group too, and they need more guidance on what to wear and how.

Pick the groups you know well and speak to them specifically. Use the words your buyers use, such as lehenga choli, anarkali, sharara, gharara, salwar kameez, kurta pyjama and sherwani, and explain them briefly for newer buyers without talking down to anyone.

### Organise the range by occasion

Shoppers rarely start with "I want a georgette suit". They start with "I need something for my cousin's mehndi". Build collections around occasions:

| Collection | What buyers need from it |
|---|---|
| Bridal | Lehengas and bridal sarees, consultation, fittings, a clear timeline |
| Wedding guest | Outfits by function (mehndi, sangeet, nikah, reception), quick delivery |
| Festive | Diwali, Eid, Navratri and Vaisakhi edits, family coordination |
| Everyday and workwear | Kurtas, co-ord sets and simple suits in reliable sizes |
| Menswear | Sherwanis, kurta sets and Nehru jackets, matched to family colours |
| Accessories | Dupattas, potlis, juttis and jewellery to complete a look |

Jewellery and accessories raise basket value and help shoppers finish an outfit in one order. If you add them, the rules differ: our guide on [how to sell jewellery online in the UK](/blog/how-to-sell-jewellery-online-uk/) covers hallmarking and descriptions.

## Get sizing and alterations right

Sizing causes more returns in this category than anything else. Indian, Pakistani and UK size labels do not line up, and much occasion wear is semi-stitched or unstitched.

### What to publish on every product page

- **Finished garment measurements**: bust, waist, hip, length and sleeve, in inches and centimetres.
- **Stitching status**: fully stitched, semi-stitched (with how much it can be let out) or unstitched fabric.
- **Seam allowance** available for alterations.
- **Fabric and weight**: georgette, chiffon, net, raw silk, velvet, cotton, with a note on how heavy the embroidery feels to wear.
- **Saree details**: length, blouse piece size, whether fall and pico are done.
- **Model details**: the model's height and the size worn.

### Offer an alterations path

Give buyers a way to make a piece fit. Options include a measurement form at checkout for stitched-to-size orders, a list of tailoring partners near your customers, or in-house alterations for bridal pieces. Record each customer's measurements, with their permission, so the next order for the family is quicker.

## Be clear about made-to-order terms

Many lehengas and bridal outfits are stitched after the order. That changes your cancellation and returns terms.

UK online buyers generally have a cancellation period after delivery. Goods made to the customer's specifications or clearly personalised can be an exception. A lehenga stitched to a bride's own measurements may fall into that exception; a standard size you simply order from a supplier after the sale often will not. The same applies to "custom colour" options that are really standard stock.

Practical steps:

1. Mark made-to-order products clearly on the collection page and the product page.
2. Explain the terms before payment, not in the confirmation email.
3. Show the lead time and the latest order date for a given event date.
4. Take a deposit for bridal work and set out what it covers in writing.
5. Keep faulty-goods rights separate: a faulty or misdescribed item is your responsibility whatever your policy says.

## Plan imports from India carefully

Many UK brands design in the UK and produce in India or Pakistan, where the embroidery and handwork skills are. Importing well is a big part of how you sell South Asian fashion online in the UK profitably.

### The basics to sort out

- **Commodity codes** for each garment type, which decide the duty rate.
- **Customs duty and import VAT**, plus any changes under trade agreements. Check current rates rather than relying on a supplier's estimate.
- **Shipping mode**: air for urgent bridal orders and small batches, sea for steady everyday stock.
- **A customs broker or courier** who handles clearance and gives you the paperwork you need for VAT records.
- **Fibre labelling**: UK rules require textiles to show their fibre content accurately.
- **Quality checks** on arrival: colour against the approved sample, embroidery, zips, hooks and loose threads.

### Lead times and buffers

Handwork takes time, and Indian festival seasons slow production. Add a buffer to supplier dates, especially for orders placed around Diwali. Keep a small stock of fast-selling sizes in the UK so guest outfits ship within days, and reserve made-to-order for bridal and statement pieces.

## Plan around wedding season and festivals

Demand in this category follows life events. Brides often begin looking many months ahead, families buy in stages, and guests leave it until the last few weeks.

| Moment | What sells | Plan by |
|---|---|---|
| Spring and summer weddings | Bridal, guest outfits, menswear | Bridal edits live early in the year |
| Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha | Suits, shararas, kidswear, family sets | Several weeks before each Eid |
| Navratri and Diwali | Chaniya cholis, festive suits, sarees | Late summer for autumn dates |
| Vaisakhi | Suits, kurtas and family outfits | Early spring |
| Winter weddings | Velvets, shawls, heavier fabrics | Early autumn |

Festival dates move each year, so check them in our [ecommerce sale calendar](/resources/sale-calendar/) and our guide to [Diwali and Eid marketing in the UK](/blog/diwali-and-eid-marketing-uk/). Show a clear "order by" date for each event, especially for express delivery.

## Sell with Instagram and TikTok

South Asian fashion is visual and social. Buyers want to see how a piece moves, how the colour reads in daylight and how heavy the work is.

### Content that helps buyers decide

- **Unedited daylight video** of each hero piece, front and back, with a twirl or walk.
- **Draping tutorials** for sarees and dupattas, which also attract new buyers.
- **"What to wear to" guides** for each wedding function, useful for guests from any background.
- **Try-ons on different body shapes and heights**, with sizes stated.
- **Behind the work**: embroidery, block printing or handloom, told without overclaiming origin.

### Use direct messages and video calls

High-value buyers often want to talk before paying. Answer Instagram and WhatsApp messages quickly, and offer short video consultations for bridal and family orders. ### Paid ads

Use Meta and TikTok ads to reach people browsing for wedding and festive outfits, and Google Shopping for specific searches such as "sage green lehenga" or "plus size anarkali". Retarget video viewers with the exact pieces they watched. Avoid ad copy that assumes someone's religion or ethnicity; speak to the occasion instead.

## Build trust online

Trust is what moves a buyer from your Instagram page to paying for an expensive outfit.

- Show real customer photos from weddings and festivals, with permission.
- Publish reviews that mention fit, colour accuracy and delivery timing.
- Give a UK address for returns and a named contact for bridal orders.
- Offer appointment-only fittings, trunk shows or pop-ups so buyers can see fabric in person.
- Be honest when colour varies between dye batches.

## Price and protect your margin

Count fabric, handwork, import costs, duty and VAT, UK delivery, alterations and the share of returns. Express delivery for guest outfits can carry a premium, while bridal deposits protect you against cancelled work. ## Common mistakes

- Using only S, M, L labels with no garment measurements.
- Hiding made-to-order terms until after payment.
- Relying on a single supplier with no buffer before festival peaks.
- Editing photos until the colour no longer matches the garment.
- Treating every South Asian buyer as one audience.
- Leaving bridal enquiries in Instagram messages for days.
