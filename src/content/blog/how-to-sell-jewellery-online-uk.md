Learning how to sell jewellery online UK-wide means learning a law most other sellers never meet. Britain has some of the oldest consumer protection in the world for precious metals, and a buyer who sees the word "gold" or "silver" in your listing is entitled to expect a hallmark behind it. On top of that, jewellery is hard to judge from a photo, often bought as a gift and sometimes worth a great deal. This guide is for UK designer-makers, demi-fine labels, fine jewellers and lab-grown diamond brands selling through their own store and marketplaces.

What follows is a general overview from an ecommerce growth team. Check the legal detail with an assay office, trading standards or a solicitor.

## Choose your segment first

Each part of the market sells differently online, so be clear about where you sit.

| Segment | Typical materials | What buyers need to see |
|---|---|---|
| Fine jewellery | Solid gold, platinum, natural diamonds and gemstones | Hallmarks, stone details, grading reports, secure delivery |
| Demi-fine | Gold plating or vermeil over silver, semi-precious stones | Base metal, plating detail, care advice |
| Sterling silver | Solid silver, often handmade | Hallmark details, tarnish care, sizing |
| Lab-grown diamond | Laboratory-grown stones in precious metal | Clear disclosure, stone specifications, comparisons explained |
| Fashion jewellery | Brass, stainless steel, alloys | Materials, nickel information, realistic durability |
| Personalised | Engraved, initial or birthstone pieces | Lead times, proofs, returns terms |

## How to sell jewellery online UK buyers trust: hallmarking

### What generally needs a hallmark

Under the Hallmarking Act, an article described as gold, silver, platinum or palladium generally has to carry a hallmark from a UK assay office before it is sold, unless it falls below the exemption weight for that metal. In broad terms the exemption weights are 7.78 grams for silver, 1 gram for gold and palladium, and 0.5 grams for platinum, but check the current guidance because details and exceptions apply.

The UK has four assay offices, in London, Birmingham, Sheffield and Edinburgh. Some marks applied under the International Convention on Hallmarks are also recognised. Pieces made abroad, including in India, Thailand or Italy for UK brands, usually need a UK hallmark or an accepted convention mark before they can be sold here as precious metal.

### Display the dealer's notice

Businesses selling precious metal articles must display a notice explaining approved hallmarks. The assay offices supply versions for shops and websites. Online, link it from product pages and the footer so a shopper checking your hallmarks can find it.

### Turn compliance into reassurance

Many buyers do not know what a hallmark shows. A short guide to reading your marks, with a close-up photo of a real one, turns a legal requirement into a reason to trust you.

### Plan hallmarking into your production schedule

Assaying takes time, and peak seasons are busy for the assay offices too. Build the turnaround into your launch and restock plans, especially before Christmas. If you work with an overseas workshop, agree in writing who sends pieces for hallmarking and who pays for it, and keep records linking each batch to its marks. If you register a sponsor's mark of your own, every hallmarked piece carries your identity, which is a quiet but useful brand signal.

## Plating, gemstones and lab-grown: describe precisely

Imprecise descriptions cause most jewellery complaints. Under the CAP Code and consumer protection law, shoppers must not be misled about what they are buying.

- **Plated pieces:** state the base metal and the plating metal, and the plating thickness where you know it. Do not describe a plated item simply as gold.
- **Vermeil:** explain what you mean by it, since buyers and brands use the term differently.
- **Gemstones:** name the stone, size and any treatment such as heating or filling.
- **Lab-grown diamonds:** say lab-grown or laboratory-grown wherever the stone is mentioned, including titles, ads and filters.
- **Sustainability:** only use words such as recycled or ethical where you can explain and evidence them; the ASA scrutinises environmental claims closely.
- **Fashion jewellery:** name the materials and mention nickel content, since UK rules restrict nickel release in items worn against the skin.

## Product page details that answer questions before they are asked

Every unanswered question is a reason to leave or an email you have to reply to. For each piece, list:

- Metal, fineness and hallmark, or base and plating metals
- Stone type, size, colour, clarity where relevant, setting style and any treatment
- Dimensions, weight for solid precious metal pieces, chain length and clasp type
- Ring sizes available, resizing options and lead time for made-to-order sizes
- Care instructions, including how to store plated pieces and clean silver
- Delivery service, insurance cover, packaging and gift options
- Returns terms, with any exclusions for personalised pieces stated plainly

Put the facts in a short, scannable block near the price, and keep the story further down for people who want it.

## Photography, video and sizing

Your photographs have two jobs: make a piece desirable and show it honestly.

### What to shoot for each piece

- A clean product shot on a plain background
- Macro close-ups of the setting, clasp and hallmark
- On-body shots that show true scale on a hand, ear or neck
- A short daylight video that shows sparkle and movement
- Packaging, so gift buyers know what arrives

Avoid retouching that changes colour or brilliance. A buyer who opens a box and sees something different from the photo returns it, and often says so in a review. Shoot the whole collection on the same background and in the same light, so a grid of products looks consistent on your store, on Etsy and in Shopping ads.

### Sizing help

Offer a ring size guide with a printable sizer, show necklace lengths on a model, and give dimensions in millimetres. For engagement rings and bridal pieces, offer a video consultation or a studio appointment.

## Payments, delivery and insurance

High-value pieces need a checkout that feels secure. Show accepted payment methods, offer buy-now-pay-later options such as Klarna or Clearpay where they suit your price points, and make the delivery promise clear before checkout.

Use tracked and signed-for services, and check how much each service insures. Royal Mail Special Delivery and courier services with declared-value cover are common choices for precious metal. Pack in a plain outer box so the contents are not obvious, and include care cards and the hallmark guide inside.

## Returns, personalisation and consumer rights

UK online buyers usually have a cancellation period. Personalised and made-to-order pieces are generally excluded, so say so on those product pages before purchase. Earrings for pierced ears may be restricted for hygiene reasons; explain your approach clearly. Faulty items must be put right whatever your policy says. A free resizing offer on rings removes a major worry for gift buyers.

## Plan for UK seasons

- **Black Friday and Christmas:** the main gifting season and a popular time for proposals.
- **Valentine's Day:** couples' gifts and engagement rings.
- **Mother's Day in March:** birthstone and initial pieces.
- **Wedding season:** bridal jewellery and bridesmaid gifts from spring to autumn.
- **Diwali and Eid:** gold and bridal jewellery for South Asian and Muslim customers.

Personalised pieces need earlier last order dates. State them on product pages and in ads.

## Getting your first buyers

Start with people already searching: Google Shopping with accurate metal, stone and size attributes, and Etsy for handmade or personalised work. Use short styling videos on Instagram and TikTok for demi-fine pieces, and retarget visitors with the exact pieces they viewed. Ask early buyers for photo reviews, and reply to every question on Etsy or social quickly, since slow answers lose sales in this category. ## Pricing and margin

Jewellery costs are easy to underestimate. Before setting prices, add up metal, stones, findings, labour, hallmarking, packaging, photography, insured delivery, marketplace fees, payment fees, returns and the advertising needed to reach a buyer who has never heard of you. Precious metal prices move, so review prices for solid gold and silver pieces regularly rather than once a year.

Look at the whole customer too. A buyer whose first purchase is a small silver piece and who returns for birthdays and anniversaries is worth far more than a single gift order, and you can afford to spend more to win them.
