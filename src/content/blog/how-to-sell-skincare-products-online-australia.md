Learning how to sell skincare products online, Australia-wide, starts with a fact that catches many founders out: in this country, a bottle of sunscreen is often regulated more like a medicine than a moisturiser. Get that and a few other rules sorted early, and Australian shoppers are a rewarding audience. They read labels, compare actives and care a great deal about sun protection. This guide walks through the work in the order a small or growing brand usually meets it, from product classification to the second order.

None of this is legal advice. Use it as a list of questions for your regulatory adviser, sponsor or lawyer.

## Step 1: Decide which products are cosmetics and which are therapeutic goods

Before you design a label or write an ad, sort every product in your range. Australia draws a firm line between cosmetics and therapeutic goods, and the side a product falls on decides almost everything else.

### Cosmetic skincare

Cleansers, toners, moisturisers, serums and masks sold to cleanse, hydrate, perfume or improve the look and feel of skin are generally cosmetics. They do not need approval before sale. The rules that matter most for them are ingredient labelling, chemical registration and consumer law.

### Sunscreens and the ARTG

When sun protection is a product's main job, the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) generally treats it as a therapeutic good. Before supply, such a sunscreen usually has to appear on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG), which involves an Australian sponsor, testing and specific labelling. Some moisturisers, tinted products and lip balms with SPF can be treated as cosmetics, but only within defined conditions on SPF level and claims. Check each one.

### Claims that change the category

A cosmetic can drift into therapeutic territory through its wording. Phrases such as "treats acne", "heals eczema" or "repairs sun damage" suggest a therapeutic purpose. Stay with cosmetic language: cleanses, softens, hydrates, improves the appearance of skin. Warnings about not making medical claims belong in your team's brief, not on the bottle.

## Step 2: Sort labels, ingredients and AICIS

Three pieces of paperwork tend to surface in the first months.

- **Ingredient labelling.** Under a mandatory information standard, most cosmetics on sale in Australia have to carry an ingredient list, usually written with standard INCI names. Online, show the same list on the product page so buyers can check it before ordering.
- **AICIS registration.** If your business imports or manufactures industrial chemicals, which includes cosmetic ingredients and finished cosmetics brought in from overseas, you may need to register with the Australian Industrial Chemicals Introduction Scheme (AICIS) and categorise what you introduce. Clarify who holds this duty if a contract manufacturer or importer is involved.
- **Pack contents and origin claims.** Net quantity has to be shown correctly, and any "Australian made" or "Australian owned" claim must be accurate under consumer law.

## Step 3: Build a range for Australian skin and weather

Australian shoppers can buy a niacinamide serum at the supermarket or a chemist on the way home, so a new label needs a clear reason to exist.

- **Start with a routine, not a catalogue.** Four or five products designed to be layered, say a gentle cleanser, one targeted serum, a moisturiser and a daily SPF, hand buyers an obvious second item.
- **Write for the climate.** Humid Queensland summers, dry Melbourne winters, air-conditioned offices and long days outdoors all change what people want from texture and finish.
- **Include something that runs out.** Cleansers and daily sunscreen are used up and rebought, which is where repeat revenue comes from.
- **Offer a trial size.** Minis lower the risk for buyers with sensitive skin, and larger sizes or duos lift order value later.

Work out what each pack earns after manufacturing, packaging, postage, payment and marketplace fees, refunds and the ad cost of each order. The [marketplace profit calculator](/tools/marketplace-profit-calculator/) shows the margin left on each channel before you commit to a price.

## Step 4: Set up a Shopify store that sells without a tester

Your own store holds the full story, the bundles and the customer list. Shopify suits most Australian skincare labels because payments, Afterpay, reviews, subscriptions and email are quick to connect.

### What each product page should answer

- The key ingredients, their strength where you can state it, and what they are there to do.
- Skin types it suits, who should patch test first, and what not to layer it with.
- A short natural-light clip showing texture, finish and scent.
- When in a routine to use it and how much to apply.
- An honest note on what someone may notice after a week and after several weeks.
- The full ingredient list, size and expiry or period-after-opening information.
- Reviews that mention skin type, so buyers can find people like themselves.

### Prices, payments and delivery

Show GST-inclusive prices in AUD once you are registered for GST, and offer Apple Pay, Google Pay and Afterpay alongside cards. Publish delivery times for Australia Post, Sendle or your courier by state, because a buyer in Perth or Darwin reads those times differently from one in Sydney. Summer heat in delivery vans can soften balms and split emulsions, so test your packaging in warm conditions before December.

## Step 5: Choose channels and give each one a job

| Channel | Who you reach | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Own Shopify store | Buyers who already know your brand | Full range, routines, subscriptions, loyalty |
| Amazon AU | Shoppers comparing by ingredient, ratings and delivery speed | Hero products, A+ content, Sponsored Products |
| TikTok Shop | Buyers who find products through video, where available to your business | Shoppable videos, affiliate creators, short offers |
| eBay AU | Value-minded buyers and gift-set hunters | Sets, end-of-line stock, older packaging |
| Kogan | Deal-focused shoppers on a large marketplace | Selected bundles, if margins allow |

Beauty retailers such as Mecca, Sephora, Priceline and Chemist Warehouse shape what Australians expect from skincare, and many shoppers check prices there. They choose their own ranges. A strong direct channel and honest reviews help any later conversation with a buyer, but nobody can promise a listing.

Keep pricing consistent. When your own site is consistently dearer than Amazon, regulars drift off and take their data with them.

## Step 6: Advertise inside Australian rules

Meta, TikTok and Google are where most young skincare brands find their first steady orders. They are also where most claim problems start.

### Consumer law, the AANA codes and Ad Standards

The ACCC expects every claim to be truthful and backed by evidence, including images and promoted reviews. "Clinically proven" or "dermatologist tested" needs testing that supports those exact words. Advertising is also judged against the AANA codes, and anyone can complain to Ad Standards. Edited faces and filtered before-and-after shots risk misleading buyers.

### Creators and disclosure

Paid, gifted and affiliate posts must be clearly labelled as advertising, early in the caption or video. Most primary sunscreens are therapeutic goods, and for those the TGA advertising code limits testimonials, paid creator content included. Brief every creator in writing.

### Search and Shopping

Shoppers who type something specific are close to buying, and Google Shopping puts your product in front of them. Think of searches such as "fragrance-free sunscreen for sensitive skin" or "vitamin C serum for dry skin". A clean product feed with accurate titles, AUD prices and good images matters as much as your bids.

## Step 7: Handle delivery, returns and consumer rights

- **Packaging.** Seal and pad pumps and droppers so they survive a cross-country trip.
- **Change of mind.** You can set your own policy for opened products on hygiene grounds, provided you explain it clearly before purchase.
- **Consumer rights.** No store policy can remove consumer rights under the Australian Consumer Law for products that are faulty, unsafe or not as described.
- **Privacy.** The Privacy Act may apply to your customer data depending on your business, and good consent records on email and SMS are wise either way.

## Step 8: Plan the Australian skincare year

1. **Spring into summer:** sunscreen, lighter textures and after-sun care as UV rises.
2. **Click Frenzy and Black Friday in November:** routine sets and limited bundles, with stock in place by October.
3. **Christmas and Boxing Day:** gift sets, clear cut-off dates for Australia Post and couriers, then value sizes.
4. **Valentine's Day:** self-care kits and gifts for partners.
5. **Mother's Day in May:** minis, gift sets and wrapping.
6. **EOFY in June:** clear older packaging, and push richer winter textures as the air dries out.

Plan creative and stock four to six weeks before each date so ads are not paused while you wait for a shipment.

## Step 9: Turn first orders into reorders

Skincare makes its money on the second and third orders.

1. Days 1 to 3 after delivery: how to use it and where it sits in a routine.
2. Around week two: a short check-in and a link to a routine guide.
3. Before the bottle runs low: a reorder reminder timed to actual use.
4. After the reorder: a review request with a photo option.

Ask for reviews once buyers have had a few weeks with a product. Never buy reviews, write them yourself or hide negative ones, because the ACCC treats fake and selectively published reviews as misleading. Keep answers to skin questions in cosmetic terms, and point people to a pharmacist or doctor when a question is really medical.
