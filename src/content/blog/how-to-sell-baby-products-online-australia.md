Anyone researching how to sell baby products online Australia-wide soon learns two things: the customer is careful, and the rules are specific. Parents read labels, check recalls and ask in parents' groups before they buy. Several common baby and nursery products are covered by mandatory safety standards, infant formula marketing is restricted, and information about families needs careful handling. Get those foundations right and marketing becomes much easier, because trust is what this category runs on. Here is a practical order of work for Australian founders.

We are an ecommerce growth team, not lawyers or product safety consultants. Use the summaries below to get your bearings, then confirm the details with a qualified adviser or testing lab.

## Step 1: Check which safety standards apply to each product

Australia has mandatory safety standards and bans for a range of children's products, enforced by the ACCC and state and territory regulators. For baby and nursery brands, the ones that come up most often include:

- Household cots and portable cots.
- Toys for children up to 36 months, which must not contain small parts that could be swallowed or inhaled.
- Prams and strollers.
- Baby bath aids and baby walkers.
- Children's nightwear, which has fire hazard labelling requirements.
- Products containing button or coin batteries, which have their own mandatory safety and information standards covering secure compartments, testing and warnings.
- Toppling furniture rules, which reach nursery chests of drawers and some change tables.

Requirements change, and new rules have arrived for some infant sleep products in recent years. Product Safety Australia lists current standards and bans.

### What to do before launch

1. List every product and variant, and ask a testing lab which standards apply.
2. Get test reports from an accredited lab and file them by product and batch.
3. Check that warnings and labels appear on the product, the packaging and your listings.
4. Write safety requirements into supplier agreements, including what happens when a batch fails.
5. Set a review date to recheck standards each year.

### Materials and design

Choose materials suitable for mouthing, avoid small detachable parts on anything meant for under-3s, and secure battery compartments with screws. Be careful with words like "non-toxic" or "chemical-free". Under the Australian Consumer Law, claims must not mislead, and vague purity claims are hard to support.

## Step 2: Prepare for recalls and safety reports

No brand plans to recall a product, but the ones that prepare handle it far better. In broad terms, suppliers must report to the ACCC within two days of becoming aware that a consumer product has been associated with a death, serious injury or illness. A supplier that starts a voluntary recall must notify the Commonwealth minister, through Product Safety Australia, within two days.

Practical preparation:

- Record batch or lot numbers against every order.
- Keep customer contact details accessible and secure.
- Draft a recall notice, email and web page template in advance.
- Know how each marketplace you sell on handles recalls.
- Decide who in the business makes the call and who speaks to customers.

## Step 3: Understand the rules on formula, foods and claims

### Infant formula

The MAIF Agreement is a long-standing arrangement under which signatory manufacturers and importers do not advertise or promote infant formula for babies up to 12 months directly to the public. The government has been reviewing how formula marketing is regulated, and the rules may tighten or extend to related products such as toddler milks. Composition and labelling also follow the Food Standards Code. If formula is part of your range, take legal advice before any promotion, and expect platforms and retailers to apply their own restrictions.

### Baby foods

Foods for infants have specific composition and labelling requirements under the Food Standards Code, and health claims are tightly limited. Check labels with a food regulatory adviser.

### Skincare and wellness claims

A nappy cream described as soothing is usually a cosmetic. One that claims to treat or prevent nappy rash may be a therapeutic good that needs an ARTG entry. Primary sunscreens are also therapeutic goods. Keep claims within what each product is legally allowed to say.

## Step 4: Build product pages that answer parents' questions

| Page element | Why parents look for it |
|---|---|
| Age or stage suitability | Saves them guessing, and supports toy safety rules |
| Materials and full ingredient list | Allergies, sensitive skin and mouthing |
| Safety standards met and warnings | Shows you know the rules and have tested |
| Dimensions, weight and folded size | Prams in car boots, cots in small bedrooms |
| Care, cleaning and assembly | Practical questions at 2am |
| Reviews and photos from real parents | The trust signal many parents weigh most |
| GST-inclusive price, delivery and returns | One clear total and no surprises |

Photos should follow recognised safe sleeping advice: no loose bedding, pillows or toys in a cot shot, and babies on their backs. Ads that ignore this draw complaints quickly. ## Step 5: Choose your sales channels

### Your own Shopify store

Your store is where you tell the full story, control safety information and build a relationship with each family. Subscriptions, gift messages and life-stage emails are easiest to run here, because you hold the order history and the consent records. Offer Afterpay or Zip for larger nursery purchases, with the full cost clear.

### Amazon AU

Amazon.com.au suits essentials and toys that parents compare and reorder. Some baby categories need approval and may ask for safety documents. Use Brand Registry, and watch for unauthorised sellers who may not handle stock or recalls properly.

### eBay AU and Kogan

Both can add reach for established lines, accessories and bundles. Read each marketplace's rules on children's products, and keep pricing consistent with your store.

### TikTok Shop

Short videos of folding a pram or packing a nappy bag can sell well. Check whether TikTok Shop is open to your business and category in Australia, and how it treats baby products, before you plan around it.

Specialists such as Baby Bunting and the supermarkets shape what parents expect on range, price and convenience. A direct brand competes on product design, information and service.

## Step 6: Market to adults, responsibly

- Ad platforms do not allow ads aimed at young children, and sensitive targeting options for parents have narrowed.
- Avoid copy that implies you know someone is pregnant or has a newborn.
- The AANA Code of Ethics applies to all advertising, and the AANA code on advertising to children applies to ads aimed at them. Ad Standards handles complaints.
- Paid or gifted creator content must be clearly identifiable as advertising.
- Speak to parents and gift buyers, who make the purchase.

## Step 7: Respect privacy from the first sign-up

The Privacy Act applies to many online businesses, and the Australian Privacy Principles cover how you collect, use and protect personal information. A children's online privacy code is also being developed. Even if a small business exemption might apply to you, following the principles builds trust.

- Collect only what you need: a due date or birth month is usually enough for life-stage emails.
- Explain why you ask, and how you will use it.
- Get consent for email and SMS marketing under the Spam Act.
- Store data securely and limit who can access it.

## Step 8: Plan repeat orders, life stages and gifting

Nappies, wipes and baby wash suit subscribe-and-save, with an easy way to change size as the baby grows. Life-stage emails suggest the next size or stage at the right time. Gift sets for baby showers, first birthdays and Christmas, with wrapping and delivery dates by state, bring in a second group of buyers: grandparents, aunts, uncles and friends. Our [LTV and CAC calculator](/tools/ltv-cac-calculator/) shows what a family is worth over a year of reorders.

### Gifting details that matter

- A gift message field and an option to leave prices off the packing slip.
- Delivery estimates by state, so a grandparent in Perth knows a parcel will reach Brisbane before the shower.
- Ready-made sets by stage: newborn, six months, first birthday.
- Wrapping that survives the courier.

## Step 9: Handle operations with care

- Track expiry dates for creams and consumables, and protect them from summer heat in transit.
- Explain change-of-mind terms for opened hygiene items before purchase. Consumer rights under the Australian Consumer Law still apply to faulty or unsafe goods.
- Pack fragile and bulky nursery items carefully, and show freight by postcode.
- Plan stock for Click Frenzy, Black Friday and Christmas, plus EOFY clearance and New Year restocking.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Assuming the factory handled compliance.** If you supply the product in Australia, you need to know which standards apply.
- **Copying claims from overseas brands.** Rules differ, and Australian regulators enforce their own.
- **Collecting more data than you need.** It adds risk without adding sales.
- **Ignoring gift buyers.** Many orders come from people who are not the parent.
