For founders working out how to start a cosmetics brand, Australia is a good place to begin, with a few conditions. Buyers here read labels, know the big beauty retailers well and are quick to try an independent label that offers something specific. The rules are lighter than in some markets for ordinary makeup and skincare, but there are traps around sunscreen, treatment claims and imported ingredients. This guide takes a first-time founder through the steps in a sensible order, from the idea to the first restock.

It is general information, not legal or financial advice. Check details with an accountant, a lawyer and a regulatory adviser.

## Step 1: Choose a niche and a small first range

### Pick one buyer

"Makeup for everyone" is hard to market and expensive to stock. A clear buyer makes every later decision easier. Examples include:

- Lightweight base products for humid climates and long, hot days.
- Shade ranges built for skin tones that mainstream ranges serve poorly.
- Fragrance-free products for sensitive and reactive skin.
- Refillable or low-waste packaging, if you can back the claims.
- Quick, minimal routines for busy workers.

### Keep the range small

Start with three to six products that suit your buyer and work together. Fewer products mean lower minimum order quantities, less cash tied up in stock and a clearer story. Lip, cheek and brow products need fewer shades than foundation, so many new brands start there and add base products later.

### Look at the market honestly

Walk through Mecca, Sephora, Priceline or a Chemist Warehouse and note what already exists at different price points. Read reviews of similar products on Amazon AU to see what buyers complain about. Your gap should be something a buyer would notice and mention to a friend.

## Step 2: Set up the business and protect the name

- **ABN and structure.** Get an Australian Business Number. Decide with an accountant whether to trade as a sole trader or set up a company through ASIC, and register your business name if it differs from your own.
- **GST.** Register once your turnover reaches the GST threshold, or earlier if it suits you. Once registered, show GST-inclusive prices.
- **Trademark.** Search IP Australia's records before you fall in love with a name, then apply to register it in the relevant classes. Check that the domain and social handles are free.
- **Insurance.** Product liability and public liability insurance are worth arranging before your first sale.
- **Bank and bookkeeping.** Keep business money separate from day one, and use accounting software that connects to your store.

## Step 3: Find a manufacturer

Most new brands use a contract manufacturer, either in Australia or overseas.

| Question to ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What are the minimum order quantities? | Decides how much cash you tie up in stock |
| Do you offer private label, custom or both? | Shapes your speed to market and story |
| What stability and preservative testing do you run? | Shows the product will last and stay safe |
| Who handles AICIS duties for imported ingredients? | Avoids gaps in your compliance |
| What documentation do you supply? | Ingredient lists, safety data, batch records |
| How do you handle shade matching and batch consistency? | Prevents complaints when you reorder |

Ask for samples, wear-test them for several weeks in real conditions, including a hot day, and get every agreement in writing.

## Step 4: Get the rules right

### Cosmetic or therapeutic good

Most makeup and skincare are cosmetics. Products that claim to treat a condition, such as acne, or whose main purpose is sun protection, are often therapeutic goods regulated by the TGA. Some foundations, tinted moisturisers and lip balms with SPF can stay cosmetics within set conditions. Settle each product's status before you design a label.

### AICIS

The Australian Industrial Chemicals Introduction Scheme covers businesses that import or manufacture industrial chemicals, which includes cosmetic ingredients and finished cosmetics brought in from overseas. You may need to register and categorise what you introduce. Australia also restricts the use of new animal test data for ingredients introduced for cosmetic use, so ask your manufacturer about each ingredient's history.

### Labels and product safety

A mandatory information standard on ingredient labelling covers most cosmetics, so plan the back label around a full ingredient list. Show net quantity correctly, and make sure "Australian made" or "Australian owned" claims are accurate. If a product is linked to a serious injury, suppliers may have mandatory reporting duties to the ACCC.

### Claims

Any claim you print or post has to be accurate under the Australian Consumer Law. "Long-wear", "waterproof", "non-comedogenic" and "dermatologist tested" all need evidence. Vegan, cruelty-free and clean claims need supplier records and a clear explanation.

## Step 5: Design packaging that sells and survives

- Choose sizes and shapes that work in a photo and fit a standard satchel or small box.
- Test compacts and palettes for breakage in transit.
- Check that lipsticks and balms cope with summer heat in delivery vans.
- Print shade names and numbers large enough to read in a product photo.
- Keep sustainability claims specific, such as "refillable case" or "recyclable glass jar", rather than vague.

### Give the brand a voice, not just a logo

Packaging is the first thing buyers see, but the brand is what they remember. Write down, in a few sentences, who you make products for, what you believe about makeup and how you talk. Then use that consistently on the pack, the product pages, emails and social posts.

A few practical points help an Australian label stand out:

- **Speak the buyer's language.** Plain, friendly wording usually lands better here than glossy luxury copy.
- **Show real faces.** Use models and customers across ages, skin tones and backgrounds, with skin texture left visible.
- **Explain your shade naming.** Numbers with undertone letters are easier to compare than poetic names alone.
- **Tell the founding story honestly.** Why you started, what you could not find in shops and how the first product was developed.

Brand photography should match what buyers receive. If a lipstick looks warmer in your photos than in the tube, returns and poor reviews follow.

## Step 6: Work out unit economics before you order

Add up everything a single unit costs you to make and deliver: the formula and its packaging, inbound freight, warehousing and packing, postage, payment and marketplace fees, an allowance for returns and the advertising it takes to win the order. Then set a GST-inclusive price that leaves room for promotions. Our [profit margin calculator](/tools/profit-margin-calculator/) helps you test different prices and costs quickly.

Hold back cash for restocks. A successful launch that sells out with no money to reorder can stall a young brand for months.

## Step 7: Build your store and choose channels

### Your Shopify store

Your own store carries the full story, the shade guidance and the customer list. Include swatches on a range of skin tones, clear ingredient lists, Afterpay, Apple Pay and delivery times by state. A short shade quiz can help with base products.

### Marketplaces

| Channel | Good for |
|---|---|
| Amazon AU | Repeat staples and products that need little explanation |
| TikTok Shop | Lip, blush and nail products shown on camera, where available to your business |
| eBay AU | Older shades, sets and end-of-line stock |
| Kogan | Selected bundles for deal-focused shoppers |

Keep GST-inclusive prices consistent across channels. Retail ranging with specialist beauty stores may come later; buyers there usually look for steady direct sales and strong reviews first.

## Step 8: Plan the launch

1. Start sharing close-up swatches and behind-the-scenes content six to eight weeks ahead.
2. Open a waitlist and offer early access to sign-ups.
3. Send product to creators who match your buyer, with written briefs covering disclosure. Paid, gifted and affiliate posts must be clearly labelled as advertising under the AANA code, and Ad Standards hears complaints.
4. Prepare Meta and TikTok ads using creator content you have permission to reuse.
5. Go live on your store, email, SMS and any marketplaces on the same day.

Time launches away from the busiest sale weeks unless you have the budget to compete. Click Frenzy, Black Friday, Christmas, Valentine's Day and Mother's Day in May work well for gift sets once you are established.

## Step 9: Run operations and look after customers

Set a clear change-of-mind policy for opened cosmetics on hygiene grounds, explained before purchase. Buyers still hold consumer rights under the Australian Consumer Law whenever a product is faulty, unsafe or unlike its description, and no policy wording changes that. Track stock by shade, answer questions quickly and handle personal data in line with the Privacy Act where it applies.

Review sales by shade and product every month. Restock the shades that sell steadily, consider dropping those that sit untouched for a whole season, and note the reasons customers give for returns or exchanges. Those notes often point to a photo that misleads, a shade description that needs work or a packaging problem you can fix before the next order.

## Mistakes to avoid in your first year

- Launching too many shades before you know which sell.
- Choosing a name without a trademark search.
- Adding SPF to a product without checking how that changes its regulation.
- Spending the whole budget on the launch and none on restocks.
- Using filtered images that make the product look different on skin.
