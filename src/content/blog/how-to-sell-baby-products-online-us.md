Before you work out how to sell baby products online, USA safety law needs your attention first. Federal rules require testing and paperwork for most children's products, some infant items have their own mandatory standards, and marketplaces check documents before they let you list. Parents, meanwhile, research more carefully than almost any other shopper. This guide walks US founders through the steps in a sensible order.

We are an e-commerce marketing team, not lawyers or product safety consultants. Use this as a general guide and confirm the requirements for your own products with a testing lab or qualified counsel.

## Step 1: Get product safety right first

### Know which rules apply

The Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) oversees most baby and children's products. Under the Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act (CPSIA), a product designed or intended primarily for children aged 12 and under is a "children's product", and that label brings obligations:

- **Third-party testing** at a CPSC-accepted laboratory for each rule that applies, such as lead content, lead in paint and phthalates.
- **A Children's Product Certificate (CPC)**, issued by the US importer or domestic manufacturer, based on passing results.
- **Tracking information** permanently marked on the product and packaging, so a batch can be traced.

### Infant and nursery product standards

Many durable infant products, such as cribs, play yards, high chairs, strollers, bath seats, baby carriers and bassinets, have their own mandatory CPSC standards. Durable infant products generally need a product registration card so parents can be told about recalls. The Safe Sleep for Babies Act bans inclined sleepers and padded crib bumpers. Toys must meet the federal toy safety standard, including small-parts rules and choking hazard warnings for young children.

### Materials and design

Even where no specific rule applies, parents ask about materials. Know what every component is made of, avoid vague words such as "non-toxic" unless you can support them, and keep your test reports organized by product and batch.

## Step 2: Understand rules on feeding, formula and claims

### Infant formula and baby food

Infant formula is a separately regulated FDA category, with strict requirements on nutrients, manufacturing, labeling and notification. Baby foods face growing scrutiny of heavy metals, and some states, California among them, now require testing and disclosure. If you plan to sell formula or baby food, bring in specialist regulatory counsel before anything else.

### Claims on skincare and wellness products

Baby lotions, washes and balms are generally cosmetics in the US, and cosmetic claims must not cross into drug claims, such as treating eczema or diaper rash, unless the product is marketed as an over-the-counter drug that follows the relevant rules. The FTC also expects claims such as "hypoallergenic", "pediatrician recommended" or "dermatologist tested" to be truthful and backed by evidence.

### Proposition 65

California's Proposition 65 may require a warning where a product can expose people to listed chemicals. Work with counsel to decide whether a warning applies and how it should appear on your site and marketplace listings.

## Step 3: Build product pages that answer parents' questions

Parents read baby product pages closely. A strong page includes:

- **Age grading and size**, clearly stated near the top.
- **Materials and ingredients**, listed in full.
- **The safety standards your product has been tested to**, described accurately, with no claims beyond your results.
- **Warnings**, such as small parts, sleep safety or supervision notes, shown prominently.
- **Use and care instructions**, including washing and assembly.
- **Dimensions and weight limits** for gear and furniture.
- **Honest FAQs** about fit, sizing, cleaning and what is in the box.
- **Reviews from real parents**, collected honestly and shown in full.

### Photos and content parents trust

Show the product in real use with a baby or toddler of the right age, in safe settings that follow sleep and supervision guidance. A photo of a baby asleep with loose blankets or a pillow will draw criticism and may break platform rules. Short videos of folding, fastening or cleaning answer practical questions quickly.

## Step 4: Choose your US sales channels

### Your own Shopify store

Your store is where subscriptions, bundles, gift sets and customer relationships live. It is also where you can explain safety and materials in full.

### Amazon US

Amazon is a large channel for baby products, and its baby registry drives gifting. It checks children's product compliance, often asking for the CPC and test reports, and may restrict listings until they are provided. Requirements change, so read Seller Central before launch.

### Walmart Marketplace and Target Plus

Walmart Marketplace reaches value-focused families and has its own compliance checks. Target Plus is invitation-only, so treat it as a future opportunity.

### TikTok Shop

TikTok Shop suits visual products such as bibs, clothing and feeding accessories, but restricts some baby categories. Check the current rules before planning stock.

### Registries and specialist retail

Babylist, Amazon Baby Registry and Target's registry shape how many US families receive gifts. Make it easy to add your products, and keep registry-friendly items in stock. Specialist retail names such as Buy Buy Baby are useful context for how parents shop, but most new brands start online.

## Step 5: Market responsibly

- Aim ads at adults, never children.
- Avoid copy that implies you know someone is pregnant or has a new baby; platforms restrict personal-attribute language.
- Keep claims within your test results and evidence.
- Use creators who disclose paid relationships clearly and follow safe-sleep and supervision guidance on camera.
- If any part of your site is aimed at children, review COPPA obligations with counsel.

## Step 6: Plan repeat orders, life stages and gifting

### Repeat essentials

Diapers, wipes, bath products and feeding supplies run out predictably. Offer subscribe-and-save with easy size changes, skip and pause, and clear renewal terms that follow federal and state automatic renewal laws.

### Life stages

With the parent's consent, send emails that match the child's stage: the next diaper size, the next clothing size, the move from bottles to cups. Our [LTV and CAC calculator](/tools/ltv-cac-calculator/) shows how repeat orders change what you can spend to win a new family.

### Gifting

Gift buyers want age guidance, gift-ready packaging, a message option and a reliable delivery date. Build gift sets for baby showers, first birthdays and the holidays.

## Step 7: Handle operations with care

- **Recalls.** Have a plan for contacting customers and marketplaces quickly if a safety issue appears.
- **Batch tracking.** Link orders to batches so you can identify affected customers.
- **Shelf life.** Track expiry dates on skincare, wipes and food products.
- **Packaging.** Keep tracking labels and warnings visible and intact.
- **Customer service.** Answer safety questions quickly and calmly; parents notice.

## Set up your store for parents

Parents often shop one-handed on a phone, late at night or during a nap. Design for that:

- Keep the menu simple, organized by age and by need, such as sleep, feeding, bath and play.
- Add a size and age guide that loads quickly and is easy to read on a small screen.
- Show delivery dates clearly, especially for gifts and items needed before a due date.
- Offer guest checkout and fast payment options.
- Make subscriptions easy to manage from the customer account.
- Keep search forgiving, so "swaddle", "sleep sack" and "wearable blanket" all return relevant products.

A slow or confusing store loses tired parents quickly, and they rarely come back to try again.

## Price and bundle with care

Parents compare prices across Amazon, Walmart and your store in seconds. Keep prices consistent across channels and use bundles, bulk packs and subscriptions to create value instead of constant discounts. For gift buyers, price points that suit a typical shower or birthday gift make choosing easier. Show the cost per unit for diapers, wipes and other essentials so comparisons are fair.

## Measure what matters

Once sales start, track a short list of numbers every month: acquisition cost by channel, repeat purchase rate, subscription retention, return reasons by product and the questions parents ask most before buying. Return reasons and pre-purchase questions are especially useful in this category, because they usually point to missing sizing, material or safety detail on the page. Fix those gaps first; they lift conversion on every channel at once, including marketplaces where you control less of the page.

## Plan around the US calendar

Prime Day in July and Amazon's fall deal event bring families stocking up on essentials and gear. Back to school matters for toddler and kids clothing. Black Friday, Cyber Monday and the holidays drive toys and gifts, and spring is a busy baby shower season.

## Common mistakes when selling baby products online

- Listing products before testing and the CPC are complete.
- Using "non-toxic", "chemical-free" or "safe" without evidence.
- Showing unsafe sleep setups in photos or creator content.
- Targeting ads with pregnancy or new-parent language.
- Ignoring Amazon document requests until a listing is suppressed.
