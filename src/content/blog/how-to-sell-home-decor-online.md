Knowing how to sell home decor online is mostly about closing the gap between a screen and a living room. Buyers cannot touch a fabric, check a colour in daylight or see whether a lamp suits their side table. Your store has to answer those questions with photos, measurements and clear delivery promises, and your operations have to get fragile and bulky items to the door in one piece. This guide covers each part, with a focus on Indian brands that also sell abroad.

## Choose products that suit online selling

Not every decor product is equally easy to sell online. Before you build a big catalogue, look at each item through three questions:

- **Can buyers picture it?** Cushion covers, wall art and planters are easy to imagine at home. A large sectional sofa needs far more reassurance.
- **Can you ship it safely?** Glass, ceramics and large mirrors need tested packaging. Flat-packed or soft items are much simpler.
- **Do the numbers work after shipping and returns?** Bulky items cost more to send and to bring back.

Many brands start with soft furnishings and smaller decor, build a following, and then add larger pieces once their delivery partners and photography are proven.

## Photograph products in real rooms

Photography does more selling in home decor than in almost any other category. A product on a white background shows what it is. A product in a room shows what it will feel like to own.

### The photo set to aim for

- **Studio shots** on a plain background, from several angles, with true colour.
- **Room shots** styled in a real or realistic room, in natural light.
- **Scale shots** beside familiar objects, a person or common furniture.
- **Detail shots** of texture, weave, grain, joints and finish.
- **Every variant**, so buyers do not have to guess what "sage" or "walnut" looks like.
- **Short video** showing the product from all sides, opened, lit up or in use.

Style rooms that look like your buyers' homes. A compact city flat with natural light will feel more relatable to many Indian buyers than a vast showroom. If you sell to the UK or US, consider a second set styled for those homes.

## Give exact size, material and care details

Unclear details are the most common cause of decor returns. "Medium" means nothing; "45 cm x 45 cm" means a lot.

Every product page should include:

| Detail | What to show |
|---|---|
| Dimensions | Height, width, depth in cm and inches, plus weight |
| Materials | Main material, fill, frame, finish and any coatings |
| Colour notes | How colour may vary with light or screen, and natural variation |
| Care | How to clean, what to avoid, indoor or outdoor use |
| Assembly | Whether assembly is needed, tools, time and who does it |
| Delivery | Expected timeline, delivery type and doorway or lift checks |
| Returns | What can be returned and how |

For handmade or natural-material products, say clearly that each piece varies. It turns a possible complaint into part of the appeal.

## Plan packaging, delivery and breakage

Delivery is where many decor brands lose their margin. Plan it before launch, not after the first broken parcel.

### Fragile items

- Double box glass, ceramics and mirrors.
- Use fitted inserts or moulded protection rather than loose fill.
- Add corner protectors for frames and edged items.
- Label boxes clearly and keep parcels within courier weight limits.
- Ship samples to yourself across different routes and check how they arrive.

### Bulky items

Furniture and large decor often need specialist delivery partners rather than standard couriers. Check which pin codes they serve, whether they deliver to upper floors, and whether they assemble on site. Tell buyers what to expect: delivery windows, a call before arrival, and doorway or lift measurements to check.

### Track damage

Log every damage claim by product, packaging type and courier. Patterns appear quickly, and fixing one weak packaging design can cut losses on a whole range. For high-value items, ask customers to record an unboxing video so claims are easier to settle.

## Write a clear returns policy

A good returns policy builds trust without inviting abuse. Keep it short and specific:

- What counts as damaged, defective or wrong, and how to report it (usually with photos within a set time).
- Whether change-of-mind returns are accepted, and for which product types.
- Who pays return shipping in each case.
- How refunds or replacements are handled and how long they take.
- Special terms for custom, made-to-order or large items.

Show the key points on every product page, not only in a footer link. Buyers making a big purchase look for it.

## Build trust with reviews and customer photos

Decor buyers want to see the product in someone else's home before it reaches theirs. Customer photos often persuade more than your own styled shots, because they show real light, real rooms and real scale.

- Ask for a review with a photo a week or two after delivery, once the item is in place.
- Show photo reviews near the top of the product page, not buried below the fold.
- Reply to negative reviews calmly and explain what you changed.
- Share customer rooms on Instagram, with permission.

Trust signals matter more as the price rises. For larger pieces, add clear contact options, a short video call or WhatsApp chat for questions, and visible delivery and returns terms.

## Price for shipping and order value

Shipping costs in home decor vary widely between a cushion cover and a console table. Decide early whether to build shipping into product prices, charge it separately or offer free shipping above a threshold. Many brands do a mix: free shipping on small items above a set order value, and a clear delivery charge for bulky pieces. Bundles such as a cushion set with a matching throw raise order value and spread shipping cost across more products.

## Pick your channels: own store and marketplaces

Most home decor brands sell through a mix of channels. Each does a different job.

### Your own store

Your website lets you show full room stories, collections and styling ideas, and keeps customer data for repeat purchases. A platform such as Shopify handles variants, collections and shipping rules well. Use a free shipping threshold to lift order values; our [free shipping threshold calculator](/tools/free-shipping-threshold-calculator/) helps you set one that protects margin.

### Amazon and Flipkart

These marketplaces bring large audiences, especially for smaller decor, furnishings and storage. Competition is heavy, so strong images, precise titles and genuine reviews matter. Check category rules and delivery options for large items.

### Pepperfry and specialist marketplaces

Pepperfry focuses on furniture and home products, and its buyers often arrive with a room in mind. Specialist marketplaces can suit larger or higher-value items, but read their commission, delivery and returns terms carefully.

### Selling abroad

Handmade decor, textiles and brass or wooden pieces from India often find buyers in the UK, US, UAE and Australia. Etsy and Amazon's global marketplaces are common starting points. Plan for international shipping costs, customs paperwork and returns before you list.

## Use Pinterest, Instagram and search

Home decor is a visual, inspiration-led category. Buyers save ideas for weeks before they buy.

- **Pinterest.** Create boards by room and style, pin room shots linked to product pages, and write clear pin titles. Pins can bring visits long after posting.
- **Instagram.** Use Reels for room makeovers, styling tips and unboxing. Tag products so viewers can shop directly. Work with home creators whose homes look like your buyers' homes.
- **Search.** Write category pages and guides for searches like "wall decor for living room" or "cotton cushion covers 16x16". Good product and collection SEO keeps bringing buyers without paying for every click.

## Plan around seasons

Home decor sales follow life events and festivals. Plan stock and campaigns around:

- **Diwali and the festive season**, when homes are refreshed and gifts are bought.
- **Wedding season**, for gifting and new homes.
- **Housewarming and new home moves**, which happen year-round but cluster around key dates.
- **Regional festivals** such as Onam and Pongal, which matter for brands strong in the south.
- **Christmas, Black Friday and Boxing Day** if you sell to the UK, US or Australia.

Start campaigns early for large items, because buyers decide weeks ahead and delivery needs lead time.

## How to sell home decor online: launch checklist

- [ ] Product range tested for shipping, margin and ease of picturing.
- [ ] Studio, room, scale and detail photos for every product and variant.
- [ ] Dimensions, materials, care and assembly details on every page.
- [ ] Packaging tested with real shipments for fragile items.
- [ ] Bulky delivery partner chosen, with pin codes and floor delivery confirmed.
- [ ] Returns policy written and shown on product pages.
- [ ] Channels chosen: own store, marketplaces, and any international plans.
- [ ] Pinterest boards and Instagram content plan ready.
- [ ] Seasonal calendar with stock and campaign dates.
- [ ] Damage, returns and margin tracked by product.

Learning how to sell home decor online takes patience, because buyers decide slowly and delivery needs care. Brands that answer every question on the page and get products home intact build the trust that brings repeat buyers and referrals.
