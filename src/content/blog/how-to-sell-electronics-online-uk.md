Working out how to sell electronics online, UK founders soon find that compliance and trust matter as much as the product. Shoppers compare specs across Amazon UK, Currys and Argos, read reviews closely and worry about what happens if something stops working. Regulators expect electrical goods to be safe, correctly marked and recyclable, and connected devices to meet security rules. This guide covers the order of work: compliance, positioning, listings, warranty, channels, peak seasons, reviews and returns, for brands selling chargers, audio, smart home devices, small appliances and accessories.

## Step 1: Understand the UK compliance basics

Treat this section as general orientation, not legal advice. Electrical rules are detailed, they change, and requirements depend on the product. Work with a qualified test house or compliance consultant before you place stock on the market.

### Product safety and conformity marking

Most electrical and electronic products sold in Great Britain must meet product safety rules covering things such as electrical safety, electromagnetic compatibility and, for wireless products, radio equipment. The manufacturer or importer usually needs technical documentation, a declaration of conformity and the right marking on the product. Depending on the product, that means a UKCA mark or a CE mark, and CE has remained acceptable in Great Britain for a wide range of goods. Northern Ireland follows different arrangements, so check if you sell there.

Products sold to UK consumers also need the manufacturer's or importer's name and a UK address in the right places, and plugs supplied with mains products generally need to meet UK plug safety requirements.

### WEEE and batteries

Businesses that sell electrical and electronic equipment have duties under the WEEE regulations. In general, sellers must offer customers a free way to return old equipment when they buy a similar new item, by running a take-back service themselves or by joining an approved scheme, and must explain the route to customers. Products should carry the crossed-out wheelie bin symbol. Battery rules add separate duties on take-back, labelling and, for producers, registration.

### Security for connected devices

The UK's product security regime applies to consumer products that connect to the internet or other networks, such as smart speakers, cameras, plugs, doorbells and wearables. In broad terms, these products must not use universal default passwords, must provide a way for people to report security issues, and must state the minimum period for which security updates will be provided. Manufacturers, importers and distributors all have responsibilities.

### Other points to check

- Energy labelling for certain product types, such as some lighting and appliances.
- Rules on lithium battery shipping with your couriers.
- Consumer rights for faulty goods and online cancellations.
- Trade marks for your brand and model names.

### Compliance checklist

| Item | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| Test reports | Do we hold reports for every model and variant? |
| Declaration of conformity | Is it signed, current and matched to the marking? |
| Marking and labels | Are UKCA or CE, importer details and the bin symbol on the product? |
| WEEE take-back | Have we chosen our own service or a scheme, and told customers? |
| Batteries | Are battery duties covered? |
| Connected products | Do we meet the password, reporting and update rules? |

## Step 2: Choose products and positioning

Electronics is crowded with similar products sold on price. A small brand needs a reason to exist:

- **A clear difference.** Longer battery life, a better microphone, a smaller size or a feature others leave out.
- **A defined buyer.** Home workers, gamers, cyclists, parents or people setting up a first smart home.
- **A hero range.** Two or three models you can stock deeply, document fully and support properly.

Avoid launching dozens of near-identical models. Each one needs testing, documentation, listings and stock.

### Research before you order stock

Read the one- and two-star reviews on rival Amazon UK listings. Complaints about short battery life, flimsy cables, poor app connections or confusing instructions tell you what to improve and what to say in your own listings. Look at Reddit threads, YouTube reviews and comparison sites to learn which specs UK buyers actually care about. Check the price bands in which rivals sell, and make sure your landed cost, duties, compliance testing, marketplace fees and returns still leave a margin at a realistic selling price.

## Step 3: Write spec-led listings in plain English

### Titles

Include brand, product type, the key spec and compatibility: "Wireless earbuds with 30-hour case, Bluetooth 5.3, USB-C". Keep claims to what your testing shows.

### Bullet points

Translate specs into outcomes. "40 hours of playback" means little until you say it covers a week of commuting. Cover compatibility, what is in the box, battery life under realistic use, charging, water resistance rating if tested, and warranty.

### Images and video

Show the product in use, the box contents, ports and buttons, size against a familiar object, and setup steps. A short video showing pairing or setup reduces support queries.

### Spec table

| Spec | Example |
|---|---|
| Battery | Hours of use in typical conditions |
| Connectivity | Bluetooth version, Wi-Fi bands, apps |
| Compatibility | Phones, operating systems, voice assistants |
| Power | Charger type, plug, input rating |
| Safety and marking | UKCA or CE, battery and recycling information |
| Support | Warranty length, security update period |

## Step 4: Make warranty and support clear

UK buyers have statutory rights when goods are faulty, and a manufacturer's warranty sits on top of those rights. Explain both plainly: what your warranty covers, for how long and how to claim. State the security update period for connected products. Give a UK-facing support email or chat, and publish troubleshooting guides for the questions you already receive. Good support content turns a potential return into a solved problem and often a good review.

## Step 5: Choose your channels

### Your own store

Your store lets you explain features, offer comparison charts, publish setup guides and keep customer data. Our [electronics marketing agency for UK brands](/uk/electronics-marketing-agency/) page sets out how we run your site, Amazon UK and paid search as one plan.

### Amazon UK

Many UK shoppers check Amazon at some point before buying electronics. Expect to provide compliance documents for many electrical categories. Build complete listings, use A+ content for comparison tables and use Sponsored Products on specific searches. Check fees and returns per model with the [profit margin calculator](/tools/profit-margin-calculator/) before you set a price.

### TikTok Shop UK, eBay and OnBuy

TikTok Shop UK suits gadgets with a visible "wow" moment: lights, mini projectors, phone accessories. eBay is strong for refurbished and accessory ranges. OnBuy adds reach with a different audience. Each marketplace has its own documentation and safety requirements, so prepare before you list.

### Retail context

Currys and Argos set expectations on delivery, installation, returns and price matching. You do not have to match their scale, but your listings and support should feel as reliable.

## Step 6: Plan for UK peaks

Electronics has a sharper calendar than most categories.

- **Black Friday and Cyber Monday.** The largest week for many gadget brands. Pick hero models, agree deals and stock early, and warm up audiences beforehand.
- **Christmas.** Gifting peaks in early December. Publish clear last order dates.
- **Boxing Day and January sales.** Shoppers return with gift money and vouchers.
- **Amazon Prime Day.** Usually in summer, important for brands selling mainly on Amazon UK.
- **Back to school and university.** Laptop accessories, headphones and desk gadgets from late July.
- **Summer.** Fans, outdoor speakers, travel adaptors and action cameras.

## Step 7: Collect genuine reviews

Ask buyers for reviews through the marketplace's approved tools and your own post-purchase emails. Never offer incentives for positive reviews or write reviews yourself; UK consumer law treats fake reviews seriously. Respond to negative reviews with help, and fix listings when reviews reveal a misunderstanding.

## Step 8: Manage returns and fraud

### Protection checklist

- Clear compatibility information to prevent wrong purchases.
- Serial number capture at dispatch for higher-value items.
- Tamper-evident packaging and photographs of outgoing parcels.
- Inspection of every return before refund.
- Refurbishment or resale routes for opened-but-working returns.
- Signature on delivery for higher-value orders.

Track return reasons by model each month. If "did not connect" keeps appearing, the setup guide needs work; if "not as described", the listing does.

## Step 9: Support customers after the sale

The first week after delivery decides whether a gadget gets a good review, a support ticket or a return.

- Send a setup email on the day of delivery with a short video and a link to the quick-start guide.
- Offer product registration so you can send firmware and security update notices.
- Answer support messages within a working day, and keep a library of answers to common questions.
- Suggest compatible accessories a few weeks later, not on day one.
- Remind customers how to recycle old equipment and batteries through your take-back route.

Customers who get help quickly tend to leave fair reviews and come back for accessories and upgrades.

## Common mistakes

- Placing products on the market before compliance checks are complete.
- Copying supplier specs without testing them.
- Forgetting WEEE and battery duties.
- Ignoring security rules for connected devices.
- Discounting the whole range for Black Friday instead of chosen hero models.
