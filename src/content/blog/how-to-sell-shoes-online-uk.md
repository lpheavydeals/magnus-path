If you want to know how to sell shoes online, UK shoppers will tell you what matters through their returns. Footwear is one of the most returned categories in online fashion because fit is personal and sizes vary between brands, lasts and even colourways. A pair that pinches goes back, and the courier, packaging and handling costs come out of your margin. This guide covers range, size and width charts, materials labelling, photography, channels, returns and ads for UK brands selling trainers, boots, sandals, school shoes and occasion footwear.

## Step 1: Know your range and your buyer

Different footwear buyers decide in different ways:

- **Trainer buyers** care about colourways, limited releases and how the shoe looks on foot.
- **Walkers and runners** judge a shoe on cushioning, grip on wet ground, waterproofing and whether a wide option exists.
- **Parents** shopping for the new term want sturdy school shoes with room to grow, delivered before September.
- **Occasion buyers** for weddings, races and parties care about delivery dates and easy size swaps.
- **Comfort buyers** look for wide fittings, removable insoles and room for orthotics.

Pick the two or three groups you serve and shape the size guide, photos and returns policy around them. A brand selling wide-fit walking shoes needs very different product pages from a brand releasing limited trainers.

## Step 2: Build size and width charts on foot length

Copying a supplier's generic size table is the fastest way to high returns. UK, EU and US sizes do not convert neatly, and your lasts may run differently from the industry average.

### How to build the chart

1. Measure the internal length of each size on your actual lasts, or ask your manufacturer for the figures.
2. Express each size as a foot length range in centimetres.
3. Convert to UK, EU and US sizes, and show children's sizes separately.
4. Add the width fitting for each style, using letters, words or both.
5. Explain how to measure at home: place the foot on a sheet of paper against a wall, mark the tip of the longest toe and measure the distance in centimetres.

### Example layout

| Foot length (cm) | UK | EU | US women's | Width fittings |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 23.0 to 23.5 | 4 | 37 | 6 | Standard, wide |
| 23.6 to 24.0 | 5 | 38 | 7 | Standard, wide |
| 24.1 to 24.6 | 6 | 39 | 8 | Standard, wide, extra wide |

Treat this as a layout example only. Your own measurements decide the numbers.

### Fit notes on every style

A one-line fit note helps more than any chart: "runs slightly small, go up half a size if between sizes" or "roomy toe box, suits wider feet". Update notes from return reasons and reviews every month.

## Step 3: Label materials and meet UK rules

Footwear has its own labelling rules in the UK. In general terms, shoes need to show the main material of three parts: the upper, the lining and sock, and the outer sole. This is usually done with standard pictograms or written words on the shoe or its packaging. Repeat the same information in your listings so online buyers can check it before ordering.

Other points to cover:

- **Consumer rights.** Online buyers generally have a cancellation period for most goods and separate rights when goods are faulty or not as described. Write your returns policy so it meets those rights, then decide what extra goodwill you offer.
- **Children's footwear.** Be careful with any claim about foot health or development. Describe features such as adjustable straps or flexible soles plainly.
- **Leather, vegan and recycled claims.** Only describe materials you can document, and avoid broad environmental claims you cannot back up, which the CMA and ASA have both warned brands about.
- **VAT.** Children's footwear can be zero-rated in some cases, which depends on size and type. Take accountancy advice.

We are not lawyers or regulatory advisers, so confirm the details with trading standards or a qualified specialist.

## Step 4: Photograph and film every style properly

Footwear shoppers want to see shape, sole, material and how the shoe sits on a foot. For each style, plan:

- Side, front, back, top and sole views on a plain background.
- Close-ups of stitching, lining and any branding.
- On-foot photos with a real person, ideally in more than one size.
- A short walking video showing flex and fit at the heel.
- Colour-accurate shots taken in daylight or with calibrated lighting.

Keep the same order and angles across the range so collection pages look tidy and shoppers can compare styles quickly.

## Step 5: Choose where to sell

### Your own store

Your store is where you keep full-price sales, the brand story and customer data. Build a fit finder, collection pages for terms people search, such as "wide fit walking shoes" or "school shoes for girls", and clear returns information. Our [footwear marketing agency for UK brands](/uk/footwear-marketing-agency/) page shows how we connect product pages, ads and returns data.

### Amazon UK

Amazon UK is where many people search for everyday shoes, slippers and school shoes. Use variations for size, width and colour correctly, fill in every attribute and keep sizes in stock. Missing sizes push shoppers to a rival listing.

### TikTok Shop UK

Trainers, sliders and fashion styles do well when creators film them on foot. Keep enough stock of core sizes before a creator push and make sure delivery times are realistic.

### eBay, OnBuy and Etsy

eBay suits trainers, resale and end-of-line stock. OnBuy adds reach for value ranges. Etsy suits handmade or customised shoes. JD Sports, Schuh and department stores set many shoppers' expectations on price and service, so look at their delivery and returns terms when you set yours.

### Keep everything consistent

Use the same product names, size charts and photos everywhere, and sync stock across channels so you never sell a size you do not have.

## Step 6: Set returns terms that work

Returns will never be zero, but they can be manageable.

- Offer free exchanges where margin allows, because an exchange keeps the sale.
- Use returns portals with label printing, drop-off points and lockers so the process is easy.
- Ask for a return reason with simple options: too small, too big, too narrow, too wide, not as pictured, changed mind.
- Inspect returns quickly and refund on time.
- Check margin after returns with the [profit margin calculator](/tools/profit-margin-calculator/) before you discount a style.

## Step 7: Get delivery and packaging right

UK shoppers are used to quick, tracked delivery and expect to choose between home delivery, lockers and parcel shops. Offer at least one tracked service through Royal Mail, Evri, DPD or InPost, and show cut-off times for next-day dispatch, especially before Christmas and the start of term.

Ship shoes in their box inside a sturdy outer mailer so the shoebox arrives undamaged; a crushed box makes a new pair look used and often triggers a return. Include a printed returns slip with exchange instructions and a QR code to the size guide. Set a free delivery threshold that encourages a second pair or accessories such as insoles, socks and care kits, and check it still leaves a margin once returns are counted.

## Step 8: Run ads on a clean catalogue

### Feed checklist

- Product titles with brand, style, gender, colour and key feature.
- Size and width as separate attributes.
- Accurate stock per size, updated often.
- Sale prices that match the landing page.
- Images with plain backgrounds for Shopping and on-foot images for social.

### Where to advertise

- **Google Shopping and Performance Max** for people searching by style or need.
- **Meta catalogue ads** for retargeting and new styles.
- **TikTok** for trainers and fashion, with creator content.
- **Amazon Sponsored Products** for everyday and school shoes.

Pause ads for styles that are out of stock in core sizes so you do not pay for frustrated clicks.

## How to sell shoes online: UK listings that convert

### Titles

Match how people search: brand, style, gender, colour, width and main feature. For example: "Women's wide fit leather ankle boots, black, side zip".

### Descriptions

Cover fit, feel, materials, care and occasion in short sections. Tell shoppers whether the style suits orthotics, how stiff it is when new and how to clean it.

### Reviews and questions

Ask buyers about fit in your review request. "Fits true to size" and "roomy toe" comments help the next shopper more than star ratings alone.

## Common mistakes

- Size charts copied from suppliers without checking your lasts.
- No width information, so wide-footed buyers guess.
- Photos only on a white background, never on foot.
- Ads that keep running when core sizes have sold out.
- Measuring success by orders rather than sales kept after returns.
