Learning how to sell shoes online is mostly about solving one problem well: fit. Buyers cannot try shoes on, sizes differ from brand to brand, and many orders turn into exchanges or returns. Brands that win online make choosing the right size easy, show the shoe honestly on a real foot, pick the right marketplaces for their range and keep exchanges fast. This guide covers each step for Indian footwear brands and makers, from size charts to catalogue ads.

## Step 1: Know your range and buyer

Footwear is not one category. A sneaker brand, a leather formal shoe maker, a jutti label and a school shoe supplier sell to different people in different ways.

Before you build anything, write down:

- **Category.** Sneakers, sports and running, formal leather, casual sandals and slides, ethnic (juttis, mojaris, kolhapuris), heels, kids' and school shoes.
- **Buyer.** Who pays and who wears. For kids' shoes the buyer is a parent; for wedding footwear it may be a family member.
- **Price position.** Value, mid-range or premium. This decides marketplaces, return terms and how much you can spend on ads.
- **Hero styles.** The two to five styles you will push hardest, ideally in your strongest sizes and colours.

### Tamil Nadu and the leather footwear base

Many Indian shoe makers already have production strength. Tamil Nadu's leather clusters around Ambur, Vaniyambadi, Ranipet and Chennai have long supplied leather and footwear for export and domestic brands. Makers moving from export or wholesale into their own online brand have quality and capacity on their side. What they usually need is the consumer side: sizing for Indian buyers, product photography, listings and ads.

## Step 2: Build size charts on foot length

This is the single most useful change most shoe sellers can make.

### How to build the chart

1. **Measure each style.** Sizes vary between lasts, so do not copy a generic chart. Measure a sample in every size.
2. **Use foot length in centimetres.** List the foot length each size fits, not just the label size.
3. **Give conversions.** Show UK, Euro and US sizes side by side. Indian buyers commonly use UK sizes, but many check Euro too.
4. **Add width.** Say if a style is narrow, regular or wide. Wide feet are common, and width is rarely mentioned.
5. **Explain how to measure.** Stand on paper, mark the heel and longest toe, measure the gap, and measure in the evening when feet are slightly larger.

### Example layout

| UK | Euro | US (men) | Fits foot length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 40 | 7 | measure each style |
| 7 | 41 | 8 | measure each style |
| 8 | 42 | 9 | measure each style |
| 9 | 43 | 10 | measure each style |

Fill in the last column from your own measurements. Conversions also vary by brand, so check yours against your lasts.

### Fit notes on every style

Add a one-line fit note to each product: "runs half a size small", "roomy toe box", "true to size, regular width". Update notes when reviews or exchange data tell you something new. Offer quick size help by WhatsApp or chat for buyers who are unsure.

## Step 3: Shoot photos and video that show the real shoe

Footwear buyers look closely at shape, material and sole. Shoot each style consistently:

- **Standard angles.** Side profile, front, back, top-down, sole and a pair shot.
- **Detail close-ups.** Leather grain, stitching, lining, insole, logo and hardware.
- **On-foot images.** Worn by a real person, with trousers or outfits that suit the style.
- **Walking video.** A few seconds of walking shows flex, cushioning and how the shoe sits.
- **Scale cues.** A side view on foot helps buyers judge bulk and heel height.

Keep backgrounds and lighting consistent across the range so the catalogue looks organised. Check each marketplace's image rules on background colour, image size and number of images, as these differ and can change.

## Step 4: Choose where to sell

### Your own store

A Shopify or WooCommerce store lets you control size help, exchanges, bundles and customer data. Product pages should show the size chart beside the size selector, fit notes, care instructions and exchange terms. Show only sizes in stock, or mark sold-out sizes clearly with a back-in-stock alert.

### Myntra and Ajio

These fashion marketplaces suit style-led footwear: sneakers, heels, occasion and ethnic shoes. Buyers browse by brand, trend and look. Expect stricter catalogue and image standards, and plan around their own sale events.

### Amazon and Flipkart

These suit everyday, sports, school and value footwear, where buyers search for a need: "running shoes for men", "black school shoes". Fill every attribute, including size, width where available, material, sole type and closure.

### Keep everything consistent

Use the same size chart, fit notes and core images on every channel. Inconsistent sizing across channels confuses buyers and leads to mismatched reviews. Our [footwear marketing agency](/industries/footwear-marketing-agency/) work starts with this consistency before ads.

## Step 5: Set exchange and return terms that work

Exchanges are part of selling shoes online. Plan for them rather than fighting them.

- **Prefer exchanges.** A size swap keeps the sale. Make it the easy default.
- **Clear timeline.** Say how many days buyers have and what condition shoes must be in.
- **Protect the product.** Ask buyers to try shoes indoors and keep the box and tags.
- **Fast reverse pickup.** Slow exchanges turn into refunds and poor reviews.
- **Record reasons.** "Too small", "too narrow", "colour different" each point to a different fix.

Marketplaces set their own return rules, so check the current policy for your category on each one.

## Step 6: Control COD and RTO

Cash on delivery remains common for footwear in India, and so does return to origin, where the buyer refuses the parcel. To reduce it:

- Confirm COD orders by OTP or WhatsApp before dispatch.
- Offer a small prepaid benefit such as faster dispatch or free shipping.
- Watch RTO by pin code, style and campaign, and set rules for high-risk areas.
- Make sure product pages and ads show the real colour and shape, so buyers are not surprised.

## Step 7: Run ads on clean catalogues

Footwear ranges multiply quickly: ten styles in four colours and eight sizes is hundreds of variants. Ads depend on a clean product feed.

### Feed checklist

- Correct title with style, colour, gender and key feature
- Size, colour, gender, age group and material attributes filled
- Live stock by size, synced at least daily
- Main image matches the colour variant
- Sale price and regular price accurate

### Where to advertise

- **Meta catalogue ads** show the right style to past visitors and similar audiences, and pair well with on-foot and creator video.
- **Google Shopping and search** capture buyers searching for specific styles.
- **Marketplace ads** on Myntra, Ajio, Amazon and Flipkart promote listings inside each platform.

Judge campaigns on net sales after exchanges and returns, not on first orders.

## How to sell shoes online with better listings

Whatever the channel, the listing does the selling. A strong shoe listing answers questions in the order buyers ask them.

### Titles that match how people search

Buyers search by type, use and feature: "men's white leather sneakers", "women's block heel sandals", "kids' black school shoes with velcro". Write titles with brand, gender, style, key material or feature and colour. Avoid stuffing every keyword; marketplaces and Google both reward clear, readable titles.

### Descriptions that cover fit, feel and care

Use short bullet points:

- Upper material, lining and sole
- Fit and width note
- Closure type: laces, velcro, slip-on, buckle
- Heel height or sole thickness where relevant
- Weight, if the shoe is notably light
- Care instructions
- Exchange terms in one line

### Reviews and questions

Reviews that mention sizing are the most useful content a footwear listing can have. After delivery, ask buyers how the fit felt, through the platform's own review tools on marketplaces. Answer questions on listings quickly and update fit notes when a pattern appears.

### Price and margin

Footwear margins must absorb marketplace fees, shipping, packaging, exchanges and returns. Work out the cost per pair for each channel before you set prices or plan discounts, and keep room for sale-event deals without selling at a loss.

## Common mistakes when selling shoes online

- Copying a generic size chart instead of measuring each style.
- No width information, so wide-footed buyers return pairs.
- Photos only on a white background, with no on-foot shots.
- Different size charts on different marketplaces.
- Running ads on styles with broken size runs.
- Treating every exchange as a loss instead of a chance to keep the customer.
