Knowing how to sell ayurvedic products in UAE markets matters to two groups at once: Indian manufacturers looking for their first serious export market, and UAE importers and distributors who want a dependable Ayurveda range on their shelves and websites. The Emirates are close to India, home to a very large Indian community and well connected to the rest of the Gulf. The hard part is paperwork. Products that sell freely in India may need registration, testing and new labels before they can be sold in Dubai or Abu Dhabi, and a few may not be allowed at all.

This guide covers entry models, product routes, documents, labels, logistics, supplier checks and a first-year plan. The rules are described in general terms. We are an ecommerce growth team, so confirm every detail with a UAE regulatory adviser, a customs broker and your legal team.

## Why the UAE is often the first export market

Several things make the Emirates a natural starting point for Indian Ayurveda brands:

- **A ready audience.** Indian families already know chyawanprash, hair oils and herbal teas, so early sales do not depend on education.
- **Short supply lines.** Sea freight from Indian ports is quick, and air freight is practical for samples and urgent restocks.
- **A regional hub.** Warehousing, payments and delivery are well developed, and many brands later use the UAE as a base for the wider GCC.
- **Curious newer buyers.** Residents from many other countries are open to herbal wellness when it is explained clearly.

None of this removes the need for registration. It simply means the effort is more likely to pay back than in a distant market with no existing demand.

## Choose an entry model

Who holds the registrations decides who controls the brand in the UAE. Decide this early.

| Model | How it works | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Exclusive distributor | A UAE company buys stock, registers products and sells to retail and online | Registrations may sit in the distributor's name, making a later switch slow |
| Importer plus your own online sales | The importer clears and stores stock; you run the store and marketing | Agree who owns customer data, pricing and marketplace accounts |
| Your own UAE company | You register, import and sell directly, sometimes from a free zone | Set-up time and running costs; take company-formation advice |
| Marketplace-led launch through a partner | A local seller lists your range on Noon and Amazon.ae | Less control over listings, pricing and brand presentation |

Indian makers often begin with a distributor and move to their own entity once sales justify it. If you do sign with a distributor, write down who holds each registration and what happens to it if the agreement ends.

## How to sell ayurvedic products in UAE: settle each product's route

The UAE does not have one category called Ayurveda. Each product is placed on a route according to what it is and how it is presented.

### Cosmetic and personal care products

Hair oils, face oils, soaps, ubtans and most skincare usually fall here. In Dubai these are generally registered with Dubai Municipality. Keep the presentation cosmetic: talk about cleansing, nourishing and the look and feel of skin and hair, not about treating conditions.

### Foods and supplement-type products

Herbal teas, spice blends, ghee-based products and some powders may be treated as foods or supplements, with their own registration and labelling steps. Halal status may be asked about for certain ingredients, so check sources such as ghee, gelatine capsules and honey.

### Health or herbal products

Capsules, tablets, tonics and powders presented for wellbeing may need registration as health or herbal products with the Ministry of Health and Prevention or the Emirates Drug Establishment, depending on category. This route usually takes the longest and asks for the most evidence.

### Products to hold back

Classical formulations containing metals or minerals, alcohol-based arishtas and asavas, and any product sold in India on disease-related claims deserve extra caution. Some may be refused or need far more work than their sales justify. Launch without them and revisit later with your adviser.

## Paperwork from the Indian side

Indian exporters generally need their usual export registrations, plus documents the UAE side will request for registration and clearance. Typical items include:

- Manufacturing licence details and a free sale certificate
- Evidence of good manufacturing practice
- Full formulas with ingredient specifications and botanical names
- Certificates of analysis for each batch
- Heavy metal, pesticide residue and microbial test reports from an accredited laboratory
- Shelf-life or stability data
- A certificate of origin and commercial invoice for each shipment

Test every batch, not only the first. A failed test after registration is far more damaging than a delayed launch.

## Labels and packaging for the Emirates

Labels are generally expected to show key details in Arabic, usually alongside English. Plan artwork with space for both languages, the importer's details and any registration number your adviser says should appear. Use metric quantities and clear dates.

Remove wording that works in India but not in the UAE. Disease names on the pack, comparisons with medicines and words that promise a result all need to go. If you sell the same product in both countries, a separate UAE pack is often cleaner than stickers.

## Customs, storage and summer heat

Use a customs broker familiar with health and personal care goods, and send label artwork and documents ahead of the shipment so nothing is held at the port. Agree in advance who pays duty and VAT and how they appear in your pricing.

Heat is the practical risk most exporters underestimate. Oils, ghee-based products and herbal pastes can change in a container or warehouse during summer. Ask for temperature-controlled storage where needed and avoid shipping large volumes in the hottest months without a plan.

Think about shelf life as well. Registration and clearance take time, and stock that arrives with only a few months left before expiry is hard to sell through a distributor, who will usually want a generous remaining life. Plan production runs around the UAE launch date rather than shipping whatever is already in the warehouse, and keep a record of batch numbers by shipment so any recall or query can be traced quickly.

## For UAE importers and distributors: choosing a supplier

If you are on the UAE side, vet suppliers as carefully as the regulator will vet their products.

1. Ask for licences, test reports and stability data before samples, not after.
2. Check that the factory tests every batch and can show recent reports.
3. Confirm the supplier will prepare a UAE-specific label and hand over registration documents quickly.
4. Agree minimum order quantities, lead times and who pays for failed batches.
5. Request a video walkthrough of the factory if a visit is not practical.
6. Start with a small range of fast-moving products and widen it once reorders prove demand.

### Marketing to two audiences

Indian residents respond to familiar brands, Diwali and Onam gift boxes and fast reorders. Newer buyers respond to ingredient stories, clear Arabic and English explanations and short videos of a ritual, such as warming an oil or brewing a tea. Social posts from creators need care: the UAE Media Council expects paid creators to hold an advertiser permit, and every caption should avoid health claims.

Retail moments help both groups. Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, White Friday, the Dubai Shopping Festival and UAE National Day bring gift and discount shoppers, and the cooler months from October suit oil massage content.

### Pricing through the chain

Work out the shelf price backwards before you agree a distributor margin. A UAE retail price has to carry freight, duty, VAT, registration and testing, storage, the distributor's or marketplace's share, delivery to the customer and some marketing. Many Indian makers discover that their domestic price structure leaves almost nothing once these are added. It is usually wiser to launch fewer products at a sustainable price than the full range at a price that cannot fund advertising.

Pack sizes matter here too. A larger family pack or a bundle of two complementary products often carries the cost of import better than a single small jar, and suits UAE buyers who like to stock up.

### Building trust as a newer brand

Indian residents may know your name from home, but many newer buyers will not. Show where and how products are made, publish batch test summaries in plain language where your adviser agrees, and answer questions quickly on WhatsApp in English and Arabic. Have a fluent Arabic writer prepare or review every Arabic line. Reviews that describe texture, aroma and routine help more than star ratings alone.

## Common mistakes

- Signing a distributor before agreeing who owns registrations
- Shipping before labels and documents are approved
- Copying Indian claims straight onto UAE packs and ads
- Leaving heat-sensitive products in unplanned summer storage
- Launching the full catalogue instead of a tested core range
- Assuming a UAE registration covers Saudi Arabia or Oman

## A cautious first-year plan

1. **Months one to three:** choose the entry model, shortlist products with clear routes, appoint an adviser and commission batch testing.
2. **Months four to six:** complete registrations, finalise bilingual labels, book freight and storage, and set up the store and marketplace accounts.
3. **Months seven to nine:** launch to the Indian community with familiar products, timed if possible before Diwali or Onam.
4. **Months ten to twelve:** add content for newer buyers, review reorder rates and decide which products to take into the wider GCC.
