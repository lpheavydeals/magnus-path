This guide explains how to sell skincare products online: USA rules, channels and seasons, in the order you will meet them. Founders often expect the answer to be about ads or marketplaces, but the harder part comes earlier. US shoppers compare actives and percentages, read ingredient decks, watch creator tests and expect fast, free shipping, while federal and state rules shape what you may put on a label or in an ad. The steps below run from classification and compliance through your range, store, marketplaces, advertising, fulfillment, tax and reorders.

## Step 1: Work out whether you sell a cosmetic or a drug

The first question in US skincare is not "who is my customer?" but "what am I legally selling?" This section is general information, not legal advice. Confirm every point with a US regulatory adviser.

### The cosmetic vs drug line

The FDA looks at intended use, and intended use is largely read from your claims. A product meant to cleanse, moisturize or improve appearance is generally a cosmetic. Promise to treat or prevent a condition, or to change how skin functions, and the same formula can be classed as a drug. A single sentence in an ad or on a product page can move it across that line.

### OTC sunscreen and acne products

Anything with an SPF value or a sun-protection claim is an over-the-counter drug. So are most acne products built on actives such as salicylic acid or benzoyl peroxide. These follow the OTC monograph system: permitted actives within set ranges, Drug Facts labeling, drug establishment registration and specific testing. If your routine includes an SPF, give it its own timeline and use a manufacturer with OTC experience.

### MoCRA duties for cosmetics

MoCRA, the cosmetics law Congress passed in 2022, widened what the FDA can ask of cosmetic brands. In broad terms it covers facility registration, product listing, safety substantiation, reporting serious adverse events and keeping records of them, plus a US contact on the label for those reports. Some small businesses are exempt from parts of it. Your manufacturer and adviser should tell you who registers what.

### Labels and color additives

Cosmetic labels generally need an identity statement, net contents in both US and metric units, an ingredient list in descending order, the name and place of business of the manufacturer or distributor, and warnings where required. Color additives must be approved for their intended use, and some need FDA batch testing. A tinted moisturizer or color-correcting cream needs this checked before you print boxes.

### State rules: California Prop 65

California's Proposition 65 requires a warning when a product can expose people to listed chemicals above certain levels. It applies to online sales shipped into California, and some marketplaces ask about it at listing. Ask your formulator and adviser whether any ingredient or packaging component triggers it.

## Step 2: Build a short routine, not a catalog

A US launch with twenty products spreads budget too thin. Fewer products mean clearer messages and less cash tied up in stock.

- **Sell a routine.** Three to five products that work together give buyers a reason to add a second item.
- **Pick one clear angle.** A single active at a stated strength, a skin concern, a texture or a sensitive-skin promise.
- **Plan the reorder.** Cleansers, moisturizers and SPF run out and bring people back. Masks and treatments alone rarely carry a brand.
- **Plan sizes for each channel.** Travel minis for trial and TikTok Shop impulse buys, full sizes for the store, multipacks for Amazon.

Work out unit economics for each size before you commit to packaging. Include cost of goods, packaging, pick-and-pack, shipping, payment fees, marketplace referral and fulfillment fees, returns and ad cost per order. ## Step 3: Build a Shopify store that answers label readers

On your own store you set the price, tell the full story and keep the customer relationship. Shopify is a common choice for US beauty brands because subscriptions, reviews, quizzes and fast checkouts are easy to add.

### What a skincare product page needs

- The key active, its percentage and the visible change it is designed to deliver.
- Which skin types and concerns it suits, and who should skip it.
- Texture, finish and scent, with a short video in natural light.
- Where it sits in a morning or evening routine and what not to layer it with.
- A realistic timeline: what buyers might notice in days and in weeks.
- The full ingredient list and a patch-test reminder.
- Reviews filtered by skin type and age range.

### Content that brings search traffic

Guides on actives, layering, barrier care and sun protection pull in Google traffic and are cited in AI answers. They also give paid traffic a useful landing place. Keep the wording cosmetic: describe appearance, not conditions.

### Checkout

Offer cards, Shop Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay and PayPal, and consider buy now, pay later for gift sets. Show delivery dates, the return policy and a free-shipping threshold near the add-to-cart button. US shoppers compare your shipping with Amazon's, so be clear and quick.

## Step 4: Add US marketplaces in the right order

Marketplaces put you where many US shoppers already start. Each plays a different role.

| Channel | Who shops there | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon US | Shoppers searching by ingredient, concern and price | Search-led listings, A+ content, Sponsored Products, Subscribe & Save |
| TikTok Shop | Viewers who discover products in creator videos | Short demos, affiliate creators, live sessions for launches |
| Walmart Marketplace | Value-minded shoppers, many also in-store regulars | Core products and multipacks once operations are steady |
| Target Plus | Invitation-only marketplace on Target.com | A later step for brands with strong reviews and supply |
| Your Shopify store | Loyal buyers and people who want the full routine | Bundles, subscriptions, quizzes, exclusive sizes |

Sephora and Ulta shape what shoppers expect from ingredient detail, sampling and returns, even if you never sell there. Read their product pages as a benchmark for your own.

Keep prices consistent across channels so buyers do not learn to wait for a marketplace coupon. File a trademark so you can join Amazon Brand Registry, and keep an eye on third-party sellers offering your products, because old or diverted units can drag down your rating. TikTok Shop and Amazon may ask for extra documents for beauty categories, including safety data sheets and proof of compliance, so keep a document pack ready.

## Step 5: Advertise within FTC rules and platform policies

Paid ads bring most new skincare brands their first steady orders, and they are where most rejections happen.

### Claims in ads

Under FTC truth-in-advertising rules, you need evidence in hand before a claim goes live, not after a complaint. Phrases like "clinically shown" or "dermatologist tested", and any figure such as hours of hydration, should map to a specific study in your file. Keep cosmetic products to appearance claims, and save "treats acne" or SPF claims for products actually sold as OTC drugs.

### Meta, TikTok and Google

On Meta and TikTok, short videos showing texture, application and routine perform well. Both platforms restrict before-and-after images, content that implies a viewer's personal traits ("Tired of your acne?") and unrealistic results. Google Search and Shopping catch buyers with clear intent, such as "fragrance-free retinol serum", so a clean product feed with accurate titles matters as much as bidding.

### Creators and the Endorsement Guides

Under the FTC Endorsement Guides, creators who get free product, payment or affiliate commission should disclose it clearly in the post itself. Brands are expected to brief creators and monitor what they post. Put the disclosure wording in every brief and keep a log of posts.

Judge ads by product, not only by campaign. A cheap-to-win serum buyer who never returns is often worth less than a pricier-to-win cleanser buyer who reorders on a steady cycle.

## Step 6: Fulfillment, returns and sales tax

### Shipping and returns

Liquids leak, pumps break and glass cracks, and damaged parcels become refunds and bad reviews. Use leak-proof seals, snug inserts and drop-tested boxes. Decide early between your own warehouse, a third-party logistics provider and Fulfillment by Amazon, since many brands use a mix. Opened skincare usually cannot be resold, so write a clear return policy and consider credit or exchange for reactions.

### Sales tax nexus

After the Supreme Court's Wayfair decision, most states require remote sellers to collect sales tax once sales into that state pass its economic nexus threshold. Thresholds and taxability differ by state. Marketplace facilitator laws mean Amazon, Walmart and TikTok Shop generally collect and remit tax on their own orders, but your Shopify sales are your responsibility. A sales tax app plus a US tax adviser keeps this under control.

## Step 7: Build reorders and honest reviews

Skincare is a routine purchase, and the second order is where the profit is.

### A post-purchase journey

1. First few days: application tips and the routine step it belongs to.
2. Week 2: a check-in and a link to a layering or routine guide.
3. Before the bottle runs out: a refill reminder, by email or by SMS for customers who opted in.
4. After the reorder: a review request with photo upload.

### Subscriptions and sampling

Subscribe-and-save on your store and on Amazon keeps routines going, as long as skipping or cancelling stays simple, which regulators watch closely. Charge something for minis and credit it toward the full size; giveaways tend to pull in freebie hunters rather than future customers.

### Reviews within the rules

The FTC rule on fake reviews and testimonials bans buying, faking or suppressing reviews. Send the request to all verified buyers, once enough time has passed for them to form a fair view.

## Plan around the US skincare calendar

Black Friday and Cyber Monday drive gift sets and stock-up offers, then the holidays carry gifting into December. Valentine's Day suits lip and body care, Mother's Day in May is a strong gifting moment, and Prime Day, usually in July, rewards brands with healthy Amazon stock. SPF demand rises from late spring through summer, back to school brings travel sizes and simple routines for students, and rich moisturizers sell through the winter.
