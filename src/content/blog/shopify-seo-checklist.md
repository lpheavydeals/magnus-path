This Shopify SEO checklist is a practical, step-by-step list you can work through to help your store rank on Google and show up in AI answers. Shopify handles many basics for you, but it also has quirks: fixed URL prefixes, duplicate product paths and apps that slow pages down. Most stores we review have the same gaps. Work through the steps below in order, tick them off, and repeat the monthly routine at the end.

If you are new to this, treat it as Shopify SEO for beginners: the setup and collection sections alone will cover most of the gains. If your store is established, the technical and AI search sections are where you will find the hidden problems.

## Setup

1. **Connect Google Search Console.** Verify your store domain and check which pages are indexed, which have errors and which search terms bring impressions.
2. **Submit your sitemap.** Shopify creates one automatically at /sitemap.xml. Submit it in Search Console and check it is processed without errors.
3. **Use one primary domain.** Set your main domain in Shopify settings so the myshopify.com address and any other domains redirect to it.
4. **Choose www or non-www and stick to it.** Shopify handles the redirect once your primary domain is set.
5. **Remove the store password.** A password-protected store cannot be indexed. Check this after launch.
6. **Set up analytics.** Connect Google Analytics 4 so you can see which organic pages drive sales, not just visits.
7. **Write a clear homepage title and meta description.** Include your main category and brand name.

## Technical

8. **Fix collection-based product URLs.** Shopify makes products reachable at /collections/name/products/item as well as /products/item. Shopify sets a canonical tag to the /products/ version, but many themes link to the collection path internally. Edit the theme so product links go straight to /products/ URLs.
9. **Check canonical tags.** Spot-check product, collection and paginated pages to confirm canonicals point where you expect.
10. **Handle tag and filter pages.** Tag pages and filtered collection URLs can create thin, near-duplicate pages. Keep them out of the index unless they target a real search with unique content.
11. **Improve speed.** Use a lightweight theme, compress images and limit third-party scripts. Most Indian shoppers browse on mobile, so test on a mid-range phone, not just a laptop.
12. **Audit your apps.** Each app can add scripts to every page. Remove apps you do not use, and check that uninstalled apps have not left code in the theme.
13. **Add structured data.** Confirm product pages output Product schema with price, availability and reviews where you have them. Add Organization, Breadcrumb and FAQ schema where relevant. Test with Google's Rich Results Test.
14. **Set up redirects.** When you delete or rename a product or collection, add a 301 redirect in Shopify's URL redirects to the closest relevant page.
15. **Fix broken links and 404s.** Check Search Console for not-found pages that still get links or traffic.
16. **Keep the robots.txt sensible.** Shopify's default is usually fine. Edit it only for a clear reason, such as blocking filter parameters.

## Collection pages

Collection pages often target the most valuable searches, such as "cotton kurtas for women" or "ayurvedic face wash". Give them proper attention.

17. **Map one main keyword to each collection.** Avoid two collections chasing the same search.
18. **Write a unique title tag and meta description** using that keyword naturally.
19. **Add useful copy.** A short intro above the products and a longer helpful section below, covering what to look for, sizes, materials or use cases.
20. **Use a clear H1** that matches the collection name and search intent.
21. **Add collection FAQs** for common buyer questions. These also help with AI answers.
22. **Keep collections stocked.** A collection with mostly sold-out products sends a weak signal.

## Product pages

23. **Write original product descriptions.** Do not copy supplier or marketplace text word for word.
24. **Use descriptive titles** with product type and key attributes, not just a style code.
25. **Write a unique meta description** for top products, focused on benefits and key details.
26. **Show reviews on the page** so shoppers and search engines can see them.
27. **Add specifications and care details** in a scannable format.
28. **Handle variants carefully.** Keep colour and size as variants of one product rather than separate thin pages, unless each variant has real search demand of its own.
29. **Decide what happens to discontinued products.** Redirect to a close alternative, or keep the page with a clear message and links to similar items.

## Blog and content

30. **Answer real buyer questions.** Guides, comparisons and how-to articles that relate to your products, such as size guides or ingredient explainers.
31. **Plan topics from search data.** Use Search Console queries and customer questions, not guesses.
32. **Link each article to relevant collections and products.** Content that never links to products rarely helps sales.
33. **Update old posts** rather than writing near-duplicates.

## Internal linking

34. **Build a clear menu structure** with main collections in the header navigation.
35. **Add breadcrumbs** so users and search engines understand the hierarchy.
36. **Link related collections** from collection copy, and related products from product pages.
37. **Use descriptive anchor text** like "linen shirts for men" instead of "click here".

## Images

38. **Compress images** before upload and use modern formats where your theme supports them.
39. **Name files descriptively** (for example, blue-cotton-saree.jpg) before uploading.
40. **Write alt text** that describes the image and naturally includes the product type.
41. **Check lazy loading** on images below the fold, but not on the main hero or first product image.

## International (Shopify Markets)

42. **Set up markets properly.** If you sell to the UK, UAE, Australia, Singapore or the USA, use Shopify Markets with country or language subfolders or domains.
43. **Check hreflang tags.** Shopify Markets adds hreflang automatically for published markets. View the page source to confirm each version references the others correctly.
44. **Localise, do not just translate.** Use local currency, spelling and terms where it matters, and avoid automatic redirects that block search engines from seeing each version.

## AI search readiness

Google AI Overviews reduce clicks to organic results, and AI-referred shoppers convert at higher rates than organic search (Similarweb/Adobe 2026 data). Your store needs to be easy for AI tools to understand and quote.

45. **Write clear, direct answers** on collection, product and FAQ sections, in short sentences that can be quoted.
46. **Keep structured data complete** so product details, prices and availability are machine-readable.
47. **Build an informative About page** with who you are, where you are based and what you sell.
48. **Keep product facts consistent** across your store, marketplaces and social profiles.

## Priority vs effort

Not every item matters equally. Use this table to decide where to start.

| Unique titles and meta descriptions on top pages | High | Low |
| Collection page copy and FAQs | High | Medium |
| Fixing collection-based product links in the theme | High | Medium |
| Removing unused apps and improving speed | High | Medium |
| Structured data checks | Medium | Low to medium |
| Redirects and broken links | Medium | Low |

## Monthly routine

SEO is not a one-time job. Each month:

- Review Search Console for new errors, indexing drops and fresh search queries.
- Check top collections and products for ranking changes and update copy where needed.
- Add redirects for any products or collections removed that month.
- Test speed on your homepage, a key collection and a key product page.
- Review installed apps and remove anything unused.
- Publish or update at least one helpful article linked to your collections.
- Check how your brand and products appear in AI answers for your main searches.

## Common Shopify SEO mistakes

- **Leaving supplier descriptions unchanged.** The same text appears on many sites, so your page adds nothing new.
- **Installing an app for every small need.** Each one adds weight, and leftover code often stays after removal.
- **Deleting products without redirects.** Links and rankings built over months are lost overnight.
- **Thin collection pages.** A grid of products with no copy gives Google little to rank.
- **Changing themes without an SEO check.** A new theme can drop structured data, headings or internal links.
- **Ignoring Search Console.** Many problems show up there weeks before sales fall.
