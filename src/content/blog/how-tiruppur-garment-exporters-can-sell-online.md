Many Tiruppur garment exporters sell online today, or plan to, because export orders alone can be uncertain and the margin sits mostly with the brand that sells to the final customer. Tiruppur already has what most new clothing brands lack: knitting, dyeing, printing and finishing skill, and the discipline of meeting overseas quality standards. What it often lacks is the retail side, meaning product pages, size charts, ads, returns handling and a brand that buyers remember. This guide explains how a Tiruppur export or contract unit can build that side step by step, on Indian marketplaces, its own Shopify store and, later, cross-border.

## Why Tiruppur garment exporters sell online now

Tiruppur is one of India's major knitwear and textile clusters, and much of its output carries other companies' labels. Selling online under your own name changes three things:

- **Margin.** Retail prices leave room that contract pricing does not, even after marketplace fees, ads and returns.
- **Stability.** A domestic online channel keeps machines and people busy between export seasons.
- **Ownership.** Customer data, reviews and a brand name are assets that grow in value; a purchase order is not.

It also brings new costs and risks: inventory you must sell yourself, returns, photography, ads and customer service. Treat it as a new business unit, with its own small team and budget, not a side project for the merchandising department.

## B2B and D2C: what changes

The biggest shift is from selling to a few buyers who order in thousands to selling to thousands of buyers who order one or two pieces.

| Area | Export or B2B | Online D2C |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | A brand or importer with a spec sheet | An individual comparing photos and prices on a phone |
| Order size | Hundreds or thousands of pieces per style | One or two pieces per order |
| Design | Often supplied by the buyer | Yours, so it must be original |
| Sizing | Buyer's size specs | Your own size chart, which must match Indian bodies and expectations |
| Payment | Letters of credit or agreed terms | Prepaid or COD, with refusals and refunds |
| Returns | Rare, handled by claims | Common, mostly for fit |
| Marketing | Trade fairs, agents, LinkedIn | Photos, reels, ads, reviews and marketplace search |

## Step 1: choose a focused first range

Do not put your whole capability online at once. Pick a small range where you are clearly strong and where online demand is steady:

- Cotton basics: T-shirts, polos, innerwear, loungewear and nightwear.
- Kidswear and babywear, where soft cotton and safe finishing matter.
- Activewear or athleisure, if you already make performance fabrics.
- Printed tees, if you have in-house design and printing.

Start with perhaps 10 to 30 styles in a few colours, and stock enough sizes to avoid constant stock-outs on the popular ones. A narrow range is easier to photograph, list, advertise and restock.

### Keep your export work and brand separate

Never sell a buyer's designs, prints or labelled goods without written permission. Many contracts forbid it. If your contracts allow sale of surplus, sell it through a clearly labelled outlet collection or a separate marketplace account, so discounted export overruns do not set the price buyers expect from your new brand.

## Step 2: build a simple brand

A brand does not need to be expensive, but it must be consistent:

- **A name** you can trademark and get as a website domain and Instagram handle.
- **Woven or printed labels and care labels** with the details required for garments sold in India.
- **Packaging** that survives courier handling and looks tidy when opened.
- **Photography** on models and flat-lay, in true colour, with fabric close-ups.
- **A short story**: where it is made, what fabric and finish you use, and why your quality is better than generic sellers.

Register for GST in the states you need, set up a business bank account and keep your invoicing clean from day one. Marketplaces will ask for these details during onboarding.

## Step 3: pick your online channels

Most Tiruppur brands use a mix of marketplaces and their own store.

### Amazon and Flipkart

These are often the easiest to start on and reach buyers across India, including smaller towns. Focus on clean titles, all images allowed, a full size chart, and fair prices after referral fees, shipping and returns. Use our [marketplace profit calculator](/tools/marketplace-profit-calculator/) to check each style's margin before you list it.

### Myntra and Ajio

These fashion marketplaces attract buyers looking for brands and trends. They may review your brand, catalogue and photography before onboarding, and their image and content guidelines are strict. They are a good second step once you have a consistent catalogue.

### Your own Shopify store

Your store is where you keep the customer: email and WhatsApp contacts, reviews, bundles, new launches and repeat orders. It also lets you run Meta ads to your own pages, where you set the story and price. Many Tiruppur brands launch the store alongside one or two marketplaces from the start.

## Step 4: get size charts and returns right

Fit is the main reason clothes come back. Export production teaches strict measurement, so use that strength:

1. Measure the actual garment for every size, not the pattern.
2. Publish chest, length, shoulder and sleeve in centimetres and inches.
3. State the fit plainly: regular, slim, relaxed or oversized.
4. Show the model's height and the size they wear.
5. Add a "how to measure" image, ideally in Tamil and English if you sell in the south.

On returns:

- Offer size exchanges before refunds; many buyers just want the next size.
- Verify COD orders before dispatch by WhatsApp or call; our guide to [reducing RTO and COD returns](/blog/how-to-reduce-rto-cod-returns/) has the full method.
- Track returns by style and size every week. A style with repeated fit returns needs a pattern or chart fix, not more ad budget.

## Step 5: market the brand

Online garment sales depend heavily on photos and video. A practical starting plan:

- **Product pages first.** Every style with model photos, fabric close-ups, the size chart and delivery dates.
- **Instagram reels.** Factory-to-wardrobe stories, fabric stretch and wash tests, try-ons and styling, in Tamil and English if you sell in Tamil Nadu.
- **Meta ads.** Start with prospecting to broad audiences using your strongest reels, then retarget visitors with catalogue ads. Our [ecommerce Meta ads agency in Coimbatore](/coimbatore/ecommerce-meta-ads-agency/) runs this for brands across Coimbatore and Tiruppur.
- **Marketplace ads.** Sponsored product ads on Amazon and Flipkart for your top-selling styles, with budgets moved weekly to what earns a profit.
- **Seasons.** Plan launches around summer cotton demand, back-to-school for kidswear, Pongal, Aadi sales and the Deepavali peak.

## Pricing and margins: what changes online

Export costing is built on fabric, trims, making charges and freight. Online retail adds a longer list, and missing any of it is the fastest way to lose money on orders that look profitable:

- **Marketplace fees:** referral or commission, closing fees and fulfilment fees, which vary by category and price band.
- **Shipping both ways:** forward delivery, plus return shipping on exchanges, refunds and refused COD parcels.
- **Returns:** some returned pieces come back creased, soiled or without tags and cannot be sold at full price.
- **Packaging:** polybags, boxes, tags and inserts for every single order.
- **Advertising:** marketplace sponsored ads and Meta ads, which can be a large share of the price in the first months.
- **Discounts:** festive offers and marketplace sale events.
- **GST:** included in the selling price shown to the buyer.

Price each style so it still earns a profit after all of these. A style that only works at export-style thin margins is usually better kept for B2B.

### Who runs the online business

The online channel needs people with retail skills: a catalogue and listings person, someone to handle orders, returns and customer messages, and either an in-house marketer or an agency for ads and content. Give the team its own targets, measured on profit after returns, so it is not judged by export metrics such as pieces per day.

## Step 6: test cross-border selling

Once domestic orders run smoothly, export experience makes cross-border retail a natural next step.

- **Amazon Global Selling** lets Indian sellers list on Amazon marketplaces abroad, such as the US and UK, using their own shipping or the marketplace's fulfilment centres. Branded basics, kidswear and loungewear with clear sizing can suit it. Our guide on [how to sell on Amazon USA from India](/blog/how-to-sell-on-amazon-usa-from-india/) explains the set-up.
- **Etsy** suits items you design or make, such as original prints, embroidered pieces or hand-finished garments, not plain basics. See our guide on [how to sell on Etsy from India](/blog/how-to-sell-on-etsy-from-india/).
- **Your own store abroad.** Shopify can show prices in other currencies and handle international shipping once you have demand.

Remember that overseas buyers expect their own size systems, so publish US, UK or EU size conversions alongside measurements.

## Checklist before launch

- [ ] First range of 10 to 30 styles chosen, with stock depth on core sizes.
- [ ] Brand name, trademark application, labels and packaging ready.
- [ ] GST, bank account and invoicing set up for marketplace onboarding.
- [ ] Model and flat-lay photos in true colour for every style.
- [ ] Garment-measured size charts in cm and inches on every product.
- [ ] Returns and exchange terms published; COD verification in place.
- [ ] Shopify store live with tracking; one or two marketplaces listed.
- [ ] Margin checked per style after fees, shipping, returns and ads.
