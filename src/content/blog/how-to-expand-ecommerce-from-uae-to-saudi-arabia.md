Many brands look to expand ecommerce from UAE to Saudi Arabia once their home market is running smoothly. It is the largest consumer market in the GCC, shoppers are comfortable buying online, and many already know UAE brands from travel and social media. But Saudi Arabia is not simply a bigger UAE. Product approvals, labelling, company set-up, tax, logistics and shopping seasons all work differently. This guide walks through each area in general terms so you can plan the right questions for your advisers. We are not lawyers or tax advisers, and rules change, so confirm every point with qualified professionals in the Kingdom.

## Step 1: Check whether your products can be sold as they are

Start with the products, not the marketing. Some categories can be sold with few changes, while others need approval before a single unit can enter the country.

### SFDA-regulated categories

The Saudi Food and Drug Authority (SFDA) oversees food and drink, cosmetics and personal care, supplements, medical devices and some related goods. Depending on the product, you may need:

- Product registration or notification in the SFDA system before sale.
- Ingredient and claim checks against Saudi rules, which may differ from UAE approvals.
- Arabic labelling that meets SFDA requirements, including product name, ingredients, usage, storage and the responsible party.
- A local representative or importer of record for some categories.

### Other consumer products

Many non-food goods, such as electronics, toys, textiles and household items, may need separate conformity certificates under Saudi product safety rules before customs clearance. Requirements vary by product type.

### Labelling and claims

Plan for Arabic labels that are complete in their own right, not just a sticker over English text. Review any wellness or performance claims carefully; claims that pass in one market may not be accepted in another. A regulatory consultant who works with Saudi approvals can save months of delay.

## Step 2: Choose your route to expand ecommerce from UAE to Saudi Arabia

There are several ways in, and many brands move from one to the next as they grow.

| Route | How it works | Things to check |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-border shipping | Sell to Saudi shoppers from UAE stock | Customs duty, VAT on import, delivery time, returns |
| Marketplace partner | A Saudi seller or service lists your products | Margin split, control of listings and pricing |
| Distributor | A Saudi company buys stock and handles approvals | Exclusivity, minimum orders, brand standards |
| Own Saudi entity | Your company holds a commercial registration | Set-up time, licences, local banking, staff |

### Company set-up in general terms

Setting up your own presence usually involves commercial registration through the Saudi Business Center, along with any investment licence that applies to foreign-owned companies, a local bank account and registrations with tax and other authorities. The details depend on ownership, activity and location, so work with a Saudi business adviser. ## Step 3: Understand VAT, duty and e-invoicing

Tax is where many expansion plans stall, so bring advisers in early.

- **VAT.** Saudi Arabia runs its own VAT system through the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA), at a different rate from the UAE. Your prices for Saudi shoppers need to be set with Saudi VAT included.
- **Customs duty.** Goods shipped from the UAE may carry customs duty depending on origin and classification, even within the GCC. Check how GCC origin rules apply to your products.
- **E-invoicing.** ZATCA requires registered businesses to issue electronic invoices in a set format, with requirements rolled out in phases. Your store, marketplace and accounting tools need to support this.

Use the [marketplace profit calculator](/tools/marketplace-profit-calculator/) to model your margin per product once you have fees, duty, VAT, shipping and returns, rather than copying your UAE prices.

## Step 4: Decide where to sell

### Noon and Amazon.sa

Both run separate Saudi marketplaces with their own seller accounts, fees and fulfilment programmes. They bring trusted delivery and search-led shoppers, which helps a brand that is not yet known locally. Our guide on [how to sell on Noon](/blog/how-to-sell-on-noon/) explains the seller set-up, and our [Amazon.ae and Noon agency](/uae/amazon-noon-agency/) page covers how we manage listings and ads across GCC marketplaces.

### Your own Saudi store

You can extend your existing Shopify store with a Saudi market, prices and currency, or build a separate store. Saudi platforms such as Salla and Zid are widely used by local merchants and integrate with local payments and couriers, which is useful context when you compare options. Whatever you choose, the store needs Arabic as a first language with right-to-left layouts, prices in riyals with VAT, and local payment methods.

### Payments

Offer mada debit cards, Apple Pay, credit cards, Tabby and Tamara instalments, and cash on delivery where your courier supports it. Check that your payment provider can settle Saudi sales in the way your chosen route needs.

## Step 5: Localise marketing for Saudi shoppers

Saudi shoppers are not one audience, and the market is not a copy of the UAE.

### Creative and language

- Write in Arabic first, with copy written or checked by a fluent Arabic writer familiar with Saudi expressions.
- Show settings, families and styling that feel local rather than reusing Dubai-focused creative.
- Respect cultural and religious norms in imagery and messaging, especially during Ramadan.

### Channels

Snapchat, TikTok, Instagram and YouTube are all important in Saudi Arabia, and Google Search catches intent-led shoppers. Saudi creators bring trust that a UAE account rarely can at first; make sure any paid creator content follows Saudi advertising rules. Run Saudi campaigns separately from UAE ones so you can see costs and results by country. Our [ecommerce Meta ads agency in Abu Dhabi](/uae/ecommerce-meta-ads-agency-abu-dhabi/) page explains how we structure catalogue and creative tests by country and language.

### Regions

Riyadh, Jeddah and the Eastern Province around Dammam and Khobar differ in taste, climate and delivery times. Start with the cities your couriers serve quickly and expand as you learn.

### Prices and offers

Set prices in riyals from your Saudi cost base rather than converting UAE prices. Check what similar products sell for on Noon and Amazon.sa, and decide whether you will match marketplace prices on your own store or offer something different, such as bundles, gift wrapping or exclusive colours. Keep promotions simple and clearly stated in Arabic, and make sure any "was" price or discount claim follows Saudi consumer rules. Watch how free delivery thresholds affect order value, because shipping across long distances changes the maths compared with the UAE.

## Step 6: Plan around Saudi seasons

| Moment | Notes for UAE brands |
|---|---|
| Founding Day (22 February) | Heritage themes and national pride; respectful creative |
| Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr | Gifting, hosting, fashion and fragrance; dates move each year |
| Eid al-Adha | Family visits, travel and occasion purchases |
| Back to school | Children's products, electronics and stationery |
| Saudi National Day (23 September) | Green-themed edits and national celebrations |
| 11.11, White Friday and Yellow Friday | Major sale period across marketplaces and stores |
| Seasonal entertainment events | City events can lift local demand; check yearly schedules |

Do not reuse UAE National Day creative for Saudi National Day or Founding Day. Build a separate Saudi calendar with its own lead times.

## Step 7: Sort logistics, COD and returns

Distances in Saudi Arabia are much larger than in the UAE, so delivery times and costs vary by city.

### Logistics checklist

1. Compare couriers by coverage, delivery days per city and COD handling.
2. Decide whether to ship from the UAE, use marketplace fulfilment or hold stock in a Saudi warehouse.
3. Show realistic delivery times by city on product pages.
4. Confirm cash-on-delivery orders on WhatsApp and request a map pin for unclear addresses.
5. Set a clear returns policy that follows Saudi consumer rules, and arrange local returns handling.
6. Track refusals, returns and delivery times by city every week.

Our guide on [how to reduce COD returns in the UAE](/blog/how-to-reduce-cod-returns-in-the-uae/) covers confirmation and fraud checks that also apply in Saudi Arabia.

## Common mistakes

- Launching ads before checking SFDA or conformity requirements.
- Copying UAE prices without Saudi VAT, duty and shipping.
- Using Dubai creative for Saudi audiences.
- Promising UAE-style delivery times across the whole Kingdom.
- Mixing UAE and Saudi results in one ad account view.

## Expansion checklist

- Product approvals and Arabic labels confirmed.
- Route chosen and company or partner set-up started.
- VAT, duty and e-invoicing mapped with a Saudi accountant.
- Marketplace accounts or Saudi store ready in Arabic.
- Payments, couriers and returns tested end to end.
- Saudi seasonal calendar and ad accounts set up.
