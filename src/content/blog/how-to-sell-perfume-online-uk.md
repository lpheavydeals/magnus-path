Anyone researching how to sell perfume online UK-wide quickly meets three obstacles that most product categories never face. Shoppers cannot smell what they are buying. Alcohol-based fragrance is classed as a flammable liquid, so posting it is not as simple as posting a T-shirt. And every bottle is a cosmetic, with its own legal paperwork. This guide is written for UK fragrance founders, niche perfumers, attar and oud houses, and home-grown brands moving from markets and pop-ups to online sales.

The rules below are summarised in general terms. We are an ecommerce growth team, so check the detail with your Responsible Person, a trade mark solicitor and each carrier.

## Choose the formats you will sell

Your range shapes your costs, your shipping options and your marketing. Most new brands launch with a small, tight collection and add formats later.

| Format | What buyers expect | Online notes |
|---|---|---|
| Extrait or parfum | Rich, long-lasting, premium | Strongest story; needs trial before purchase |
| Eau de parfum | The everyday hero size | Most searches and gifts land here |
| Eau de toilette and colognes | Lighter, fresher, often cheaper | Good for summer and Father's Day |
| Attar and perfume oils | Concentrated, usually alcohol-free | Strong with South Asian and Muslim buyers; check shipping class |
| Solid perfume and roll-ons | Portable, giftable | Easy add-ons to raise basket value |
| Discovery sets and travel sprays | Low-risk trial | Usually the first purchase from a new brand |

Four to eight scents across clearly different families is plenty for a launch. A shopper choosing between three woody ambers will usually choose none.

## Get UK cosmetics compliance in place

Every fragrance counts as a cosmetic under the UK Cosmetics Regulation. Broadly, that brings four duties:

- **A UK Responsible Person** who holds the product information file and answers to the authorities. This can be your own company if it is based in the UK.
- **A safety assessment** for every product, prepared by a qualified assessor and recorded in a cosmetic product safety report.
- **Notification** of each product through the government's cosmetic notification service before it goes on sale.
- **Labelling** that includes the ingredient list, the Responsible Person's name and UK address, batch code, volume, precautions and the named fragrance allergens above set thresholds.

Many perfumers also work to IFRA standards on how much of certain materials may be used. Contract manufacturers and compliance consultancies can handle much of this, but the Responsible Person carries the legal duty, so make sure you know who that is.

## How to sell perfume online UK shoppers can picture

The product page has to do the job of a fragrance counter.

### Notes first, then plain English

List the top, heart and base notes, but keep the list short. Then translate: "sharp grapefruit for the first half hour, soft rose through the afternoon, warm sandalwood by evening". Most shoppers do not speak perfumery, but everyone understands a description of how a scent changes.

### Family, mood and moment

Many buyers know they like fresh, floral, woody, amber or gourmand scents even if they cannot name a note. Tag every scent with its family and suggest moments: a crisp scent for the office, a deeper one for winter evenings, something light for a summer wedding.

### Honest performance

Say whether a scent stays intimate or fills a room, and describe how long it typically lasts while noting that skin, weather and clothing change things. Overstated longevity is a quick route to poor reviews, and the CAP Code expects claims to be backed up.

### People on video

Short clips of real wearers describing the scent in their own words beat a rotating bottle. Ask reviewers specific questions, such as how it smelt after six hours, so their answers help the next buyer.

## Let shoppers try before they commit

Sampling is how most UK customers find a new perfume house online.

- **A paid discovery set** containing every scent in small sprays, presented as a proper product rather than a freebie.
- **A credit back** so the cost of the set comes off a later full-size order.
- **A sample in every parcel**, chosen to complement what the customer bought.
- **A follow-up email or text** once the set has had a few days of wear, asking for their favourite and linking to that full bottle.

Measure what proportion of discovery-set customers later order a full size, and the gap between the two orders. That figure tells you what you can afford to pay to win a new sampler.

## Shipping alcohol-based perfume in the UK

### Why it is restricted

Most perfumes contain enough alcohol to be classed as a flammable liquid for transport. Carriers accept them within dangerous goods rules, which usually cap the volume per item and per parcel and set packaging standards. Royal Mail, Evri, DPD and other couriers each publish their own restricted items guidance, and the limits differ. International shipping is much tighter, and some destinations and services exclude perfume completely.

### What to do before launch

- Ask your manufacturer for the safety data sheet for each fragrance.
- Confirm in writing which carrier services accept your bottle sizes, at home and abroad.
- Use leak-proof caps, a shrink band and a sealed inner bag, with bottles held upright in fitted inserts.
- Send test parcels to friends around the country and check how they arrive.
- State clearly at checkout where you can and cannot deliver.

### Marketplaces add their own checks

Amazon UK and TikTok Shop UK review fragrance listings as dangerous goods and may ask for the safety data sheet before accepting stock or listings. Build this into your launch timeline.

## Returns and consumer rights

Online shoppers in the UK usually have a cancellation period after delivery. Sealed goods that are unsuitable for return for hygiene reasons can be excluded once opened, which is why many brands seal boxes in cellophane. Explain your policy before checkout, and remember that faulty, leaking or incorrect items must be put right regardless. Generous sampling prevents more disappointment returns than any policy wording.

## Inspired-by names and trade mark risk

Some brands build a business on scents that resemble famous fragrances. The scent itself is rarely the main danger; the words are. Using a designer's name in product titles, descriptions, ad keywords, hashtags or URLs can lead to trade mark complaints, delisted products and suspended ad or marketplace accounts. Copying bottle shapes or packaging adds risk. Under the CAP Code, a comparison such as "smells the same as" has to be accurate and capable of proof.

The lower-risk route is to name every scent yourself, describe it by family and notes, and register your own brand name as a UK trade mark early. Take advice from a trade mark solicitor before launching any comparison-led range.

## Plan around the UK gifting calendar

Much fragrance in Britain is bought as a present. Work backwards from each peak, with gift sets made, photographed and listed well before demand rises.

- **Black Friday and Christmas:** the biggest season for most fragrance brands; publish delivery cut-offs early.
- **Valentine's Day:** couples' sets and romantic scent families.
- **Mother's Day in March:** earlier than in many countries, so do not plan by the American date.
- **Father's Day in June:** colognes and woody scents.
- **Diwali, Eid and wedding season:** strong for attar, oud and gift boxes with South Asian and Muslim customers.

Between peaks, keep self-buyers interested with launches, layering suggestions and refill reminders.

## Count the real costs

Before pricing, add up the juice, the bottle and atomiser, the box, the samples you give away, dangerous goods postage, breakage, marketplace fees, returns and the advertising needed to reach someone who has never smelt your work. Then compare a gift buyer who orders once with a sampler who becomes a twice-yearly customer, because they justify very different acquisition costs.

## Getting your first customers

Start where intent already exists. Search campaigns on scent families and occasions reach people who know what they like. Creator seeding with fragrance reviewers puts your scents in front of audiences who trust a good nose. Markets and pop-ups let people smell your work and join your list. Point every route at the discovery set, then let the follow-up message and the credit do the rest. Once you know which scents convert samplers into full-bottle buyers, put more of your budget behind those.

## How to sell perfume online UK-wide: a launch checklist

- Four to eight scents across distinct families, plus a discovery set
- Responsible Person, safety reports and notifications completed for each product
- Labels with ingredients, allergens and UK address checked
- Your own name on every scent; no designer brands in titles, ad keywords or hashtags
- Product pages with notes in plain English, family, mood and honest performance
- Discovery-set credit and follow-up messages switched on before ads
- Carrier approval in writing and packaging tested
- Returns terms for opened, sealed and damaged bottles written plainly
- Gift sets and cut-off dates planned for the next peak
