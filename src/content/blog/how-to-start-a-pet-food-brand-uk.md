This guide explains how to start a pet food brand: UK feed rules, recipe development, packaging, sales channels and the reorder habits that decide whether a brand lasts. British owners are generous with their pets and fussy about what goes in the bowl. They read the back of the pack, compare brands on Amazon UK, ask the groomer and swap notes in breed groups. Win their trust and the same customer may buy from you every few weeks for years. Lose it with a vague label or an upset stomach, and they will go back to the bag they used to buy at the supermarket.

## Step 1: Pick a corner of the market you can own

Large manufacturers already sell complete dry food for every life stage, and they fill the aisles at supermarkets and Pets at Home. A start-up will struggle to beat them head on. Choose a narrower space:

- **Species.** Dog products dominate most shelves. Cat owners, and owners of rabbits or small pets, often have fewer independent brands to choose from.
- **Format.** Air-dried treats, single-ingredient chews, baked biscuits, wet food in trays, freeze-dried toppers or gently cooked fresh food.
- **Owner need.** Small breeds, working dogs, senior cats, single-protein recipes or British-sourced ingredients.
- **Buying moment.** Training rewards, puppy starter kits, Christmas stockings or a weekly topper that makes dry food more appealing.

Write your positioning in a single sentence, for example "air-dried single-protein treats for small dogs, made with British lamb". If the sentence needs three clauses, the pack and the ads will be muddled too.

### Test demand cheaply

Read the reviews on the top Amazon UK and eBay listings for your format. One-star reviews about crumbling, smell, hard textures or tiny pack sizes are a free product brief. Look at what owners ask in Facebook groups and on TikTok, and talk to independent pet shops, dog walkers and groomers near you. They hear the same objections every week and will tell you which products sit on the shelf.

## Step 2: Develop the recipe with a co-packer

Very few founders build their own kitchen or factory. Most work with a contract manufacturer, often called a co-packer, that already produces pet food or treats in the format they want.

### Questions to ask a co-packer

- Which formats, recipes and batch sizes can you run, and what is the minimum order?
- Are you registered or approved as a feed business for this type of product?
- Where do you source meat, fish and other ingredients, and how are they checked on arrival?
- Which tests do you run on each batch, and will you share the results?
- How do you set and check shelf life?
- Can you fill and seal our printed packs, or only supply in bulk?
- Who owns the recipe if we move production later?

Visit the site before you sign anything. Look at storage, cleaning, how raw materials are kept apart and how each batch is traced. Then put specifications, tolerances, lead times and recipe ownership into a written agreement.

### Nutrition comes first

A complete food has to provide everything a pet needs day to day, so it should be formulated by a qualified animal nutritionist and checked against recognised nutritional guidelines. UK Pet Food, the industry's trade body, publishes guidance that many manufacturers use as a reference. Treats and toppers are complementary foods and are simpler, but they still need a sensible feeding guide so owners do not overdo them.

Keep a file for each product with recipe versions, supplier documents, test results and label proofs. Retailers, marketplaces and curious customers will all ask questions, and an organised file lets you answer quickly.

## Step 3: Register, label and check the rules

This is the step that delays launches most often. Treat what follows as general orientation, not legal advice, and confirm the details for your products with your local authority or a specialist adviser.

### Feed business registration

In broad terms, businesses that make, import, store or sell pet food in Great Britain need to be registered as feed businesses with their local authority, usually through trading standards. Registration is separate from any registration your co-packer holds, and some activities need approval rather than simple registration. If your recipes use animal by-products such as meat or fish, further controls may apply to the people who make them.

### Pet food labelling

Pet food labels follow their own set of rules, which differ from the rules for food sold to people. A typical label needs to show:

- Whether it is a complete or complementary food, plus the animal and age group it is intended for.
- The composition, listing ingredients or categories in the required way.
- Analytical constituents, such as protein, fat, fibre, ash and moisture.
- Additives where they must be declared.
- Feeding instructions, net weight, batch number and a date of minimum durability.
- The name and address of the business responsible for the product.

UK Pet Food publishes labelling guidance that helps translate the rules into practical layouts. Ask your co-packer to check the label as well, and get a specialist to review it before you print a large run.

### Claims and the Veterinary Medicines Directorate

Food can be described accurately; it cannot be sold as a treatment. If a pack, listing or ad says a product treats, cures or prevents an illness, it can start to look like a veterinary medicine, which the Veterinary Medicines Directorate regulates. Foods for particular nutritional purposes, sometimes called dietetic feeds, have their own rules on wording. Advertising also falls under the CAP code, enforced by the ASA, so lines such as "vet recommended" or "natural" need to be accurate and supported.

### Business basics

Register your company, think about VAT (pet food can be treated differently from human food, so take accountancy advice), protect your brand name with a UK trade mark and arrange product liability insurance.

## Step 4: Design packs owners trust in seconds

Packaging has to keep the food safe, carry the legal wording and answer an owner's main question at a glance: is this right for my pet?

**Protection.** Choose barrier film that keeps out air and moisture for the full shelf life. Resealable pouches suit treats eaten over a few weeks. Post sample packs to yourself through Royal Mail, Evri and DPD before launch and see how they arrive.

**Front of pack.** Species, life stage, protein and weight should be readable on a phone-sized thumbnail. Amazon UK and TikTok Shop images are small.

**Back of pack.** Composition, analytical constituents, feeding table by weight, storage advice, batch and date, and your contact details.

### Packaging checklist

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Species and life stage on the front | Owners filter by these first |
| Complete or complementary stated | Required, and it sets feeding expectations |
| Feeding table by pet weight | Prevents over-feeding and complaints |
| Resealable closure | Keeps treats fresh between uses |
| Pack size matched to a feeding period | Makes reorder timing predictable |
| QR code to a switching guide | Reduces upset stomachs after a change of food |

## Step 5: Choose where to sell

### Your own store

A Shopify store gives you the customer relationship, subscriptions and space to explain sourcing, feeding and switching. Give each product page pack sizes matched to pet weight, a feeding table, composition, real photos of customers' pets and reviews. Our [pet brand marketing agency for UK brands](/uk/pet-brand-marketing-agency/) page explains how we connect store, ads and reorder journeys.

### Amazon UK

Many owners search Amazon first for food, treats and chews. Build listings with clear images, full attributes, A+ content and variations by size and flavour, and enrol suitable products in Subscribe and Save. Work out fees, delivery and returns per pack before you set a price.

### TikTok Shop UK, eBay, OnBuy and Etsy

TikTok Shop UK suits treats and toppers that look good on camera, especially with pet creators. eBay and OnBuy add reach for bulk packs and value buyers. Etsy can work for baked treats and personalised gift boxes, but check its food policies first. Keep prices and stock consistent across all of them.

### Independent shops

Pets at Home and the supermarkets set many owners' expectations on price and range, but independent pet shops, groomers, farm shops and dog shows are realistic first stockists for a small brand and a useful source of honest feedback.

## Step 6: Plan the UK selling calendar

- **Christmas.** Stocking fillers, advent calendars for dogs and gift boxes. Order packaging early.
- **Black Friday and Boxing Day.** Owners stock up on larger bags; bundle offers work better than deep discounts on new products.
- **January sales.** New Year routines and new puppies make January a good time for starter kits and feeding guides.
- **Summer.** Travel-sized packs, cooling recipes such as frozen treat ideas, and content on feeding during hot weather.
- **Bonfire Night.** Owners look for advice on fireworks season. Offer practical tips, not calming claims.

## Step 7: Build reorders from the first order

The first sale costs the most. Profit comes from the second, third and tenth.

- **Trial packs** reduce the risk for owners worried their pet will refuse something new.
- **A switching guide** explains mixing old and new food over several days.
- **A day-seven message** asks how the switch went and fixes problems early.
- **Timed reminders** arrive a few days before the pack is likely to run out, based on pack size and pet weight.
- **Subscriptions** let owners pause, skip or change size without emailing support, and make cancelling as easy as signing up.

## How to start a pet food brand: UK launch checklist

**Months 1 and 2.** Lock the niche, shortlist co-packers, run sample batches, test with local owners and groomers, and contact your local authority about feed registration.

**Months 3 and 4.** Finalise recipe and label, complete specialist checks, order packaging, build the store and Amazon UK listings, and photograph real pets eating the product.

**Months 5 and 6.** Launch with modest ad budgets on Meta and Amazon, collect reviews, switch on reminders and subscriptions, then add TikTok Shop UK and one other marketplace once stock is steady.

### Mistakes to avoid

- Launching ten products before one has proven people reorder it.
- Printing thousands of packs before the label has been checked.
- Copying health wording from overseas brands.
- Pack sizes that run out at awkward times.
- Ignoring how packs survive the courier.
