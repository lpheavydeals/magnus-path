If you are working out how to sell supplements online Australia-wide, the first question is not which platform to use. It is which set of laws your product sits under. A fish oil capsule, a protein powder and a magnesium gummy can look alike on a shelf, yet in Australia one may be a therapeutic good and another a food. That single decision shapes your label, your claims, your ads and the marketplaces that will accept you. This guide walks through the launch in the order founders usually meet each problem.

We are an ecommerce growth team, not regulatory consultants. Treat the rules below as orientation, and confirm your own position with a qualified regulatory adviser before you print packs or spend on ads.

## Step 1: Decide whether you are selling a medicine or a food

Australia splits the supplement shelf in two, broadly speaking.

- **Listed complementary medicines.** Many vitamins, minerals, herbal products and fish oils are therapeutic goods. Before they can be supplied they are generally entered on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) and carry an AUST L number on the label. Listed medicines are lower-risk products made from pre-approved ingredients, and they may only carry indications chosen from the TGA's list of permitted indications.
- **Foods, including sports foods.** Protein powders, bars, many pre-workouts and electrolyte drinks are usually regulated as foods under the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code. Formulated supplementary sports foods have their own standard, and any health claims follow the food health claim rules rather than the TGA list.

The boundary is not always obvious. An ingredient, a dose or a claim on the pack can move a product from food into therapeutic goods, and the TGA publishes guidance on how it judges borderline cases. Settle this product by product before you design anything, because every later step depends on it.

## Step 2: Line up a sponsor, a manufacturer and your paperwork

For listed medicines, the business that holds the ARTG entry is called the sponsor, and in general the sponsor must be an Australian resident or carry on business here. Overseas founders often work with an Australian sponsor or set up a local entity. The sponsor carries legal responsibility for the product, including holding evidence for every indication it claims.

Listed medicines are normally made by a manufacturer licensed by the TGA, or by an overseas maker whose site has been accepted as meeting good manufacturing practice. Sports foods and other food products are made by food businesses under state food safety rules, and imported food may be checked at the border under the Imported Food Inspection Scheme.

Keep one shared folder with:

- ARTG entry details, or written confirmation that the product is a food.
- Manufacturer details and evidence of their licence or food registration.
- Evidence files for each indication or health claim.
- Certificates of analysis for each batch and any independent test reports.
- Label proofs, signed off and dated.

Marketplaces, retailers and payment providers ask for these more often than founders expect.

## Step 3: Write the label first, then the marketing

Treat the approved label as the master copy. If a claim is not on the pack, not in your evidence file and not allowed for your product type, it should not appear in an ad, a listing or a creator script.

### What usually appears on the pack

| Product type | Typical label elements |
|---|---|
| Listed medicine | AUST L number, active ingredients and amounts per dose, permitted indications, directions, warnings required for certain ingredients, sponsor details, batch and expiry |
| Sports food | Name and description, ingredient list, nutrition information panel, allergen declarations in the required format, any required advisory statements, net quantity, date mark, supplier details |

Formatting rules are detailed for both. Medicine labels follow TGA labelling requirements, and food labels follow the Food Standards Code, including the newer plain English allergen format. Ask your adviser to check proofs before you order a print run.

### Claims to handle with care

- Listed medicines may use only permitted indications, in the approved wording, and only those you hold evidence for.
- Disease claims are off the table for both types: nothing should promise to treat or prevent a serious illness.
- Sports foods cannot be presented as if they have therapeutic effects. Words such as "detox", "immune booster" and "fat burner" attract scrutiny.
- Plain facts usually sell well: what is in each scoop, how much per serve, how it tastes and when to take it.

## Step 4: Build product pages for sceptical Australian buyers

Australians compare supplements in pharmacy chains such as Chemist Warehouse, in supermarkets and online, often with a phone in one hand. Your page has to win that comparison in the first scroll.

| Page element | What it does |
|---|---|
| Front and back pack images | Lets buyers and marketplace reviewers read the label |
| Ingredients and amounts as text | Readable on mobile, by Google.com.au and by AI assistants |
| AUST L number or sports food description | Shows you know which rules apply |
| Serves per pack and serving size | Lets buyers work out the cost per day themselves |
| Flavour, texture and capsule size | Drives reviews and repeat purchases |
| Who should check with a doctor or pharmacist | Covers pregnancy, regular medicines and under-18s |
| GST-inclusive price and delivery estimate | One clear total, no surprises at checkout |

Reviews about taste, mixability and delivery are fine to show. Reviews that describe health outcomes are another matter, especially for listed medicines, because the advertising rules limit testimonials. Moderate before you republish anything. ## Step 5: Pick channels that suit each product

### Your own Shopify store

Your store is the only channel where you own the customer relationship, set subscription terms and keep the margin on every reorder. Show prices including GST, offer Afterpay or another BNPL option if it suits your order values, and put your most detailed content here: ingredient explainers, test results and how-to-use guides. Australia Post and Sendle cover most parcel sizes.

### Amazon AU

Amazon.com.au is where many shoppers line up tubs side by side. Expect category checks for dietary supplements that can include label images and, for listed medicines, ARTG details. Approval timing varies, so build a few weeks into the launch plan, then use Brand Registry to protect listings from unauthorised sellers.

### eBay AU and Kogan

Both can add reach for established lines and multi-packs. Each has its own policies on therapeutic goods, so read the current seller rules before you list, and keep prices aligned so you do not undercut your own store.

### TikTok Shop

Creator videos can move gummies, electrolyte sachets and protein flavours quickly. Check whether TikTok Shop is open to your business and category in Australia when you plan, because availability and health product rules have been changing. Creators are bound by the same claim limits as you.

Pharmacy and specialist retail is a separate route with its own buying process. Many brands build an online sales record first and approach retailers later.

## Step 6: Advertise within the TGA code and platform rules

Advertising a listed medicine to the public must comply with the Therapeutic Goods Advertising Code, which covers your website, social posts, marketplace listings and paid creator content. In broad terms, ads must be accurate and balanced, use only claims the product may make, include required statements such as a prompt to read the label, and avoid testimonials from anyone you pay or gift product to. Health professionals face limits on endorsing products too.

Sports foods and other food products sit under the AANA Code of Ethics and food law. Paid posts must be clearly distinguishable as advertising, and Ad Standards handles complaints. Meta, Google and TikTok add their own policies on weight loss, body image and some ingredients, so supplement ads usually run to adults only.

Creative that tends to pass review shows routine and format: a shake after training, a capsule with breakfast, a sachet in a gym bag.

## Step 7: Plan reorders and subscriptions from the first order

Every tub has a predictable finish date. Divide serves by daily use and you know, within a few days, when a customer will need more.

1. Capture email and SMS consent at checkout in line with the Spam Act.
2. Send a reminder a few days before the pack is likely to run out.
3. Offer subscribe-and-save with simple online pause, skip and cancel options.
4. Explain renewal terms plainly. Subscription practices have drawn ACCC attention, so keep cancelling as easy as signing up.
5. Suggest a larger pack once someone has reordered twice.
6. Ask for a review after a few weeks of use, not the day the parcel lands.

## Step 8: Handle returns, stock and the Australian calendar

- **Consumer rights.** Customers have consumer rights under the Australian Consumer Law when goods are faulty, unsafe or not as described, whatever your change-of-mind policy says. You may decline change-of-mind returns on opened supplements, but say so clearly before purchase.
- **Expiry and storage.** Record expiry dates by batch, rotate stock, and check Amazon's minimum shelf-life rules before you send inventory.
- **Heat.** Summer deliveries to hot regions can affect gummies and chocolate-coated products. Test packaging and consider insulated options.
- **Seasons.** New Year brings resolutions and gym sign-ups. EOFY in June suits stock clearance. Father's Day in September works for sports nutrition gift packs. Click Frenzy, Black Friday, Christmas and Boxing Day close out the year. Have stock and approved creative ready weeks ahead.

## How to sell supplements online Australia-wide: launch checklist

- [ ] Each product classified as a listed medicine or a food, with adviser sign-off.
- [ ] ARTG entry and Australian sponsor in place for listed medicines.
- [ ] Manufacturer licence or food registration on file.
- [ ] Labels checked against TGA or Food Standards Code requirements.
- [ ] Every claim matched to a permitted indication or allowed food claim, with evidence.
- [ ] Batch certificates and test reports stored in one folder.
- [ ] Product pages show the label, ingredients, serves and GST-inclusive price.
- [ ] Amazon AU category approval requested with documents ready.
- [ ] Ad copy and creator briefs checked against the TGA code, the AANA codes and platform policies.
- [ ] Refill reminders and subscriptions live, with consent recorded.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Borrowing a competitor's claims.** They may hold a different listing, or simply be out of line.
- **Launching on every channel at once.** Prove the product on your store and one marketplace first.
- **Ignoring true margin.** Count marketplace fees, freight, GST, returns and ad costs per order.
- **Measuring January only.** The second and third orders decide whether the brand works.
