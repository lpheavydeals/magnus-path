Learning how to sell home decor online Australia-wide means solving a problem many other categories never face: distance. A ceramic vase that costs little to post within Melbourne can arrive in pieces in Townsville, and a sideboard that is simple to deliver in Sydney may need a specialist carrier to reach Kalgoorlie. Add buyers who want to touch fabric before they commit, and you have a category where photos, freight and returns decide profit as much as ads do. This guide covers the decisions in the order Australian homewares and furniture founders usually face them.

We are an ecommerce growth team, not lawyers or product safety consultants. Use the rule summaries below as a starting point and check the details with a qualified adviser.

## Step 1: Sort your range by how it ships

Before you plan photos or ads, split your catalogue into delivery groups. The group shapes your pricing, your channels and how much you can afford to spend winning an order.

| Group | Examples | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Satchel-friendly | Cushion covers, throws, table linen, prints | Cheap to send, suits impulse buys and marketplaces |
| Fragile parcels | Ceramics, glassware, mirrors, lamps | Needs tested packaging and damage tracking |
| Large parcels | Rugs, side tables, flat-pack shelving | Freight varies by zone, so set thresholds with care |
| Bulky freight | Sofas, beds, dining settings, wardrobes | Specialist carriers, often two-person delivery, longer lead times |

Most brands sell across several groups. That is fine, as long as each group has its own freight logic and margin target.

## Step 2: Photograph products in rooms Australians live in

Photography is your showroom. Shoot in spaces your buyers recognise: open-plan living areas full of light, compact apartments and rentals, and outdoor areas that get used for much of the year.

### The photo set to aim for

- A styled room shot that shows the product in context.
- A clean shot on a plain background for marketplaces and feeds.
- A scale shot with a person or a familiar object.
- Close-ups of texture, grain, weave and finish in natural light.
- Underside, back and joinery shots for furniture.
- A short video that shows movement, drape or how drawers open.

Reuse the same room sets across ads, product pages and social posts so the brand looks consistent wherever a buyer meets it.

## Step 3: Give exact details buyers can measure

Returning a bulky item is expensive for you and frustrating for the customer, and most returns begin with something the page did not say.

- Assembled dimensions and the packed carton size, so buyers can check lifts, hallways and car boots.
- Weight, materials and finishes, named precisely.
- Care instructions, including outdoor suitability and exposure to strong sun.
- Assembly time, tools needed and whether delivery includes carrying the item inside.
- Colour notes: screens vary, so offer swatches for upholstery and rugs where you can.

## Step 4: Check product safety rules before you list

Some home products are covered by mandatory safety requirements enforced by the ACCC and state and territory regulators. For this category the obvious one is toppling furniture. Chests of drawers, wardrobes, bookcases, TV units and similar storage pieces above certain sizes now face requirements on stability testing and warning labels, which took effect after a transition period. Products containing button or coin batteries, such as remote-controlled lights and flameless candles, have their own mandatory safety and information standards.

What this means in practice:

1. Ask your supplier or a testing lab which standards apply to each product, and keep the reports on file.
2. Supply anchoring hardware and instructions where required, and show them on the product page.
3. Make sure warning labels are on the product and reflected in listings.
4. Never show unsafe use in ads, such as children climbing shelves.
5. Watch Product Safety Australia for recalls that affect similar items in your range.

Some marketplaces may ask for evidence of compliance before they allow a listing, so keep it ready.

## Step 5: Price freight honestly for a big country

Surprise freight at checkout is one of the quickest ways to lose a homewares order, and shoppers in Perth, Hobart, Darwin and regional towns notice it first.

### Parcels

Australia Post, Sendle and several couriers handle small and medium parcels. A free delivery threshold works well here, provided it sits a little above your typical order value and protects margin. Our [free shipping threshold calculator](/tools/free-shipping-threshold-calculator/) helps you set one.

### Bulky items

- Show an estimate by postcode on the product page, not only at checkout.
- Use clear delivery zones instead of padding every product price.
- Say whether you deliver to remote areas and how long it takes.
- State whether the courier delivers to the door, inside, or to a room of choice.
- Explain what happens if an item arrives damaged and how to report it.

### Track damage

Record damage and returns by product, carrier and region. Fix packaging for the worst offenders first, and update photos or descriptions where returns point to a mismatch between page and product.

## Step 6: Write returns terms around the Australian Consumer Law

Under the Australian Consumer Law, customers have consumer rights when goods are faulty, unsafe, not as described or not fit for purpose. Those rights apply whatever your own returns policy says, and you cannot write them away. Change-of-mind returns are different: the law does not require them, so you decide whether to offer them, for how long and who pays freight.

A clear returns page answers:

- Whether change-of-mind returns are accepted, and on which products.
- Who pays return freight in each case.
- How to report transit damage, with photos, and how quickly.
- How faulty goods are handled, in plain words.
- How custom and made-to-order pieces are treated.

Show prices as a single GST-inclusive amount, compare sale prices only against prices you genuinely charged, and keep reviews real. These are recurring ACCC concerns in retail.

## Step 7: Build trust with reviews and customer photos

Homewares buyers want to see a piece in someone else's home. Ask for a review a couple of weeks after delivery, once items are assembled and styled, and make photo uploads easy. Reply to complaints publicly and helpfully, and never filter out negative reviews in a way that paints a misleading picture.

## Step 8: Choose your channels

### Your own Shopify store

Your store is where high-value pieces sell, because you control photos, freight logic, delivery promises and customer contact. Collections by room and style, swatch requests and Afterpay or Zip at checkout all help. Show the freight-inclusive total early, so BNPL instalments hold no surprises.

### Amazon AU, eBay AU and Kogan

Marketplaces suit satchel-friendly and parcel-sized homewares, plus flat-pack items with sensible freight. Large furniture depends on each marketplace's bulky delivery options and your margin after fees. Our [marketplace profit calculator](/tools/marketplace-profit-calculator/) shows what is left per order.

### TikTok Shop

Decor that looks good on camera, such as lamps, vases and throws, can sell through creator videos. Check that TikTok Shop is open to your business and category in Australia before you plan around it.

Large retailers such as Temple & Webster and Bunnings shape what buyers expect on range, delivery and price. A smaller brand competes on style, story and dependable service rather than on discount depth.

## Step 9: Use Pinterest, Instagram and search

Pinterest is built for saving room ideas, and boards made from your room sets bring steady visits. Instagram Reels show styling in seconds. Search content, such as how to choose a rug size for an open-plan lounge or which timbers suit a coastal home, answers questions buyers type into Google.com.au and AI assistants before they have a brand in mind. Pair it with an accurate Google Shopping feed in AUD.

## Step 10: Plan around the Australian calendar

| Moment | What tends to sell |
|---|---|
| New Year | Storage, organisation, fresh starts |
| EOFY (June) | Home office furniture and big-ticket clearance |
| Father's Day (September) | Outdoor and barbecue accessories, gifts |
| Spring | Refreshes, outdoor settings, soft furnishings |
| Click Frenzy and Black Friday | Hero pieces and bundles |
| Christmas | Hosting, tableware, gifts |
| Boxing Day | Clearance and summer outdoor living |

Bulky stock needs lead time. Book freight capacity, publish delivery cut-off dates and prepare landing pages six to eight weeks before each peak.

## How to sell home decor online Australia-wide: launch checklist

- [ ] Range grouped by delivery type, with a margin target for each.
- [ ] Room, scale, close-up and plain-background photos for hero products.
- [ ] Assembled and packed dimensions on every page.
- [ ] Safety standards checked, reports filed and anchoring details shown.
- [ ] Freight estimates by postcode and clear delivery zones.
- [ ] Returns page that separates change of mind from consumer rights under the Australian Consumer Law.
- [ ] GST-inclusive prices and genuine "was" prices only.
- [ ] Review requests timed after assembly.
- [ ] Marketplaces chosen only for products whose margin survives fees and freight.
- [ ] Seasonal calendar with delivery cut-offs.

## Mistakes that cost homewares brands money

- **Hiding freight until the last step.** It lifts abandoned carts and invites complaints about misleading prices.
- **Listing bulky pieces everywhere.** Marketplace fees plus long-distance freight can turn a sale into a loss.
- **Shooting only in a studio.** Buyers struggle to judge size, colour and texture without a room around the product.
- **Treating safety as a supplier problem.** If you sell it in Australia, you need to know which standards apply and show the right warnings.
- **Judging ads on next-day sales.** A sofa or dining setting can take weeks to decide on, so measure over a longer window before switching campaigns off.
