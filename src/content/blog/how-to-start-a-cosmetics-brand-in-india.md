If you are researching how to start a cosmetics brand in India, you are entering a crowded but growing market. Shoppers now discover makeup and skincare on Instagram, compare on Nykaa and Amazon, and expect new launches every season. The good news is that you do not need a factory or a huge budget to begin. You need a clear niche, a reliable manufacturer, the right paperwork, packaging that survives delivery and a launch plan. This guide covers each step.

## Step 1: Choose a niche and a first range

The most common mistake is launching with too many products for too broad an audience. Start narrow.

### Pick a clear buyer

Describe the person you are making products for. Examples:

- Office-goers who want long-wear, sweat-resistant makeup for humid cities.
- Buyers with deeper Indian skin tones who struggle to find matching shades.
- Students looking for affordable, simple everyday makeup.
- Buyers who want vegan, fragrance-free or minimal-ingredient products.
- Brides and wedding guests looking for festive looks.

### Keep the first range small

Three to eight products are enough to launch. A lip range in several shades, a kajal and a liner, or a tinted moisturiser and a compact can all form a coherent first collection. Fewer products means lower minimum order quantities, simpler content and more ad budget behind each item.

### Check the market

Read reviews of similar products on Nykaa, Amazon and Myntra. What do buyers complain about: patchy shades, dry finish, poor wear, packaging that breaks? Those complaints are your product brief.

## Step 2: Find a contract manufacturer

Most new brands use third-party manufacturers who already have a licensed facility.

- **Licence:** confirm their cosmetic manufacturing licence covers your product types.
- **Formulation:** ask whether they offer ready formulas you can adapt, or custom development.
- **Minimum order quantities:** these vary widely by product and packaging; ask early.
- **Samples:** test several rounds on different skin tones, in heat and humidity, over a full day.
- **Testing:** agree on stability testing, packaging compatibility and any claim-supporting tests.
- **Formula ownership:** clarify in writing who owns the formula and whether it can be sold to others.
- **Quality and records:** batch records, certificates of analysis and a complaint process.

Visit the facility if you can. Speak to two or three manufacturers before choosing, compare their samples side by side, and ask for references from other small brands they work with.

## Step 3: Sort out licences, labels and legal basics

This section is general guidance, not legal advice. Rules change, so work with a regulatory consultant.

### Under the Cosmetics Rules

Cosmetics in India are regulated under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and the Cosmetics Rules. Manufacturing needs a licence from the state licensing authority. Many brands make products under a contract manufacturer's licence through a loan licence or similar arrangement. Imported cosmetics need registration with the central regulator before sale.

### Labels

Labels generally need the product name, ingredients, net quantity, manufacturer name and address, licence number, batch number, manufacturing date, expiry or use-before date, and warnings or directions where needed. Legal metrology rules add MRP, consumer care details and other declarations for packaged goods. Small packs such as lipsticks may use outer cartons or leaflets for some details; check what applies.

### Business registrations

- Register your company or LLP.
- Get GST registration, which marketplaces require.
- Open a current account and payment gateway.
- File a trademark application for your brand name and logo in the cosmetics class.

### Claims

Keep claims cosmetic and supported by tests. "Long-wear" and "transfer-resistant" need wear testing; "suitable for sensitive skin" needs dermatological testing. Avoid any claim that sounds like treating a condition.

## Step 4: Design packaging that sells and ships

Cosmetics packaging has to look good in a photo, feel good in the hand and arrive undamaged.

- **Stock versus custom:** stock components are faster and have lower minimums; custom moulds give distinctiveness but add time and cost. Many brands start with stock and customise later.
- **Shade visibility:** use clear windows, colour caps or shade stickers so buyers and warehouse teams can see shades.
- **Tamper evidence:** seals or shrink wrap reassure buyers that the product is new.
- **Shipping protection:** compacts and glass bottles need inner trays and cushioning.
- **Sustainability:** refills, recyclable materials and less plastic matter to many younger buyers; only claim what you can show.

## Step 5: Build a brand people remember

A good formula is not enough in a market where buyers scroll past hundreds of products a day. Decide early:

- **Name:** short, easy to say in several Indian languages, available as a trademark, domain and Instagram handle.
- **Promise:** one sentence on what you do differently, such as shades made for Indian undertones or makeup that lasts through humid commutes.
- **Look and voice:** colours, typefaces, photo style and tone of writing that stay consistent across packaging, website and ads.
- **Proof:** what you can show to support the promise, such as wear tests, swatches on many skin tones or ingredient choices.

Write these down in a short brand guide so designers, creators and marketplace teams all work from the same page.

## Step 6: Work out unit economics

Before setting prices, cost each product fully:

| Cost line | What to include |
|---|---|
| Product | Formula, filling, testing, wastage |
| Packaging | Primary pack, carton, shrink, inner tray |
| Fulfilment | Warehouse, packing, shipping, COD and payment fees |
| Channel | Marketplace commission and fees, quick commerce margins |
| Returns | COD refusals, damage, shade returns |
| Marketing | Ad cost per order, creator products, samples |

## Step 7: Build your store and listings

### Your own store

Shopify is a common choice for Indian beauty brands because it handles themes, payments, COD verification apps, reviews and shade variants well. Product pages need:

- Swatches on several skin tones, in natural light.
- Undertone guidance and comparisons with familiar shade names.
- Finish, coverage and wear time, described honestly.
- Ingredient list and how to apply.
- Reviews with photos and skin tone.

### Marketplaces

Nykaa is the core beauty marketplace for many brands. Amazon brings search-led buyers, and Brand Registry helps protect your listings. Myntra Beauty suits fashion-led makeup, and Flipkart reaches value buyers. Each needs shade images, compliant labels and your GST and licence details.

Keep pricing consistent across channels so your own store stays credible.

## Step 8: Plan the launch

A cosmetics launch needs a run-up, not just a "go live" date.

1. **Tease** the range on Instagram with close-up textures and swatches.
2. **Open a waitlist** on your website for early access.
3. **Seed creators** across skin tones a few weeks early, with clear ASCI disclosure and a brief on allowed claims.
4. **Prepare ads** on Meta and Google using creator content and swatches.
5. **Go live** on your store first, then marketplaces, with stock to cover the first weeks.
6. **Collect reviews** with photos from the first buyers.

Ask creators not to use filters that change how the product looks; ASCI guidance discourages filters that exaggerate a beauty product's effect.

## Step 9: Run operations and control returns

- **Inventory by shade:** track sell-through so popular shades do not run out while others pile up.
- **COD verification:** confirm cash on delivery orders by OTP or WhatsApp before dispatch.
- **Shade returns:** reduce them with better swatches and a shade finder rather than strict refusals.
- **Batch records:** keep them for every batch, in case a complaint or recall comes up.
- **Customer service:** reply quickly on WhatsApp and Instagram; makeup buyers often ask about shades before ordering.

## Mistakes to avoid in your first year

- **Ordering too much stock of every shade.** Start with smaller quantities and reorder the shades that sell.
- **Skipping wear tests in Indian weather.** A product that works in an air-conditioned office may slide off on a humid commute.
- **Launching without reviews.** Seed products with creators and early buyers so your listings are not empty on day one.
- **Spending the whole budget on launch week.** Keep money for restocks, ongoing ads and the second collection.

## Starting a cosmetics brand for buyers abroad

Some Indian brands sell to the diaspora and wider audiences in the UK, UAE, US and Australia. Each market has its own rules: the UK and EU require a responsible person and product notification, the US has registration and listing requirements for cosmetics, and the UAE requires product registration. Ingredient restrictions and label languages differ. Plan for this early if exports are part of your vision, because reformulating or relabelling later costs time.
