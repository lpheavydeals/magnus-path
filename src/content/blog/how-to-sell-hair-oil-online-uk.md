Anyone looking into how to sell hair oil online, UK edition, will notice how crowded the shelf has become. Rosemary oil went viral, traditional South Asian oiling rituals found new fans, and textured-hair buyers have long relied on oils that the high street barely stocked. That interest is real, but it has also brought copycat products and risky claims. This guide covers the steps to launch and grow a hair oil brand in the UK properly: rules, formula, packaging, product pages, channels, pricing, ads and repeat orders.

The regulatory points are general guidance only. Confirm them with your Responsible Person or adviser.

## Step 1: Understand how hair oil is regulated in the UK

Most hair oils are cosmetics under the UK Cosmetics Regulation. That means the same duties apply as for any other cosmetic sold in Great Britain.

### The cosmetic basics

- **A UK Responsible Person** who holds the product information file and answers to regulators.
- **A safety assessment** of the formula by a qualified assessor, covering ingredients, concentrations and use.
- **SCPN notification** for each product before it goes on sale.
- **A compliant label**, including the Responsible Person's name and address, contents, durability or period-after-opening symbol, batch number, precautions and the full ingredient list.

### Essential oils and allergens

Many hair oils blend carrier oils with essential oils such as rosemary, peppermint or tea tree. These contain fragrance allergens that may need naming on the label above certain levels, and some carry their own warnings. Your safety assessor will set safe concentrations. Do not assume "natural" means low risk.

### The line with medicines

Once a product claims to regrow hair, stop hair loss or treat a scalp condition, it starts to look like a medicine, which falls under the MHRA rather than cosmetics law. Ayurvedic or herbal oils with traditional medical claims face the same test in the UK, whatever their status elsewhere.

## Step 2: Choose a formula and a manufacturer

You have three common routes:

| Route | Suits | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Make it yourself | Small batches, Etsy, markets | Safety report, hygiene and batch records still apply |
| UK or EU contract manufacturer | Brands ready for steady volume | Minimum orders and lead times |
| Import a finished oil | Brands with a proven formula abroad | UK labels, Responsible Person and claims all need redoing |

Ask any manufacturer for stability data, a sample run and clear batch records. Oils can go rancid, separate or change colour over time, especially in clear bottles on a sunny windowsill, so stability testing matters more than it looks. Agree how batch numbers will be printed and recorded, so you can trace any complaint back to a production run. If you make the oil yourself, keep the same discipline: written recipes, weighed ingredients, dated batch sheets and clean equipment. Test the oil on different hair types: an oil that suits fine straight hair may feel thin on coily hair, and one made for thick curls may weigh down fine hair.

## Step 3: Package oil to survive the post

Oil finds every gap. A leaking parcel ruins the bottle, the box and often other items in the van.

- Use bottles and closures designed to seal, with an induction seal or shrink band.
- Ship droppers and pumps with a screw cap or locking collar.
- Bag each bottle and pack it upright with padding that absorbs.
- Test-ship parcels through Royal Mail, Evri and DPD before launch.
- Choose amber glass or opaque PET to protect light-sensitive oils, and weigh up glass breakage against plastic's look.
- Check how your parcel size affects postage band and whether it fits a letterbox.

## Step 4: Write product pages for each hair type

The first question every shopper has is whether this oil will work on their hair. Page structure should help them decide quickly.

### What to include

- Which hair types and textures it suits, from fine and straight to tight coils.
- How heavy it feels, and whether it works as a pre-wash treatment, a scalp oil or a finishing oil.
- How much to use and how often, with a short video.
- The main oils and extracts, and what each one adds, in everyday words.
- A realistic timeline: what someone may notice after one use versus a few weeks.
- Patch test advice and who should check with a pharmacist first.
- Reviews tagged by hair type.

### Collections and filters

Group products by hair type and concern (dry ends, frizz, a tight-feeling scalp) rather than by ingredient alone. Many buyers know their hair better than they know botanical names. Add a short quiz or a "which oil is right for me" guide that asks about texture, thickness and routine, then points to one or two products with a clear reason for each.

### Ingredient stories without overclaiming

Buyers love a story: an oil blend passed down in a family, a ritual from a grandmother's kitchen, a herb with centuries of traditional use. Tell it, because it makes your product memorable. Just keep the story about heritage, ritual and how the oil feels, rather than what it supposedly does to hair growth. "Inspired by the Sunday oiling my mother did for us" is a story; "the ancient remedy for thinning hair" is a medicinal claim.

## Step 5: Pick your UK channels

| Channel | What it is good for |
|---|---|
| Own Shopify store | Full story, sets, subscriptions, customer data |
| Amazon UK | Search by ingredient and hair type, Prime delivery |
| TikTok Shop UK | Video demos, oiling rituals, creator affiliates |
| Etsy | Genuinely handmade or small-batch oils and gift sets |
| eBay | Sets and clearance, used sparingly |

Salons, barbers and independent beauty shops can be strong stockists once you have reviews and repeat buyers. Retailers like Boots and Superdrug decide their ranges on their own terms; treat them as a later goal, not a launch channel.

## Step 6: Price for profit, not for a click

Hair oil looks cheap to make, which tempts brands into low prices. Count everything:

- Oil, packaging, labels and seals.
- Packing materials and postage, including breakages.
- Payment fees and marketplace fees.
- Refunds and replacements.
- The ad cost of winning each order.
- VAT, once registered, since UK consumer prices must include it.

## Step 7: Advertise within the rules

### Meta and TikTok

Show the ritual: warming the oil, massaging the scalp, the finish on lengths. Avoid before-and-after hairline photos, "regrow your hair" headlines and anything that points at a viewer's hair loss. Label creator content as ads.

### Google Search and Shopping

Capture searches such as "hair oil for curly hair" or "lightweight hair oil for fine hair" with accurate feed titles. Keep ad copy cosmetic.

### Amazon Sponsored Products

Use them to get new listings seen and reviewed, then move budget to the sizes that bring buyers back.

### Getting your first reviews

New listings with no reviews struggle on every channel. Ask early buyers for an honest review a few weeks after delivery, once they have used the oil several times. Amazon UK offers its own programmes for requesting reviews; use them rather than offering rewards for positive feedback. On your store, show all reviews, including critical ones, and reply politely to problems. A handful of honest, detailed reviews from people with different hair types is worth more than a pile of one-line five-star ratings.

## Step 8: Handle returns, delivery and VAT

UK online buyers generally have a cancellation period. Sealed toiletries opened after delivery can be an exception on hygiene grounds if you say so clearly before purchase, but leaking or faulty bottles are your problem whatever the policy says. Publish delivery options and cut-off times, and register for VAT once your taxable turnover passes the threshold. HMRC guidance or an accountant can confirm the details.

## Step 9: Keep buyers through the results window

Hair oil is judged over weeks, not days. Many buyers give up before they have used it properly.

1. On delivery: how much to use, how often, and how to wash it out.
2. Week one: a short ritual video.
3. Weeks three to four: a check-in explaining when to judge results.
4. Before the bottle runs out: a refill reminder, a larger size or a subscription.
5. After the reorder: a review request.

Plan around the UK calendar too. Gift sets sell for Black Friday, Christmas and Mother's Day in March; January brings new-routine buyers; winter heating and summer sun both send people looking for something for dry hair.

## Who buys hair oil in the UK, and what they need

Several quite different groups buy hair oil, and each needs its own message:

| Buyer | What they look for | What helps them buy |
|---|---|---|
| South Asian households | Familiar oils and rituals, good value sizes | Larger bottles, family sets, reorder ease |
| Textured-hair buyers | Oils that suit coils, curls and protective styles | Hair-type pages, matched creators, wash-day routines |
| Trend-led buyers | The oil they saw on TikTok | Honest timelines, clear usage, no hype |
| Men and beard care | Simple, non-greasy products | Short instructions, small sizes, quick results on feel |

Trying to speak to all of them in one advert usually means speaking clearly to none.

## Common mistakes new UK hair oil brands make

- Copying viral growth claims from social media into ads.
- Launching without testing on more than one hair type.
- Skipping allergen checks on essential oil blends.
- Using bottles that leak after a day in a sorting centre.
- Pricing so low there is nothing left for ads.
- Forgetting the follow-up, so buyers stop before they see a difference.
