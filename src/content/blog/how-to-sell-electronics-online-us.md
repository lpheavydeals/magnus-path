Figuring out how to sell electronics online? USA buyers, marketplaces and regulators all expect more paperwork and more proof than most product categories. A charger, a pair of earbuds or a smart plug needs the right FCC authorization, safety and battery documents before a marketplace will keep it live, and shoppers compare specs, reviews and warranty terms line by line. This guide covers the steps in order: compliance basics, positioning, listings, warranty, channels, peak events, reviews, returns and support.

## Step 1: Understand the compliance basics

We are not lawyers or compliance consultants. Treat this section as orientation and confirm requirements with an accredited test lab and qualified advisors.

### FCC equipment authorization

Most electronic devices that can emit radio frequency energy need FCC equipment authorization before they are marketed in the US. There are two main routes:

- **Certification.** Generally required for devices with intentional radio transmitters, such as Bluetooth earbuds, Wi-Fi plugs and wireless chargers. Testing by an accredited lab leads to a grant and an FCC ID, which must appear on the device or, in some cases, electronically.
- **Supplier's declaration of conformity (SDoC).** Used for many devices without radio transmitters. The responsible party, which must be based in the US, keeps test records and provides compliance information with the product.

If you are importing, check who acts as the US responsible party. Marketplaces commonly ask for FCC IDs or test reports during listing review.

### Safety testing and UL or ETL listing

For most consumer electronics, a UL or ETL mark is not a federal requirement, but listing by a nationally recognized testing laboratory is widely expected. Retailers, marketplaces and insurers often ask for safety reports, especially for chargers, power banks, adapters and anything plugged into a wall outlet. Treat safety testing as part of the product cost, not an optional extra.

### Lithium battery shipping

Lithium batteries are hazardous materials in transport. You will usually need:

- A UN 38.3 test summary for each battery or battery-containing product.
- A safety data sheet.
- Packaging, markings and labels that follow transport rules and your carriers' requirements.

Amazon US runs a dangerous goods review that can block listings or inventory until documents are approved. Submit them early, and expect extra questions for power banks and loose batteries.

### Other rules that may apply

- **Proposition 65.** California may require warnings for products containing listed chemicals, which can include some plastics, cables and coatings.
- **Warranty law.** The Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act governs written warranties on consumer products (Step 4).
- **Advertising claims.** FTC rules expect performance claims to be backed by evidence.
- **State e-waste and battery recycling rules.** Some states have take-back or labeling requirements.
- **Product safety.** The Consumer Product Safety Commission oversees hazards such as overheating; recalls must be handled promptly.

## Step 2: Choose products and positioning

Electronics categories on Amazon US are crowded with near-identical products. Before you launch, decide what makes your model different and who it is for:

- **A clear difference.** Longer cable life, a specific codec, a smaller footprint, better app support or a longer warranty.
- **A defined buyer.** Runners, gamers, remote workers, students, travelers or parents.
- **A price band.** Know the two or three competitors you sit next to in search and where your price lands.
- **Hero models.** Launch with a few models you can keep in stock, rather than a long list of colors and variants.

### Check the numbers before you commit

Electronics margins get squeezed from several sides at once: marketplace referral and fulfillment fees, ad costs in competitive searches, returns that cannot be resold as new, and deal discounts at peak events. Model all of them for each hero model before you place a large production order. If a product only works at full price with no returns, it is not ready to launch.

## Step 3: Write spec-led listings

### Titles

Include brand, product type, the key spec and the main compatibility point, such as "65W USB-C Charger, 3-Port, for Laptops and Phones". Keep it readable.

### Bullet points

- Lead with the two or three specs your buyers compare first.
- Translate each spec into everyday use, such as "about a full workday of playback" under stated test conditions.
- List compatibility plainly: devices, ports, wattage, operating systems and network bands, for example 2.4 GHz only.
- Show what is in the box.
- State the warranty period and how support works.

### Images and A+ content

Photograph ports, buttons, size in a hand and the product in use, not only 3D renders. Use A+ content for a comparison chart of your own models and a short setup guide.

### Spec table

| Spec | What to include |
|---|---|
| Power and charging | Input, output, wattage, charging time under stated conditions |
| Connectivity | Bluetooth version, Wi-Fi bands, codecs, range |
| Battery | Capacity, rated playback or runtime, how it was measured |
| Size and weight | In inches and ounces, with metric alongside |
| Compatibility | Devices, operating systems, apps, voice assistants |
| Compliance | FCC ID where applicable, safety listing you hold |
| Warranty | Full or limited, period, how to claim |

## Step 4: Write warranty terms buyers can read before checkout

Under the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act, a written warranty on a consumer product needs to be clearly worded, designated as full or limited where the law requires, and available for buyers to read before purchase. Online, that means the terms on or linked from the product page. Cover:

- The exact warranty period and when it starts.
- What is covered and what is excluded.
- Whether claims lead to repair, replacement or refund.
- How to start a claim and who pays shipping.
- Any registration step, without making coverage depend on it where the law does not allow that.

Never describe a warranty as longer or broader than it is.

## Step 5: Choose your channels

### Amazon US

Much US gadget and accessory shopping starts on Amazon. Strong listings, steady reviews and Sponsored Products, Brands and Display campaigns work together. Use our [marketplace profit calculator](/tools/marketplace-profit-calculator/) to check margin per unit after referral fees, fulfillment, ads and returns before you set a price.

### Your own store

A Shopify store holds your full range, bundles, warranty registration, setup guides and firmware notes. It is also where comparison and support content wins brand searches.

### Walmart Marketplace, TikTok Shop and eBay

Walmart Marketplace reaches value-focused households. TikTok Shop suits products that demo well in seconds, such as phone gear, lights and desk gadgets. eBay is a good home for refurbished, open-box and older-generation stock.

### Retail context

Big-box electronics retail, such as Best Buy, shapes what US shoppers expect: hands-on demos, clear return windows and support after purchase. Your listings and store should answer the questions a store associate would.

## Step 6: Plan for peak events

US electronics demand gathers around a handful of moments:

| Event | Typical focus |
|---|---|
| New Year | Gift card spending, fitness wearables, setup of holiday gifts |
| Prime Day (July) | Deals on hero models and accessories |
| Back to school | Laptop accessories, headphones, chargers, desk gear |
| Amazon's fall deal event | Early holiday shoppers |
| Black Friday and Cyber Monday | Bundles, hero model deals, stock depth |
| Holidays | Gift guides, delivery cut-offs, easy setup content |

Start planning weeks ahead: deal submissions, stock, coupons, budgets and reviews all need time.

Not every model should be discounted at every event. New launches often do better with bundles, such as a charger with a cable, than with deep price cuts, and constant discounting teaches shoppers to wait. Keep enough stock to avoid running out mid-event, because a stockout during a deal can hurt your ranking for weeks afterwards.

## Step 7: Collect genuine reviews

Reviews decide whether a buyer trusts an unknown brand over a known one. Request reviews through the marketplace's own tools or neutral post-purchase emails. Never pay for reviews, offer rewards for positive ones or filter who gets asked. Read negative reviews weekly; they often point to a listing fix.

## Step 8: Manage returns and fraud

Electronics returns often come from compatibility confusion, setup trouble or buyer's remorse, not faults. Some come from fraud, such as swapped or empty boxes.

### Protection checklist

- Clear compatibility and network notes on every listing.
- A quick-start card in the box and a setup video online.
- Serial numbers recorded at dispatch.
- Tamper-evident packaging for higher-value items.
- Return reasons logged by model, with the top reasons fixed on the listing.
- A grading process for returned units, with a plan for refurbished resale.

## Step 9: Keep helping after delivery

Fast, friendly support protects ratings. Publish setup guides, troubleshooting FAQs and firmware update notes. Answer questions within a day. Follow up after delivery with setup help, then offer compatible accessories. ## Common mistakes

- Listing before FCC, safety and battery documents are ready.
- Unsupported claims such as "longest battery life" or "fastest charging".
- Vague warranty terms, or terms hidden until after purchase.
- Ignoring compatibility questions in reviews and Q&A.
- Planning Prime Day or Black Friday too late to get deals approved.

## How to sell electronics online (USA): a launch checklist

1. Confirm the FCC route and collect grants or SDoC records.
2. Gather safety reports, UN 38.3 summaries and safety data sheets.
3. Write full or limited warranty terms and publish them before purchase.
4. Build spec-led listings with compatibility tables and setup guides.
5. Launch on Amazon US and your own store, then add channels.
6. Plan stock and deals for the next peak event.
7. Track returns, reviews and support tickets by model every week.
