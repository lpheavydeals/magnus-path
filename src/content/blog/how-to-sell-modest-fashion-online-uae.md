Working out how to sell modest fashion online in the UAE starts with respect for the customer. Many shoppers here dress modestly every day, not only on occasions, and they know exactly how an abaya should fall, how a shayla should sit and which fabrics stay comfortable through a long, hot day. They will notice a sheer fabric, a short hem or careless photography at once. This guide covers range, product detail, sizing, imagery, seasons, sales channels, creators and returns, so your store earns trust from the first order.

## Step 1: Choose a clear place in the modest fashion market

Modest fashion covers several quite different categories, and each has its own buyer and buying rhythm.

- **Abayas.** Everyday black abayas, coloured and embellished occasion abayas, and open styles worn over clothing. Buyers judge cut, fabric, finishing on cuffs and hems, and how the piece moves.
- **Kaftans and jalabiyas.** Popular for home, family gatherings and Ramadan evenings. Comfort, print and colour matter, along with a sense of occasion.
- **Shaylas and headscarves.** Bought often and in several colours, so fabric grip, breathability and colour range drive repeat orders.
- **Modest everyday wear.** Long shirts, wide trousers, maxi skirts and layering pieces for work and daily life, bought by women of many backgrounds living in the UAE.

Lead with one or two categories. A store known for well-cut everyday abayas, or for kaftans in good fabrics, is easier to advertise and easier to recommend than a store that sells a little of everything.

### Research before you buy fabric

Read reviews of modest fashion on Namshi, Noon and Amazon.ae, and the comments under creator posts. Complaints about sheer fabric, sleeves that are too short, colours that differ from photos or embellishments that snag tell you what to fix in production and what to explain clearly on the page.

## Step 2: Describe fabric and fit as if the shopper could touch it

Shoppers cannot feel the fabric online, so your words and images have to do that job.

### Fabric details to include

| Detail | What to write |
|---|---|
| Fabric name | Crepe, nida, chiffon, linen blend or jersey, named plainly |
| Weight and drape | Light and flowing, or structured and heavier |
| Opacity | Whether a lining or under layer is needed in daylight |
| Comfort in heat | Breathability and how it wears through a long day |
| Care | Washing, ironing and steaming advice |
| Finishing | Cuffs, hem, stitching and any embellishment |

Be honest about opacity. A shopper who finds a fabric more sheer than expected will not trust the next description, and will often say so in a review.

### Sizing for long garments

1. Give body measurements for bust, shoulder width and sleeve length.
2. Give the garment length from shoulder to hem in centimetres, because length is the most common reason for exchanges on abayas and kaftans.
3. Offer several lengths for core styles where production allows it.
4. Add a fit note per style, such as relaxed, straight or tailored.
5. Explain how to measure, ideally with a simple diagram and a short video.

Update fit notes from your exchange reasons. If one style keeps coming back as too long, say so on the page before the next order.

## Step 3: Photograph with care for your audience

Imagery for modest fashion should be something your customer would happily share in a family group chat.

- Style models modestly and consistently with the product, including hair coverage where it suits the collection.
- Many brands shoot some images without faces, or on hangers and well-pressed flat lays, which also suits shoppers who prefer them.
- Show the back, the sleeves, the cuffs and the hem, plus close-ups of fabric texture and any embroidery.
- Add a short video of the piece moving, because drape sells an abaya more than a still image can.
- Photograph colours in natural daylight and say if a shade looks different under indoor lighting.

Have Arabic product titles and descriptions written or checked by a fluent Arabic writer, and build right-to-left layouts properly so the Arabic store feels like a first choice rather than a translation.

## Step 4: Build the year around Ramadan and Eid

Ramadan and the two Eids are the most important moments for modest fashion, but the calendar holds more than that.

| Moment | What tends to sell |
|---|---|
| Weeks before Ramadan | Kaftans, jalabiyas and new abayas for iftar gatherings |
| Late Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr | Occasion abayas, matching family pieces, gift sets of shaylas |
| Eid al-Adha | Occasion wear for family visits and travel |
| Summer | Light fabrics, travel pieces and end-of-season sales |
| Cooler months and wedding season | Embellished and statement abayas |
| White Friday and Yellow Friday | Everyday abayas, shaylas and multi-buys |
| UAE National Day | Limited colourways and local-themed edits |

Ramadan follows the lunar calendar and moves earlier each year, so plan production from the expected dates and leave room for delivery delays. Set and publish a clear last order date for delivery before Eid, and keep it visible on product pages during the final week. Messaging during Ramadan should be calm and respectful: focus on gatherings, generosity and comfort rather than loud discount language.

## Step 5: Decide where to sell

### Your own store

Your Shopify or WooCommerce store holds the full collection, lookbooks, size guidance, exchanges and customer details. Offer cash on delivery, cards, Apple Pay and Tabby or Tamara instalments on higher-priced occasion pieces. Add a WhatsApp button for sizing questions, because many shoppers want a quick answer about length before they order.

### Namshi

Namshi suits fashion-led modest wear and shoppers who browse by trend. Its image and content standards are strict, so prepare consistent photography, measurements and descriptions before you apply.

### Noon and Amazon.ae

These reach shoppers who search by type, colour and price, which helps for everyday abayas, shaylas and basics. ## Step 6: Work with creators who already dress modestly

Instagram and Snapchat creators are central to modest fashion discovery in the Gulf, and TikTok is growing too.

### Choosing creators

- Pick creators whose everyday style already matches your collection, so the content feels natural.
- Look at comments and saves, not only follower numbers, to see whether their audience asks where pieces come from.
- Smaller creators with a close following often produce more useful content than large accounts.
- Agree how the piece will be styled and how it should be described, and clearly mark paid content as advertising in line with UAE rules.

### Using the content

Ask for styling videos, "how I wear it" reels and close-ups of fabric. With permission, use the strongest clips in Meta and Snapchat ads. ## Step 7: Make returns and exchanges simple and fair

Modest fashion buyers often want to exchange for a different length or size rather than return outright.

### Exchange checklist

- State the exchange window, condition rules and who pays for the courier on every product page.
- Arrange courier pickup rather than asking customers to drop off parcels.
- Ship the replacement quickly and confirm the new size on WhatsApp.
- Record every exchange reason by style, size and length.
- Fix the page or the pattern when the same reason repeats.

Confirm cash-on-delivery orders before dispatch with a short WhatsApp message that repeats the style, size and length. Hygiene rules for some items, such as undershawls or caps, may differ, so state them clearly. Make sure your policy follows UAE consumer protection rules; we are not lawyers, so check the wording with an adviser.

## Common mistakes

- Vague fabric names and no word about opacity.
- Sizes without garment length.
- Imagery that does not match how your customers dress.
- Ramadan collections launched in the middle of Ramadan.
- Exchanges that are hard to find or slow to arrive.
- Arabic pages produced by machine translation alone.

## How to sell modest fashion online: a launch checklist

1. Pick one lead category and a focused first collection.
2. Write fabric, opacity, care and length for every piece.
3. Shoot modest, consistent imagery and drape videos.
4. Launch an Arabic and English store with COD, cards and instalments.
5. List core pieces on Namshi, Noon or Amazon.ae.
6. Brief three to five creators whose style fits.
7. Set up WhatsApp confirmations and an exchange process.
8. Plan the Ramadan and Eid edit backwards from the expected dates.
