Learning how to sell Ayurvedic products online is partly a marketing question and partly a regulatory one. Ayurveda covers products licensed as medicines, products sold as cosmetics and products sold as foods, and each group has its own rules for labels, listings and ads. Brands that sort this out first launch faster, get fewer rejected ads and suppressed listings, and can focus on what actually grows the business: clear product pages, trust and repeat orders. This guide walks through the steps for Indian brands, from licensing to the first reorder.

We are an ecommerce growth team, not lawyers or regulatory consultants. Treat the legal points below as a checklist of questions to take to your adviser, not as legal advice.

## Step 1: classify every product before you sell Ayurvedic products online

Start with a simple spreadsheet. List every product and put it in one of three groups:

- **Ayurvedic medicines.** Classical formulations made to recipes from recognised texts, such as chyawanprash, triphala churna or an arishta, and proprietary Ayurvedic medicines that are your own formulations.
- **Ayurvedic or herbal cosmetics.** Face oils, ubtans, herbal shampoos and soaps that are meant for cleansing or beautifying rather than treating anything.
- **Herbal foods and nutraceuticals.** Teas, herbal powders sold as food, and some supplements, which generally fall under food safety rules.

This split matters because it decides who licenses the product, what the label must say, which marketplace category accepts it and how you can advertise it. Some products sit on a borderline. A hair oil, for example, can be licensed as a medicine or sold as a cosmetic, and the wording you can use differs between the two. Settle these questions with your adviser before you write any copy.

Then add a second column: classical or proprietary. Classical products compete on quality, sourcing and faithfulness to the text. Proprietary products compete on your brand and the experience of using them. Your product pages will treat them differently.

## Step 2: get licensing and labelling in order

### AYUSH licensing in general terms

In India, Ayurvedic medicines are manufactured under a licence from the state licensing authority under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and its rules, with the Ministry of AYUSH setting policy for the sector. Many D2C brands do not own a factory. They work with a contract manufacturer and sell under that manufacturer's licence, or take a loan licence that lets them use the manufacturer's facility under their own name. Each route has implications for whose name and licence number appear on the label.

Cosmetics and foods follow separate routes. Herbal cosmetics are covered by cosmetics rules, and herbal teas or food powders generally need registration or a licence from the food safety regulator. You will also need GST registration and, for packaged goods, labels that meet legal metrology requirements such as MRP and net quantity.

### What a compliant label usually carries

Labels for Ayurvedic medicines are generally expected to include:

| Label item | Why it matters online |
|---|---|
| Product name and type (classical or proprietary) | Marketplaces and ad reviewers check it |
| Ingredients with quantities | Buyers compare formulations before they buy |
| Manufacturing licence number | Asked for during category approval |
| Batch number, manufacturing and expiry dates | Needed for complaints and recalls |
| Dosage or directions for use | Must match what your product page says |
| Manufacturer name and address | Shows who made it, especially for loan-licence brands |
| Warnings and storage notes | Reduces misuse and returns |

Treat the physical label as the source of truth. Every listing, product page and ad should match it. Mismatches between what the label says and what the website claims are a common reason for marketplace suppressions and for awkward questions from regulators.

## Step 3: understand the advertising rules

Advertising is where most Ayurvedic brands run into trouble online. Three layers apply at once.

### Indian law

The Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act restricts advertisements that claim to diagnose, treat or prevent a listed set of diseases and conditions, and it applies to medicines of every system, including Ayurveda. The Drugs and Cosmetics Rules have also contained provisions specific to advertising Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani medicines, and these provisions have been changed and challenged in court in recent years. Consumer protection rules on misleading advertisements apply as well. Because this area keeps moving, ask your adviser for the current position before every major campaign.

### Platform policies

Meta, Google, Amazon, Flipkart and Nykaa all limit health claims, restrict the promotion of unapproved substances and review health-related ads and listings more strictly than other categories. Repeated rejections can restrict an ad account or a seller account, which pauses everything, not only the product in question.

### Practical wording rules

- Describe ingredients, where they come from and how the product is prepared.
- Explain how and when the product is used: a morning tea, a weekly oil massage, a spoonful of chyawanprash in winter.
- Name the classical text or the herb rather than a condition.
- Avoid words that promise to treat, heal or reverse anything.
- Avoid before-and-after images and disease-first hooks in ads.
- Keep dosage and safety notes on the product page, where buyers can read them in full.

## Step 4: build a store that earns trust

Your own website is where you can tell the full story, keep customer data and offer subscriptions. Shopify and WooCommerce both work well for Ayurvedic brands. What matters more than the platform is the product page.

### What every Ayurvedic product page needs

- **Ingredients** with common, Sanskrit and botanical names, and the quantity where the label shows it.
- **Source and preparation.** Where the herbs are grown, how the oil is cooked or the churna is ground, how long the process takes.
- **How to use it.** Dose or amount, time of day, with or without food, and how long a pack lasts.
- **Who it is for and who should be careful.** Pregnancy, children and existing medication notes, worded with your adviser.
- **Label image.** A clear photo of the back label so buyers can check licence and batch details.
- **Real reviews** about taste, texture, smell and routine, not outcomes.
- **Policies** for returns, damaged parcels and shipping times.

Classical products also benefit from a short note on the source text and how your version follows it. Proprietary products need a clear reason the blend exists.

### Content that brings search traffic

Many buyers search with questions: how to take triphala, ashwagandha powder or capsules, how to use kumkumadi oil. Short guides that answer these questions, linked to the matching product, bring buyers from Google and AI assistants. Keep the same claim discipline in content as in ads.

## Step 5: choose your marketplaces carefully

Marketplaces put you in front of buyers who already search for Ayurvedic products. They also have the strictest review processes.

| Channel | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon | Search-led demand for herbs and classical products | Category approval, document checks, claim-based suppressions |
| Flipkart | Reach in value-conscious and smaller-town markets | Price competition, returns on liquids |
| Nykaa | Ayurvedic skincare and haircare | Beauty positioning, content standards |
| Quick commerce | Fast-moving teas, oils and daily-use products | Dark-store listing rules, margins |

Before you list, prepare a document pack: manufacturing or loan licence, label images, brand trademark details and invoices from the manufacturer. Write titles and bullet points from the label, not from your ad copy. ## Step 6: packaging, shipping and returns

Ayurvedic products include oils, ghee-based preparations, powders and glass bottles. Each brings shipping risks.

- Use leak-proof caps and shrink bands on oils, and pack bottles upright with cushioning.
- Seal powders against moisture and add a desiccant where the formulation allows.
- Print the batch and expiry on the outer carton too, so damaged items can still be traced.
- Decide your policy for opened products. Most brands accept returns only for damaged, wrong or expired items, and say so clearly.
- Keep cash on delivery but confirm COD orders before dispatch to cut failed deliveries.

## Step 7: market for repeat purchase, not one-off sales

Ayurveda is a repeat category. A churna lasts a month, a hair oil a few weeks, a jar of chyawanprash a season. Your marketing should be built around that cycle.

### Acquisition

Run Meta and Google ads on two or three hero products that buyers reorder. Use creative about ingredients, ritual and daily use, and send traffic to product pages that match the ad's tone. Search campaigns on ingredient names and classical product names often bring buyers with clear intent.

### Retention

- Send a WhatsApp or email reminder a few days before a pack is expected to run out.
- Offer a subscription or a refill pack at a fair saving.
- Bundle products used together in a routine, such as an oil and a herbal hair wash.
- Ask for reviews after the buyer has used the product for a while, not on delivery day.

### Measuring

Track acquisition cost, repeat rate and revenue per customer over 90 days, by product. A product that looks expensive to acquire on the first order may pay back well once reorders come in. Our [LTV to CAC calculator](/tools/ltv-cac-calculator/) is a quick way to check that maths.

## How to sell Ayurvedic products online: a launch checklist

Before you switch on ads, work through this list:

- Every product classified as medicine, cosmetic or food, and as classical or proprietary
- Licence route confirmed for each product, and documents filed in one place
- Labels reviewed and photographed, with listings matching them
- A list of words your brand will never use in ads, agreed with your adviser
- Product pages with ingredients, directions, label image, reviews and policies
- One marketplace approved and listed, with fees checked
- Packaging tested for leaks and breakage
- Refill reminders and review requests set up from the first order
