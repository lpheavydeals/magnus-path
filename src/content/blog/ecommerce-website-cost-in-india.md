The ecommerce website cost in India ranges from almost nothing for a do-it-yourself template store to several lakh rupees for a custom or headless build. Most growing brands land somewhere in the middle: a template-based store set up by an agency for ₹50,000 to ₹60,000, or a custom Shopify build for ₹1.5–5 lakh. The setup fee is only part of the bill, though. This guide breaks down one-time and recurring costs, the factors that move the price, and how to choose without overpaying.

## Ecommerce website cost in India by store type

The table below shows typical one-time costs for the four common routes. The agency prices are our own starting prices, so use them as a reference point when comparing quotes.

| Store type | Typical one-time cost | Best for | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIY template store | Your time, plus platform plan and optional paid theme | Testing an idea, very small catalogue | A working store you set up yourself on Shopify or WooCommerce |
| Template + agency setup | From ₹50,000 (WooCommerce) or ₹60,000 (Shopify) | New D2C brands, small to mid catalogues | Theme setup and styling, product upload, payments, shipping, basic SEO, training |
| Custom Shopify store | ₹1.5–5 lakh | Brands with a clear identity and growing sales | Custom design, custom sections, integrations, speed and conversion work |
| Custom or headless build | From ₹2,00,000 | Complex catalogues, B2B pricing, unusual workflows | Bespoke front end and features, built around your operations |

A few notes on reading this table:

- **DIY is not free.** You still pay the platform plan, a domain, possibly a premium theme, and your own hours. Mistakes in payments, tax or shipping settings can cost more than the agency fee.
- **Template does not mean generic.** A good template store is styled to your brand, structured for search and fast on mobile. Most new brands should start here.
- **Custom is worth it when the template blocks you.** If your product needs a configurator, bundles, B2B price lists or deep ERP integration, a custom build pays for itself.

## Shopify store cost in India

Shopify is a hosted platform, so you do not pay for hosting separately. Your costs are:

- **Setup:** from ₹60,000 for a template-based store with an agency, or ₹1.5–5 lakh for a custom build.
- **Monthly plan:** Shopify charges a monthly subscription that rises with the plan tier. Check Shopify's current India pricing before you budget, as plans and offers change.
- **Theme:** free themes work well for many brands. Premium themes are a one-time purchase.
- **Apps:** reviews, subscriptions, WhatsApp, upsells and loyalty are usually paid apps with monthly fees.

Shopify suits most D2C brands because it is stable, secure and quick to manage without a developer. If you want a team to build it, see our [Shopify store development service](/shopify-development/).

## WooCommerce website cost

WooCommerce is a free plugin that runs on WordPress. The software costs nothing, but the running costs move elsewhere:

- **Setup:** from ₹50,000 with an agency for a template-based store.
- **Hosting:** you need good managed hosting. Cheap shared hosting is the most common reason WooCommerce stores feel slow.
- **Plugins:** many useful extensions are paid, often as yearly licences.
- **Maintenance:** WordPress, WooCommerce and plugins need regular updates, backups and security checks.

WooCommerce makes sense if you already run a WordPress site, want full control of your data and code, or need content-heavy pages alongside the shop. Our [WooCommerce development team](/woocommerce-development/) can help you decide if it fits.

## Recurring costs to budget for

The ecommerce website price in India that agencies quote is usually the setup fee only. These are the costs that continue every month or year:

| Recurring cost | Shopify | WooCommerce | Custom / headless |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform or software | Monthly Shopify plan (check current India pricing) | Free core software | Depends on stack; may include licences |
| Hosting | Included in plan | Paid separately, monthly or yearly | Paid separately, often cloud hosting |
| Domain | Yearly renewal | Yearly renewal | Yearly renewal |
| Payment gateway | Around 2% per transaction; check your gateway | Around 2% per transaction; check your gateway | Around 2% per transaction; check your gateway |
| Apps and plugins | Monthly app fees | Yearly plugin licences | Custom code or third-party services |
| Maintenance | Light: theme and app updates | Regular: core, plugin and security updates | Developer retainer usually needed |

Payment gateway fees deserve a closer look. Most Indian gateways charge a percentage per transaction, commonly around 2% for standard methods, with different rates for some cards, international payments or EMI. Always confirm the exact rates with your gateway, because the fee applies to every order for the life of the store.

## Factors that change ecommerce website development cost

Two stores on the same platform can differ in cost by several times. These are the factors that drive ecommerce website development cost the most:

### Design approach
A styled template is the cheapest route. Custom page designs, custom product page layouts and brand-specific sections add design and development time.

### Catalogue size and product data
Fifty products with clean data upload quickly. Two thousand products with variants, size charts and missing images take real effort. Clean spreadsheets save money.

### Integrations
Each integration adds scope: shipping aggregators, ERP or inventory software, accounting tools, WhatsApp, CRM, marketplaces like Amazon and Flipkart, or B2B quote systems.

### Features beyond the basics
Subscriptions, bundles, product customisers, multi-currency selling, wholesale price lists, loyalty programmes and multi-language stores all increase cost.

### Content and photography
Product photos, lifestyle shoots, product descriptions and category copy are often left out of quotes. They matter for both sales and SEO.

### Speed, SEO and conversion work
A store that loads fast on mobile, has clean URLs and structured data, and has a simple checkout costs more to build properly. It also sells more, so this is not the place to cut.

## Hidden costs most brands miss

These are the line items that surprise founders after launch:

- **Paid apps that add up.** Five apps at a modest monthly fee each quickly become a meaningful recurring cost.
- **Premium theme licence.** Often not included in the agency fee.
- **Product photography and copy.** Needed before launch, not after.
- **Tax and invoicing setup.** GST-compliant invoices and correct tax settings may need an app or accountant time.
- **Post-launch fixes.** Small changes in the first few months are normal. Check whether your quote includes a support period.
- **Migration.** Moving from an old site means redirects, data transfer and SEO protection. Skipping redirects can lose rankings you already have.
- **Marketing.** A store with no traffic makes no sales. Budget for ads, SEO or marketplace work from the start.

## How to choose the right option for your budget

Use this simple decision path:

1. **Just testing an idea with a few products?** Start with a DIY or template store. Keep the budget for marketing.
2. **Launching a serious D2C brand?** Go for a template-based Shopify store with agency setup. It is the best value for most brands.
3. **Already selling and outgrowing your template?** Move to a custom Shopify build in the ₹1.5–5 lakh range.
4. **Complex catalogue, B2B pricing or unusual workflows?** Consider custom or headless development from ₹2,00,000. Read our guide to [custom ecommerce website development](/ecommerce-website-development/) for what that involves.

If you are unsure which platform fits, our guide to the [best ecommerce platform in India](/blog/best-ecommerce-platform-in-india/) compares Shopify, WooCommerce, Magento and custom builds in detail.

A good rule: spend enough to get a fast, trustworthy, mobile-friendly store, then put the rest into getting customers. A beautiful store with no marketing budget will struggle.

### How to build a first-year budget

Instead of asking "what does a website cost?", work out what the store costs to own for the first twelve months. List these lines in a simple spreadsheet:

- **One-time setup:** the agency or developer fee for your chosen route.
- **Pre-launch content:** product photography, descriptions and any brand assets you still need.
- **Platform and hosting:** twelve months of your Shopify plan, or hosting plus plugin licences for WooCommerce.
- **Apps:** each paid app's monthly fee multiplied by twelve.
- **Domain and email:** yearly renewals.
- **Payment fees:** around 2% of your expected sales (confirm with your gateway).
- **Maintenance:** a support retainer or an hourly budget for changes.
- **Marketing:** ads, SEO or marketplace management to bring buyers to the store.

This view often changes the decision. A cheaper setup that needs many paid plugins and frequent developer help can cost more over a year than a slightly higher, cleaner build.

## Questions to ask an agency before you sign

Ask every agency the same questions so you can compare quotes fairly:

- What exactly is included: pages, sections, number of products uploaded, integrations?
- Is the theme free or premium, and who pays for the licence?
- Which paid apps or plugins will the store need, and what will they cost monthly?
- How many design revisions are included?
- Who owns the store, domain, theme and code after launch?
- What does post-launch support cover, and for how long?
- How will you handle speed, mobile experience and basic SEO setup?
- If we are migrating, how will you protect existing rankings with redirects?
- What do you need from us, and by when, to launch on time?

A clear written scope protects both sides. Be wary of quotes that are very low with no detail, or very high with no explanation of where the money goes. Our [pricing page](/pricing/) lists our starting prices openly so you have a benchmark.
