Most guides on how to sell jewellery online Australia brands find were written for other countries, and they get the most important detail wrong. Australia has no compulsory national hallmarking system. There is no government stamp to point to, so the burden of proof sits with you: your product page, your photos and your records. That makes honesty the strongest marketing tool a jeweller has here. This guide is for Australian makers, demi-fine labels, opal sellers, engagement ring studios and South Asian gold jewellers selling online.

The rules are summarised in general terms. We are an ecommerce growth team, not lawyers or gemmologists, so check anything specific with a legal adviser and your suppliers.

## Choose your segment first

Each segment buys differently, and mixing them on one store without structure confuses buyers.

| Segment | How buyers decide | What the store needs |
|---|---|---|
| Fashion jewellery | Quickly, on look and price | Strong photos, fast checkout, Afterpay |
| Demi-fine and vermeil | On style plus material quality | Clear plating details and care notes |
| Sterling silver | On design and value | Fineness stated, tarnish care |
| Solid gold fine jewellery | Slowly, comparing metal and finish | Karat, weight, scale shots, reviews |
| Engagement and bridal | Over weeks, often as a couple | Stone details, video calls, studio visits |
| Opal and Australian stones | On colour, origin and story | Opal type, origin and honest photos |
| Gold for South Asian buyers | On purity, design and occasion | Karat, weight, wedding and festival edits |

## Describing metals when there is no national hallmark

Stamps such as 9ct or 375, 18ct or 750, and 925 for sterling silver are common in Australian jewellery, and many makers use them. But the law that matters most online is the Australian Consumer Law, which prohibits misleading or deceptive conduct. The ACCC can act on descriptions that mislead, and buyers can claim a remedy when a piece is not as described.

In practice, that means using precise words everywhere a piece appears: title, description, filters, ads, marketplace listings and packaging.

- **Solid gold:** give the karat, and the weight where it matters to buyers
- **Gold-plated:** name the base metal underneath and say plating can wear
- **Gold-filled:** explain that a thicker layer of gold is bonded to a base metal
- **Vermeil:** say it is gold over sterling silver, and give the gold karat where known
- **Silver:** state 925 sterling or another fineness, and how to deal with tarnish

If a piece contains nickel or other metals some people react to, say so. Buyers with sensitive skin search for this and will return items that surprise them.

## Lab-grown, natural and simulant stones

Lab-grown diamonds are now a normal part of engagement and fine jewellery in Australia. They are diamonds in composition, but buyers have a right to know which is which. Put "lab-grown" in the product title, filters and ads, not only deep in the description. Avoid wording such as "real diamond" without the origin beside it.

Simulants are different again. Moissanite and cubic zirconia look like diamonds but are other materials, and should be named plainly. Treated stones, such as heated sapphires or filled emeralds, should be described as treated. Where you hold grading reports from a recognised laboratory, link them.

Claims such as ethical, recycled, conflict-free or Australian-made need evidence. These are claims a buyer cannot check for themselves, and the ACCC has shown strong interest in environmental and ethical claims. Keep supplier documents, say exactly what each claim covers and avoid broad statements.

### A note on opals

Opal is Australia's national gemstone and a strong online story, but it is easy to describe loosely. A solid opal is one piece of natural opal. A doublet is a thin slice of opal glued to a backing, and a triplet adds a clear cap on top. All three are legitimate products at very different prices. Say which one a piece is in its title, and show the side profile in a photo so buyers can see the layers.

## How to sell jewellery online Australia buyers trust

The product page does the work of a counter assistant, so it needs to answer every question a careful buyer would ask in person.

### What to shoot for every hero piece

- A clean product shot on a neutral background, true to colour
- On-body photos and a short video in daylight, showing how it sits and moves
- A scale shot on a hand or beside a coin, so size is obvious
- Close-ups of clasps, settings and any stamps
- Each metal colour the design comes in

Avoid retouching that makes a stone brighter or a metal warmer than it is. That can mislead and leads to returns.

### Sizing

Australia uses letter ring sizes, as the UK does, rather than the numbers common in the US. Show a size chart with letters, offer a printable ring sizer or a free sizer by post, and explain resizing terms. Give chain lengths in centimetres with a photo of each length worn.

### Product page details

Include metal, karat or fineness, plating, stone type and origin, dimensions, weight where useful, care instructions, delivery times, the returns and resizing policy, and how to contact you before buying. For engagement rings, offer a video call so buyers can see the ring move in real light. ## Payments, fraud checks and order confirmation

Afterpay, Zip and other buy now, pay later options are widely used for gifts and mid-priced pieces, so show them on product pages where they apply. Prices should include GST. For high-value orders, use your payment provider's fraud screening, check that billing and delivery details match, and confirm the order personally by email or phone before dispatch. A short confirmation message with the expected delivery date reduces anxious enquiries.

## Insured delivery and packaging

Jewellery is small, valuable and a target for theft. Choose services with tracking, signature on delivery and cover that matches the value of the order, and read each carrier's rules on jewellery and maximum cover before relying on it. Australia Post and courier services set their own limits, so confirm them in writing for your typical order values.

Use plain outer packaging that does not advertise your brand, seal it so tampering is obvious and keep the presentation box inside. Photograph or video high-value pieces as they are packed. State delivery times by region, since Perth, Darwin and regional addresses take longer.

## Returns, resizing and consumer rights

Australian law does not require refunds for change of mind, so your policy decides that. Many jewellers offer a short exchange window for unworn pieces and exclude engraved or custom work. Separately, the consumer rights under the Australian Consumer Law apply when a piece is faulty, not as described or different from its photos, and no policy can remove them. Set out resizing, repairs and re-plating services on the same page.

## The Australian jewellery calendar

- **Valentine's Day:** gifts at every price point; plan creative from early January
- **Mother's Day in May:** birthstones, initials and personalised pieces
- **EOFY in June:** clear older designs and samples
- **Click Frenzy and Black Friday:** gift-with-purchase offers protect your pricing better than deep cuts
- **Christmas and New Year:** gifting plus the busiest stretch for proposals, so build engagement content through spring
- **Boxing Day:** self-purchase and post-Christmas treats
- **Diwali and wedding season:** gold, temple and bridal sets for Indian-Australian families; spring and autumn are also busy wedding months more widely

## Count the real costs

Before setting prices, add up metal and stones, making, findings, packaging, photography, insured postage, payment and marketplace fees, returns, resizing and the advertising needed to win a buyer who has never heard of you. Gold and silver prices move, so review prices for solid pieces regularly rather than once a year. A brand selling mostly gifts at modest prices and a studio selling engagement rings can afford very different costs per customer, so set targets for each segment separately.

## Getting your first buyers

Start where buying intent already exists. Search campaigns on specific designs, such as solid gold huggies or lab-grown oval engagement rings, reach people ready to compare. Instagram and TikTok styling videos suit fashion and demi-fine pieces. Markets and design fairs let people touch your work and join your email list. Ask your first buyers for photo reviews, because real hands wearing real pieces persuade the next buyer more than studio shots. Gift buyers who were happy once often return for birthdays and anniversaries, so ask for the occasion at checkout and send a reminder the following year.
