Anyone researching how to start a cosmetics brand in the UAE soon meets three challenges: demanding buyers, strong mall brands and a climate that puts every formula under stress. Shoppers here compare products at Sephora and Faces, on Amazon.ae and Noon, and through creators on Instagram, Snapchat and TikTok. There is still room for focused brands that serve a clear buyer well. This guide walks through the steps in order, from niche and company set-up to registration, packaging, numbers, store, launch and day-to-day operations.

It is general guidance, not legal or business set-up advice. Check each step with a set-up consultant, regulatory adviser or accountant.

## Step 1: Define your niche and first range

### Name the buyer

"Makeup for UAE women" is not a niche. A young professional who wants office-friendly makeup that survives the commute, a bride preparing for a long wedding night or a woman looking for alcohol-free products for daily wear are each specific enough to build a brand around. Decide who you serve first and write down what she buys today and why she would switch.

### Start with a short range

Three to six products are plenty. Lip tints, kohl, brow products and setting sprays are easier to start with than foundation, which needs many shades and generates more returns. A small range also means fewer products to register, label and keep in stock.

### Study the market first

Spend a few weeks browsing Amazon.ae, Noon and Namshi, visiting mall counters and reading reviews in Arabic and English. Note what buyers praise, what they complain about and which needs are poorly served, such as products that slide in humidity or shade ranges that skip deeper tones.

## Step 2: Set up the business

In general terms, you will need a UAE company licensed for the activities you plan, including e-commerce or trading. You can usually choose a mainland licence or a free zone company; each has different rules on where you can sell, how goods move and what premises you need. A business set-up consultant can explain the trade-offs.

Other basics to plan:

- **Bank account and payment gateway.** Needed before your store can take cards, Apple Pay, Tabby or Tamara.
- **VAT.** Registration is required once taxable turnover passes the threshold, and VAT is currently 5%. An accountant can confirm when you need to register.
- **Trademark.** Register your brand name with the Ministry of Economy before investing heavily in packaging, and check it is free in the GCC markets you may enter later.
- **Data protection.** The UAE's personal data protection law applies to customer details and marketing consent.

## Step 3: Choose a manufacturer

Very few new brands build their own factory; nearly all start with a contract manufacturer. Options include manufacturers in the UAE, including free zones, and suppliers in India, Korea, Turkey or Europe.

Ask each candidate:

- Can they supply the documents needed for registration, including full formulas and ingredient percentages?
- Have the formulas been stability tested at high temperatures?
- What are the minimum order quantities per shade?
- Can they support halal-friendly or alcohol-free formulations if that is part of your position?
- How do they handle batch records, recalls and complaints?

Order samples, wear-test them through a humid evening and a long air-conditioned day, and ask friends with different skin tones to try them before you commit.

## Step 4: Sort registration, labels and claims

### Montaji registration

Broadly speaking, Dubai requires cosmetics to be on Dubai Municipality's Montaji register before sale, with the application usually filed by a company licensed in the UAE. Requests can include formulas, label artwork, manufacturer details and documents from the country of origin. Marketplaces may ask for proof before approving listings. Other emirates and federal bodies have their own requirements, so confirm the route for each product.

### Arabic labels

Labels are generally expected to carry key details in Arabic as well as English, printed or on a sticker: product name, function, ingredients, directions, warnings, origin, batch and dates. Have your adviser review artwork before you print.

### Claims

Keep claims cosmetic and supported. Long-wear, transfer-resistant and sweat-resistant wording needs test results behind it. Halal-friendly and alcohol-free wording needs a recognised certificate or clear ingredient evidence. Anything that sounds like treating a skin condition can move a product into a medical category.

### How long it usually takes

Formula work, stability testing, company set-up, registration and printing often run one after another. Many founders plan several months from first sample to first sale and add a buffer before Ramadan or White Friday.

## Step 5: Design packaging and a brand people remember

### Packaging for heat and delivery

UAE summers are hard on cosmetics. Lipsticks soften in parked cars, compacts can crack in transit and liquids expand. Choose components that tolerate heat, use inner seals and shrink bands on liquids, and test shipping by sending samples to yourself in summer. Leave space on packs for Arabic text rather than squeezing it onto a sticker later, and make sure shade names are readable in both languages.

### A name and look that work in two languages

Check how your brand name sounds and reads in Arabic as well as English, and make sure it does not carry an unintended meaning in either. Shade names deserve the same care: playful English names can confuse Arabic-speaking buyers, so many brands pair a short name with a number. Decide early on a small set of colours, fonts that support Arabic script and a photography style with natural light and real skin.

Give buyers a reason to remember you beyond the logo. That might be a clear promise, such as makeup tested for humid evenings, a values-led angle such as alcohol-free formulas where you can support it, or a shade range that genuinely covers deeper and olive tones. Whatever you choose, repeat it on every pack, page, ad and creator brief until people associate it with your name.

## Step 6: Check the unit economics

Before ordering stock, add up what each unit really costs: formula, components, printing, freight, duties, registration, storage, delivery, payment and marketplace fees, refused cash on delivery parcels, returns and the ad cost of each order. Then compare that with the price buyers will pay. Try different prices in our [profit margin calculator](/tools/profit-margin-calculator/) to see what each unit leaves behind. If the margin only works with no returns and cheap ads, rethink the range or the price before you launch.

## Step 7: Open your store and marketplace listings

### Your own store

Shopify suits most new UAE cosmetics brands. Launch with both languages, the Arabic drafted or reviewed by a fluent Arabic writer and set right to left. Each product page needs swatches on several skin tones, finish and wear details, ingredients, AED prices including VAT and clear delivery and return terms. Checkout should accept cards, Apple Pay, cash on delivery and Tabby or Tamara, and a WhatsApp link should sit close by for pre-sale questions.

### Marketplaces

| Channel | Role for a new brand |
|---|---|
| Own store | Full range, shade guides, bundles, customer data |
| Amazon.ae | Search demand for hero products, sponsored ads |
| Noon | Price-aware buyers, strong around White Friday and Ramadan |
| Namshi | Giftable sets for fashion and beauty browsers |

Mall retailers such as Sephora, Faces and Boots UAE choose what they stock. Strong direct sales and reviews help a future conversation, but no one can promise you a place on their shelves.

## Step 8: Plan a staged launch

1. **Six to eight weeks before:** teasers in both languages and a waitlist on your store.
2. **Four weeks before:** send products to a small group of creators, checking each one's UAE Media Council advertiser permit first.
3. **Launch week:** ads on Instagram, Snapchat and TikTok, messages to the waitlist and listings live on marketplaces.
4. **The following weeks:** tutorials, real-skin swatches, review requests and restock alerts.

Time launches around the UAE calendar: Ramadan and the run-up to Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Mother's Day in March, White Friday, UAE National Day, the Dubai Shopping Festival and Diwali for the Indian community. Ramadan launches call for gentler, more modest creative that respects local expectations of taste in advertising. ## Step 9: Fulfilment, COD and returns

- **Fulfilment.** Use a fulfilment partner or marketplace fulfilment for faster delivery, and ask how stock is stored in summer.
- **Cash on delivery.** Confirm COD orders by WhatsApp before dispatch and judge ads on delivered, paid orders.
- **Returns.** Set clear hygiene rules for opened makeup, offer shade exchanges where you can, and always take back faulty products.
- **Customer service.** Reply in Arabic or English, quickly, and keep answers within cosmetic wording.
- **Reporting.** Review sales, returns, refusals and reorders by product every month.

## First-year mistakes to avoid

- Launching foundation in a few shades and facing high returns.
- Printing packaging before Arabic label details are confirmed.
- Listing products before registration is complete.
- Making long-wear claims without tests in real heat.
- Paying creators without checking permits.
- Spending the whole budget on launch week, with nothing left for follow-up.

## Thinking about the GCC

Once the UAE is working, Saudi Arabia is the usual next step, followed by Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman. Saudi Arabia's SFDA and the regulators in other Gulf states set their own registration and label rules, so give each market its own compliance plan.

## How to start a cosmetics brand: UAE checklist

- [ ] One clear buyer and a range of three to six products
- [ ] Company licensed for e-commerce or trading, with bank account and gateway
- [ ] Trademark application filed
- [ ] Manufacturer chosen, with heat stability tests and registration documents
- [ ] Registration status confirmed for every product and shade
- [ ] Arabic label details approved before printing
- [ ] Claims matched to tests and certificates
- [ ] Unit economics worked out per channel, including COD refusals
- [ ] Arabic and English store with COD, Tabby or Tamara and clear returns
- [ ] Launch plan with permitted creators and stock ready
